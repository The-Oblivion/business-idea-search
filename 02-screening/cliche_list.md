# Cliché reference list (predictable / crowded 2026 startup patterns)

Three independent agents (a tired seed investor, a '1,000 people + AIs' consensus predictor, and an accelerator partner) each listed the idea patterns that are predictable, crowded, or already dominated. An idea matching one of these is a cliché unless it has a genuinely unusual customer, wedge, or insight.

## List 1
- **[AI apps] AI customer support agent** — e.g. "An AI chatbot that answers customer tickets for small businesses." / "A voice agent that picks up the phone for restaurants and clinics." (Support is the default use case people know from ChatGPT, and 2026 funding coverage (for example Sierra's roughly $950M raise at a $15B+ valuation) keeps it in the news. It is also one of the categories flagged as saturated.)
- **[AI apps] AI personal / executive assistant** — e.g. "An AI that runs your inbox, calendar and to-dos." / "A Jarvis for busy professionals." (Every assistant is pitched with the sci-fi 'Jarvis' image. It is the first thing someone imagines when they hear 'agents', so the idea is extremely crowded.)
- **[AI apps] AI writing / content / marketing-copy generator** — e.g. "AI that writes SEO blog posts and social captions." / "ChatGPT for marketers." (This is the first product any LLM user tries. Search results list AI writing assistants as having 100+ funded competitors, with incumbents shipping the feature natively.)
- **[AI apps] AI meeting notetaker / summarizer** — e.g. "An AI that joins Zoom, takes notes and emails action items." (It is a visible daily annoyance with an obvious LLM fix. It is also listed among the oversaturated AI categories because Zoom, Teams and Google now bundle it.)
- **[AI apps] AI resume / job-search / career coach** — e.g. "AI that rewrites your resume and applies to jobs for you." / "An AI interview coach." (Job hunting is a universal pain that people have personally felt, and it is easy to imagine as a prompt. Resume builders appear on the 'skip it' lists of saturated AI ideas.)
- **[AI apps] AI tutor / personalized education** — e.g. "A personal AI tutor for every kid." / "AI that turns any textbook into a custom course." ('Every student gets a private tutor' is the standard AI-and-education story. Education is a safe, virtuous answer that most people reach for.)
- **[AI apps] AI companion / friend / therapist chatbot** — e.g. "An AI friend that remembers you." / "An AI therapist available 24/7." (Loneliness and the mental-health access gap are widely discussed. Consumer chat products make companionship the obvious consumer-AI extension.)
- **[AI apps] AI sales / SDR outreach agent** — e.g. "An AI SDR that finds leads and sends personalized cold emails." (Revenue-generating agents sound easy to sell. 'Replace a role with an agent' is the dominant 2026 framing echoed by YC-style requests for startups.)
- **[AI apps] AI coding assistant / vibe-coding app builder** — e.g. "Describe an app and the AI builds and deploys it." / "Lovable, but for [niche]." (Coding tools are the most visible success story in AI, so people copy the winners and assume a niche version will also work.)
- **[AI apps] AI image / video generation for ads and creators** — e.g. "AI UGC video ads for e-commerce brands." / "AI that turns a product photo into a commercial." (Generative media is the most demoable and shareable AI category. Combined with the e-commerce boom, ad creative is the obvious low-effort pitch.)
- **[AI apps] Generic 'ChatGPT for [industry]' wrapper** — e.g. "ChatGPT for lawyers / dentists / real-estate agents." (It is the lowest-effort template for an AI idea. Sources on startup ideas describe it as the first bucket, generic wrappers with no moat, and say it is already commoditized.)
- **[AI apps] AI agent infrastructure, security, evals and compliance** — e.g. "Guardrails and testing for AI agents." / "AI compliance software for the EU AI Act." ('Picks and shovels' is the sophisticated-sounding consensus answer. Agent reliability and safety were a major cluster in the 2026 YC batches, and EU AI Act enforcement in August 2026 gives it a news hook.)
- **[AI services] AI automation agency** — e.g. "I'll build AI workflows and chatbots for local businesses." / "An n8n / Zapier automation consultancy." (It needs almost no capital and rides the hype, so influencers and AI assistants recommend it as the first move for a non-technical founder.)
- **[Vertical AI] AI for legal (contract review, drafting, discovery)** — e.g. "AI that reviews contracts in minutes." / "An AI paralegal." (Law is the standard example of 'high hourly rates plus lots of text.' It is the canonical vertical-AI pitch, supported by the 2026 investor move from generic AI toward vertical AI.)
- **[Vertical AI] AI medical scribe / healthcare admin automation** — e.g. "An AI that writes doctors' notes and handles prior authorizations." / "AI billing and insurance-claims automation." (Healthcare paperwork burden is a well-known problem, and scribes are a much-cited early AI success. Both people and assistants name it when asked for a big-market vertical.)
- **[Vertical AI] AI or SaaS for 'boring' trades and SMBs (HVAC, roofing, plumbing, pest control)** — e.g. "AI dispatch and quoting software for HVAC companies." / "An AI receptionist for plumbers." ('Go after boring industries' is now the standard advice to anyone worried about generic AI competition. Search results name it among the lowest-saturation categories, so it is the default contrarian answer.)
- **[Vertical AI] AI for accounting, bookkeeping and finance ops** — e.g. "AI bookkeeping that closes the books automatically." / "AI tax prep for freelancers." (Repetitive, rules-based, and painful, so it looks like an easy fit for automation. Founders often add 'for small business' or 'for freelancers' as the angle.)
- **[Vertical AI] AI for real estate (listings, lead follow-up, property analysis)** — e.g. "AI that writes listings and follows up with leads for agents." / "AI underwriting for landlords." (Real estate is familiar and large, and agents are seen as tech-hungry. It combines two consensus themes, property and AI.)
- **[Physical AI] Humanoid / home robots** — e.g. "A robot that folds laundry and does the dishes." / "Affordable humanoid robots for warehouses." (Robotics is the loudest 2026 funding story. Crunchbase-style coverage reports about $18.8B raised by robotics startups so far in 2026 versus $15B in all of 2025. Industrials also grew to about 23% of a recent YC batch.)
- **[Physical AI] Defense tech and drones** — e.g. "Low-cost drone swarms." / "Counter-drone systems for the military." (Anduril's roughly $5B raise at a $61B valuation made defense the visible 'serious' sector. At least 8 companies in a YC batch were building munitions, drone or counter-UAS products, and sovereignty concerns keep it in the news.)
- **[Physical AI] Energy for AI data centers (nuclear/SMR, solar plus storage, grid)** — e.g. "Small modular reactors to power data centers." / "Battery storage startups for the grid." ('AI needs power' has become the standard second-order idea. Infrastructure-heavy, mission-critical sectors are where investors are concentrating.)
- **[Climate] EV charging / carbon credits / sustainability tech** — e.g. "A network of EV chargers for apartment buildings." / "A marketplace for verified carbon credits." (Climate is the classic 'business that does good' answer that people have heard for over a decade. It is less fashionable than AI but still commonly named.)
- **[Health] Longevity / personalized health / wearables plus AI health coach** — e.g. "An AI health coach that reads your bloodwork and wearable data." / "A longevity clinic or supplement brand." (Ozempic-era attention to weight, plus the longevity boom and wearables, make personalized health the obvious consumer-health pitch.)
- **[Health] Mental health / sleep / meditation app** — e.g. "A wellness app for anxiety and burnout." / "A sleep-improvement app." (Mental health is socially salient, and the app-plus-subscription model is familiar. It is the go-to idea for anyone wanting a mission-driven consumer product.)
- **[Health] Senior care / aging-in-place tech** — e.g. "Home monitoring tech for elderly parents." / "An app connecting families with caregivers." (The aging-population demographic story is well known. Search results list senior care tech among the low-saturation categories, so it now gets named as the 'smart demographic bet'.)
- **[Consumer] Healthy food / functional beverage / protein-snack brand** — e.g. "A protein soda or gut-health drink." / "A healthy snack brand for kids." (Grocery-aisle success stories such as Olipop, Prime and Quest give a visible template. 'Make a healthier version of X' is the reflexive food idea.)
- **[Consumer] Pet products and services** — e.g. "Premium fresh dog food subscription." / "An Airbnb for pets." (Pet owners spending like parents is a well-worn talking point. It is a safe, emotionally driven consumer category.)
- **[Consumer] Subscription box / meal kit** — e.g. "A monthly box for [hobby]." / "Personalized meal kits." (This is the 2010s consumer-startup formula, and it still comes up because it is easy to picture and needs no invention.)
- **[Consumer] Sustainable / eco-friendly / refill products** — e.g. "Zero-waste household refills." / "Biodegradable packaging." (Sustainability is the standard 'good business' theme and needs no domain knowledge to suggest. It is mostly a legacy of the 2018-2022 wave, but people still say it.)
- **[Consumer] Dropshipping / print-on-demand / Amazon FBA** — e.g. "Start a Shopify dropshipping store." / "Sell custom t-shirts with print on demand." (This is the most-promoted low-capital business online, and social media pushes it as the entry point for anyone starting from nothing.)
- **[Consumer] Thrift / resale marketplace** — e.g. "A resale app for [niche: sneakers, kids' clothes, luxury bags]." / "Vintage clothing reselling." (Depop, Vinted and ThredUp proved the model, and sustainability and cost-of-living pressures reinforce it. People copy it with a niche.)
- **[Creator] Content creator / newsletter / personal brand / social media agency** — e.g. "Start a YouTube channel or newsletter in your niche." / "Manage social media for local businesses." (Creator-economy success stories are highly visible, and a social media agency is the standard first service business for anyone with no capital.)
- **[Creator] Online courses / coaching / consulting** — e.g. "Sell a course on what you know." / "Become a productivity or business coach." (This is the classic answer for anyone with any expertise. It is the default 'no-inventory' recommendation from both people and AI assistants.)
- **[Marketplace] 'Uber for X' gig marketplace / freelancer platform** — e.g. "Uber for home repairs / tutors / dog walkers." / "A freelancer marketplace for [niche]." (It is the timeless startup template. It is easy to apply to any industry and rarely comes with a plan for supply-side liquidity.)
- **[Social] Niche social network / community / dating app** — e.g. "A social network for [hobby or profession]." / "A dating app for busy professionals." (Everyone has felt the gap between big platforms and niche needs. It keeps recurring even though network-effect cold starts are very hard.)
- **[Local / low capital] Home and outdoor services (cleaning, lawn care, pressure washing, car detailing)** — e.g. "Start a pressure washing or window cleaning business." / "A mobile car detailing service." (These require little capital and are widely recommended by side-hustle content. Assistants suggest them as the safest offline answer.)
- **[Local / low capital] Café / restaurant / food truck / ghost kitchen** — e.g. "Open a specialty coffee shop." / "Start a food truck or cloud kitchen." (Food is the most tangible business people can picture, and it is the default 'I could run that' dream. Assistants list it because it is common, despite low margins.)
- **[Real estate] Short-term rentals / Airbnb arbitrage / property management** — e.g. "Rent apartments and list them on Airbnb." / "A property-management service for absentee owners." (Property ownership is the classic wealth-building answer, and the short-term-rental boom made it an internet-famous business model.)
- **[Fintech] Stablecoin / crypto payments / AI-native finance tools** — e.g. "Cross-border payments on stablecoins." / "A crypto wallet for everyday spending." (Funding coverage says fintech is back with money favoring payments, stablecoins and AI-native finance. Stablecoin payments have become the new 'obvious' crypto use case.)
- **[Fintech] Personal finance / budgeting / investing app for Gen Z** — e.g. "A budgeting app that automatically saves for you." / "An investing app for beginners." (Money stress is universal, and Robinhood, Mint and Acorns give clear templates. It is the standard consumer-fintech answer.)

## List 2
- **Ambient AI scribe for one more medical specialty** — e.g. "Abridge, but for dentists / vets / physical therapists / behavioral health." "Ambient documentation for home health nurses." (Abridge raised $300M at a $5.3B valuation in 2025 and added another $316M in 2026. Ambience, Heidi, Commure, Suki and Nuance already sell the core product, and the EHR vendors are bundling it. A niche specialty tweak is a feature request to an incumbent, not a company.)
- **AI SDR / autonomous outbound sales agent** — e.g. "An AI BDR that researches leads, writes hyper-personalized emails and books meetings while you sleep." "Replace your SDR team at 1/10th the cost." (11x, Artisan, Regie, Clay-based agencies and every CRM's native agent already do this. Inbox providers are cracking down on the spam it produces, and reply rates fall as volume rises. I get pitched this weekly, often through AI-written cold emails that demonstrate the problem.)
- **AI customer support agent that resolves X% of tickets** — e.g. "Deflects 70% of Tier-1 tickets with a Zendesk/Shopify integration." "AI support for [vertical: ecommerce, telecom, SaaS]." (Sierra, Decagon, Intercom Fin, Zendesk and Salesforce Agentforce own the category. Support chatbots were one of the first categories named oversaturated in 2026. Every deck shows the same deflection-rate slide.)
- **Meeting notetaker / call summarizer** — e.g. "Records your Zoom and emails action items." "Meeting notes plus CRM auto-update for [profession]." (Otter, Fireflies, Fathom and Granola already have millions of users. Zoom, Google Meet, Teams and the OS vendors ship it natively for free, so standalone margins are gone. The pitch has become table stakes for another product.)
- **"Cursor for X" vertical copilot** — e.g. "Cursor for lawyers / bankers / CAD engineers / spreadsheets / hardware design." "Claude Code for finance teams." (The analogy is the whole thesis. It skips why the workflow is different, what proprietary context you hold, and why the foundation labs' own agents will not move into your vertical next quarter. I hear it in about one pitch in ten.)
- **Prompt-to-app builder / vibe-coding platform** — e.g. "Describe your app and we build and deploy it." "Vibe coding for non-technical founders / for mobile / for internal tools." (Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0, Base44 and the labs' own app builders are all running hot. Users churn once the demo ends and the first hard bug appears. A new entrant is competing on the same models and the same templates.)
- **AI contract review / legal copilot** — e.g. "Harvey for immigration / real estate / in-house teams." "Redline NDAs in 30 seconds." (Harvey, Legora, Spellbook, EvenUp, Eudia and Robin AI have raised huge rounds. Each vertical variation has usually been funded already. Legal buyers are also famously slow, so the pitch rarely addresses distribution.)
- **Enterprise "ChatGPT over your company data" / RAG search** — e.g. "Ask questions across Slack, Drive, Notion and Jira." "Internal knowledge assistant with permission-aware retrieval." (Glean, Dust, Guru, Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise connectors and Claude connectors already ship this. A weekend RAG demo is easy to build and hard to differentiate on.)
- **LLM observability, evals and tracing** — e.g. "Datadog for LLM apps." "Automated evals that catch regressions before prod." (LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, Langfuse, Helicone, Galileo and Weights & Biases all exist, and the cloud providers are adding their own. The YC batches keep producing more. The gap left is in execution and distribution, not in the idea.)
- **Agent security, guardrails and prompt-injection firewalls** — e.g. "Zero trust for AI agents." "Runtime protection that stops prompt injection and data exfiltration." (Agent security is now among the most competitive seed categories. Crunchbase notes AI seed money flocking to cybersecurity, and Palo Alto, CrowdStrike and Zscaler have been acquiring in the space. Each deck cites the same OWASP LLM risks.)
- **Agent primitives: phone numbers, inboxes, wallets, identity, memory or sandboxes for AI agents** — e.g. "Email inboxes for agents." "Persistent memory layer for agents." "Stripe for agents." (The YC Spring 2026 batch has dozens of startups selling infrastructure to agents as the new customer. Mem0, E2B, Browserbase, AgentMail and Skyfire were early and the labs keep absorbing the primitives. It is a crowded picks-and-shovels field whose customer base is other startups selling to the same pool.)
- **Agent marketplace / agent-to-agent commerce** — e.g. "App Store for AI agents." "Protocol for agents to discover, hire and pay each other." (It is the GPT Store pitch and the crypto-agent pitch recycled. MCP, A2A and the payment networks' own agentic-commerce standards are being set by large players. Two-sided marketplaces with no supply or demand do not get through the first meeting.)
- **"AI employee" for a specific role** — e.g. "Your AI accountant / paralegal / recruiter / analyst / project manager, priced per seat." "Hire Ava, the AI BDR." (Naming the agent and giving it a face is the branding move of 2025 and 2026. Every role has multiple funded contenders, and they are usually demo-grade on a narrow slice. Buyers ask what happens when it is wrong, and the deck has no answer.)
- **Voice AI receptionist / phone agent for SMBs** — e.g. "AI receptionist for HVAC, dental and law firms that never misses a call." "AI that takes restaurant reservations." (Vapi, Retell, Bland, ElevenLabs agents and dozens of vertical wrappers such as Goodcall, Rosie and Slang already compete. Building on the same voice APIs means near-identical demos. Churn and per-minute margin compression are the hidden issues.)
- **AI agents for freight brokerage, dispatch and logistics ops** — e.g. "AI freight broker that quotes, books and calls carriers." "Voice agent that chases PODs and check calls." (Logistics has been a YC favorite for several batches, with Parade, Pallet, HappyRobot and many others. The pitch is nearly always email and phone automation for a fragmented, low-margin industry. Every founder cites the same $1T trucking market.)
- **Prior authorization / revenue cycle / medical billing automation** — e.g. "AI that automates prior auth for specialty clinics." "Denial management agent for hospitals." (Infinitus, Cohere Health, Akasa, Smarter Technologies, Commure and many YC companies all target this. The pain is real, which is why everybody sees it. Payers are deploying counter-AI and the sales cycle into health systems is long.)
- **AI-native insurance brokerage, claims or underwriting** — e.g. "AI-native MGA that quotes in seconds." "Claims automation agent for P&C carriers." (Every accelerator cohort in 2026, including Antler's, has an insurance entry. Incumbent carriers move slowly and the licensing and capital requirements are heavy. Founders usually underestimate both.)
- **AI bookkeeping, tax and back-office finance for SMBs** — e.g. "Autonomous bookkeeping that closes your books daily." "AI CFO for solopreneurs." (Pilot, Puzzle, Digits, Basis, Rillet and the QuickBooks, Xero and Intuit AI features are already there. Techstars and Antler cohorts each have a tax or finance solopreneur platform. Accuracy and liability are the real blockers and get skipped.)
- **AI roll-up of a legacy services industry** — e.g. "We acquire accounting firms / MSPs / HVAC shops / law practices and automate them with AI." "Private equity playbook plus LLMs." (General Catalyst popularized the thesis and it is now a common template. Founders rarely have operating experience or acquisition capital, and the seed check cannot fund a roll-up. The pitch is often software-margin dreaming over services-margin reality.)
- **Compliance automation (SOC 2 in a box), now with AI** — e.g. "Vanta but AI-native." "Continuous compliance for ISO 27001 and HIPAA in a week." (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, Thoropass, Sprinto and Delve exist, and the new AI-native clones differ mostly in marketing. Compliance-audit customers care about the auditor relationship more than the software.)
- **AI SOC analyst / autonomous pentester** — e.g. "AI agent that triages every alert so your SOC doesn't have to." "Continuous autonomous pentesting." (Seed money has poured into AI security, so Prophet, Dropzone, Torq, XBOW, Horizon3 and a long tail all compete. The differentiator is trust and integrations, not model quality. The deck is typically identical across founders.)
- **AI code review, test generation or AI SRE** — e.g. "An AI that reviews every PR." "Autonomous QA agent." "AI on-call engineer that resolves incidents." (CodeRabbit, Graphite, Greptile, Qodo, Cursor's Bugbot, GitHub Copilot review, Resolve and Traversal are already shipping. The coding labs are moving down the stack fast. A standalone add-on to the IDE agent is a feature.)
- **Text-to-SQL / chat-with-your-data analytics copilot** — e.g. "Ask your warehouse questions in plain English." "Self-serve BI for non-technical teams." (Every warehouse and BI vendor, including Snowflake, Databricks, ThoughtSpot, Hex, Mode and Looker, ships this. It demos beautifully on a clean schema and fails on real messy data. Semantic-layer reality is the actual product and nobody pitches it.)
- **Browser / computer-use agents that automate web workflows** — e.g. "An agent that fills out forms and navigates any website for you." "RPA 2.0 with LLMs." (OpenAI Operator/Agent, Anthropic computer use, Browserbase, Browser Use, Skyvern, Adept-lineage teams and UiPath's own agents cover it. The pitch is horizontal and vague. Reliability at scale and login/2FA handling are the hard parts, and seldom addressed.)
- **General-purpose humanoid or robot foundation model** — e.g. "The ChatGPT moment for robotics." "A foundation model for dexterous manipulation trained on teleop data." (Figure, Tesla Optimus, 1X, Agility, Apptronik, Physical Intelligence, Skild and Chinese players have giant war chests. Industrials are 23% of the Spring 2026 YC batch. A seed-stage team cannot out-fund them, and the deck rarely says why they'd win on data.)
- **Defense tech: drones, counter-UAS and "Anduril for X"** — e.g. "Low-cost interceptor drones." "Autonomous ISR for the Pentagon's Replicator program." (At least eight companies in a single YC batch build munitions, swarms or counter-drone systems. Founders cite the same DoD budget lines and Ukraine as validation. Procurement timelines are punishing and hardware capital needs are underestimated.)
- **Data labeling, RL environments and expert data marketplaces** — e.g. "Scale AI for [domain: medicine, finance, code]." "RL gyms for agents." "Pay PhDs to grade model outputs." (Mercor, Surge, Turing, Handshake, Micro1, Invisible, Scale and Snorkel all exist. It is a small set of concentrated customers, the labs, that are often building it in-house, and every seed deck shows the same lab-spend chart.)
- **Cheaper GPU cloud / inference optimization / neocloud** — e.g. "Airbnb for idle GPUs." "Inference at half the cost of the hyperscalers." (CoreWeave, Lambda, Together, Fireworks, Baseten, Modal, RunPod and dozens of others fight on price. The marketplace idea has been tried repeatedly and hits reliability, networking and utilization walls. Margins vanish as the hardware cycle turns.)
- **AI recruiting and job-hunt tools** — e.g. "AI recruiter that sources, screens and interviews candidates." "Auto-apply to 1,000 jobs while you sleep." "AI resume builder and interview coach." (On the employer side, Mercor, Paradox, HeyMilo, Juicebox and Ashby compete. On the candidate side there are countless resume and auto-apply tools, all flooding the same funnel and forcing employers into more filters. One side's product breaks the other's.)
- **AI tutor / homework helper** — e.g. "Every child gets a personal Socratic tutor." "AI study buddy that turns your lecture into flashcards." (Khan Academy's Khanmigo, Duolingo, Quizlet, Speak, Google's LearnLM and the chat labs' own study modes are all here. Students are the users but schools and parents pay, and the pitch ignores who the buyer is and how they distribute.)
- **AI companion, friend or therapist chatbot** — e.g. "An AI friend that remembers your day." "Affordable AI therapy in your pocket." "AI girlfriend with voice and memory." (Character.ai, Replika, Wysa, Woebot and the general chatbots occupy it. Regulatory pressure, lawsuits and app-store scrutiny around minors and mental health are growing. Retention is high and monetization is thin.)
- **AI travel planner / itinerary generator** — e.g. "Plan your dream trip in 30 seconds." "An AI concierge that books everything." (This has been a first-weekend LLM project since 2023. Expedia, Booking, Google and every chat assistant offer trip planning, and travel is an affiliate-margin business that punishes low-intent traffic. Booking integration is where these die.)
- **Second brain / memory app and AI wearable pendant** — e.g. "An always-on pendant that remembers every conversation." "Chat with your notes, photos and screenshots." (Rewind/Limitless, Bee, Friend, Plaud, Humane's ashes, Notion AI, Mem and Apple/Google/Meta device roadmaps all overlap. Hardware plus privacy issues make it worse. The demo is compelling and the daily-use habit rarely holds.)
- **AI video / avatar / UGC ad generator** — e.g. "Turn a product URL into 50 TikTok ads with AI actors." "Talking-head explainers from a script." (HeyGen, Synthesia, Arcads, Captions, Creatify, Higgsfield, Runway, Sora and Veo are all covering this. Underlying models are commoditizing every quarter, so wrappers lose their edge as the base model improves. Platforms are also clamping down on synthetic ads.)
- **AI writing, copy and SEO content generator** — e.g. "Generate blog posts at scale that rank." "AI copywriter for ecommerce product descriptions." (Jasper, Copy.ai, Writesonic and 100+ funded competitors already exist, and the chat assistants do this natively. Search engines penalize low-quality mass output. The category is what people mean by "thin AI wrapper.")
- **GEO / AEO: "rank inside ChatGPT" visibility tracking** — e.g. "SEO for the AI era." "See how often ChatGPT and Perplexity mention your brand." (Profound, Peec AI, Otterly, Athena, Scrunch, Goodie and every SEO incumbent, such as Semrush and Ahrefs, ship it. The methodology is scraping prompts and counting mentions, which is trivially copyable. It is 2026's version of the social listening dashboard.)
- **AI coach for a consumer wellness or lifestyle vertical** — e.g. "Snap a photo of your meal for instant calories." "AI stylist and virtual try-on." "AI wingman for dating apps." "AI personal trainer." (Cal AI, MyFitnessPal, Whoop, Oura, Hinge/Tinder AI features and Google try-on all cover pieces of it. The apps are cheap to build and paid acquisition is expensive and volatile. Retention curves after the first month look nearly identical.)
- **Stablecoin payments rails / cross-border remittance** — e.g. "Stripe for stablecoins." "Send money to Latin America and Africa instantly with USDC." "Stablecoin-powered B2B payouts." (After the GENIUS Act, Circle, Stripe/Bridge, BVNK, Paxos, Coinbase, Rapyd and every fintech incumbent piled in. A seed founder rarely has licenses, liquidity or a distribution wedge. The FX-fee slide is the same in every deck.)
- **Prediction market for a new vertical** — e.g. "Kalshi for sports / culture / local politics / startups." "Polymarket for [niche] communities." (Kalshi and Polymarket, plus Robinhood, DraftKings and FanDuel, dominate and are entangled in state-by-state regulatory fights. Liquidity is the whole product, and a niche market typically has none. It is the 2026 version of the NFT marketplace pitch.)
- **"The Stripe / Palantir / Shopify / Figma of X" meta-pitch with no wedge** — e.g. "We're building the Stripe of healthcare." "Palantir for mid-market manufacturing." "Operating system for [industry]." (An analogy stands in for a specific customer, painful problem and wedge product. "Operating system for" and "AI-native [incumbent]" appear in a startling share of decks. The founder is telling me the end-state and skipping how they get the first ten customers.)

## List 3
- **Ambient AI scribe for a specific specialty or clinic type** — e.g. "AI scribe built for dermatologists / physical therapists / dentists / behavioral health that writes the note while you talk to the patient." (Abridge (about $5.3B valuation, roughly $800M raised), Ambience ($243M Series C), Nabla, Heidi, Commure, Suki, DeepScribe and Microsoft DAX all already sell specialty templates, and solo-doc tools like Freed sit at about $100/month. Epic is shipping native ambient documentation, which squeezes any scribe that lacks a workflow moat. Scribe revenue is real, but the wedge is now table stakes. ([Sacra on Abridge](https://sacra.com/c/abridge/), [Startup Fundraising guide](https://startupfundraising.com/ai-medical-scribe-fundraising)))
- **AI revenue-cycle, prior-auth and medical-coding automation** — e.g. "AI agent that calls insurers, verifies benefits and chases prior authorizations for clinics." / "Autonomous coding that cuts claim denials 30%." (Commure, Waystar, R1/Ensemble, AKASA, Cohere Health, Infinitus (voice agents that call payers), Availity and Smarter Technologies all pitch this. Payers are automating denials too, so it is an arms race that incumbents with EHR and clearinghouse data are best placed to win. Sales cycles are long, and buyers ask for outcome-based pricing you must underwrite.)
- **Personal AI health or therapy coach (chat with your labs and wearable data)** — e.g. "Your AI doctor that reads your bloodwork and Oura data and coaches you daily." / "24/7 AI therapist for anxiety." (Generalist assistants now cover this (OpenAI's ChatGPT Health, Apple and Google health coaching), and Oura, Whoop, Function Health and Levels own the data. Woebot shut down its app in 2025, Wysa, Talkspace and Slingshot's Ash are still in the space, and Illinois, Nevada and Utah restricted AI therapy in 2025. The models are commodity, the data belongs to the wearable makers, and the regulatory risk is yours.)
- **Direct-to-consumer GLP-1 / weight-loss telehealth clinic** — e.g. "GLP-1 clinic for perimenopausal women / for men over 40 with personalized coaching." (Hims & Hers, Ro, Noom, WeightWatchers, and manufacturer-direct channels (LillyDirect, NovoCare) plus Costco and Amazon already compete on price.

# C16 refined: Regional Partner Group (rollover-first anchor-and-fill consolidator of independent uniform and linen laundries)

Pool P067. Written 2026-10-19 after three serious critiques. "Mine" marks my own arithmetic or judgment. The model script is a rough one-shot calculation (all deals bought at t=0, no staging), so treat IRRs as direction, not forecast.

## 1. Verdict

**Alive, but only as a conventional buy-and-build with a thin edge, and conditional on one number: the price sellers will accept.** The critics are right that the original pitch (hire an operating bench, price-lock customers, wait for an FTC remedy, raise $150-250M blind) is a generic roll-up with a damaging retention device and a lottery ticket. I removed all four. What survives is the part nobody refuted: $100M of revenue can be bought (about 8-12 deals, about 1% of the 1.8M locations TRSA counts, about 8% of industry deal flow at two deals a year, all mine) and sellers exist. The honest cost: median returns are poor (about 8% IRR in my median model) and good returns appear only if entry is at or below about 6.5x normalized EBITDA and the platform reaches roughly $20M+ EBITDA, where the exit multiple expands. Score about 4/10. If the 2-week test below fails, set alive=false; do not narrow it.

## 3. The refined business

- **Wedge customer (the seller).** A family-owned route-based uniform or linen laundry: $15-25M revenue, $2.5-4M EBITDA (financeable; one lender blog puts the institutional floor at about $2M), owner 58+ or a next generation that wants to stay, in a region chosen by screen: no Wildman plant, no strategic plant doing a natural fold-in, 3-6 independents within about 150 miles. The universe is about 300 independents under $15M (gate) and 100-200 route-based firms above $8M (dossier, mine); the anchor sits in the upper third, which is a thin pool, so expect 6-12 months to source it.
- **Payer and signer.** Sell side: the family owner signs the sale and rollover. Buy side: a capital partner (family office or PE capital partner) signs equity at each LOI; a regional bank or unitranche lender signs senior. Customers (restaurants, auto shops, factories, clinics) keep paying weekly under their existing contracts.
- **First product: the Partner Offer.** Standard term sheet: 65-80% cash at close on normalized three-year EBITDA at 5.5-6.5x; 20-35% rolled into group equity pari passu with the sponsor; earn-out or holdback up to 1x EBITDA tied to 24-month revenue retention at or above 93%; family retains the real estate on a 10-15 year market lease; three-year regional-president role; name, route reps and plant GM kept 24 months; proof of funds at the LOI and a 120-day close.
- **Pricing.** Entry: 5.5-6.5x normalized EBITDA (about 0.85-1.0x revenue at 15% margin); **walk away above 7x** (model: 12.5% IRR at 7.5x, no business). End customers: contracts honored, normal escalators (Baird 2Q26 renewals +3.1%), no price lock, term-for-price swap capped at 25% of the book. Sponsor economics: closing fee about 2-3% of EV plus management fee and carry, per independent-sponsor norms (vendor-cited).
- **Sequencing.** Weeks 0-2: the test below. Days 0-60: three regions screened, capital partner and lender pre-wired, counsel on covenants. Months 2-12: anchor LOI to close. Year 1: no operational change, install shared finance, garment purchasing and CRM. Year 2: 2-3 tuck-ins, convert short-term accounts to multi-year. Year 3: with 3 closed deals as references, raise a committed vehicle or senior facility; first hub consolidation. Years 3-6: second region, 7-9 more deals. Year 6-7: exit or continuation fund.
- **Why now (honest).** Real: a succession wave among family owners; deal-by-deal capital is abundant relative to deal quality (McGuireWoods: deal supply, not money, is the constraint); Cintas/UniFirst integration (no close before 2026-12-11, about $375M of cost take-out) will release managers and reps from 2027. Not evidenced: customers fleeing the merged company. I found no G&K attrition data, and Cintas guided G&K FY18 revenue to $870-900M against a $965M run rate, which could be pruning. Headwind: the 2.26x-revenue UniFirst print raises seller anchors. The thesis would have worked in 2019 too, so the why-now is moderate at best.
- **Moat (7 Powers).** None at the start. Reachable: scale economies at cluster level (plant utilization, wash-line and route density within a 100-150 mile cluster, garment purchasing, shared back office), real only from deal 3-4 in a region. Temporary cornered resource: peer-owner trust and sourcing, which erodes as Wildman-type groups spread. Returns come mainly from entry-price discipline plus a platform-scale multiple, not from a structural moat.

## 4. Economics (mine unless sourced)

Bottom-up: about $1-2.5B of acquirable revenue (100-300 targets at about $8-10M) and I need 8-12 deals, 5-10% of it. About 20-25 independent-laundry deals a year industry-wide (mine, from Alsco about 2.7/yr, Cintas 3-5/yr, ImageFIRST about 3/yr, a dozen PE platforms), so two a year is about 8% of deal flow. Location sanity: $100M at $5-9k per location is 11,000-20,000 locations, 0.6-1.1% of TRSA's 1.8M.

| Original dossier book ($100M rev, 18%, 1.3x, 7x exit, no synergy) | 7.2x | 7x | 1.35x | 5% |
| Refined, base ($100M, 15% margin, +3.5 pts net synergy) | 6.5x | 8.5x | 2.8x | 17% |
| Refined, exit 7x / 10x | 6.5x | 7x / 10x | 2.2x / 3.4x | 13% / 21% |
| Refined, no entry edge | 7.5x | 8.5x | 2.15x | 12.5% |
| Refined, entry edge | 5.5x | 8.5x | 3.9x | 23% |
| Refined, no synergy at all | 6.5x | 7x | 1.3x | 4% |
| **Median (about $42M acquired, +3 pts, 3.5x lev)** | 6.5x | 7.5x | 1.5x | **8%** |
| **Upside ($110M acquired, +4 pts)** | 6.25x | 9.5x | 3.5x | **21%** |

Takeaways: returns are set by entry multiple and by whether synergies exceed platform overhead (the +3.5 pts is net of about 3% overhead at scale; if overhead eats it, the row is 4%). The median is a mediocre investment; the platform multiple at $20M+ EBITDA is where the money is. Leverage: 3.0-3.5x on the anchor, 4.0x only at platform scale (Kleen, Patriot and Lighthouse are the leveraged-laundry failure precedents).

## 5. Cases (annual revenue, years 5-7)

- **Median $45M.** Anchor about $20M plus 3-4 tuck-ins at about $7M, plus about $3M organic. Competent execution, one region.
- **Upside $130M.** Anchor $25M, 11 tuck-ins at about $8M ($88M), about $12M organic (3.6% growth plus first-aid and mat cross-sell), plus about 5-10% option from a second platform or released-merger accounts. Named assumptions: deal-by-deal capital converts to a committed vehicle after deal 3; about two closings a year in two regions at 5.5-6.5x; churn at or below 7%; net synergy of 3-4 pts; exit at 9-10x EBITDA to a PE secondary, Alsco or Wildman. Excluded: any FTC remedy package, any Vestis route book. Probability of reaching it: about 10% (mine).

## 6. Competitors and edge

Wildman (nine locations, 13,500+ customers, 500 staff, keeps names), Alsco (Churchill, MacIntosh, Topper, Advanced Linen), Cintas tuck-ins ($164.5M cash in FY26, 0.4% of uniform growth), UniFirst, Prudential (publishes a 12-month price guarantee), Surge, Falcon, Quintex, PureStar/Cornell, Sterling/HLSG, CVC/Unitex, Calera/ImageFIRST. Edge, stated modestly: (1) a partial-exit structure (rollover, earn-out, real estate kept, family role) aimed at owners who want liquidity now and upside later; (2) regions and sellers where strategic synergy bids are weakest; (3) peer-owner sourcing instead of brokers (the vendor-cited 30-50% of "proprietary" deals via intermediaries and 1-5% cold reply rates are the baselines to beat); (4) price discipline. None is hard to copy.

## 7. Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test

**Riskiest assumption:** a repeatable flow of $8-25M family-owned laundries will sell to a new, financed buyer at or below about 6.5x normalized EBITDA (about 0.9-1.0x revenue) with 20-35% rollover. Nobody, including the gate, the dossier or the critics, found a closed 2025-26 multiple for a sub-$15M independent; the model says 7.5x kills the business.

**Test (days 1-14, real world):**
1. Days 1-3: list 60 family-owned independents, $8-30M revenue, from the TRSA directory and state associations.
2. Days 3-12: 25 owner conversations through the operator co-founder and TRSA peers. Give first: a free confidential "options report" (worth to a cash strategic, a PE platform, a rollover partner) in exchange for three years of pounds, revenue and EBITDA. Ask: approached in the last 24 months, by whom, at what multiple; who would you sell to and why; cash versus rollover plus earn-out; would you take a regional-president role.
3. Days 5-14: three calls with M&A advisers (IMG, EdgePoint, Stephens) on closed 2025-26 sub-$15M comps; two lenders on 3.5-4.0x for a $3M-EBITDA anchor with rollover.

**Pass:** at least 5 of 25 (20%) agree to a second meeting or share financials (baselines: cold 1-5%, Baird 21 replies from about 500, TRSA performance report 35 operators in 2024); at least 3 owners name or accept a price at or below about 7x EBITDA / 1.2x revenue, or accept rollover plus earn-out at 6.5x; advisers cite at least 2 closed sub-$15M deals at or below 1.2x revenue; at least 1 lender signals 3.5x or more. Pass moves to the 60-day gate: 3 signed IOIs, two from owners already approached by Alsco, Wildman or PE.

**Kill (alive=false):** fewer than 2 owners engage, or every number named is 8x or 1.5x revenue and up, or advisers say two-strategic processes are the norm at these sizes. Do not respond by narrowing to a boutique.

## 8. Pre-mortem and checkpoints

1. Cannot buy at or below 6.5x because strategics and PE set the price (test above).
2. Cannot close the anchor within 9-12 months (no deal-by-deal capital, or a lender walks at under $3M EBITDA).
3. Integration stumble on deals 1-2 at 3.5-4.0x leverage (rep defections, plant or environmental surprise).
Checkpoints: 2026-12-11 (remedy watch only), 60 days (IOIs), month 12 (anchor closed or stop).

## 9. What I checked on 2026-10-19 and what stays unverified

Checked: no FTC remedy reported (Cintas 10-Q, ASI); no public G&K attrition data found; Wildman states no rollover terms publicly and keeps names and teams; independent-sponsor market stats are vendor and law-firm sources, so verify; TRSA's Industry Performance Report already benchmarks pounds per operator hour and route cost (35 respondents in 2024), so "benchmarking" is not a gap, only a door-opener. Unverified: all seller multiples, the 15% margin (consistent with the TRSA median pre-tax 8.1% plus about 6% D&A), synergy size, churn, deal-flow count. Critics' sources (Wildman, Baird, KPMG, CT Acquisitions, TRSA) were taken as reported.

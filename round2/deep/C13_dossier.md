# C13 dossier: Run-Base Shrinker (vendor-neutral pay-from-audited-savings for the application run estate)

Researched 2026-10-18 for pool P129 (US spend on offshore-delivered IT services, about $100B a year, a [my estimate] from the pool file). Tags: **[src]** read in a source; **[vendor]** a seller's own claim; **[my est.]** my arithmetic or assumption; **[unverified]** could not be confirmed. About 45 searches and fetches; several fetches were blocked (403), which I flag where it matters.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Not dead, but the upside case does not hold.** The gate's $110M upside needs about 79 clients at $1.26M each. I could not verify any of its five inputs at the level used; the share is closest (25% is documented, 30-35% is not). With evidence-anchored inputs the same arithmetic needs about 220 clients.
- **The "nobody does this" claim is wrong in spirit.** Rimini Street, the AMS vendors themselves, contingency advisers, FinOps software, and well-funded AI-SRE startups each cover a slice of the four levers. The unoccupied slot is narrower: vendor-neutral, engineering-funded, paid only from audited net reduction on the client's ledger.
- **Two new legal and structural problems the gate missed:** (a) the proposed "Big 4 audit partner co-signs the measurement protocol" may collide with SEC auditor-independence rules on contingent fees (my inference; section 7); (b) the ESCO analogy does not carry its financing mechanism across, so the 36-month receivable tail is hard to finance.
- **My read:** a real $10-25M services business with a plausible path to a protocol/data asset; a $100M outcome has roughly a 10-15% chance [my est.].

## 2. Market context and industry economics (workflow steps 0-2)

**Metrics this industry runs on:** net run-cost reduction as % of baseline; savings share %; payback months; dispute rate on baselines; contract tail; clients per year.

- Gartner puts 2026 worldwide IT services spend at about $1.87T, up 9.0% [src](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-22-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-13-point-5-percent-in-2026-totaling-6-point-31-trillion-dollars). The pool's $100B US offshore figure is an assumption of two-thirds of India's roughly $149B IT services [src](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/india-fy26-tech-revenues-hit-usd-315-billion-enn26022404879), not a measured number.
- Managed services is the weak part of that market: ISG shows 1H26 managed-services growth of 2.7%, the Americas down 6%, and a 2.1% full-year forecast [src](https://isg-one.com/articles/index-insider---1h26-isg-index-recap). The same recap says providers are pricing around assumed AI savings and that "neither providers nor enterprises have full visibility into how the savings will be achieved." That is the strongest single piece of why-now evidence for an independent referee.
- Value flow: client pays vendor on FTE, ticket or fixed-bid terms; Cognizant says fixed-bid and transaction-based work is now over 50% of revenue [src](https://www.aol.com/articles/cognizant-ctsh-q4-2025-earnings-151319282.html). Under fixed-bid, a ticket reduction raises the vendor's margin, not the client's saving, until a reset. Under ticket pricing, TCS itself notes that low-volume scenarios could not cover fixed costs, which is why ticket pricing struggled [src](https://www.tcs.com/insights/blogs/generative-ai-outcome-based-pricing-models-service-desk). Revenue minimums are standard and can swallow a volume reduction [src](https://www.networkworld.com/article/782328/saas-beware-of-minimum-commitments-for-outsourcing-contracts.html).
- Renegotiation is real: clients are reopening deals within 24 months, with contracts above $50M ACV under most pressure [src](https://www.hfsresearch.com/news/ai-drives-it-contract-renegotiations-within-24-months-as-pricing-models-shift/). The HFS article gives no rate. The gate's "25% a year" (a Cognizant CFO forecast) is [unverified]: I could not fetch its source (403) and found no corroboration.

## 4. Bottom-up market and the upside assumptions

**Reachable payers.** Tier counts are global and for one vendor at a time: TCS had 66 clients above $100M and 139 above $50M at Q4 FY26 [src](https://arihantresearch.arihantcapital.com/Mumbai_Research/TCS_Result_Update_Q4FY26.pdf); Infosys's $50M+ and $100M+ cohorts each grew by three in FY26 per a call transcript that may be garbled [src](https://www.infosys.com/investors/reports-filings/quarterly-results/2025-2026/q4/documents/transcripts/earningscall.pdf). Clients overlap across vendors, offshore AMS is only part of a client's vendor spend, and the US is a fraction. My estimate is **300-600 US enterprises** with $30M+ of offshore run spend [my est.; no source counts them]. The gate's 79 clients is therefore **13-26% of reachable payers**, at or over the lessons file's 20% "heroic share" line.

**Scenario arithmetic** (revenue per client = base x reduction x share) [my est.]:
- Gate case: $30M x 12% x 35% = $1.26M, so 79 clients for $100M.
- Evidence-anchored: $30M x 6% x 25% = $0.45M, so about 222 clients (37-74% of reachable payers).
- Large-base variant: if the savings base is the client's whole application run bill (labor, licences, hosting) at $100M, then 8% x 30% = $2.4M, so about 42 clients. This is the most plausible path to $100M, and it depends on lever 1 and lever 4 (client-controlled lines), where Redress-style advisers and SaaS tools already compete on the same dollars.

## 5. Demand and willingness to pay

- **For:** self-funded transformation is standard or selective at 80% of surveyed enterprises, though the survey is vendor-co-produced (section 3). Providers are re-pricing on unverifiable AI assumptions (ISG, section 2). PE sponsors do pay for portfolio-wide cost programs (the Redress case, though its fee was undisclosed). Broadcom's VMware repricing forces right-sizing spend: a survey reports 59% of customers saw increases above 25% and only 4% fully migrated (secondary coverage; I did not open the primary survey) [src](https://petri.com/vmware-pricing-fears-enterprise-virtualization-shift/). SAP ECC mainstream maintenance ends 31 Dec 2027 with paid extension to 2030 (sources conflict on the extension premium, from 2 points to about 12%; the "39% still on ECC" figure is from an adviser blog) [src](https://www.crowe.com/id/insights/sap-ecc-support-ends-in-2027-what-leaders-should-do).
- **Against:** I found no RFP, tender or published contract for third-party, share-of-savings application-run reduction. Disputes over gainshare baselines are documented: Somerset County Council's gainshare with Southwest One ended in a settlement after the provider's savings claims were rejected [src](https://www.localgovernmentlawyer.co.uk/litigation-and-enforcement/311-litigation-features/13717-council-and-outsourcing-provider-settle-multi-million-pound-dispute), and Sprint and IBM fought over the productivity formula [src](https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/sprint-sues-ibm-over-outsourcing-contract).
- **Who signs:** CIO and CFO; the PE operating partner is the forcing function (inference). The person who owns the run budget is also the person whose vendor is being cut, which slows deals.

## 6. Unit economics, analogs and capital

- **ESCO scale and shape.** LBNL: US ESCO revenue $10.7B in 2024; K-12 and public agencies about $5B (47%); commercial and industrial only 6%, mostly design-build rather than performance contracting; M&V on about 80% of projects; real growth 56% since 2014 [src](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/energy-services-companies-are-becoming-a-15b-industry/831664/). The whole US ESCO industry is $10.7B of revenue and mostly public-sector, while the gate's pool view implies one new IT category capturing a $1.68B annual fee pool ($40B x 12% x 35%).
- **Public ESCO comp.** Ameresco: 2025 revenue $1,932.1M, net income $44.3M (2.3%), adjusted EBITDA $237.2M (12.3%), Q4 gross margin 16.2%, backlog over $5B [src](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260302626462/en/Ameresco-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results). The model scales but earns thin margins on a heavy balance sheet.
- **Contingency recovery comps.** PRGX, the scaled contingency-fee auditor, was taken private for about $195M in 2021 [src](https://www.sheppard.com/news-and-events/press-releases/sheppard-advises-ardian-in-acquistion-of-prgx-for-195-million). Vaudit's own pages disagree ($50M+ versus $100M+ lifetime recoveries; "no percentage" versus "success fee") [vendor](https://www.vaudit.com/vendor-audit-native). The one scaled public contingency-fee auditor selling for about $195M is a ceiling signal, not proof.
- **Financing gap.** In ESCO deals the customer finances through loans, leases and bonds [src](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/energy-services-companies-are-becoming-a-15b-industry/831664/) while the ESCO guarantees the savings [src](https://goodwindco.in/blog/energy-service-company-esco/). A private enterprise will not issue tax-exempt-style debt for IT savings, so the Shrinker would carry the performance risk and a receivable that is variable and disputable. Rough capital [my est.]: $1.0-1.5M of engineering per client up front, 12-15 new clients a year, 18-month payback, so $30-70M of peak outlay before receivables turn. Lenders will treat contingent receivables as equity-like.
- **Accounting.** Shared savings is variable consideration; revenue is recognized only to the extent a significant reversal is not probable [src](https://www.revenuehub.org/variable-consideration-constraint/), so reported revenue lags cash and invites auditor scrutiny [src](https://bakertilly.com/insights/the-revenue-recognition-transition-determining-the-transaction-price).

## 7. Legal, regulatory and liability landscape

1. **Auditor independence (new).** SEC Rule 2-01(c)(5) makes an accountant non-independent if it provides services to an audit client for a contingent fee or receives one from it; staff treat it as a direct financial interest, with no materiality test, and affiliates count [src](https://www.sec.gov/info/accountants/noaction/aapwc.htm). EY was sanctioned in 2021 for contingent-fee work for an audit client [src](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2021/34-93749.pdf). PCAOB Rule 3521 adds indirect fees. The gate's idea of a Big 4 audit partner co-signing the protocol must avoid any client for which that firm is the auditor, and a business relationship with a contingent-fee vendor could itself raise independence questions [my inference; verify with counsel]. Cleaner alternatives: a non-auditor firm, a standards-body model like IPMVP, or Internal Audit only.
2. **Contract access.** Minimum revenue commitments, fixed-bid pricing and vendor-held ITSM data can block or erase vendor-line savings (section 2). Lever 1 and lever 4 avoid this by cutting client-owned invoices.
3. **Retirement liability.** Retiring an application requires validated extracts, preserved metadata, retention schedules and legal holds, and SOX 404 evidence may be needed for years after [vendor-written guide](https://dev.to/sam_mitchell_ee4afb8d68c3/application-retirement-the-complete-guide-to-retiring-legacy-applications-without-losing-critical-5b2i). A shutdown that breaks a hidden dependency is an operational loss with the Shrinker's name on it. I found no standard indemnity or insurance for this [gap]; liability caps and E&O cover must be priced in.
4. **No statute prohibits** third-party share-of-savings IT fees that I found; the risks are contractual and accounting, not regulatory.

## 8. Is the why-now real?

Partly. The pricing reset is real and verified: ISG's unverifiable AI-savings assumptions, HFS's 24-month renegotiations, and flat managed-services growth. But the same shock cuts against the product in two ways. (1) Vendors are cutting their own prices; if clients win 25-30% cuts at renegotiation (Persistent's CEO, per the idea file; not re-verified) a third party earning on a baseline "beyond contract pass-through" may have little left to claim. (2) AI-SRE and ticket-deflection tooling is becoming cheap software ($1B-valuation Resolve AI), so engineering for lever 2 may be bought outright. The SAP deadline is soft (extension to 2030), and VMware pressure feeds licence advisers first.

## 9. Synthesis

**Moats.** Counter-positioning against AMS vendors: Moderate, and only where the Shrinker never needs vendor cooperation. A cross-client root-cause and retirement library: Moderate, but AI-SRE vendors learn across clients too. An auditor-accepted protocol: potentially Strong, but unproven and legally fragile (section 7). Switching costs: Weak after the 36-month tail, with receivable finance the real barrier to entry.

**Structural vulnerabilities:** baseline disputes; private CFOs unwilling to hand 25-35% of savings to a third party when advisers charge 25% for less risk; and the savings pool shrinking as vendors self-deflate.

| Scenario | Probability [my est.] | Annual revenue, year 6-7 | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | 12% | $80-110M | Large-base variant works; protocol becomes a standard; PE channel mandates it |
| Base | 38% | $12-30M | 30-60 clients, mostly lever 1 and 4, licence-adviser overlap |
| Bear | 50% | under $8M | Baseline disputes, CFOs cap share near 20%, AI-SRE tools substitute |

**Tests that would change my mind (60-90 days):** (1) three written LOIs from CFOs accepting a share of 30% or more on client-controlled lines; (2) minimum-commitment, reset and data-custody clauses read in five real MSAs; (3) a repeat-ticket heatmap showing at least 6% addressable fee reduction on unit-priced contracts (the gate's kill line, which I endorse); (4) written acceptance of the measurement method from one Internal Audit group; (5) a counsel opinion on independence with a named Big 4 firm; (6) a count of US enterprises with $30M+ of offshore run spend from two former AMS delivery heads, to replace my 300-600 guess.

**Pivot note.** The largest reachable pool I found is the client-controlled application run bill (licences, hosting, retirement), not the vendor-labor pool. That moves the idea toward the Redress and SoftwareOne pool with engineering funding as the difference. That is a real wedge, but a smaller and more contested one than the pitch implies.

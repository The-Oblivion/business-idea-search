# C10 refined: Signed-Off v2 (buy-and-build, AI-leveraged nuclear engineering house, sold through the buyer co-op)

Pool P038, refined 2026-10-18 after three serious critiques. Evidence tags: [V] primary page read, [S] search summary or secondary, [I] my inference or arithmetic. New evidence gathered this pass is listed in section 11.

## 1. Verdict

**Alive, wounded, and not what it was.** Three things the first version claimed are gone:

- It is not an AI company. AI is an operating-cost lever that any rival can buy.
- It is not a QA-status moat. The regulator and the tool vendors are widening that gate.
- It is not a developer/licensing play. The gate already killed Clock Keeper and Uprate Kit, and the critics showed the developer line is mostly alliance-captured.

What is left is a **buy-and-build nuclear engineering company** with a measurable AI cost position, entered through the buyer-side co-op and the primes' overflow, and bought at orphan prices rather than auction prices. That is the consensus private-equity trade with a different sourcing rule and a different operating thesis. I say so plainly because the critics are right that it is consensus.

- Median outcome: about $25M revenue by year 7.
- Upside: about $110M revenue by year 7.
- Probability of the upside: about 5-8% [I]. That is the dossier's 5-10%, nudged down by the critics and up by cheaper capital needs.

It stays alive under the "no shrinking" rule because the upside needs about 8% of the 2030 outsourced-engineering pool, not a heroic share. Every assumption that gets it there is named in section 6, and one of them can be killed in two weeks (section 8).

## 3. The refined business

**One-liner.** A buy-and-build nuclear engineering house that sells finished, independently verified calculation revisions and change packages per deliverable, at a discount tied to measured hours saved, produced by a two-tier bench with AI-assisted drafting.

**Payers and signers.**
1. *Buyer co-op (USA Inc.).* Nine member organizations at ten sites (Comanche Peak, Fermi, Susquehanna, Cooper, Prairie Island, Monticello, Cook, STP, Palisades, Columbia) [V]. Members reported $72M+ of documented value and a 37:1 return in 2025, with a stated goal of $100M of collective value by 2030 [V]. The 2020 S&L engineer-of-choice award stated its aim as exploiting "measurable duplications and similarities" [V, from the critic's fetch and my search]. That is the template thesis, so the co-op is a channel and not only a rival. USA took four new supplier partners in 2026 (Enercon, Blue Wave AI, AMS, System One) [V]. It also runs an open web-form application, "Become a USA Supplier Partner", and considers applicants for its bid list when a need arises [S]. GSE became a USA specialty engineer of choice in 2019, expanded 2023 [S via critic]. Signer: the member's Design Engineering Manager on a task-order PO, after the co-op lists us. The S&L term was awarded for "up to five years" from Jan 2020; I found no renewal notice, so the term may be at or past a rebid. Unconfirmed [S/I].
2. *Primes and engineers of choice, as sub-tier.* S&L grew headcount 14.5% [S], and the critics showed all the big firms are hiring flat out. Overflow they cannot staff is the opening. Signer: the prime's nuclear business-unit director plus its supply chain. The prime flows the QA requirements down. I treat this as a minority channel: it is concentration risk and the prime may see us as a competitor.
3. *Owners outside alliances with thin benches,* including restarts. Signer: Director of Design Engineering or VP Engineering, with the QA director accepting the supplier. Named v1 targets are re-sorted. Energy Northwest and Palisades sit inside USA, so they are reached through door 1. Wolf Creek runs in-house engineering, and NextEra says it is evaluating Duane Arnold itself, so those are low-probability. Crane is unknown.

**First product: the Change-Package Lane.** Fixed-price calculation revisions and technical sections of design-change packages. Each includes the 50.59 screening, the independent verification (Criterion III) and one revision cycle. Sold in annual blocks (for example 30-60 deliverables per site-year). The first 10 deliverables are a calibration pilot to fix the rate card. 50.59 evaluations stay in as proof of quality, not as a revenue line (about 3 full evaluations per plant per year, under $15M fleet-wide [I]).

**Pricing and liability.**
- Rate card = the acquired firm's own historical median hours per deliverable class x blended rate x (1 - discount).
- Pilot discount 8-10%, widened only as owner-measured hours savings are proven. The discount is capped at half the measured hours reduction. Section 5 shows why (break-even price cut = 0.65 x hours cut).
- Gain-share option: split the hours saved against the historical baseline.
- Remedy for a deliverable rejected for defects in first review: no-charge re-performance plus a fee credit. Liability is capped at fees (1-2x annual), with no consequential damages and no lost-generation exposure. The Vogtle analogy applies to fixed-price EPC. We refuse that scope; our unit is a $10-50k deliverable.
- Insurance: get quotes with AI disclosed before any fixed-price commitment. Affirmative AI cover exists: Armilla (Lloyd's/Chaucer) launched in May 2025, and CFC started adding affirmative AI wording to professional-liability products from June 2026 [S]. Against that, ISO filed generative-AI exclusions in Jan 2026, AIG and Berkley are following, and Beazley/QBE use AI sublimits near 10% of limits [S]. I saw no engineering-specific wording. If cover is unavailable at about 3% of revenue or less, fixed price degrades to time-and-materials with a not-to-exceed, and the business is a plain roll-up until cover exists.

**AI and QA architecture.** AI output is treated as an unverified draft, like a junior's first pass.
1. Every number is recomputed in qualified tools or controlled spreadsheets.
2. A second qualified engineer independently verifies and signs (Criterion III).
3. Model changes are a controlled procedure change, tested on a "gold set" of past deliverables, and not a software requalification.
4. Deployment runs in the enclave the owner's data requires; the model lag costs drafting speed, and the verifier catches errors.

Whether NQA-1 Subpart 2.7 reaches such a drafting aid is unresolved in the dossier [I]. The design is robust either way. If it does apply, we hold a procedure. If it does not, we lose no moat because we never relied on one. The INL case study is why every number is recomputed: the weaker model put a containment leak-rate limit at 10,500 cc/s against a 5,500 source value [V via critic]. It also explains why I treat the productivity claim as unproven.

**Labor model (two tiers).** The scarce resource is senior independent verifiers and signatories. Retain them as employees and as part-time or retired signatories (inference: the workforce is aging; no retirement-eligibility figure was checked). The second tier is preparers, including engineers from adjacent regulated industries trained into the QA program and working under verifiers. Where a state PE seal is required, a PE stays in responsible charge of the design decisions, not only review [per NSPE, S]. Hiring stays the hardest operating constraint. Operating-plant engineers are expected to rise from 6,900-7,400 FTE (2025) to 9,200-10,400 (2030) [V, dossier], and S&L alone grew 14.5%.

**Sourcing and sequencing.**
- *Phase 0 (weeks 0-2):* the bake-off in section 8, plus a USA supplier-partner form, broker E&O inquiries and teasers on orphan boutiques.
- *Phase 1 (months 1-4):* sign the nuclear QA/licensing co-founder (non-negotiable; a first-time founder without that record cannot open these doors, per the dossier); an E&O quote; two owners agree to a paid 10-deliverable pilot; 5+ orphan targets with data-room access.
- *Phase 2 (months 4-12):* close platform acquisition #1 under bid discipline. Cap: 6.5x EBITDA and 0.9x revenue, at least 30% as seller note or earn-out. Do not compete in auctions. Keep the audited QA manual, the QA lead and the customer contracts untouched on day one. Notify customers, then add the AI procedure as a controlled revision. If nothing clears the cap by month 12, stop buying. Do not chase.
- *Phase 3 (months 12-30):* convert legacy T&M scopes to the Change-Package Lane at renewal, obtain the USA listing, close deals #2 and #3.
- *Phase 4 (months 30-84):* deals #4 and #5, EPC/prime sub-tier, accepted per-plant packages, an exit to a strategic or sponsor.

## 4. Moat (7 Powers, honestly)

- **Process power** is the primary one: measured cost per accepted deliverable, a gold set of verified deliverables, and a trained two-tier bench. Reached around year 3-4 if the productivity result holds.
- **Switching costs** are secondary. Accepted packages in an owner's reviewers' routine and multi-year contract positions (USA listing, MSAs) are sticky.
- **Scale economies** show up at 4-5 acquisitions through a shared QA program, shared AI infrastructure and shared G&A.
- **Counter-positioning is not relied on.** The critics showed incumbents can price bounded scopes fixed and are capacity-constrained, so they do not need to cannibalize anything.
- **Cornered resource and QA status are not moats.** Listing is an entry ticket that we buy. NEI 22-04 Rev 1 and the proposed Appendix T widen the gate.

Robustness across regulatory branches:
- If supplier oversight stays, scarce listed shells are available at orphan prices and listing keeps its value.
- If it loosens, owners can buy outsourced engineering from more suppliers, so more volume is addressable, and cost and throughput become the moat.
- The business never sells scarcity of status, so the branch matters less. That is the point of the change.

## 5. Unit economics (my arithmetic, [I])

Baseline: labor 65% of revenue, other cost 20%, EBITDA 15%. Let h be the hours cut and p the price cut per deliverable. Then EBITDA per base deliverable = (1 - p) - 0.65(1 - h) - 0.20.

| Hours cut h | Price cut p | EBITDA margin | EBITDA per deliverable vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15% | 15% | 11.5% | -35% (gives away more than it saves) |
| 15% | 8% | 18.2% | +12% |
| 20% | 10% | 20.0% | +20% |
| 30% | 15% | 22.9% | +30% |
| 40% | 20% | 26.3% | +40% |

- Break-even price cut = 0.65 x h. The dossier's "a few EBITDA points" holds only if the discount eats the gain. With a discount of half the hours cut, the gain is large. This is why the discount is a rule and not a negotiation.
- Revenue per head: about $180-200k baseline, about $235k with a 20% AI lift [I from the dossier's DP Engineering and Enercon ratios].
- Owner view: the owner pays less vendor fee and its own qualified-reviewer time does not drop to zero. The pilot measures both, because the owner's real saving is capped by its review hours.

## 6. Bottom-up path to $100M

**Pool [dossier, I].** Outsourced fleet engineering is about $0.7-1.2B today and $1.0-1.6B by 2030, about $16M per site at $0.9B across about 55 sites. No published figure exists. This is the dossier's triangulation and not a verified number.

**Median case (about $25M revenue, year 7).**
- One platform acquisition of about $10M revenue in year 1 (EV about $8M), growing 6% a year, reaching about $14M.
- One tuck-in of about $6M in year 3, reaching about $8M.
- A fixed-price lane of about $4M from 5-6 accounts.
- Productivity of 15-20% hours cut, a discount of 8-10%, EBITDA of about 17%, so about $4M of EBITDA, worth about $30M at 7-8x.
- No USA listing at scale, no prime channel, no further deals because capital stays small.
- This is a fine outcome for a founder with a small equity cheque. It is not venture-scale.

**Upside case (about $110M revenue, year 7).**

| Line | Build | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Acquired legacy book | Five boutiques, about $58M at close (platform $14M, then $10M, $12M, $12M, $10M), grown about 5% a year after close | about $65M |
| Fixed-price lane (won) | 14 accounts x $2.5M: at least 5 USA member sites, 4 restart/thin-bench owners outside USA, 5 larger-owner surge scopes | about $35M |
| Prime/EPC sub-tier | Overflow factory for engineers of choice, EPCs and developers' US sub-tier | about $10M |
| **Total** | | **about $110M** |

- Staff: about 470 at $235k per head [I].
- Capital: acquisitions at 0.7-0.9x revenue are about $41-52M of enterprise value, plus working capital and integration, so about $60-75M deployed over 5 years. Roughly 40% could be cash-flow debt and seller notes against about $8M of acquired EBITDA at 3x. Equity is about $30-40M [I].
- Share check: fleet revenue of about $100M is about 7.7% of a 2030 pool of $1.3B (6-10% across the range). I assume only about 40% of the pool is reachable by an outsider before 2030 (the rest sits in engineer-of-choice alliances) [I, my assumption], so the *won* lane of $35M is about 7% of the reachable pool. The acquired $65M is bought and does not have to displace incumbents. This is under the 20% heroic-share threshold in the lessons.

**Named upside assumptions.**
- U1: five orphan boutiques close at or below 6.5x EBITDA and 0.9x revenue, with $60-75M raised.
- U2: AI cuts total accepted hours (including independent verification) by 30% or more on calculation and change-package classes, with 90%+ first-pass acceptance.
- U3: the discount rule holds, so margin reaches 20%+.
- U4: the USA listing or an equivalent contract position is achieved by month 18, and 14 fixed-price accounts average $2.5M by year 7.
- U5: acquired customers accept the AI procedure layer without losing more than 10% of revenue.
- U6: hiring reaches 420-500 staff with attrition under 15%.
- U7: incumbents do not match narrow-class pricing for 24 months, or cannot without cannibalizing.
- U8: E&O is available at about 3% of revenue or less with AI disclosed.
- U9: prime/EPC sub-tier delivers about $10M.

**Sensitivity.** If multiples run 9-12x EBITDA (the critics' range for quality assets, about 1.2-2.5x revenue), the same $58M of acquired revenue costs $70-145M. The upside falls to about $50-60M, because bid discipline means we simply buy less. If U2 fails (hours cut under 15%), there is no price edge and this is an ordinary boutique roll-up.

## 8. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test

**Riskiest assumption.** On real calculation-grade work, AI-assisted production plus independent verification cuts total accepted hours by 30% or more, with first-pass acceptance of 90% or more.

Why this one:
- It is the only thing that separates this business from the Pelican/Arlington roll-up.
- It sets the price, the margin (section 5) and the hiring need.
- The evidence is drafting-stage only. INL's experts estimated about 50% savings on narrative drafting by non-specialists, but found hallucinations and missing parameters. Westinghouse claims 25% (sponsored). Nobody has measured calculations or design changes.
- Demand for a discount is likely given USA's stated savings goals. Productivity is not.

**Test: a public-record bake-off, about 13 days, under $10k.**
1. *Days 1-3.* Build 12 bounded tasks from public docket material (UFSAR chapters, NRC-docketed licensing calculations, public 50.59 summaries): 4 screening/evaluation write-ups, 4 calculation revisions with changed inputs, 4 change-package technical sections. Pair them by difficulty.
2. *Days 4-10.* Hire one experienced ex-licensee design engineer for about 25 hours. Arm A does half the pairs manually, time-logged. Arm B does the matched half AI-assisted, with every number recomputed, then a second qualified person verifies (about 12 more hours). Log all hours, including verification.
3. *Days 11-13.* Two ex-QA or licensing reviewers score every package blind against an owner-style acceptance checklist: accept or reject, and defect list.
4. *Pass:* Arm B total hours at or below 70% of Arm A, first-pass acceptance at or above 90%, zero uncaught critical numerical errors. *Kill:* hours cut under 15%, or any uncaught critical error. Between 15% and 30% means price the discount at half the cut and treat the case as the median, not the upside.

**Free, same two weeks.** These do not change the verdict but they de-risk the next gate:
- Submit the USA supplier-partner form.
- Make 10 calls to design-engineering leaders and ask whether bounded scopes sit outside their alliance, and whether their QA group would accept an AI-as-unverified-draft procedure.
- Ask 3 brokers for E&O indications with AI disclosed.
- Ask a deal broker for 3 orphan-boutique teasers.

## 9. Pre-mortem (it is 2028 and this is dead)

1. No orphan under the cap by month 12, or we get outbid. Fix: stop buying. We never chase.
2. The bake-off saved under 15%, or acceptance failed. Fix: found by the test, cheaply.
3. Owner or co-op QA groups demand tool-level qualification or re-audit after the acquisition, and top customers leave. Fix: layer, not rewrite; ask in the calls; hold the day-one QA manual and QA lead constant.
4. Owners' in-house AI absorbs overflow demand. Fix: partially measurable through the outsourced-share question in the calls; otherwise unfixable.
5. No E&O at a sane price. Fix: degrade to T&M with a not-to-exceed until cover exists.
6. Hiring. Fix: two-tier bench, acquisitions bring heads. Not fully fixable.

# C05 Dossier: The Non-GE Foundry (P079, Dilemma 1)

Prepared 2026-10-18. Convention: "[unopened]" marks a figure taken from a search-result summary whose page I did not open. "(my arithmetic)" marks a derived number. Everything else I read in the cited source.

## 1. Verdict

**Dead as a founder-led startup. At best it is a capital-allocation bet for infrastructure or PE money.** Four findings decide it:

1. **The "gap" is a listed company's sales pitch.** Doncasters' June 2026 IPO prospectus says it is "currently the only competitor able to provide Aerospace and IGT OEMs with dedicated capacity" and describes four signed partnerships with customer-funded capex, capacity reservation payments and guaranteed volumes ([424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2107018/000110465926077968/tm269965-19_424b4.htm)). Customer funding covers "up to 80%" of capital investment. That is this idea's business model, already operating at a qualified incumbent.
2. **The "lost neutral supplier" premise is mostly false.** See section 2.
3. **The economics need the customer to fund most of the plant, and customers already do that for incumbents.** An independent caster earns roughly 18-22% EBITDA margins (Doncasters), not Howmet's 37.7%.
4. **Timing and cycle.** A plant started in 2027 produces after Howmet's August 2028 capacity wave and Doncasters' 2028-29 ramp, and Wood Mackenzie expects turbine orders to peak in 2026.

## 2. Premise test: did non-captive buyers lose a neutral supplier?

- **The deal is real.** GE Aerospace agreed on 2026-09-08 to buy CPP for $11.75B, about 18x 2027 EBITDA with synergies and about 26x without, closing 2H 2027. CPP has about $2.0B of 2027E revenue, about 6,600 staff and more than 20 facilities ([GE deck](https://www.geaerospace.com/sites/default/files/GEAerospace_to_acquire_Consolidated_Precision_Products_Presentation.pdf)).
- **Only about 20% of CPP is "power and other."** CPP's mix is about 60% commercial aerospace, 20% defense and 20% power and other, so power is roughly $400M (same deck, my arithmetic). The deck cites Avio Aero, Unison and Dowty as a "proven model" that "serves external customers." Press reports say GE will run CPP as a separate unit and keep working with Howmet and ATI ([Leeham](https://leehamnews.com/2026/09/09/ge-aerospace-moves-to-secure-critical-engine-casting-capacity/)). Culp calls the shortage "much more a supply-side challenge" than a demand one (same source).
- **GE Vernova is not GE Aerospace.** The largest gas-turbine maker files separately ([GE Vernova Q2 release](https://www.gevernova.com/news/press-releases/ge-vernova-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-raises-2026-financial)). The deal makes no IGT maker's supplier a rival's subsidiary. Only the aeroengine OEMs (Pratt, Rolls-Royce, Honeywell) are exposed, and they have other routes.
- **Neutral capacity exists and is spoken for.** Howmet holds over 50% of global IGT blade share and says it has "completed negotiations with the last of our 7 major customers" ([Q2 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/howmet-aerospace-hwm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Siemens Energy signed a $1B+ multi-year deal with Doncasters in Aug 2023 ([Doncasters](https://www.doncasters.com/news/contract-worth-over-1-billion-usd-signed-with-siemens-energy)). Doncasters lists Ansaldo, Doosan, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Honeywell, Pratt, Rolls-Royce, Safran and GE as customers (424B4).
- **The real squeeze is slower.** GE expects its airfoil demand to grow over 30% from 2026E to 2030F (GE deck), and CPP supplies about a quarter of GE's needs ([Reuters analysis](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/analysis-ge-aerospace-bets-black-art-casting-secure-jet/)). Allocation inside CPP will drift toward GE after a 2H 2027 close, and AlixPartners expects GE to be required to divest some facilities (same Reuters piece). One analyst said it "remained to be seen" whether non-GE customers are affected.

## 3. Competitor map (confirmed, refuted, new)

**Scaled independents.** Jefferies counts four: Howmet, PCC, CPP and Doncasters ([Turbomachinery](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/ge-aerospace-cpp-acquisition-spacex-turbine-blade-foundry); [AIN](https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/aerospace/2026-06-26/ipo-boosts-engine-castings-specialist-doncasters)).
- **Howmet**: Q2 2026 revenue $2.55B, Engine Products $1.37B at 37.7% segment EBITDA margin, gas turbines $321M (+38%), 2026 capex above $500M and rising in 2027 ([Q2 release](https://www.howmet.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/08/Howmet-Aerospace-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.pdf); [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000004281/000000428126000025/hwm-20260630.htm)). Its CFO says "Investments are backed by customer contracts." A commitment made in August 2026 yields capacity around August 2028 (Q2 call).
- **Doncasters (DPC)**: FY25 revenue $837M, adjusted EBITDA $138M (16.5%), capex $31M, top-2 customers 38% of sales, 70% of revenue under LTAs (424B4). Four partnerships (two aero, two IGT) are expected to add more than $200M of annual revenue at full run rate, reached in 2028.
- **PCC (Berkshire)**: no public detail on airfoil capacity or customer terms found.
- **Chromalloy (Veritas)**: runs the Siemens-exclusive Tampa JV ($139M, 350 jobs, deliveries from 2018, [Foundry](https://foundrymag.com/molds-cores/article/21931673/chromalloysiemens-investment-casting-venture-slated-for-florida)). This is a 2017-era precedent for exactly this structure. It also announced a $16.5M Tampa expansion in May 2026 [unopened, [AIN](https://backend.ainonline.com/sites/default/files/article_export/rtf/Chromalloy_Plans_$16.5_Million_Tampa_Expansion-21-05-2026.rtf)].

**Captives (in-house teams).** Pratt's Asheville plant (at least $650M through 2027, [Foundry](https://www.foundrymag.com/molds-cores/article/21145965/engine-builder-plans-new-turbine-airfoil-plant-pratt-whitney)), Safran Rennes (about 200 staff, opens 2027, [Foundry](https://www.foundrymag.com/melt-pour/article/21283776/engine-builder-plans-investment-casting-foundry-safran-aircraft-engines)), Rolls-Royce Rotherham (about £19-21M to double blade output by 2030 [unopened, [The Manufacturer](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/rolls-royce-invests-21-3m-to-double-turbine-blade-output-at-rotherham-facility/)]), SpaceX Bastrop (blades and vanes foundry; jobs posted April 2026, [Foundry Magazine](https://www.foundrymag.com/issues-and-ideas/article/55401710/ive-got-a-job-for-you-editors-note-september-2026)). GE Vernova advertised a "Supplier Capacity Leader, Investment Castings" role in Jan 2026 (search snippet; the posting page now returns 404). Hadrian is hiring casting engineers ([posting](https://jobs.buildsubmarines.com/jobs/538260256-lead-manufacturing-engineer-casting-special-projects-at-hadrian)). I found no Anduril casting source.

**PE roll-ups.** Pursuit Aerospace (CD&R and Greenbriar, $635M unitranche, [CD&R](https://www.cdr.com/news/cdr-and-greenbriar-equity-complete-acquisitions-to-create-a-new-leading-supplier-to-the-aerospace-industry-called-pursuit-aerospace)) bought Creasey Castings on 2026-08-11, its sixth deal since 2023, and is expanding a Tunisian nickel and cobalt casting site to over 1,000 staff ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/pursuit-aerospace-acquires-creasey-castings-and-expands-aerospace-casting-capacity/)). CPP is itself buying GF's Precicast (about 600 staff, CHF 220M proceeds, [GF](https://www.georgfischer.com/en/newsroom/media-releases/newsdetail.html/news/gf/2026/gf-signs-agreement-to-divest-aerospace-and-industrial-gas-turbine-operations-of-the-former-casting-solutions-division)).

**Startups.** Fabri has raised about $13.5M with RTX and Lockheed venture arms as investors and plans a second foundry for 2027 [unopened, [Tectonic](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/fabri-announces-its-raised-13-5m-for-new-age-casting/)]. Rangeview won a Kratos development contract on 2026-07-30 and holds a $1.43M Air Force SBIR Phase II ([Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/rangeview-kratos-turbine-engine-superalloy-castings)). I found no seed or Series A for a turbine-casting startup.

**Free or public substitutes.** DoD casting and forging modernization money exists ($2B over five years was proposed in a 2024 trade article [unopened]), and OSC lends $10-150M for facilities with $97.8M of FY26 credit subsidy ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13215.html) via search summary). I found no 2026 award or OSC loan for airfoil casting capacity.

## 4. Bottom-up market

- **IGT airfoil pool.** Howmet's gas-turbine sales annualize to about $1.28B (Q2 $321M x4, my arithmetic). At over 50% share, the pool is roughly $2.6B or less (rough, because Howmet's "gas turbines" line may include non-airfoil work). Doncasters' IGT sales are $351M (424B4). CPP's power and other is about $400M.
- **Aero pool.** Howmet Engine Products alone runs about $5.5B annualized (includes rolled rings). CPP adds about $1.6B of commercial and defense, Doncasters about $0.3B of aerospace. PCC is undisclosed. The relevant pool is therefore roughly $8-10B or more, not the $53.7B US metalcasting figure.
- **Payers.** About 7 major IGT OEMs (Howmet's own count) plus roughly 5-6 non-GE aeroengine makers. All hold LTAs with an incumbent.
- **Share.** One $100M plant is about 4% of the IGT pool or about 1% of the combined pool (my arithmetic). It would need 4-5 buyers at $20-25M a year each. Pool share is not the obstacle. Capital, qualification and incumbent contracts are.

## 5. Evidence of demand and willingness to pay

**Strong, but it flows to incumbents.**
- Doncasters: a 15-year aeroengine blade and vane deal with customer-funded capacity; a 7-year IGT extension with guaranteed volumes; a 9-year IGT extension for directionally solidified airfoils; three agreements underwriting a greenfield superalloy plant that starts production in 2029 (424B4).
- Siemens-Chromalloy dedicated JV (2017) and the Siemens-Doncasters $1B+ LTA, whose CEO called it "the backbone" of capacity investment.
- GE Vernova's gas equipment backlog plus slot reservations hit 116 GW, with 63 GW as paid reservations, and it targets 20 GW of annual output in Q3 2026, 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030 (Q2 release). Wood Mackenzie sees turbine prices near $600/kW by end-2027, with turbines 20-30% of combined-cycle cost, six-year lead times and 60-70 GW of global capacity against 110 GW of orders ([Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/turbines/gas-turbine-prices-climb-195-as-supply-crunch-reshapes-power-development/)). Castings are a small slice of a very expensive, delayed asset, so buyers can rationally pay to secure supply (my inference).

**Not found.** Any OEM prepayment, take-or-pay or capacity deposit to a new, unqualified entrant. Fabri's backers are equity investors. Rangeview's Kratos award is a development contract of undisclosed value. No source shows an OEM signing a 10-year take-or-pay with a plant that does not yet exist.

## 6. Unit economics and capital

- **Margins.** Doncasters' segment EBITDA margins are 18.2% (North America) and 21.9% (Europe). CPP's implied pre-synergy margin is about 22.6% ($11.75B / 26 = $452M on $2.0B, my arithmetic). Howmet's 37.7% reflects scale, alloy and process ownership.
- **Capex per plant.** Greenfield anchors: $139M for 350 jobs (2017 JV), $650M+ for Pratt's integrated Asheville, SpaceX Bastrop reported at about $280M [unopened, [Foundry Planet via search](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/spacex-lays-groundwork-for-bastrop-gas-turbine-blade-foundry), not confirmed on the page I opened]. The idea's $150-300M per $100M of revenue is credible for greenfield. Brownfield expansion is far cheaper: Howmet Wichita Falls about $70M for 230 jobs and 100,000 sq ft ([Area Development](https://www.areadevelopment.com/newsItems/9-29-2026/howmet-aerospace-wichita-falls-texas.shtml)), Doncasters Bochum $52M, Auburn $50M (superalloy melt, 70 jobs). Incumbents hold the cheap option.
- **Illustrative plant (my arithmetic).** $100M revenue at 18-22% EBITDA is $18-22M. On $150-300M of capex that is a 6-15% yield and 7-17 years simple payback, with no ramp losses. If customers fund 80% (the Doncasters ceiling), founders' equity is $30-60M. Without customer money it does not clear a hurdle rate.
- **Valuation.** CPP is priced at 5.9x revenue. Doncasters' first-day close implied about $6.8B of market value ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/doncasters-gains-33-after-1-06-billion-ipo-private-placements)), or about 8x sales and 49x FY25 adjusted EBITDA (my arithmetic), at the top of a cycle. A de novo plant lacks the qualified positions that earn those multiples. Precicast's sale (about CHF 0.37M per employee) is the more honest mid-size comparable, though its revenue is undisclosed.
- **Inputs and people.** Doncasters says vertical integration into superalloys and ceramic cores matters because external supply chains "are currently experiencing significant capacity constraints," and critical quality roles take 6-12 months to train (424B4). A.Y. McDonald's "nine-figure" foundry cited elsewhere in this pool is a brass plumbing foundry ([Foundry Planet](https://www.foundry-planet.com/d/usa-new-foundry-estimated-to-cost-more-than-100-million/)), so it is a weak anchor.

## 7. Regulatory, qualification and liability landscape

- **OEM approval is the gate.** Doncasters says OEM approvals "often tak[e] years." Typical credentials are AS9100, Nadcap for special processes and GE's S-1000 review [unopened summary, [Certivo](https://www.certivo.com/frameworks/ge-supplier-requirements-compliance)]. A 1995 GAO decision shows the OEM's testing defines an approved source for engine blades ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/470676)).
- **Liability.** Recalls, customer penalties and product liability attach to flight-critical parts (424B4 risk factors). Scrap risk is high on new designs: "half or more" may be discarded (AeroDynamic Advisory, in the Reuters piece).
- **Antitrust.** GE-CPP faces review, with Avio Aero as the stated overlap (Reuters piece). No remedy has been reported. A forced divestiture would be sold to an approved buyer, which could create an acquisition nucleus, but regulators favor experienced operators.
- **Export controls.** Fabri advertises ITAR registration and CMMC Level 2 [unopened]. Defense parts need the same.
- **Tax and capital.** OBBBA allows 100% expensing of qualified production property if construction begins after 2025-01-19 and before 2029-01-01 and the property is in service before 2031-01-01 ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-expands-100-depreciation-expensing-opportunities)). A four-year build starting in 2027 would barely fit.
- **No rule forces this spend.** The shock is commercial. The DoW plan names castings as single points of failure but adds no dollars (per the P079 idea file; I did not open the plan).

## 8. Why-now check

The shortage is real: Rystad reports 100 GW of gas turbine and engine orders in 2025, 30% above what OEMs could deliver ([Rystad](https://www.rystadenergy.com/insights/gas-turbine-report-H2-2026)). But the window is closing. Howmet's August 2028 wave, Doncasters' 2028-29 ramp, Safran's 2027 plant and GE's CPP expansion all land first. Jefferies puts SpaceX's products about four years out [unopened]. Cycle risk is on the record: Wood Mackenzie expects turbine orders to peak in 2026, Siemens Energy's CEO says the industry "will remain cyclical," and Mitsubishi Power Americas' CEO expects some orders will not materialize [unopened, [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/energy/mitsubishi-heavy-industry-gas-turbines)]. A 10-year take-or-pay signed in 2027 runs to 2037 or later, past the likely peak.

## 10. What would change the call

- A signed take-or-pay or prepay from a turbine OEM to a non-incumbent (none found).
- A divested CPP plant package offered to an OEM-backed buyer after the antitrust review.
- Doncasters or Howmet publicly declining new partnerships (Doncasters says more are "under negotiation").
- A distressed qualified foundry available at roughly 1x revenue with an OEM willing to co-fund expansion. That is an acquisition, not a startup, and it competes with Pursuit, CPP and Doncasters.

# C15 Dossier: Hot-Spot Contract (capitated root-cause care for the costliest homes)

Date: 2026-10-18. Pool P003, England social landlords' repairs. About 55 searches and fetches. Tags: [V] = read on the page; [S] = search-result summary only, page not read or blocked; [A] = my arithmetic or assumption. GBP/USD 1.30.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: weak, leaning kill as a $100M business. Median about $13M, upside about $58M (5-7 year revenue). The gate's "strong, $20M / $100M" does not survive.** The core observation (repair demand is skewed) is real. The business built on it fails on seven counts:

1. Whole-stock "price per property" contracts already take capitated repair risk, and incumbents are locked in until 2031-36 (section 2).
2. The $100M path needs about a third of all reachable large landlords to hand over 100% of their worst homes (section 3).
3. Pricing at 80% of trailing cost collides with a repair cost curve rising 13-14% a year. The fee basis is gone in about two years without indexation (section 5).
4. The idea's accounting "earned secret" is wrong for the regulator's own metric (section 8).
5. The worst homes are often negative-value assets that boards sell or regenerate, not contract out (section 2).
6. The cross-landlord dataset is not cornered, because Mobysoft already sells it (sections 2 and 8).
7. It needs tens of millions of risk capital and the stop-loss product I could not find (sections 5 and 6).

The kill test (top 5% of homes carry at least 15% of repair spend) is still unrun. I found no published top-5% split. Closest data: section 3.

**Not found:** any firm selling a worst-cohort carve-out with capitated risk. Absence in about ten targeted searches is weak evidence, but it matches the gate.

**Why the carve-out is hard to sell.** Price-per-property is old and normal: St Albans pays "a fixed price for each property every year" on a roughly GBP90M, 15-year deal [S], and Thurrock's own 2015 policy refers to a "Per Annum Price per Property Contract" [V]. A prime on such a contract already carries the hot-spot cost and is already paid to fix root causes. It would see a carve-out as cherry-picking its upside, and its exclusivity clause likely forbids it. Only landlords on visit-priced schedule-of-rates contracts are obvious targets, and I found no data on how many remain.

**The hot spots are often an asset decision.** Landlords test each home's 30-year net present value and normally dispose of negative ones. Southwark's Savills survey found over 4,000 homes below zero; Haringey about 500 (3.4% of stock); the Northern Ireland Housing Executive 44% of stock in negative-average groups [S]. A board's answer for the worst homes is often sell or regenerate. [Southwark](https://www.southwarknews.co.uk/news/your-home-is-worth-less-than-nothing-council-report-evaluates-southwark-properties/), [Haringey](https://www.minutes.haringey.gov.uk/documents/s88245/Stock%20Investment_Oct%2016%20App2%20Options%20Appraisal%20v1%200.pdf).

## 3. Bottom-up market

- Stock: 4.1M social-rented households in 2024-25 [V] ([EHS](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6930595d4bedc0e762303ffa/2024-25_EHS_Headline_Report.pdf)); the idea used 4.4M. A 5% cohort is about 205,000-220,000 homes.
- Reachable payers: the 19 largest private providers hold 42% of units, and 200 groups hold over 96% [V] ([RSH](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/regulator-of-social-housing-2025-global-accounts-published)). The 40th largest association has about 36,000 homes [S] ([Housing Today](https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/onward-homes-appoints-new-chief-executive/5139808.article)). I could not find a count above 25,000 homes; my estimate is about 45-55 including a few big councils [A].
- Spend per home is the weak link. Whole-stock total-asset contracts run about GBP2,240 per home a year (Dacorum GBP23.5M on 10,500 homes [S]; Thurrock GBP24M on about 10,000 [A]), but that includes planned and compliance work. Responsive plus voids was GBP595 per property in 2019-20 (HouseMark via [Broxtowe](https://democracy.broxtowe.gov.uk/documents/s11707/7.1%20NOTE%20Housemark%20Committee%20Report%202.2.21%20003.pdf)). The idea's GBP2,800 "four times average" for the cohort is an unsourced assumption [A].
- Sector scale: GBP10bn of repairs and maintenance in 2024/25, GBP3.9bn capitalised [V] ([RSH](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/regulator-of-social-housing-2025-global-accounts-published)). The cohort pool is about GBP570-620M a year [A], all of it money landlords already spend.

**$100M path.** GBP77M needs about 35,000 homes at GBP2,200. If cohorts run about 2,000 homes per 40,000-home landlord, that is about 17 landlords each handing over 100% of their hot spots. At 45-55 reachable landlords, that is 30-40% of the payer set. The gate counted it as 16% of the national cohort. I count it as heroic share by the lessons' own rule (over 20%).

**Skew evidence, and what it can and cannot support.**
- Thurrock (data Aug 2013-Jul 2014): 3.7 repairs per home against a national 2.5. Of 10,315 homes, 5,450 had two or fewer repairs, 2,072 had three to four, and **2,793 (27%) had five or more**, out of about 38,000 jobs [V]. If the low band averaged 1.2-2.0 repairs, the 5+ band produced roughly 52-64% of jobs [A]. The council attributed this to "particular issues with the property" and to resident approach, both. [Thurrock policy](https://democracy.thurrock.gov.uk/documents/s2411/Responsive%20Repairs%20and%20Maintenance%20Policy.pdf).
- Leicester: 9,007 homes with four or more repairs in 2019-20, GBP88.07 average per repair [S] (page blocked to me).
- The "80% of repairs on 20% of stock" figure is from 2007 Oxford minutes citing KPMG [S]; I could not retrieve the source.
- A 2022 case study of one association found repair costs heavily right-skewed [S] ([paper](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/analysis-and-modelling-of-social-housing-repair-and-maintenance-c/)).
- Reading: concentration is broad (a quarter of homes), not a sharp 5% tail. These are job counts from one council, 12 years old; they are consistent with a 15-25% top-5% share but do not prove it.

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

**Real:** landlords pay for bulk disrepair work (Riverside, PfH lots above). Damp and mould spend is visible: Wigan GBP2M in 2025/26 ([FOI](https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Council/Disclosure-Logs/2025/18547.pdf)), InCommunities about GBP3M ([RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/incommunities-treasury-plc--94bd/half-year-financial-report/9290482)). The Ombudsman says [S] repairs are 45% of complaints, complaints about substandard conditions rose 474% in five years, and repeat failures come from "temporary fixes rather than permanent repairs" and poor diagnosis ([Repairing Trust](https://www.housing-ombudsman.org.uk/2025/05/29/call-for-national-tenant-body-and-funding-review/)). Only 4% of landlords call themselves "very prepared" for phase 2 [S] ([Housing Today](https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/next-phase-of-awaabs-law-to-come-into-force-in-november/5143162.article)).

**Not shown:** anyone paying for risk transfer on a cohort. Demand is for works and software, not capitation.

**The disrepair "second payer line" is small and concentrated.** 78 councils and 3 associations spent about GBP25M in 2024, with claims up from 1,829 to 8,999 a year ([Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/foi-data-shows-big-rises-in-housing-disrepair-claims-and-costs)). Hackney paid GBP1M compensation plus GBP3.6M claimant legal fees on 508 claims ([Hackney Citizen](https://www.hackneycitizen.co.uk/2025/06/27/town-hall-legal-costs-disrepair-past-year/)). Lambeth's claimed GBP15.4M of 2025/26 legal costs plus a GBP19.7M provision comes from a party press release ([Lib Dems](https://fleet.libdems.org.uk/001b000003cug4e/news/article/lib-dems-reveal-shocking-cost-of-housing-disrepair-in-lambeth)); the FOI figure is GBP8.4M of damages over five years ([Brixton Buzz](https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2026/05/lambeth-council-pays-out-more-than-8-4m-in-housing-disrepair-claims-as-london-payouts-top-31-million/)). Nationally this is tens of millions, mostly London. Claimant costs stay uncapped until at least October 2028 ([Anthony Collins](https://blog.anthonycollins.com/post/102kq77/disrepair-claims-fixed-recoverable-costs-exemption-extended-to-2028)).

**Landlord's gain:** 20% of GBP2,800 is about GBP560 a home a year, or GBP1.1M on a 2,000-home cohort [A], plus avoided claims. Real, but small next to a 5-10 year lock-in and a Procurement Act process.

**Signer:** Finance Director or Director of Assets, with board approval. Sector cash interest cover was 59% in the quarter to June 2026, and the regulator is watching covenants ([RSH Q1](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/rsh-publishes-its-quarterly-survey-for-q1-april-to-june-2026)). That squeezes the capital for root-cause works a capitation depends on.

## 5. Unit economics and analogs

Model per cohort home [A]: trailing cost GBP2,800 (idea); fee 80% = GBP2,240; overhead, data and insurance 12% of fee (GBP270); root-cause works GBP2,000 amortised over five years (GBP400 a year). For scale, current damp cases cost GBP1,150 per case at Stevenage ([minutes](https://democracy.stevenage.gov.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=18381)) and GBP1,480 per property at Redditch ([report](https://moderngovwebpublic.redditchbc.gov.uk/documents/s53486/Damp%20and%20Mould%20Committe%20Report%20AWR%20final%20covering%20report.pdf)); roofs and structure cost more.

| Cut in recurring cost | Margin if provider funds the fix | Margin if landlord funds the fix |
|---|---|---|
| 25% (analytics-level, as at Gloucester) | -GBP530 a home, -24% | -GBP130, -6% |
| 40% | -GBP110, -5% | +GBP290, +13% |
| 60% | +GBP450, +20% | +GBP850, +38% |

Break-even needs a 44% cut when the provider funds the fix and 30% when the landlord does. The idea's 12-18% margin needs 40-60% cuts. The only measured cut I found is 25% on repeat visits from analytics alone, a vendor case ([GCH](https://www.housing-technology.com/gch-slashes-repeat-repairs-with-mobysoft/)). Mobysoft's "up to 35%" and Switchee's results are unmeasured vendor claims.

**Cost trend breaks the 80% price.** Sector repairs spend was GBP9.7bn over the last 12 months and is forecast at GBP11.1bn for the next, +14% ([RSH](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/rsh-publishes-its-quarterly-survey-for-q1-april-to-june-2026)); 2024/25 was +13%. At 13%, 80% of trailing cost is about 102% of true cost two years on [A]. The fee needs indexation and a volume re-opener, which fixed-price buyers resist. ForHousing's early exit is the live warning: it cited rising "number and cost" of repairs under a 2017 fixed arrangement.

**Analogs.** Managed-care capitation is the idea's model. It is capital-hungry and unforgiving: Cano Health filed Chapter 11 in February 2024 with about $1.2bn of assets against $1.4bn of debt, tied to capitated Medicare Advantage ([Healthcare Finance](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/cano-health-files-chapter-11-bankruptcy)); CVS paid $10.6bn for Oak Street ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/cvs-closes-106b-acquisition-oak-street-health-expand-primary-care-footprint)), but I found no post-deal profitability. Closest in-sector benchmark: Mears earns 5.7% on GBP1.1bn.

**Capital [A].** At 35,000 homes: first-loss buffer about 10% of a GBP98M cost base, roughly GBP10M; provider-funded fixes up to GBP70M over six years (about GBP12M a year); plus bonds and professional indemnity. Pilot scale: a 500-home cohort is GBP1.1M of fee and about GBP1M of fix spend. Total: GBP20-40M of risk capital by year five. That is balance-sheet business, not venture software.

## 6. Regulatory and liability

- **FSMA.** The FCA treats a seller's warranty matching its usual quality obligations as unlikely to be insurance, but one given by someone other than the seller, or "significantly more extensive in content, scope or duration", as likely insurance; and where the event happens regardless of the provider's work quality it may be insurance ([PERG 6.7](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg6/perg6s7); [Freeths 2026](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/warranty-service-contract-or-insurance-navigating-the-regulatory-landscape-for-product-protection-arrangements/)). A capitated service where the provider does every repair resembles St Albans-style pricing and is likely fine. The gate is right that the five-year uncapped guarantee is the risk. Stop-loss for repair-cost aggregates: I found no product, and insurers exclude gradual damage [S, via gate].
- **Procurement Act 2023.** Registered providers are contracting authorities ([PfH](https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/article/is-your-housing-association-subject-to-the-procurement-act-2023)). The services threshold is GBP207,720 including VAT from 1 January 2026 ([Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2025/procurement-updated-thresholds-for-january-2026-published/), [S]). A 500-home pilot at about GBP1.1M a year is over it, so even a pilot needs a compliant route.
- **Duty stays with the landlord.** The s10A implied term sits on the landlord, with damages and specific performance in the County Court ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/08/awaab-s-law-phase-2-the-next-compliance-challenge)). A contractor can only sit behind an indemnity. Litigation needs SRA-regulated lawyers.

## 7. Why-now

**Real:** phase 2 regulations were laid 13 July 2026 and start 30 November 2026, adding electrical, falls, fire and explosion, excess cold and heat, structural and hygiene hazards ([CIH](https://www.cih.org/news/second-phase-of-awaabs-law-laid-before-parliament/), [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-phase-2-guidance-for-social-housing-landlords/awaabs-law-phase-2-guidance-for-social-landlords)). Clyde & Co expect a "significant increase in claims activity".

**Cuts against the idea:** the same shock raises the cost basis the fee is set from, drives insourcing (Basildon, Cheshire West), and is already being absorbed by framework disrepair lots. The one-off electrical catch-up deadline is 1 November 2026.

## 8. Upside-case assumptions: tested

| Gate assumption | Result |
|---|---|
| Top 5% carry at least 25% of spend | Unproven. Thurrock is consistent with fat-tail concentration but is old, count-based and half tenant-driven. |
| 15-20 landlords hand over 1,500-2,500 homes each within six years | Weak. No precedent for a cohort carve-out; price-per-property incumbents locked to 2031-36; landlords insourcing; each contract of about GBP4.5M a year is a flexible-procedure tender. |
| Reinsurer prices the tail on a shared dataset | No product found. Mobysoft already holds a multi-landlord repair dataset (7M jobs claimed) and sells analytics for under GBP300k a year, so the data is not cornered. |
| Framework call-off avoids 12-18 month tenders | Mostly closed: PfH's winners are named and Riverside's term runs to about 2029. A newcomer must subcontract to a winner, making the prime channel and competitor. |
| Capitalising a fix lifts the EBITDA-MRI covenant | **Wrong for EBITDA-MRI**, which deducts capitalised major repairs; it helps only plain-EBITDA covenants, which many providers have moved to ([Chatham](https://www.chathamfinancial.com/insights/covenant-trends-in-the-uk-social-housing-sector)). |

Realistic range [A]: median 3 landlords x 1,500 homes = 4,500 homes = about GBP10M (about $13M); upside 10 landlords x 2,000 homes = 20,000 homes = about GBP45M (about $58M). $100M needs the 17-landlord case.

## 9. What would change my mind, and gaps

**Change my mind:** (a) job-level data from three landlords with the top 5% at 25% or more of cost; (b) a 100-home pilot with a measured cost cut of 45% or more; (c) two schedule-of-rates landlords with written carve-out clauses; (d) a stop-loss quote; (e) a prime willing to white-label.

**Gaps:** no top-5% cost split exists publicly; mix of schedule-of-rates versus price-per-property contracts unknown; Leicester, Oxford, Ashford and Riverside source pages were blocked or unreadable (marked [S]); the Thurrock and Dacorum home counts are dated or unconfirmed; I did not read the full 2022 skew paper; the Lambeth cost figures conflict; I found no damp-and-mould cover or repair stop-loss product, which is absence of evidence only.

# C10 Dossier: Signed-Off (fixed-price, AI-native nuclear engineering house; acquire the audit, automate the work)

Pool P038, researched 2026-10-18. Evidence tags: [V] primary source read this session; [S] secondary, aggregator or search snippet only; [I] my inference or arithmetic.

## 1. Bottom line

- The money is real but the idea's own market number is not sourced. NEI's 2025 capex figure is now verified. The $1.1-1.4B "outsourced engineering" pool is still an assumption, and my headcount cross-check puts it at the high end (about $0.7-1.2B today) [I].
- The proposed wedge (50.59 work) is tiny: about 3 full evaluations per plant per year.
- The "QA accountability" premise is half wrong. Appendix B leaves the licensee responsible, and AI vendors already sell to licensee engineers.
- The acquire-and-automate thesis is already under way. Pelican's nuclear-dedicated fund owns or backs GSE Solutions, EXCEL Services and Everstar (AI licensing drafting). Arlington (Enercon) and New Mountain (Jensen Hughes) set the price of acquisition targets.
- My view: median outcome is $25-45M revenue by year 6. Reaching $100M needs five hard conditions (Section 8) to hold together; I put that at roughly 5-10% [I]. It is the best idea in the pool and still wounded.

## 2. Market, bottom-up

Industry metrics: revenue per head, utilization, EBITDA margin, approved-supplier and customer count, backlog.

**Verified anchors.** NEI's August 2026 cost report (EUCG data): total cost $36.46/MWh (+5.1%); capital $7.59B vs $6.10B in 2024; labor about $8.8B; uprate capex $365M in 2025 vs $66M in 2022 but $3.3B at the 2012 peak; regulatory capex $270M vs a $2.66B peak in 2014. NEI attributes the 2025 capital rise "largely" to inflation plus life-extension upgrades, and capital cost is still 36.4% below 2012 [V] ([NEI](https://www.nei.org/getContentAsset/47fa8caa-9b0d-4029-932c-07f902e82f4f/8d8ff8d6-b2ae-401b-a63c-f6b108e809d2/2024-Costs-in-Context-final.pdf?language=en-US)). So "capex +24%" is a rebound from a trough, not a record engineering wave.

**Outsourced engineering pool: no published figure found.** Three triangulations:
- The idea's assumption (10% of capital plus 5% of non-labor opex) gives about $1.1B [I].
- Supplier revenue: Enercon, which says it supports about 90% of US plants, shows about $210M in aggregator data and $220M in a 2012 company case study [S] ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/enercon-services), [Great Game](https://www.greatgame.com/resources/case-studies/topic/oil-gas-chemicals)). Sargent & Lundy (S&L) revenue is undisclosed. A boutique, DP Engineering, had $20M+ revenue and about 110 staff [V] ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/944480/000094448019000006/exh99-2.htm)).
- Headcount: operating-plant engineers are 6,900-7,400 FTE in 2025, rising to 9,200-10,400 by 2030 [V] ([NCEA/USC](https://energyanalytics.org/nuclear-workforce/)). At $120-180k revenue per supplier head, $1.1B implies 6,000-9,000 outsourced engineers, nearly as many as in-house. That is plausible only if licensing consultants and design houses are counted, so I use $0.7-1.2B for 2026 and $1.0-1.6B by 2030 [I].

**Payers x spend [I].** About 25 owners and 55 sites. At $0.9B, that is about $16M of outsourced engineering per site per year. $100M is about 8-14% of today's pool (6-10% of the 2030 pool), or $1.8M from every site. Ten single-site operators at 20% share give $15-35M. Beyond that you must displace multi-year "engineer of choice" alliances: Exelon-S&L covered 17 units (2002, current status unverified) ([Power Eng](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/sargent-lundy-signs-engineer-of-choice-contract-for-exelons-nuclear-and-fossil-fleets/)), and TVA moved most nuclear design to S&L in 2006 [S] ([Power Eng](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/sargent-lundy-selected-for-tva-nuclear-plants/)).

**The 50.59 wedge does not pay.** Wolf Creek filed six full 50.59 evaluations in 2017-18 [V] ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1908/ML19086A114.pdf)); Callaway reported four across May 2016-Dec 2017 [S] ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1815/ML18158A557.pdf)). That is about 3 per plant per year, or 150-300 fleet-wide; at $30-50k each, under $15M [I]. Screenings are more numerous but low value. This is a quality-proof pilot, not revenue. Real volume sits in calculations, design changes and project engineering; I found no hours or cost data for those.

**Timing.** Vistra says most uprate growth capital falls after 2028 [S] ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/vistra-corp-q4-2025-earnings-010956189.html)). Crane restart is targeted for 2027 and Duane Arnold for 2029 [S] ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2025-10-28/article-7501/nextera-and-google-ink-a-deal-to-restart-duane-arnold/)).

**Free and in-house substitutes.** DOE/INL/Argonne run AI-drafting work in the open. Owners' own 6,900-7,400 engineers, equipped with Nuclearn, Atomic Canyon or Westinghouse tools, are the real substitute: every point of productivity they gain shrinks overflow demand [I]. Utilities Service Alliance already contracts procurement-engineering services for members [S] ([USA Inc.](https://usainc.org/?p=2983)).

**Not found:** any startup selling fixed-price, AI-produced Appendix B deliverables. The white space is real but narrow. AI-enabled services roll-ups exist elsewhere (Thrive Holdings is reportedly raising $2B for accounting/IT; General Catalyst earmarked $1.5B) but I found no engineering example [S] ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3926838)). Critics argue acquired legacy pricing, workflows and client relationships resist change [S] ([Emcap](https://emcap.substack.com/p/the-ai-roll-up-trap)).

**Moat view.** S&L: switching costs, intangibles and scale all strong. Enercon/Pond: moderate-strong. Pelican platform: moderate. Signed-Off at launch: weak on all four; by year 5 only moderate switching costs (listed status, accepted per-plant templates) [I].

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

For: owners outsource design under multi-year alliances (above); one major utility awarded GSE a $35M/2-year staff-augmentation master agreement in 2020 [S] ([Staffing Industry](https://www.staffingindustry.com/Editorial/Industrial-Staffing-Report/Dec.-17-2020/Contract-watch-GSE-Systems-awarded-two-master-services-agreements)); AtkinsRéalis-FANCO is up to CAD 250M (US$181M) over five years [S] ([NEI Mag](https://www.neimagazine.com/news/fanco-atkinsrealis-forge-alliance/)); PE paying a reported $1.25B of equity for Enercon.

Against or missing: I found no RFP, tender or owner statement asking for fixed-price AI-produced deliverables. MIT Technology Review's uprate story names price and equipment, not engineer shortage, as the constraint [V] ([MIT TR](http://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/15/1146577/nuclear-power-upgrade)). Shortage data is workforce-level (IEA, USC), not overflow-demand-level.

## 5. Unit economics and capital

- **Rates.** One S&L 2022 invoice in a Texas filing shows $102-$270/hr from Associate I to Principal [S] ([PUC](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/56211_218_1394038.PDF)). At about $160-200 blended and 1,500 billable hours, that is $240-300k per billable engineer. Actual all-in revenue per head: DP Engineering about $180k, Enercon about $120k [V/S]. So "400 engineers at $250k" understates staffing: about 450-560 total staff at today's productivity (rate model vs DP Engineering actuals; Enercon's ratio implies more) [I].
- **Margins.** DP Engineering's earn-out EBITDA thresholds ($2.75-4.3M on $20M+) imply 13-21% [V/I]; consulting firms generally run low-to-mid 20s% [S] ([Chaffe](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/consulting-firm-multiples-climb-with-size-per-chaffe-analysis)).
- **AI lever [I].** Assume 30% fewer hours per deliverable and a 15% price cut: revenue per hour worked rises about 21% ($200 to $243). $100M then needs about 370-410 staff instead of 450-500 (rate model). That is a few EBITDA points, available to any rival using the same models, and independent verification (Criterion III) does not compress.
- **Capital.** DP Engineering sold in 2019 for $13.5M cash plus up to $5M earn-out (about 0.7x revenue upfront) [V]. 2026 pricing is higher: lower-middle-market engineering 4-8x EBITDA, specialists 9-12x [S] ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/?p=15590)); technical services index 12.5x in Q1 2026 [S] ([Investec](https://www.investec.com/advisory?p=58126)). Reaching $100M by acquisition at about 1.0-1.5x revenue is $60-120M plus working capital: PE-scale, not founder-scale [I]. Pilot plus a software-QA plan is low seven figures [I, unsourced].

## 6. Regulatory and liability landscape

- **Appendix B** [V] ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/chapter-I/part-50/appendix-Appendix%20B%20to%20Part%2050)): the licensee "shall retain responsibility" for the QA program (Criterion I); design checking must be by people other than the original designers (III); the licensee assesses supplier effectiveness at intervals (VII). A supplier cannot absorb the owner's accountability; it sells process comfort.
- **Part 21** [V] ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/chapter-I/part-21/section-21.3)): "basic component" includes safety-related design, analysis and consulting services, so suppliers carry defect-evaluation and reporting duties.
- **AI.** No NRC rule for licensee AI use was found; DOE proposes an NRC working group [V]. Whether an AI tool in a human-verified workflow needs NQA-1 Subpart 2.7 software QA is unresolved in my sources. If not required, the QA moat is moot; if required, no vendor claims it [I].
- **Approved-supplier path.** NUPIC audits need a supplier with at least five NRC-licensee customers; the standard cycle is 33 months [V] ([NUPIC](https://www.nupic.com)). I found no source for the idea's "12-24 months." A greenfield entrant is in a catch-22, which is why acquisition is the route. Rebuilding an acquired firm's QA program and tools may trigger customer re-acceptance [I]. NEI 22-04 Rev 1 (Aug 2026) would let ISO 9001 suppliers serve safety-related work because NQA-1 suppliers are shrinking [S] ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2621/ML26219A186.pdf)); helpful for scarcity now, erodes it if approved (aimed at components).
- **Professional licensure.** NSPE says reviewing documents after preparation, without involvement in design, does not meet "responsible charge" [S] ([NSPE](https://nspe.org/node/25565)). Where state PE seals apply, AI-drafts-human-reviews is legally weaker.
- **Insurance.** ISO generative-AI exclusion endorsements took effect Jan 1, 2026 and some carriers exclude AI claims from E&O [S] ([Jenner](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/in-law360-partner-david-kroeger-examines-emerging-ai-insurance-exclusions)); no policy text seen. A fixed-price, rework-guaranteed AI house may carry uninsured risk. Price-Anderson (to 2065) covers nuclear-incident claims, not contract or rework losses [S] ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/upandatom/2024/04/congress-gives-40-year-extension-for-price-anderson-nuclear-liability-framework)).

## 7. Is the why-now real?

Partly. Real: restarts, $4.3B of Constellation uprates, DOE loans, thin engineer pipeline (about 15,400 nuclear engineers nationally, median wage $135,760 [V]). Overstated: the drafting speedup is public (INL, DOE) and open to all; DOE's roadmap is closing the "unowned AI output" gap; incumbents are buying AI and capital. A first-mover window is 12-24 months at most [I].

## 9. Synthesis

Scenarios [I]: Bull 10% (requires b through f together; $100M+ by year 7). Base 40-45% (roll up two or three boutiques, a handful of owners, $25-45M). Bear 45-50% (stalls under $15M or is absorbed by Pelican/Enercon).

Durable advantage: approved-supplier listing plus accepted per-plant templates (moderate switching costs). Structural vulnerabilities: services margins; a commoditizing AI edge; owners keep the liability so will not pay a premium for "accountability"; E&O insurability. Current state is early and contested.

What would change my mind: one owner signing a multi-year fixed-price deliverable contract; NRC or EPRI language that independently verified AI output needs no separate tool QA; a qualified boutique available below 6x EBITDA.

Cheapest next test (2-3 months): interview 10 design-engineering directors on percent outsourced, appetite for per-deliverable pricing, and whether their QA group would require NQA-1 on the tool; run one paid calculation-revision pilot with the owner's engineer verifying; get E&O quotes with AI use disclosed; ask a broker for 2-3 boutique targets and valuations. Founder fit: this needs a credentialed nuclear QA and licensing insider plus institutional capital.

**Unverified:** outsourced-engineering pool; S&L and Enercon revenue; per-plant calculation and design-change volume; E&O policy text; NRC licensee AI guidance; Nuclearn's NRC deck and the Axios Atomic Canyon piece (403 on fetch; snippet only).

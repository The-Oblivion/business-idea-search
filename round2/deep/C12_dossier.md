# C12 dossier: Outside Care Plan (P108, the DOC's own off-site health plan)

Prepared 2026-10-18. Tags: (S) read in a source this session, (A) my arithmetic or assumption, (U) secondary, aggregator or search-summary only. About 45 searches and fetches, including the Pew 2018 PDF, the DOJ OIG reports, Polk County's sole-source notice and Limestone County's Heritage contract.

## Bottom line

The pain is real. State prisons pay for off-site care at rates that auditors have called far above Medicare, and failed vendors left hospitals unpaid. But I found no state that has paid a third party to take hospital risk on a per-member-per-month basis, and none that I found tried it in 2026. Every 2026 buyer swapped one full-service vendor for another. The spending that does get unbundled goes to low-fee claims and network administrators. A risk-bearing version can reach $100M of revenue only as pass-through premium. At 5-8% margin that is $5-8M a year of profit, which needs 3-4 statewide wins in a market that procures a few contracts a year. Verdict: **weak, closer to kill than to advance.** My view of year 5-7 premium: $0 (about 55%), $25-40M (about 35%), $100M+ (about 10%) (A).

## 1. Competitors and substitutes (gate list checked, then extended)

**Confirmed, with new numbers**
- **Heritage Health Solutions** (Coppell, TX) is the closest incumbent, and the gate saw only its county work.
  - It has run the U.S. Marshals' nationwide managed-care contract since 2007, re-awarded 2017 and running to FY2027. From Oct 2017 to Sept 2024 the USMS spent about $398M on medical care and $222M on drugs through it, and paid Heritage about $40M in fees. It reprices every claim to the lowest of Medicare, the network rate or billed charges, under 18 U.S.C. 4006. [DOJ OIG 25-096](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-09/25-096.pdf) (S)
  - County work: Polk County FL is sole-sourcing a five-year TPA contract in 2026. The notice requires a TPA license, URAC "Full" accreditation, and contracts with all five Polk hospitals, and it applies Medicare-based statutory repricing. [Polk ISS 26-293](https://www.polkfl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ISS_26-293_Inmate_Medical-Pharmaceuitical_Services.pdf) (S)
  - Limestone County AL pays Heritage $40 per claim. Claims are invoiced at 140% of the Medicare allowable, and in-network providers are paid within 45 days. [Limestone minutes](https://limestonecounty-al.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/meetings-and-minutes/2024/07/7-15-24-cmm.pdf) (S) Coshocton OH planned $50 per claim. (U)
- **Prime Health Services** runs savings-share repricing through seven sheriff-association programs since 2011 and claims $88M+ of cumulative savings with no savings, no fee. [Prime](https://primehealthservices.com/markets/corrections/) (S for the claim, vendor-reported).
- **CorrectCare Integrated Health and NaphCare** (Safety Net Connect, which the gate listed, was not rechecked): NaphCare describes itself as managing off-site care for the BOP. [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/prison-health-care-provider-naphcare-agrees-settle-false-claims-act-allegations) (U) CorrectCare is a corrections-only UM, network and claims shop, with revenue of about $7.6M from an unverified profile. [agentman](https://agentman.ai/medman/payers/correctcare-integrated-health-non-louisiana-jails) (U)

**Found that the gate missed**
- **Correctional Health Partners** (Denver) is a physician-owned TPA doing claims, prior authorization, network and pharmacy. It lists partnerships in CO, CA and OR and stopped delivering care in 2022. [Marit](https://www.marithealth.com/e/correctional-health-partners-oh3h3) (U) It bid Nebraska's DOC claims-payment RFP 120174 O3 against **BCBS of Nebraska, UMR (a UnitedHealthcare unit), Falling Colors and Innovative Claims Strategies**; intent to award was posted 4/29/25. [Nebraska DAS](https://das.nebraska.gov/materiel/purchasing/120174%20O3/RFP%20120174%20O3.html) (S) Even the plain claims function drew five bidders, including a UnitedHealthcare unit and the state Blue.
- **PGBA** pays North Carolina's inmate hospital claims. [NC DPS report](https://www.ncleg.gov/Files/Library/agency/dps12805.pdf) (S) California's network and prior-authorization contract was held by Health Net from 2014, and its current round (SD25-00019) uses a state-designated TPA for claims. [Govly](https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16772111) (U; sources give 8/18 and 10/30/2026 as the response date).
- **State self-administration is the biggest substitute.** Pew counts 17 states delivering care directly and 4 on a university model, and these pay hospitals from their own line. Five carve inpatient care out of vendor contracts (AR, IL, PA, VT, VA), 6 share risk and 8 use all-inclusive capitation. [Pew 2018](https://www.pew.org/-/media/assets/2018/07/prisons-and-hospital-care_report.pdf) (S, read from PDF text) The idea's "36 outsource, 14 in-house" does not match this.
- **Statute is the free substitute.**
  - Florida caps non-contracted providers at 110% of Medicare (125% if the provider has a negative margin). Enrolled county bills (HB 4059, 4041) and DOC bill HB 913 follow the same pattern. [FL House analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/913/Analyses/h0913c.JUB.PDF) (S)
  - New Hampshire's cap is 110% (S), North Carolina's default is twice Medicaid or 70% of charges (S), and Georgia law has a Medicaid-rate ceiling for non-contracted emergency care (U).
  - State Medicaid inpatient billing is a second substitute: North Carolina saved about $10M and Massachusetts about $5.8M a year. [Mass. auditor](https://www.mass.gov/news/audit-finds-58-million-in-annual-inmate-health-care-cost-savings) (S)

**Refuted or corrected:** the "Centene's Centurion" competitor. Two secondary sources say Centene sold Centurion in January 2023 to an undisclosed buyer; I could not find the primary filing. [AFSC](https://investigate.afsc.org/node/960) (U) No funded startup doing risk-based off-site care turned up. I searched Crunchbase-style sources and found only unrelated correctional firms (U).

**Moat read:** Payers (BCBS, UMR, PGBA) and Heritage already hold hospital contracts and TPA licenses, and statutes hand every competitor Medicare-based pricing for free. Only the risk transfer is new, and states have not bought it.

## 2. Bottom-up market

- **Pool:** Pew put state prison health spending at $8.1B in FY2015, which I grow to about $11B (A). Off-site is 20-27% of that: 20% across ten states, Virginia 27%, New York 23%. (S, Pew) That is $2.2-3.0B.
- **Reachable only where the DOC holds the risk.** Vendor-risk states are 8 of 40 classified (AZ, DE, FL, IN, KS, KY, MA, MO) and include big budgets. I take about 65% of spend as DOC-risk (A), then remove expansion-state inpatient stays that Medicaid already pays (Pew describes a "large shift"). That leaves **$1.1-1.8B** (A), close to the gate's $1.4-2.0B.
- **Per-state scale (S):**
  - Virginia: $62M off-site in FY2016, with nearly half spent on 179 people.
  - Oregon: $790K-$922K a week, or about $41-48M a year (A), and "nearly 25%" of its health budget. [Oregon LFO](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025I1/Downloads/CommitteeMeetingDocument/311156) (S)
  - North Carolina's UNC network alone: about $29-32M a year.
  - Texas hospital line: $137M of $435M in FY2012. [LBB](https://www.lbb.texas.gov/Documents/Publications/Info_Graphic/Correctional%20Managed%20Health%20Care%20Prepared%20for%20the%20Senate%20Finance%20Committee.pdf) (S, dated)
- **Revenue math:**
  - Risk-bearing: $100M of premium is roughly 5-9% of the pool. That is 2-3 mid-size states or one large one, at a 5-8% margin (gate and idea figure) or $5-8M of profit.
  - Fee model: Heritage's USMS take is about 10% of medical spend ($40M on $398M, A). Applied to the whole pool, the fee pool is about $110-180M, so 20% share is $20-35M. County claim fees at $40-50 are tiny: Polk's roughly 7,600 outpatient claims would be about $0.3M a year (A).
- **Federal and jails:** the BOP spent $327M on outside medical in FY2014 under per-institution contracts with third-party administrators. [DOJ OIG E-16-04](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/e1604.pdf) (S) The USMS is about $60M a year. Jails are bought county by county, and Wellpath's local-government contracts used "spending caps" on off-site care (U), so counties rarely hold uncapped risk to hand over.

## 3. Demand and willingness to pay

**For (real pain, weak proof of pay):**
- Oregon asked its legislature for $14.35M one-time because clearing an off-site backlog pushed weekly spend up 17% (790K to 922K). The DOC also bought new off-site billing services. (S)
- Unpaid hospital arrears from failed vendors: more than 750 Georgia providers claim $75.6M from Wellpath [JEMS](https://www.jems.com/ems-management/wellpath-bankruptcy-may-stick-ga-ems-with-state-prisoners-medical-bills/) (S); Michigan hospitals say they are owed $35M+ and EMS about $6M, under a risk-share contract. [Michigan AG letter](https://audgen.michigan.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/MDOC-Prisoner-Healthcare-Contract-Legislative-Request-–-AG-Letter-178293.pdf) (S)
- Overpayment audits: BOP institutions paid 115-385% of Medicare, and the OIG estimated at least $100M of excess in FY2014. North Carolina's audit found 467% and California's 250% of Medicare (U, PLN-style summaries).

**Against (what buyers actually did):**
- Alabama cancelled YesCare's roughly $1.06B contract and moved to an emergency NaphCare deal, 24 months, terms undisclosed. [ADOC](https://www.doc.alabama.gov/?p=534221) (S)
- Illinois ended Wexford's $4B deal for a one-year, $507M Centurion contract. [WBEZ](https://www.wbez.org/health-medicine/2026/07/07/illinois-department-of-corrections-health-care-contract-centurion-wexford) (S) Indiana extended Centurion by $213M and expected a new RFP, and Kansas moved to VitalCore at about $89M a year. [PLN](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2026/jun/1/kansas-doc-replaces-centurion-with-another-prison-healthcare-contractor/) (S)
- Florida went the other way from risk transfer: a cost-plus Centurion contract, with a markup cut from 13.5% to 11.5%, and a cost-reimbursed maximum of $572.6M rising to $584.8M. [Capitolist](https://thecapitolist.com/florida-department-of-corrections-locks-in-guaranteed-profit-for-vendor-despite-resistance-from-lawmakers/) (S)
- Texas's "capitated" university model leaves all deficits with the state, per the State Auditor. [SAO 25-012](https://sao.texas.gov/Reports/Main/25-012.pdf) (U) Governments keep the risk themselves.
- The closest live unbundling is California's SD25-00019: network plus prior authorization for about 90,000 people, five years plus five one-year options, and no risk transfer. It is a bellwether for the idea, not a win for it. (U)

**Who signs:** DOC commissioner or CFO, with legislative appropriation. No signer has been shown to hold a budget line for a risk transfer.

## 4. Unit economics

- **Fee analogs:** Heritage 10% of medical spend at federal scale; $40-50 per claim at county scale; Prime keeps a share of savings it documents itself.
- **Risk analog is bad.** Wellpath's court filings reportedly showed a combined negative gross profit of about $40M on its Michigan and Georgia contracts, 2022 to mid-2024. [Elevenflo](https://elevenflo.com/blog/wellpath-bankruptcy) (U; an aggregator summarizing filings). It also said it ended about 65 contracts for risk and insurance reasons. Michigan was a 50/50 risk share to a cap, and Georgia held hospital risk inside the vendor price. Wyoming's 50/50 off-site risk share lowered cost for the state, so risk sharing can help the buyer. [Wyoming LSO](https://wyoleg.gov/LSOResearch/2017/17RM011%20%20Binder%20Final%20(3)%207.21.2017.pdf) (U)
- **Pricing is adverse.** The DOCs with the worst off-site problems are the ones that most want to outsource, and their historical claims understate true demand (Oregon's 17% rise). Priced on the old run rate, a carve-out would have lost about $6.9M a year on a $41M base (A). Concentration is high (179 people drove nearly half of Virginia's $62M), so stop-loss matters. In a 2004 audit, hospital-contract stop-loss triggers cost California up to about $9.3M in one year. [Cal. auditor](https://information.auditor.ca.gov/reports/summary/2003-125) (S)
- **Price gap:** county contracts sit near Medicare plus 40%, while North Carolina's UNC deal is 240% of Medicare. A 15-day-pay discount is real in counties and uncertain at big state hospital systems.

## 5. Capital needs (A unless noted)

- **Operating:** At $100M of premium, a 15-45 day gap between paying hospitals and being reimbursed by the state ties up $4-11M of working capital. A TPA license and URAC accreditation are table stakes (Polk). Add a performance bond, which is typical of state contracts (U).
- **Loss capital:** to survive one 15-20% volume shock (Oregon's was 17%) I would want surplus or a reinsurance quote of 15-20% of premium, $15-20M. The gate figure of "$60-70M" applies to an insurer and is not what this idea needs.
- **Funding path:** a services business with low margin and a few large customers is a poor venture fit. It is closer to a Medicaid managed-care shop and would likely need a strategic backer.

## 6. Regulatory and liability

- **Licensing:** TPA licensing follows NAIC Model 90-1 in most states, and insurers and HMOs are often exempt from it (Delaware text). [Delaware](https://legis.delaware.gov/SessionLaws/Chapter?id=41967) (S) Eight states have vendors holding hospital risk under all-inclusive capitation (Pew), and none of the sources I read mention an insurer license, so a service-contract structure is plausible but unconfirmed. I found no state opinion on a stand-alone per-member off-site contract (gap).
- **Liability:** the idea's own advantage, utilization review that gets referrals done on time, makes the company the gatekeeper. Private medical contractors in prisons act under color of state law for civil-rights claims (West v. Atkins, background knowledge, not re-verified). Recent verdicts show denied-care exposure, for example a $763K Indiana verdict against Centurion for denied kidney treatment (PLN headline, U). Professional-liability capacity is tightening (Wellpath cited insurance issues).
- **Rates:** state statutes can cut the discount you sell and also cap what hospitals will take, so a Medicare-plus network can fail to form. Florida's DOC bill HB 913 would let DOC-contracted providers be paid above 110% of Medicare, which I read as a sign that contracted hospitals want a premium (A; bill status unconfirmed).

## 7. Is the why-now real?

Partly. The failure wave is real (Wellpath Chapter 11, YesCare, Illinois, Alabama, Kansas). But buyers answered with replacements, and the thin hospital-payment arrears show up as vendor risk, not as an appetite for a new structure. Medicaid expansion shrinks the manageable inpatient pool, and nine states have trigger laws to end expansion if the federal share falls below 90% (AZ, AR, IL, IN, MT, NH, NC, UT, VA). [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/medicaid-expansion-funding-trigger-laws-9-states-trump-administration/) (S) Work requirements start in 2027 (U). I found nothing on how the 2025 federal budget law changed the inmate inpatient exception (gap).

## 8. The upside-case assumptions

| Assumption | Evidence | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 2-3 states split hospital payment out to a third party | No 2026 precedent; five 2025-26 awards stayed full-service. CDCR unbundles network and UM but keeps risk | Low |
| Hospitals take Medicare-plus for 15-day pay | Counties at about 140%; BOP 115-385%; NC UNC 240%; Florida needs contract premiums | Medium in counties, low at big systems |
| Licensing is manageable | TPA license and URAC are routine; risk-bearing unknown | Medium |
| Stop-loss prices workably | Exists for jails (PRISM CIMI, $1M per inmate [PRISM](https://prismrisk.gov/coverages/property-casualty/miscellaneous-coverages/catastrophic-inmate-medical-insurance-cimi), S); cost not found | Medium |
| 5-9% of the pool in 5-7 years | Needs 3-4 statewide awards, each a five-year contract | Low |

## 9. Verdict and next step

- **Do not advance** as a venture. It is a thin-margin risk business with free substitutes (statute, Medicaid billing, TPAs) and no buyer who has asked for risk transfer. The best honest version is a Heritage-style fee manager at about $10-35M of revenue, not a $100M company.
- **What would change my mind:** a state or large county that has already unbundled (Oregon, California, Polk) agreeing, in writing, to a fixed per-member price for off-site care with a hospital-payment guarantee.
- **Cheapest test:** public-records requests for three years of off-site claims in Oregon, Virginia and North Carolina, repriced at 140% of Medicare, plus a call to each DOC finance director and to the SD25-00019 contact on how the award was structured.

**Gaps:** no primary source for Centurion's sale; HB 4059 and HB 913 final status; CDCR SD25-00019 dates conflict; Wellpath's Michigan and Georgia loss figure is secondary; no state-level off-site totals past 2016 outside Oregon; BOP rate changes since 2016 not found.

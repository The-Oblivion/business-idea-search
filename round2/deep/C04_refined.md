# C04 Refined: Time-to-Power Desk (P032 idea 5, rebuilt after three fatal critiques)

Date: 2026-10-18. Verdict: **alive, on probation.** The original (a $0.7B fleet of multi-ratio 30-100 MVA units, leased to developers) is dead and I am withdrawing it. What survives is a different, capital-light business that still reaches $100M+ of revenue in the upside case, but with dealer-like margins and a weak moat. The two-week test at the end decides whether it lives.

## 1. What I concede (the critics are right)

1. **"Nobody holds stock" is false.** Maddox lists Powerclass units to 160 MVA at 69-230 kV and says it "keeps substation transformers in stock" (page re-fetched 10/18). JCL claims one of the largest independent inventories, to 345 kV, "ready for same-week shipment." Giga, Sunbelt Solomon and Delta Wye sell similar things. MARS Transformers lists three Prolec 30/40/50 MVA units "in production, available 2027" on Surplus Record, which is slot-holding by an intermediary. The idea is a late entrant, not a new category.
2. **The multi-ratio fleet premise is unsourced.** "About 80% are the same few designs" has no source; DOE, GAO, DHS/EPRI and Dominion's per-plant approved lists all point the other way. Withdrawn.
3. **The lease fleet fails on its own math.** Break-even is about 78% utilization, a 20% price mark-down wipes about two-thirds of the equity, and no transformer lease comp exists. Withdrawn from the core.
4. **Speculative developers are the wrong customer.** The credit-worthy buyers hold OEM frames; the ones left are the tier lenders are rationing. Withdrawn.
5. **Owning OEM slots is not a moat a newcomer can buy.** OEMs allocate to volume commitments. I do not claim it.

## 2. What survives (and one fact that moved in the idea's favor)

- The job is real: date-certain delivery of 69-230 kV units. Lead times are still 128 weeks (power) and 144-160+ weeks (GSU) per Wood Mackenzie via Power Magazine and Reuters.
- Sightline Climate, via Bloomberg and secondary outlets, says 30-50% of planned 2026 US data-center capacity may slip, mainly for lack of electrical equipment. PJM's own milestone data put supply chain at 23% of post-approval changes against 29% for permitting. Both are secondary and contested, but they undercut the claim that the transformer is never the binding constraint. It is one of several, on perhaps a quarter of delayed projects.
- Substation cost is increasingly assigned directly to the large load that causes it (Virginia SCC order on Dominion; PG&E and Oregon precedents; [Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/dominion-energy-scc-transmission-costs-data-center/827693)). This is a regulatory direction, not a ruling on who buys the transformer. It does mean the party that values time most can be the one that pays the premium.
- Queue attrition flips sign. In the original it was a risk the fleet pre-funded. Bernstein expects cancellations to accelerate into 2027, and cancelled orders become **inventory I can buy at a discount**, not units I pre-bought at the peak.
- Existence proofs for the buyer segment: Ayr reports orders above $500M from IPPs, renewable developers and data-center developers at 40-72 weeks (company claim, [TechCrunch via summaries](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/this-energy-startups-bet-on-100-year-old-grid-tech-is-paying-off/)). Giga's CEO says infrastructure sales "surpassed $150 million in 2025" ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/giga-energy-profile-matt-lohstroh-interview/)), but that mix is padmounts, MV substations and switchboards, not 30-100 MVA units, and its funding is undisclosed. Both show intermediaries reach $100M+ with tens of millions, not hundreds. Neither proves the fast tier (8-30 weeks) of the 40-200 MVA class.

## 3. The rebuilt business

**One line.** A deposit-funded, matched-book seller of date-certain, OEM-warranted, allied-origin 40-200 MVA substation transformers. It sources from (a) cancelled and over-ordered units and (b) slots reserved with pooled buyer deposits, and sells to utilities and EPC/IPP buyers with a contracted delivery date and remedies for slip.

**What changed, by lever**

| Lever | Original | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Own a multi-ratio fleet, lease it | Sell date-certain units on a three-tier ladder (below). No speculative fleet. |
| Payer and signer | Developer VP Power / CFO, neoclouds | Tier A: utility VP Supply Chain or chief engineer (investment-grade), with the premium funded by the large load through direct-assigned facility costs. Tier B: EPC procurement VP and IPP / BESS / data-center developers, with at least 30% deposit and an LC for the balance if not investment grade. Speculative lessees excluded. |
| Sale vs lease | Lease at 12-15% a year | Sell. A sale goes into a utility's rate base; a lease is an expense with no return. Financing sits with partner lenders (we earn origination, not the book). |
| Sourcing | OEM slots, the idea 3 consortium, the idea 4 foundry | Cancelled/in-production units bought at a discount; slots reserved at 2 allied-origin plants with pooled deposits and an LC facility. No dependence on rejected ideas. |
| Spec | "Multi-ratio" as the premise | Spec-first. Developer-owned substations (owner controls spec; collector low side typically 34.5 kV, generator terminals typically about 13.8 kV, to be verified) and per-utility repeat specs (Dominion keeps filing 84 MVA). A cross-fit engine matches cancelled units on voltage, MVA, impedance, BIL, vector group and tank dimensions. |
| Band | 30-100 MVA | 40-200 MVA. Wood Mackenzie says the shortage is most acute at 100 MVA and above, and EO 14420 closes the Chinese import channel that data centers had turned to. Larger units mean fewer transactions for $100M. |
| Timing | Buy a fleet now, at peak price | Matched-book now. Large forward commitments only in 2028-29, when new OEM capacity (Hitachi Virginia 2H 2028, Siemens Charlotte, Eaton, HD Hyundai Alabama 2, Hyosung Memphis) needs backlog and I have leverage, not before. |

**Product ladder and pricing** (benchmark: $30-35k per MVA for 50-100 MVA awards, Rystad; premiums are assumptions the test must measure)

| Tier | What | Delivery | Premium over benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor | Finished, tested units, mostly from cancelled orders | 8 weeks or less | 15-20% |
| Reassigned | Units in production for a cancelled buyer | 12-40 weeks | 8-12% |
| Reserved | Capacity reserved with pooled deposits, date-certain | 30-60 weeks | 4-6% plus a 2-3% non-refundable reservation fee, credited at order |

Evidence for the premium is thin: Bolt Electrical claims 12-15% for 12-14 months (hard to verify), and a Michigan city paid 3.4% to save 2-3.5 years between two OEM bids. Both are on the table and the plan does not assume the high end.

**Guardrails (these answer the capital, hoarding and storage critiques)**
- Title passes only when a buyer PO and deposit exist, or the unit is bought at 12% or more below replacement cost. Unsold units may not exceed 25% of trailing-12-month sales.
- No unit is held more than 6 months. OEM-warranted units only; storage plan and warranty-tolling terms agreed with the OEM before purchase, or priced in.
- Allied-origin only, with sub-tier bill-of-materials attestations and a refund clause if a DOE determination makes the unit unusable. Section 232 changes pass through to the buyer at delivery.
- Counsel opinion on the DPA hoarding statute (50 U.S.C. 4512) before the first purchase. Selling to identified projects, with no accumulation beyond demand, is the posture; I found no scarcity designation for transformers.

**Sequencing**
- Weeks 0-2: the test below. Weeks 3-12: fee-only brokered deals, no inventory.
- Months 3-12: matched principal, at most $15M at risk, 4-6 units, first utility vendor registration.
- Months 12-30: reserved capacity at 2 plants; Series A of about $20-25M; warehouse line at 60-70% advance on cost.
- Years 3-6: scale; financed sales via infrastructure-lender partners; attach breakers and controls through partners, because a transformer in weeks does not help if the rest of the substation is gated.
- Total equity about $30M and a $50-60M warehouse, against $210M of equity for the fleet.

## 4. Bottom-up market and the cases

- **Class:** 30-100 MVA is about 800-1,600 units and $1.5-3B a year (dossier inference; DOE had about 750 units above 60 MVA in 2019 and Wood Mackenzie says demand is up 116% since). The 100-200 MVA addition is unmeasured.
- **Delivery-critical subset:** Wood Mackenzie's 2026 shortage share of 8% (substations) to 15% (power) is about $120-450M a year in the 30-100 MVA band. That is the honest lower bound of the pool.
- **My count of reachable payers (assumptions, not sourced):** about 20 mid-size or load-growth utilities and G&Ts at about 3 expedited units a year, plus about 40 EPC / IPP / data-center buyers at about 2, all at $2.4M, is roughly $335M a year.
- **Share needed:** $100M is about 30% of that count and 25-80% of the Wood Mackenzie lower bound. **By the lessons' rule that is heroic, and I say so.** Against the whole 40-200 MVA class it is about 2-5%, and Ayr's and Giga's results hint that the fast-delivery pool is larger than my count (inference: at least $0.5-1B a year across classes).

**Median case, about $30M of revenue by year 5-6.** The test passes and I raise about $20M. 11-12 units a year at $2.3M, plus about $3M of reservation and service fees. Gross profit about $4-5M, roughly break-even after a 25-person team. This is a mid-tier dealer-financier.

**Upside case, about $120M by year 6-7.** Named assumptions:
1. The shortage persists in 60 MVA and up through 2029, so speed keeps a 10% or better premium.
2. Two allied-origin plants take pooled-deposit reservations, 25 or more units a year combined, by month 24.
3. Three to five utilities register me as an expedited-supply vendor and two sign blanket or framework POs.
4. Cancelled and stranded units supply 10 or more units a year at 12% or more under replacement.
5. A warehouse lender advances 65-70% on cost.
6. Sales are 30 units at $2.2M plus 8 units at $5M, about $106M, plus about $14M of fees: option fees, financing origination at 1.75% on about $250M financed, and storage / logistics / commissioning.

**Honest margin.** Gross profit is 11-13% on sales, about $13M, plus fees of about $10M. After roughly $10M of opex and $2M of interest, EBITDA is $8-12M. That is a good small company worth perhaps $80-150M to an acquirer (Sunbelt Solomon / TJC, United Rentals, Quanta, an OEM). It is not a venture-scale margin pool. The $100M is revenue, not profit.

## 5. Moat (7 Powers), stated plainly

- **At launch: none.** The critics are right that cost of capital and scale belong to Sunbelt / TJC, United Rentals and Quanta, and I do not compete there. The plan sells financed units into their lenders.
- **Reachable, in order of likelihood:** (1) cornered resource, meaning allocation at 2-3 allied-origin plants plus a portfolio of utility-approved plant / spec listings, earned by bringing OEMs the bankable backlog they say they need and by timing the big commitments to the 2028-29 capacity wave; (2) process power, meaning a cancellation and cross-fit database with test records that raises redeployment yield. Both are real but modest.
- Dealers can copy this. The defense is speed of building the OEM and utility relationships and the distressed-sourcing network, and the realistic exit is acquisition.

## 6. How each critique is answered

- **Critic 1, dealers exist:** conceded, and the claim is reframed from stock to date-certain, warrantied, provenance-attested delivery of new units. Maddox's "common kVA sizes" and JCL's "emergency replacements" language suggest their stock serves a different job (hypothesis; the test checks it). Surplus Record's oil-transformer category showed only about 291 listings across all sizes, with no posted prices ("Get Your Price"), which points to a thin, quote-driven market (visible listings only).
- **Critic 1, can copy and lower cost of capital:** conceded, no power at launch (section 5).
- **Critic 1, OEM slots go to volume:** pooled deposits and an LC create backlog; MARS shows intermediaries can hold units in production; large commitments wait for 2028-29.
- **Critic 1, demand shape and substitutes:** band moved up. Behind-the-meter supply still needs step-up transformers (engines and turbines at generator voltage); this is my inference and unverified. Cancellations become sourcing.
- **Critic 1, spec acceptance:** multi-ratio premise dropped; spec-first segments; cross-fit engine; no claim of a few universal designs.
- **Critic 2, unit economics:** lease core dropped; small float; fee lines; margin stated honestly.
- **Critic 2, adverse selection and who signs:** payer moved to utilities (investment-grade) and EPCs with deposits; sales not leases.
- **Critic 2, price of speed and the true constraint:** tiered pricing; premium unproven and tested; a critical-path attestation is required from buyers; Sightline and PJM data cited with caveats; partner bundling for breakers and controls.
- **Critic 2, shrinking volumes:** the upside needs about 38 units in an extended class, with heroic share flagged.
- **Critic 2, supply depends on rejected ideas:** removed.
- **Critic 3, capital:** about $30M of equity, matched-book, buy-low timing.
- **Critic 3, counterparty:** same as above, plus correlated risk is turned into a sourcing advantage.
- **Critic 3, slot timing and interconnection:** attestation of critical path, reserved tier timed to 2028+, interconnection acknowledged as a limit on the pool.
- **Critic 3, regulatory tails:** matched-book against hoarding optics; counsel; provenance clauses; tariff pass-through; public-utility bid rules handled by qualifying as a bidder with evaluated-delivery criteria.
- **Critic 3, spec and idle-time liability:** 6-month cap, OEM-warranted units, storage and warranty terms before purchase.

**Not answered:** a transformer lease comp (none found); written spec sign-off from three utilities; an OEM buy-back or resale floor; a lender above 70% LTV; DOE's December rules. These matter only for the phase-3 financing option, which is why the core no longer depends on them.

## 7. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test

**Riskiest assumption.** A buyer with a signed project and a binding energization date will pay at least 8-10% over the OEM benchmark, or a 2-3% non-refundable reservation fee, for a date-certain 40-200 MVA unit, **and** existing dealers cannot already deliver new, warranted units of the right spec in 20 weeks or less. This is the "gap" that critics 1 and 2 both say is false.

**Test (about $0, 14 days, disclosed).**
1. Days 1-3: write three example specs (for instance 40/53/67 MVA 138 to 34.5 kV with LTC; 84 MVA 230 to 34.5 kV; 60 MVA 69 to 13.8 kV GSU).
2. Days 2-8: ask 8 sellers (Maddox, JCL, Giga, Sunbelt Solomon, Delta Wye, MARS Transformers, Belyea, Power Asset Recovery or Transformer Exchange) for budgetary quotes, saying openly that I am a new equipment-sourcing firm. Record new vs remanufactured, plant of origin, weeks to delivery, price per MVA, factory acceptance testing, warranty, storage terms and origin attestations.
3. Days 3-14: 20 calls with 8 utility supply-chain or engineering leads, 6 EPC / IPP procurement leads and 6 data-center developers. Ask for specific cases in the last 12 months where a transformer was the critical path, what they paid, and whether they would sign a 2-3% reservation for a date-certain unit. Collect at least 2 written indications with price terms.

**Kill rules.**
- 3 or more of 6 dealers quote 20 weeks or less at 8% or less above benchmark on at least 2 of 3 specs: the gap is closed. Kill.
- Fewer than 5 of 20 buyers name a documented transformer-critical delay of 6 months or more: no demand. Kill.
- No buyer gives a written indication at 8% or more or a reservation fee: pricing fails. The only fallback is fee-only brokering, which is a boutique, so call it dead.

**Pass.** At least 3 documented cases, at least 2 written indications, and fewer than 3 dealers closing the gap. Then phase 1.

## 8. Pre-mortem, 2028

1. New OEM capacity lands, the premium collapses to a 5% dealer spread. Partly fixable: short inventory duration and fee lines, but the business shrinks toward the median case.
2. JCL, Maddox or Giga widen their programs to match. Not fixable; only the relationships and the distress network are defenses.
3. Slot access never materializes beyond cancelled units, which are lumpy. Fixable by the 2028-29 reservation timing, partly.
4. A DOE definition change or a hoarding action strands a batch. Mitigated by matched-book and counsel.

Probability estimate: upside (about $120M) is about 5-8% conditional on passing the test and about 2-3% unconditional. Median (about $30M) is the likely good outcome. Failure at the test is the most likely single result.

# C06 dossier: Overnight Yard Crews (pool P102, idea 2)

Researched 2026-10-18. Tags: (S) read this session; (S-snip) from a search result I did not open; (A) my arithmetic or assumption; (U) unverified.

## 1. Bottom line

The gate's "weak" verdict holds, and on market size I would grade it lower.

- The pain is real. ATRI found 65.5% of shops understaffed and 19.3% of positions unfilled (S: https://truckingresearch.org/2025/08/new-atri-research-addresses-shortage-of-qualified-diesel-techs-in-trucking/). Fullbay's 2026 report shows median technician pay at $36.50 an hour, up 14.1% per FreightWaves' summary (S: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fullbay-2026-heavy-duty-repair-report-technician-shortage).
- At least eight scaled players already sell mobile, on-site or standard-work PM to fleets. Several have 2 to 15 times the technician base (about 640 people) the idea needs for $100M.
- The outsourced checklist-labor pool inside the idea's own customer segment is about $0.25-2.2B a year (A). $100M is then 5-41% share.
- The unit economics only work with cheap Level-1 labor at high utilization. Posted pay for entry-level mobile technicians is $34-36 an hour at one national player.
- The remote-master-tech, OEM second-payer and Workforce Pell legs are each weaker than pitched.

Best realistic outcome (judgement): a $10-25M regional services business. Under round 2's no-shrinking rule this is a kill unless the section 9 pilot tests pass.

## 3. Bottom-up market

I queried the FMCSA census (https://data.transportation.gov/resource/az4n-8mr2.json) on Oct 18, 2026 for active carriers whose MCS-150 was updated since Oct 2024 (S). Results:

- 15,040 carriers hold 50 to 1,999 power units, with 2.41M units in total (about 44% of the roughly 5.5M units once 352 records reporting 2,000+ units, which look inflated, are excluded).
  - 9,171 are for-hire (1.42M units).
  - 5,692 are private (0.96M units).
- Self-reported driver mix puts 36% of for-hire drivers and 68% of private drivers in the "within 100 miles" bucket. That implies about 1.19M yard-returning units (A).
- This is the realistic reach of an overnight-yard model. Over-the-road trucks sleep on the road.
- Caveats: the data is self-reported and ages; it omits school, transit and municipal fleets that lack USDOT numbers.
- Context: ATA's 2020 data says 97.4% of fleets run fewer than 20 trucks (S: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/ata-stats-2020-volume-lower-but-trucking-still-rules-freight-movement). The idea's customer is about the top 1.7% of fleets by count (15,040 of 904,630 carriers in my pull, A).

Spend per payer is my assumption: 20 labor-hours a year of PM, inspection, tire, brake and lighting work per truck at $115, about $2,300.

| Basis | Gross labor if 100% outsourced | At 9% outsourced | At 25% | At 40% | $100M as share (9% / 25% / 40%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yard-returning units (1.19M) | $2.7B | $0.25B | $0.68B | $1.09B | 41% / 15% / 9% |
| All 50-1,999 fleets (2.41M) | $5.5B | $0.50B | $1.39B | $2.22B | 20% / 7% / 5% |

Outsourcing shares come from MacKay: fleets did 91% of PM labor in-house, and about 75% of all service work, in 2013 (S: https://www.truckinginfo.com/articles/service-survey-fleets-say-they-want-to-outsource-more). By 2015 only 68% of fleets did all service in-house, down from 75% in 2011 (S: https://www.ttnews.com/articles/technician-shortage-spurs-fleets-outsource-more-work). I found no current PM-specific outsourcing rate.

The idea's $35B "checklist labor" pool is unsourced (the gate also could not source it). It includes in-house labor and fleets of under 50 trucks that the idea does not target.

Cross-check from the supply side: a crew billing 3,000 hours covers about 150 trucks at 20 hours each. The 290-crew plan therefore serves about 43,500 trucks (A), or 3.7% of yard-returning units in the target fleets. The truck share is small, but only if fleets hand over all of their PM labor, which has historically been the least-outsourced category. Ryder's SelectCare and related revenue was $680M in 2025 on 44,100 contract vehicles, about $15.4k a vehicle including parts (S, Ryder 10-K above). The full-service incumbents already hold the biggest slice.

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

For demand:
- The shortage is documented. Fullbay reports 54% of shops understaffed and 40% saying hiring got harder (S, FreightWaves link above).
- Rush's 10-K says demand for technicians is very high and reports 35.0% technician turnover in 2025 (S, Rush 10-K).
- Incumbents are paying to expand: Ryder's two acquisitions, Cox's two, Rush's mobile investment, the True North platform.
- The freight market has flipped. DAT data cited in September 2026 coverage shows spot rates about 35% above a year earlier and tender rejections near 13-14%. Fleets have cash and miles (S-snip: https://ielfreight.com/truckload-market-update-september-2026/ and https://www.ryantrans.com/september-2026-industry-update-truckload-rates).

Against demand:
- Only 33% of fleet respondents in Fleet Maintenance's December 2025 benchmarking said they were understaffed; 54% said staffing was about right (S: https://www.fleetmaintenance.com/shop-operations/employees-and-training/article/55338548/2025-fleet-maintenance-benchmarking-report-the-technician-shortage). The 65.5% ATRI number is shops, not fleets.
- Fleets that run yards already staff or contract the work: Ryder has 155 on-site shops inside customer yards. A 2018 HDT piece quotes C&S Wholesale Grocers as doing 70% in-house, with no one right model (S: https://www.truckinginfo.com/279627/how-fleets-are-looking-to-outsource-maintenance-and-repairs).
- The cheapest substitute is the buyer hiring its own PM or lube technician. A truck-capable lube-tech posting pays $16-22 an hour (S-snip: https://recruiting.paylocity.com/Recruiting/Jobs/Details/4134626). Even at $30 loaded, that is about a quarter of $115.
- I found no RFP, contract or complaint set showing private fleets paying a premium for hourly L1 crews. The hard demand evidence I found is public-sector, which belongs to idea 6.

Price benchmarks. Fullbay's median shop labor rate is $149 and mobile runs about $160 (S, FreightWaves link). So $115 is a 23-28% discount (A). Public bids for full-skill heavy-duty labor are $105-165 an hour in Erie County NY in 2025 (S-snip: https://www3.erie.gov/purchasing/sites/www3.erie.gov.purchasing/files/2025-04/memo-trucks-heavy-equipment.pdf) and $175-215 in San Antonio (S-snip: https://webapp1.sanantonio.gov/RFPFiles/IFB_5481_202408020219120.pdf). A $115 price is credible but not a bargain against incumbents who can match it.

## 5. Unit economics

Anchors:
- Ryder's whole fleet-management segment earned 8.6% pretax on $5.845B in 2025 (S, Ryder 10-K).
- Posted pay for L1-type roles:
  - $16-22 an hour for a truck-capable lube technician (Paylocity link above).
  - $25-35 for a Rush Level-1 union diesel job (S-snip, aggregator: https://grabjobs.co/us/job/full-time/others/level-1-union-diesel-mechanic-147718944).
  - $34-36 for Kooner's entry-level mobile diesel technician, with a conflicting $25-30 salary field (S-snip: https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/entry-level-mobile-diesel-mechanic-technician-1909-hayward-ca--60b0334c-a99a-40a7-bb3d-e77d162a8a78).
- Conflict on median pay: FreightWaves gives $36.50 (+14.1%); the gate's forum source reports $32 (https://forum.freightwaves.com/t/fullbay-s-2026-report-heavy-duty-shops-face-structural-technician-shortage/9024). I could not reach Fullbay's primary report.

My model (A): the crew bills 2 techs x 2,080 hours x utilization x $115. Costs are:
- L1 wage x 1.3 burden.
- One $108k loaded mentor per 4.8 crews (the idea's 60 mentors per 290 crews).
- $25k a year for van, tools and fuel.
- 28% of revenue for insurance (6%), consumables (4%), regional management, recruiting and training (10%), and sales and G&A (8%).

| L1 wage | EBITDA margin at 72% utilization (the idea's 1,500 hrs) | at 65% | at 55% | Break-even utilization |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $20 | 27% | 22% | 13% | 46% |
| $24 | 21% | 15% | 5% | 52% |
| $30 | 11% | 5% | -8% | 61% |
| $34 | 5% | -2% | -16% | 68% |

If the wage is $30-34 (consistent with the Kooner posting and 14% wage inflation), the model needs 60-70% utilization just to break even. I found no published utilization benchmark for mobile or yard heavy-duty crews; the only mention is a low-quality bookkeeping blog describing a 50% example. Utilization is an open gap.

Capital (A): 290 crews x about $100k for van, tools and parts kits is about $29M. Add roughly $8-12M of receivables on net-30 to net-45 terms, plus 12-18 months of ramp losses. That is roughly $40-60M of capital to reach $100M, all of it assumption. The credible alternative is a PE roll-up, which is the True North route.

## 6. Regulatory and liability landscape

- Annual-inspection qualification, 49 CFR 396.19: an inspector needs a federal- or state-sponsored program, or a combination of training and experience totaling at least one year (S: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.19). The gate was right that a 4-6 week hire cannot be the inspector.
- Brake-inspector qualification, 49 CFR 396.25, bars carriers from letting unqualified employees be responsible for brake inspection and repair. It requires a recognized apprenticeship, a state or federal program, or one year of related training or experience (S: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.25). My reading: each crew needs at least one qualified person of record, so this is a staffing constraint, not a legal bar.
- A remote video mentor cannot be the inspector of record. Diesel Laptops and OEM remote diagnostics exist, but nothing I read lets a remote party sign the inspection.
- Carrier duty does not transfer. 49 CFR 396.3 lets a carrier have maintenance performed by others, but the carrier stays responsible and keeps the records (S: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/396.3).
  - California's *Maloney v. Rath* treats the brake duty as nondelegable (S-snip: https://www.pastpaperhero.com/resources/maloney-v-rath-69-cal-2d-442-cal-1968).
- Repair-liability exposure is real. A plaintiff firm reports an $18.79M Alabama verdict over a dealer shop's detached lateral control rod (S-snip: https://www.beasleyallen.com/article/mobile-county-jury-returns-18-79-million-verdict-to-man-paralyzed-by-negligent-repair/). Brake work needs garage liability with completed-operations cover plus garagekeepers (S-snip: https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2014/02/10/319390.htm). I found no premium figures (gap).
- Workforce Pell does not help in the first years:
  - Programs must run at least 8 weeks and 150 clock hours, hit 70% completion and 70% placement, and come from Title IV institutions with governor approval (S: https://www.newamerica.org/insights/workforce-pell-what-you-need-to-know/).
  - By Aug 4, 2026, Pennsylvania had approved 2 of 40 submitted programs (both CDL) and I found no diesel-technician approval (S: https://tradecolleges.org/blog/trades-news/trades-news-roundup-week-of-july-27-2026).
- I found no state licensing regime for heavy-duty technicians (absence of evidence, not confirmation).

## 7. Why-now: partly real, partly absorbed

- Real: shortage metrics, wage inflation, and a freight recovery that gives fleets cash.
- Absorbed by incumbents: Torque (2023), Cox's FleetNet and Corcoran's deals (2023), True North (Nov 2025), Rush's mobile push, and software vendors now handling the PM scheduling the idea treated as an edge.
- Working against the idea: 14% wage inflation erodes the labor arbitrage, and a tight freight market means all bidders chase the same technicians.
- ATD's 2024 dealer study put entry-level and trainee technician turnover at 44% (S-snip: https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/what-can-trucking-fleets-do-to-hire-retain-technicians/1003212233).

## 8. Audit of the upside-case assumptions

| Assumption | Finding |
|---|---|
| 290 crews, about 580 L1 plus 60 mentors | Torque had about 200 technicians in July 2024, a year after launch with Pit Stop folded in, and added 20+ in Jan 2026. Hiring 580 L1 from scratch would be about 3x Torque's headcount (A). Revenue per technician in the plan ($172k of labor alone) is above the roughly $125-130k per technician implied by Amerit's aggregator revenue estimate (U). |
| $115 an hour at 1,500 billable hours per tech | Unproven, no benchmark. The 23-28% discount to market is what the incumbents can match. Unit economics hinge on L1 pay under about $25. |
| 4-6 week L1 training | Penske's TechStart already runs six weeks. ATRI says untrained entrants need 357 hours and $8,211 of trainee wages, and over 30% of program graduates were unqualified in 20 core skills (S, ATRI link). It is feasible but not a moat. |
| Fleets outsource their checklist work | PM was 91% in-house in 2013. The 1998 and 2003 MacKay studies predicted more outsourcing and it did not materialize (S, truckinginfo link above). No current PM rate found. |
| OEM campaign budget as second payer | Love's holds Freightliner ExpressPoint (400+ locations; light warranty, roadside warranty, campaigns) (S: https://www.loves.com/news/2024/january/loves-and-freightliner-complete-rollout-of-freightliner-expresspoint-exclusive-repair-service). Its International page covers warranty work of three hours or less, excluding Texas and Louisiana (S: https://loves.com/international). The on-highway claims pool is about $0.8B in the gate's WarrantyWeek read, not $3.94B (gate-cited: https://warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20260521.html). I found only dealer-run warranty programs at Volvo and Mack, and nothing on PACCAR. |
| Workforce Pell ladder | Not usable (section 6). |
| Remote master tech as differentiator | Commoditized and legally non-substitutable (section 6). |

Scale comparison (A): Ryder's Torque at about 200-220 technicians and $150-170k per technician implies roughly $30-40M of revenue, built in three years with a large balance sheet and two acquisitions. That is a third to two-fifths of this idea's $100M. The path to $100M runs through M&A or an incumbent's balance sheet.

## 9. Verdict and what would change it

Scorecard (judgement, 0-10): market size 2, competition 2, unit economics 3, legal and liability 5, why-now 4. Median revenue in 5-7 years about $10-15M; upside about $40-60M with acquisitions; $100M is not a credible organic outcome. This matches the gate (median $18M, upside $90M) on the median and is more skeptical on the upside.

Three cheap tests that would reopen it:
1. Three fleets with yards each pay at least $115 an hour for L1 crews for 90 days at 65% or higher utilization, with all-in L1 wage under $25.
2. A written insurer quote for brake and tire work at 6% of revenue or lower, plus a documented inspector-of-record structure.
3. Evidence from a fleet maintenance director that PM outsourcing is rising materially above the 2013 level, with named buyers and budget lines. I found none.

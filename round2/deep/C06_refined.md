# C06 refined: Level-1 Bench (was: Overnight Yard Crews), pool P102

Written 2026-10-18 after three critiques. Tags: (S) page fetched and read this session; (S-snip) from a search summary only; (A) my arithmetic or assumption; (C) a critic's claim I accepted without re-checking; (U) unverified. The model behind every (A) number is a 60-line script I ran; inputs are in section 5.

## 1. Verdict

Alive, but not as the thing that was pitched. The yard-crew-for-fleets service business is dead as a $100M company. The critics are right that Ryder, Cox, Amerit, Rush, Love's and Coltrain already sell that service, that the price and wage assumptions were wrong, and that account-by-account selling cannot reach $100M.

What survives is the one insight those critiques did not touch: the shortage is partly a task-allocation and training-capacity problem, and the buyers who feel it most (shops and dealers with five technicians, not Penske) cannot build a pipeline. So the company changes from "sell PM crews to fleets" to "hire, train and deploy Level-1 technicians to the shops, dealers and yard fleets that cannot fill entry slots." The unit of sale becomes a bench of 1-24 Level-1 FTEs, not a share of a fleet's PM wallet.

This is a 5-12% EBITDA staffing-plus-training company, not a software business. $100M of revenue is about 950-1,050 people on payroll. My honest odds of reaching it are roughly 3-5% unconditional, about 10% if the 90-day gates in section 9 pass. The make-or-break number is the bill rate (about 1.9x the Level-1 wage), and nothing I found shows that shops pay it. That is the first thing to test.

## 3. The refined business

**One-liner.** A hire-train-deploy technician workforce company. We recruit people in adjacent $16-22 jobs, run a six-week academy, employ them W-2 hourly as Level-1 technicians, and place them into customers' shops and yards under the customer's own certified lead. Customers convert them to their payroll for a fee.

**Wedge customer (first 10).** Independent heavy-duty repair shops and franchised-dealer service departments with 4-30 technicians and at least one open requisition older than 60 days, plus private-fleet in-house shops with the same problem, all in one dense metro (Dallas-Fort Worth, Atlanta, Houston or the founder's home metro, wherever a diesel instructor can be hired). Fullbay's own data shows 54% of shops understaffed and a median shop of 5 technicians and 8 employees (S: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fullbay-2026-heavy-duty-repair-report-technician-shortage). Understaffing is a shop problem more than a fleet problem: only 33% of fleet respondents said understaffed (C, Fleet Maintenance Dec 2025), versus 54-65% of shops.

**Payer and signer.** The shop owner or service director (labor, overtime and recruiting budget; the open requisition is the budget line). At fleets, the maintenance director. At dealer groups, lessor networks and roll-ups, the VP of service operations plus procurement for a master services agreement. No fleet-by-fleet PM contract. Second payer: state workforce boards reimburse employers up to 50% of trainee wages during on-the-job training, up to 75% in some local policies (S-snip: https://www.dwd.wisconsin.gov/wioa/policy/08/08.5.4.101.htm). That cuts training cost; it is not revenue I count on.

**First product: Level-1 Bench.** Pre-trained W-2 Level-1 technicians (PM, lube, tire service with OSHA 1910.177 training, lighting, brake inspection assist, DOT annual inspection assist), billed hourly, two-week minimum, 30-day free replacement, convert for a fee that falls to zero after about 1,000 hours. They never act as the inspector of record under 49 CFR 396.19 or 396.25; the customer's certified lead does. That also covers a state like Michigan, where trainees need direct supervision by a certified mechanic in the same category (C: critic read MCL 257.1310b and the Department of State manual; I did not re-read it, so confirm in writing before placing anyone there).

**Academy design (cost control).** Two weeks classroom and shop at a hub, then four weeks billed in-bay at a trainee rate next to a buddy. Compare ATRI's figure of 357 hours and $8,211 in trainee wages for an employer to bring in an untrained entrant (S-snip). Target all-in cost per graduate: $8,000 base, $6,500 upside, including 22-25% washout and recruiting.

**Pricing.** Base bill rate $52 an hour (range $48-58); night differential +$3; tire- and brake-qualified pods $55-58. Conversion fee $7,000 (below the $8,211 the customer avoids; the published staffing norm is 11-21% of first-year salary, which on a $56k salary is $6-12k, S-snip). Weekly invoicing, ACH autopay for new accounts.

**Second product (year 2+).** Night Yard Pods: for a fleet with a yard and its own certified lead, a pod of three Level-1s works the parked trucks overnight, priced per service from the fleet's own menu. This keeps the original overnight idea, but only where the supervision and liability structure already exists.

**Why a bench and not PM-as-a-service.** Selling PM labor means winning a small slice of each fleet's budget against Ryder and Love's. Selling Level-1 capacity means the customer's open requisition is the demand. A shop that bills Level-1 work at its $149 median labor rate (S) and pays us $52 keeps the spread, provided it has backlog.

## 5. Unit economics (per FTE-year, model run 2026-10-18)

Inputs for the median case: wage $27, burden 1.30, bill $52, 2,000 paid hours at 92% billed, 30% attrition plus 30% conversion at $7,000, $8,000 cost per graduate, 16% operating overhead.

| Case | Revenue per FTE | Gross margin on billed hours | EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Downside (wage $28, bill $49, 40% attrition, $10.5k per graduate) | $87.7k | 15.6% | -$6.8k (-7.8%) |
| Median (above) | $97.8k | 26.6% | +$7.1k (7.3%) |
| Upside (wage $27, bill $55, 94% billed, 25% attrition, 35% conversion at $7.5k, $6.5k per graduate, 14% overhead) | $106.0k | 32.1% | +$17.1k (16.1%) |

Sensitivities on the median (A):
- Bill rate: breakeven is about $47.50 (1.76x wage). $50 gives 4.3% EBITDA; $54 gives 10.1%; $46 loses 2.5%. Each $1 of bill rate is about 1.5 points of margin.
- Wage: each $1 costs about 2.7 points. $30 at a $52 bill is -0.7%.
- Cost per graduate: $4k gives 9.8%; $12k gives 4.8%. It matters less than price.
- Staffing benchmark check: skilled-trades markup of 35-45% over pay is typical, 60-75% for specialized roles (S-snip, vendor sources). Our $52 over a $27 wage is a 93% markup. We justify it only because the customer receives training, a replacement guarantee and a conversion option. If customers will only pay a 40-75% markup ($38-47), the business does not work. That is exactly what the test measures.

## 6. Bottom-up market and the path to $100M

- Open slots: BLS 2024 has 319,900 diesel service technicians (S in the gate); ATRI says 19.3% of positions are unfilled; so about 76,500 open slots (A).
- L1-fillable share: unknown. Fullbay line shares (tires 43%, PM 12%, brakes 9%, lighting 5%) suggest a large share of work is Level-1 type, but those are lines, not hours or dollars. I use 15-35% of open slots as Level-1-fillable: 11,500-26,800 slots, or $1.1-2.6B of annual billing at $97.8k (A).
- $100M is about 1,000 FTE: 4% of fillable slots at the 35% case, 9% at the 15% case. Under the 20% "heroic share" line even in the pessimistic case.
- Payer cross-check (A): Fullbay's 5,000 shop locations at 54% understaffed and about 1.5 open Level-1 slots each is about 4,000 FTE or $390M; dealers, lessor shops (NationaLease 900 locations, S-snip), private-fleet shops and school and transit garages are additional and unquantified. Off-highway, bus and ag-equipment dealers have the same shortage (AED Foundation 2026, S-snip) and are not counted.
- Revenue path (A): median year-end FTE 15, 45, 90, 140, 175, 200, 215 gives $0.7M, 2.9M, 6.6M, 11.2M, 15.4M, 18.3M, 20.3M. Upside 25, 90, 220, 420, 650, 850, 1,000 gives $1.3M, 6.1M, 16.4M, 33.9M, 56.7M, 79.5M, 98.1M, with a year-7 exit run-rate of about $106M.
- Hiring at upside maturity: about 830 graduates and 1,060 enrollees a year, 12 hubs, 3-4 cohorts of 24 a year each.
- Capital: ramp losses $6-10M, academies about $3M, equity-funded working capital $2-10M (A). No acquisitions assumed.

## 7. Moat (7 Powers)

Honest: weak at the start, moderate at best later.
- Scale economies, reachable by year 3: a fixed recruiting, instructor and curriculum engine spread over more cohorts. The test is cost per graduate falling from about $11k to under $7k.
- Switching costs, reachable via enterprise master agreements: customer-specific work cards, records and conversion ledger, and embedded benches that are painful to replace.
- Cornered resource, reachable by year 3-4: exclusive pipelines (high-school career programs, community colleges, military transition slots) and an instructor bench.
- Process power, only if retention data proves it: 12-month retention clearly above the 35-44% turnover quoted for the trade (Rush 35.0% in the dossier, ATD 44% for entry techs, S-snip).
- No counter-positioning. Consolidators could copy the model; the most likely end state for the founder is being acquired by one.

## 8. Competitors and edge

- LAUNCH TransTechs: staffing of technicians for bus, delivery and truck fleets, W-2 employer of record; TrueBlue divested it, reason not found (S-snip, https://www.launchtws.com/launch-transtech/). Its postings are experienced-tech roles at $18-26 an hour. It does not train entrants.
- Perm-placement recruiters (Boggs and similar) sell experienced techs, not trained entrants (S-snip).
- General staffing firms: do not understand ASE and OEM requirements (marketing claim, S-snip).
- Single-employer academies: Penske (about 9,000 techs, 1,000+ Lincoln Tech hires in 10 years, S-snip), Love's, Amerit Trailer Tech Academy, Torque.
- Schools: UTI revenue $835.6M in FY2025 across all programs, about 29,800 new starts (S-snip, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1261654/000126165425000022/exhibit991-q42025earningsp.htm). They deliver graduates 9-18 months later at student cost.
- Analog scale: Tradesmen International is a skilled-trades staffing firm that crossed $100M of U.S. revenue per Staffing Industry Analysts (S-snip, https://www.tradesmeninternational.com/news-events/2021-largest-staffing-firm). It shows $100M of skilled-trade staffing exists; it is not a diesel analog.
- Mobile and yard PM providers (Ryder Torque, Cox, Rush, Amerit, Coltrain, Kooner): competitors for a fleet's PM wallet in the old plan; here they are labor competitors and possible acquirers.
- Edge: a multi-employer, employer-paid, six-week program with a conversion option and a ladder to the 12-month inspector qualification, sold to buyers who cannot run their own academy.

## 9. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test

**Riskiest assumption.** Independent shops, dealers and private-fleet shops with open Level-1 work will pay a staffing-style bill rate of at least $50 an hour (about 1.85x the L1 wage) for pre-trained Level-1s with a conversion option, instead of hiring direct at $24-28. If they will not, the economics break at roughly $47.50 and the business is dead.

**Test (cost: under $500, two weeks, one person).**
1. Write a one-page offer: a pre-trained Level-1 at $52 an hour, two-week minimum, 30-day replacement, $7,000 conversion fee after 520 hours, start date four to six weeks out, with a one-page non-binding order form that states plainly that cohorts are not yet running.
2. Contact 40 targets in one metro: 15 independent heavy-duty shops, 10 dealer service managers, 10 private-fleet maintenance managers, 5 tire dealers with commercial service. Ask each for (a) open Level-1-type requisitions and how long open, (b) work turned away last month, (c) their current all-in cost of a direct L1 hire, (d) a signed order form.
3. In parallel, run two paid job ads for a six-week paid program ($19 an hour in training, $26-27 after) with a $300 budget, to measure the supply side.

**Pass:** at least 5 of 40 sign order forms at $50 or more, at least 3 of those report a requisition older than 60 days, and at least 40 qualified applicants arrive at under $15 each. **Fail:** fewer than 2 signed, or quoted willingness below $44. Between: reprice or retarget (for example, dealer groups only) and rerun.

**90-day gates after that:** three paying pilots at $50+ an hour, 90-day retention of at least 80%, gross margin on billed hours of at least 24%, a written workers' comp and general-liability quote at or below 12% of payroll, and one customer asking for four or more people.

## 10. Pre-mortem (it is 2029 and this is dead)

1. Shops hire direct at $27 and see $52 as a tax. Fixable only by proving the spread (the shop's $149 labor rate on the hours the L1 performs). Test in section 9.
2. Retention. Our trainees quit or get hired away before the conversion fee pays for training, which at 40% attrition turns the model negative. Fix: retention bonus, ladder, WIOA reimbursement; unproven.
3. Consolidators or Penske-style programs insource, and general staffing firms copy us; margin stays at 3-5% with no moat. Fix: get an enterprise master agreement signed in year 2; accept acquisition as the exit.

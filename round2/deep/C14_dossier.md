# C14 dossier: Covenant Parks (pool P017, manufactured-home communities)

Dated 2026-10-18. About 50 searches, fetches and primary-file reads (NY park registry, statutes, term sheets, SEC filings). Tags: [S] read in a source this run; [D] my arithmetic from sourced numbers; [A] my assumption; [C] sources conflict.

## Bottom line

The gate's pick survives only as a capital-intensive real-estate sponsor, not as a scalable company, and its two best arguments are weaker than the gate said.

- **The gap is real but the beneficiary is the nonprofit.** I found no for-profit owner at scale that records a rent covenant. But every public tool that pays for covenants is built for co-ops and nonprofits, and the state tax breaks for sellers exclude for-profit buyers.
- **New York, the wedge state, already caps rents by statute.** RPL 233-b limits increases to 3% unless costs justify more, with a 6% ceiling. Maine charges large buyers $10,000 a lot. The covenant's differentiation is smaller than pitched in both.
- **The price risk is quantifiable.** A covenant buyer needing an 8% levered return bids about 6% to 24% below a market buyer, depending on the cap and expense growth. At the cap the pitch proposes ("lesser of CPI or 4%", so about 3% if CPI is about 3% [A]), the gap is 17-24%.
- **The $100M path is capital-bound.** It needs about 14,000 to 20,000 sites, $0.8-0.9B of assets and about $320M of equity. It also needs about 5% of all communities sold nationally each year, in a market where the typical park has 30-40 sites.

Verdict: wounded. Alive only if sellers, lenders and allocators pass the three tests at the end.

## 1. Competitors: confirmed, corrected, added

- **ROC USA (confirmed, bigger than the gate showed).** [S] 361 resident-owned communities in 22 states, 24,500+ sites, and it claims ROC lot rents rise about 1% a year against 7% in commercially owned parks ([ROC USA FHFA letter, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.fhfa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/ROC%20USA%20Duty%20to%20Serve%20Comment%20Letter%207.24.2026.pdf)). It has deployed $450M+ of mortgages since 2008, about $26M a year [D], and has a $47M acquisition pool backed by RWJF, JPMorgan and others ([ImpactAlpha](https://impactalpha.com/financing-resident-owned-mobile-home-communities-to-preserve-affordability/)). Its own page shows "59 of 68" against a 2026 goal; the unit is ambiguous [C] ([ROC USA](https://rocusa.org/our-impact/)).
- **New York MHAP (confirmed and more relevant than the gate said).** [S] For-profit and nonprofit owners are eligible. It lends at up to 3%, up to $60,000 a pad, and requires a recorded regulatory agreement. For existing homeowners it expects no more than a 3% lot-rent increase, and lot rents must stay at or below 40% of the 2-bedroom fair market rent ([HCR term sheet](https://hcr.ny.gov/manufactured-home-advantage-program-term-sheet-lender-addendum)).
- **Cascade Acres, NY is the closest analog to Covenant Parks.** [S] The residents' association used the new right of first refusal against a private-equity buyer. A local owner-operator, Brandon Montag, is "property manager and financier" and the association can buy him out. Financing was a $4.7M MHAP loan (20-year), a $420k village loan, a $1.6M bank loan and his equity ([Adirondack Daily Enterprise](https://www.dailygazette.com/ade/news/local_news/cascade-acres-makes-history/article_e4b455d1-3239-5955-a967-01d3f8ae03da.html), [HCR](https://hcr.ny.gov/news/new-york-state-homes-and-community-renewal-announces-tenant-led-acquisition-essex-county)). So the structure exists, but as a one-off, subsidy-dependent deal. The bidder, Crown Communities, overbid a roughly $5.5M appraisal by $1.1M, about 20% ([WCAX](https://www.wcax.com/2025/08/06/lake-placid-residents-race-fend-off-sale-mobile-home-community/)). Unit counts conflict across sources (118, 124 and 165) [C].
- **Minnesota (verified, and the gate's claim holds).** [S] The Manufactured Home Community Redevelopment program accepts for-profit owners. It attaches a 25-year recorded Declaration of Restrictive Covenants, bars rent increases above 5% a year without approval, requires lot rents affordable at 115% AMI, requires approval to sell, and the covenant runs with the land ([Minnesota Housing FAQ, Sep 2026](https://mnhousing.gov/documents/45205/mhcr_rfp_faq/view)). A for-profit will take a recorded cap when a grant pays for it.
- **Public REITs (confirmed, still growing).** [S] Sun's Q2 2026 MH same-property revenue was +6.2%, NOI +8.8%, base rent $766 a site (+4.9%), occupancy 97.8% ([Sun 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000912593/000091259326000231/exhibit991pressreleaseands.htm)). ELS core MH base rent was reported +5.6% at about $956 ([MarketBeat summary](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/equity-lifestyle-properties-nyseels-announces-quarterly-earnings-results-2026-07-22/), secondary). UMH same-store rental income was +8.7% in October ([UMH](https://s205.q4cdn.com/229505774/files/doc_news/UMH-PROPERTIES-INC--THIRD-QUARTER-2026-OPERATIONS-UPDATE-2026.pdf) via search summary). Their rent engine is intact, so they cannot offer a cap.
- **Private equity and funds (added).** OZ Impact Funds bought 21 communities with 2,740 pads for $100M in 2021, or about $36,500 a pad [D] ([IREI](https://irei.com/news/oz-impact-funds-acquires-21-strive-communities/)). Institutional buyers are reported at 23% of purchases, up from 13% in 2017-19 ([Propmodo citing PESP](https://propmodo.com/institutional-capital-floods-manufactured-housing-as-scarcity-meets-demand/), secondary). Sen. Hassan wrote to six corporate owners in Dec 2025 ([JEC](https://www.jec.senate.gov/public/index.cfm/democrats/2025/12/senator-hassan-presses-corporate-owners-of-mobile-home-communities-for-answers-on-affordability-and-resident-living-conditions)).
- **Not found:** a for-profit owner-operator at scale with a recorded rent covenant, or a fund raised on that thesis. Searches for one returned only nonprofit, co-op and subsidized models.

## 2. Bottom-up market

- **Universe.** [S] More than 43,000 communities and 4.3M homesites ([Philadelphia Fed, citing MHI 2022](https://philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/Community-Development/Reports/manufactured-housing-communities-report-june-2023.pdf)). Lot rent runs from $766 a month at Sun and about $956 at ELS down to a Maine average of $479 ([Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2026-08-18/new-data-shows-just-how-much-maine-mobile-homeowners-are-paying-to-rent-land-beneath-them)) and a 2021 national median of about $414 (same Philadelphia Fed report). The lot-rent pool is therefore about $25-40B a year [D]. The pool is large; the hard part is the part you can buy.
- **The wedge states are small-park states.** I analyzed New York's state registry ([data.ny.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/manufactured-home-park-registrations-beginning-2020)) [D]. In 2025 it lists 1,167 parks with 61,706 sites. The median park has 30 sites. Only 167 parks (14%) have 100 or more sites, and they hold about half the sites. 1,119 of 1,228 owner names (91%) own one park, using each park's latest 2020-25 record. Maine has 475 licensed communities and 19,348 lots, an average of 41 ([Maine Public](https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2026-08-18/new-data-shows-just-how-much-maine-mobile-homeowners-are-paying-to-rent-land-beneath-them)). Pennsylvania is 62.5% medium (11-99 sites), 26.2% small and 11.4% large (Philadelphia Fed, above).
- **Annual supply is thin.** [S] Berkadia counts 460 communities sold nationwide in 2025 ([Berkadia](https://www.berkadia.com/lp/manufactured-housing-annual-report-2025/)); its coverage rules are not stated. In the NY registry, owner-name changes ran 32 in 2023 and 37 in 2024 out of about 1,075 matched parks. Only 27 changes in 2020-25 were parks of 100+ sites, about 5 a year [D]. Renames can inflate this proxy.
- **Conflict on the market.** [C] A [blog attributing the figure to Northmarq](https://rets.ai/blog/manufactured-housing-highest-yielding-asset-class) puts 2025 cap rates at 6.0%, down from 6.8%. Fannie Mae's own 2025 report says institutional-quality sales were on track for the lowest since 2015 through Q3 and cap rates rose after almost a decade of compression, with "credit-stressed exits by inexperienced or undercapitalized sponsors" ([Fannie Mae Duty to Serve 2025](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/d/dtsp/mh_tplp_1_nr_2025.pdf)). I trust Fannie. Northmarq's Q1 2026 median price was $58,400 a space, +12% ([Northmarq](https://northmarq.com/insights/insights/manufactured-housing-transaction-activity-rises-year-over-year-q1-2026)). Berkadia's +1.4% effective rent growth conflicts with Northmarq's 6.8% and the REITs' roughly 5%, probably on definitions [C].

## 3. Demand and willingness to pay

- **Residents do not pay for the covenant.** They already pay lot rent. The payers are (1) the state, through MHAP and Minnesota money; (2) the seller, through a price discount; (3) LP capital, through a lower return. Only (1) has a documented budget line.
- **Sellers accept discounts mainly for non-financial reasons and with tax help that Covenant Parks cannot use.** Vermont co-ops paid $3.6M against a $5M ask and $1.84M against $2.4M, where families wanted the park kept affordable ([VPR](https://www.vermontpublic.org/vpr-news/2015-11-24/proud-new-land-owners-a-mobile-home-park-success-story), [Seven Days](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/?p=107764), via search summaries). Minnesota gives a seller a credit of 5% of the sale price only for sales to co-ops, 501(c)(3)s and resident-representing nonprofits ([Minn. Stat. 290.0694](https://revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2023/cite/290.0694/pdf)) [S]. Maine exempts up to $750,000 of capital gains on sales to resident-owned communities and housing authorities ([Maine Senate](https://www.mainesenate.org/transformational-new-laws-to-protect-residents-of-mobile-home-communities-take-effect/)) [S]. HUD's FHA 223(f) community loan excluded for-profit owners at launch in 2024 ([MHI](https://www.manufacturedhousing.org/news/new-hud-and-fha-community-loan-program-fails-to-include-for-profit-community-owners/)) [S].
- **The one documented contested price went the other way.** A private-equity bid beat the appraisal by about 20% at Cascade Acres. Maine's first right-of-first-refusal sale (Hidden Circle) was a $1M purchase of 31 homes, about $32,000 a lot, with $310,000 from the state fund ([Gov. Mills](https://www.maine.gov/governor/mills/news/governor-mills-celebrates-purchase-hidden-circle-mobile-home-park-jay-residents-2026-08-18)) [D].
- **Resident and political demand is real.** Washington capped manufactured-home lot-rent increases at 5% in May 2025, and owners sued in Jan 2026 ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/01/22/manufactured-home-park-owners-sue-washington-rent-cap/)). Pennsylvania HB 1250 (a lot-rent cap) passed the House in June 2025 and awaits the Senate ([PA Lt. Gov.](https://www.pa.gov/ltgovernor/newsroom/lt--gov--austin-davis-highlights-policies-to-make-housing-more-a)). A search summary, not opened, says SB 746 (right of first refusal) cleared committee in June 2026.
- **Not found:** any seller, allocator or lender stating they will pay for a for-profit covenant. Owner age data for communities does not exist in what I found. "Sellers aged 65+" is an untested hypothesis.

## 4. Unit economics (my model, not a sourced figure)

Setup [A]: $7,000 revenue a site, 40% expense ratio, $250 a site capex, 6.5% going-in cap, 65% LTV at 6.4% interest-only, 10-year hold, 2% buy and 1.5% sell costs. The 6.4% debt is inside the 6.2-6.9% agency range quoted by brokers on [6 Oct 2026](https://www.apartmentloanstore.com/fullerton/california/apartment-loan-rates) and [Greystone](https://www.greystone.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Small-Rates_2026.08.11.pdf), against a 10-year Treasury of 4.69% in August.

| Rent growth / expense growth | Levered IRR (exit +50 bp) | Levered IRR (exit +150 bp) |
|---|---|---|
| Market: 5.5% / 3.5% | 19.1% | 17.3% |
| Cap 4% / 3.5% | 14.5% | 12.5% |
| Cap 4% / 5.0% | 12.3% | 10.1% |
| CPI-linked ~3% / 3.5% | 10.8% | 8.5% |
| CPI-linked ~3% / 5.0% | 7.8% | 5.1% |

- **The cap itself is survivable. The squeeze between the cap and expense growth is the risk.** A Spokane Valley owner cited utilities up about 22% as a reason to sell ([Spokane Public Radio](https://www.spokanepublicradio.org/regional-news/2026-01-26/washington-sued-rent-control-mobile-home-parks-sell)).
- **Price tolerance.** Solving for an 8% levered return, a covenant buyer's maximum price is 6% below a market buyer needing 12% (cap 4%, expenses 3.5%). It is 13% below at 5% expense growth, and 24% below with CPI-linked rent and 5% expenses. Against a 15%-hurdle private-equity buyer the gap is -16% to +5%. REITs buying at "low-4%" caps (an AI-generated summary of Sun's Q1 call, low authority) are not catchable.
- **Not modeled, all negative for returns:** the resident equity glide path, the formula-priced buyout in years 7-10, a thinner exit buyer pool, and reassessed property tax.
- **Leverage is roughly neutral.** Debt costs 6.2-6.9% against entry caps of 6-7.5%.

## 5. Capital needs

- **Equity and assets.** 14,300 sites at the model price is about $870M of assets and $320M of equity at 65% LTV [D]; the gate said $290M. I found no data for an alternative.
- **Agency debt does not fit the niche.** Freddie's MHC loan wants "existing, stabilized, high-quality and professionally managed" communities, a $1M minimum, five pads, and a sponsor with two years of MHC operating experience who owns another MHC ([Lument term sheet, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.lument.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Freddie-Mac-Manufactured-Housing-Community-Loan_7.6.26-1.pdf)). Fannie lowered its minimum from 50 to 40 pads in 2025 (Fannie report above). The small, rural, deferred-maintenance parks that sellers over 65 own are the least agency-eligible. Fannie's tenant-protection pricing is small: a search summary of its [2019 memo](https://multifamily.fanniemae.com/sites/g/files/koqyhd161/files/migrated-files/content/announcement/lm1901.pdf) gives a 7.5 bp nonprofit discount, and broker [term sheets](https://lument.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Fannie-Mae-Manufactured-Housing-Term-Sheets.pdf) say tenant-protection pricing carried a 10% payment guaranty. Current basis points are unconfirmed.
- **Exit financing.** Residents buying out need ROC-type debt. By the time buyouts arrive, about $125M of purchases a year for 22 parks is roughly 4.8 times ROC USA's historical annual deployment of about $26M [D].
- **No allocator evidence.** Nuveen's impact housing fund seeded with $250M from TIAA invests in rent-restricted and naturally affordable housing, with no target return published ([ConnectCRE](https://www.connectcre.com/stories/nuveen-secures-250m-for-impact-housing-fund/)). It shows the buyer type exists, not that it accepts this deal.

## 6. Regulatory and liability landscape

- **New York RPL 233-b [S]** ([text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/233-B)). Increases may not exceed 3% unless justified by operating expenses, taxes or capital costs. Increases are irrebuttably justified up to the resident's pro-rata share of costs, and a 6% ceiling applies unless a court grants a temporary hardship. Parks under an affordability regulatory agreement are excluded. The pitch's "lesser of CPI or 4%" adds little in New York.
- **Right of first refusal.** New York's runs 140 days from notice and needs more than 50% of homeowners (NCLC [2026 white paper](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026.09_White-Paper_Summary-of-Manufactured-Home-Laws.pdf)). In Maine residents must match price and terms and have 90 days to finance. Residents can match a Covenant Parks bid, so it can lose any deal in these states.
- **Maine fee [S]** (10 M.R.S. 9094-B, [text](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/10/title10sec9094-B.html)). Buyers pay $10,000 a lot unless they and affiliates, including any 20% holder, have net worth under $75M. The statute as amended by PL 2025 c. 688 says $75M; the press and the original law said $50M [C]. At 41 lots, the fee is about $410,000 a park, or about 30% of a $32,000-a-lot price [D]. A fund over the net-worth line loses Maine.
- **Lender conflict.** Freddie's term sheet says "Leases cannot contain options to purchase pad site." A pre-granted resident right to buy must sit outside the leases and be cleared with the lender. Fannie's section 603.05 was blocked to me; this is unverified for Fannie.
- **Unverified legal risks.** I did not find whether resident "equity credits" create securities or installment-sale (contract for deed) characterization. The gate cited New York and North Carolina rent-to-own statutes; treat it as a counsel item.

## 7. Is the why-now real?

Partly. The pressure is real: right-of-first-refusal laws, the Hassan inquiry, Washington's cap, Pennsylvania's bill, 5-9% REIT rent growth and Fannie's "softening NOI" report. But policy money and tax breaks flow to co-ops, nonprofits and housing authorities; for-profits are the regulated party (Maine's fee) or excluded (FHA 223(f), the Minnesota and Maine seller credits). The shock helps ROC USA first.

## 9. Verdict and what would change it

**Score on the lessons.** Size: credible at $100M of consolidated rent, but a real-estate fund, not a startup; the sponsor's own fee and carry would be a fraction [A]. Incumbents: none for-profit, but the subsidized nonprofit lane is crowded and state-backed. Payer: the state, which exists in NY and MN. Legal premise: weakened (NY statute, Maine fee, lender purchase-option rule). Why-now: real but mis-routed. Shrink-to-survive: the realistic version is 3-8 subsidized parks, about $1-6M of rent [D], well below the gate's $25M median.

**Three kill tests (30 days).**
1. Written answer from a Freddie/Fannie MHC lender on whether a recorded resident purchase right outside the leases is financeable, and a quote for 40-100 pad parks at 60-65% LTV.
2. Ten seller or broker conversations in states with no seller credits and no cap statute (not NY or Maine) on a covenant bid at 6-10% below market.
3. Two allocator responses on a 7-8% levered target at $50M+ tickets with the resident equity share included.

**Gaps.** No owner-age data; no national park-size distribution; no pricing data on capped vs uncapped sales; the national churn extrapolation from NY is mine; Fannie guide text not read; ELS and UMH figures are secondary.

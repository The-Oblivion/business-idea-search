# C05: Anchor-Funded Independent Foundry Platform

**One-liner:** Buy a qualified, sub-scale independent superalloy caster that several OEMs already depend on, then expand it with those OEMs' own reservation payments, with contract terms that protect their capacity from conflicted owners.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $55M/yr; upside $200M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C05_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C05_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (fatal): THE PRODUCT ALREADY EXISTS, AND THE MONEY GOES TO QUALIFIED INCUMBENTS, AND IT IS STILL A POOR BUSINESS FOR THEM. The Doncasters 424B4 describes the same model: customer funding of up to 80% of capex, guaranteed volumes, and 7-15 year agreements. It calls itself the only competitor offering dedicated capacity, and it says it is signing more. Siemens Energy and Chromalloy built a dedicated, Siemens-exclusive $139M airfoil JV in 2017-18. Howmet says its capex is 'backed by customer contracts' and that it has finished negotiating with all 7 major IGT customers. A new entrant would pitch a 10-year prepay to the same buyers who already fund incumbents with approved parts. The incumbent version has not produced good returns. Doncasters lost about $173M net on $837M of 2025 revenue, with about $712M of gross debt before the IPO paydown. Its 2020 restructuring wiped about 900M pounds of debt and handed ownership to its lenders. The last 'independent caster with customer money' ended with equity wiped out. The idea's own margin math (18-22% EBITDA on $150-300M of capex, 7-17 year payback) matches this picture.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (fatal): The keystone customer behavior, a 10-year take-or-pay and prepayment from buyers to an unqualified new plant, has no precedent, and the contracts it imitates are not take-or-pay. Doncasters' own IPO prospectus says its long-term agreements 'typically guarantee us a minimum level of market share', which is a share guarantee, and that volume commitments appear only 'in certain cases'. It also says some agreements allow customers to terminate for convenience, reduce volumes or delay orders, and that about 70% of 2025 sales sit under agreements that can be re-priced or renegotiated. The 'up to 80%' customer funding goes to a qualified incumbent expanding existing plants. The DoW plan the idea cites calls this exact gap a 'financing impasse': investors want a customer commitment first and customers want a proven product first. Its proposed fix is government-funded pilot demonstrations, not buyer prepayment. The researcher, the gate and I each searched and found no OEM prepay to a new entrant. Without it the capital stack does not close, and the idea's own kill test says so.
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (fatal): CAPITAL. The money that would fund this business already goes to qualified incumbents, so a new entrant is not the cheapest way to buy capacity. Doncasters' prospectus describes customer-funded capex of up to 80% at an incumbent. Howmet's CFO says its investments are backed by customer contracts. Siemens Energy, the obvious 'non-GE' IGT buyer, announced about $1B of US investment on 2026-02-03. That includes a Tampa blade-and-vane expansion and sits next to its existing $139M dedicated Chromalloy JV. Chromalloy separately put $16.5M into Tampa casting in May 2026, with new vacuum furnaces installed in July and a ramp planned for early 2027. Incumbent brownfield adds cost $50-70M (Howmet Wichita Falls about $70M, Doncasters Auburn $50M, Bochum $52M). Greenfield is $150-300M per $100M of revenue. At independents' 16-22% EBITDA margins, one plant earns $18-22M a year on that capex. Even Doncasters is not self-funding: its FY2025 net loss was $173M on $222M of interest, which the prospectus blames mostly on non-cash PIK interest. A founder with no qualified positions cannot get a lower cost of capital than the incumbents. Nobody has prepaid an unqualified entrant; the researcher and I both found zero cases.

## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** At least one non-captive buyer will sign a dollar-denominated, multi-year capacity reservation with make-whole to fund a line at a qualified caster owned by a non-incumbent. In other words, customer money follows the qualified asset and a protective contract rather than the incumbent's name. No OEM prepay to a non-incumbent owner has been found by me or any of the three critics.
- **2-week test:** Days 1-3: list 12 qualified independent superalloy casters, excluding Howmet, PCC, CPP, Doncasters, Pursuit and Arcline, and note each one's likely top customers. Days 3-12: reach 20 supplier-capacity, blades-and-vanes sourcing and procurement leads at non-captive buyers (Ansaldo, Baker Hughes, Solar, Siemens Energy and GE Vernova dual-sourcing teams, Honeywell, Kratos, Anduril, Williams) through ex-employee referrals or paid expert calls. Also reach 3-5 sell-side advisors who ran recent aerospace casting sales. In each call, show a one-page term sheet and ask: is any part family's only qualified source at risk on capacity or ownership; what reservation payment and minimum term would you sign if an independent owner with committed capital bought it; who signs and how long does it take. Pass: at least 3 of about 10 responding buyers name $10M or more over 5 or more years, at least 1 agrees to mark up a draft LOI, and at least 2 advisors confirm 3 or more targets could trade at 2.5x revenue or less within 24 months. Kill: one buyer or fewer gives a number, or the answer is "only through our existing partners" or "we would buy it ourselves".

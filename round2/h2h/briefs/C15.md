# C15: Root-Cause Contract: outcome-priced fixes for social housing's costliest homes

**One-liner:** We find the 5-8% of a social landlord's homes that keep failing for the same physical reason, fix the cause, and are paid against what an untreated control group actually costs, climbing from a 50-home sprint to multi-year capitated contracts financed by third-party capital.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $17M/yr; upside $107M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C15_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C15_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (serious): The strongest incumbent already sells the adjacent product and already holds everything the entrant would need to borrow. Wates launched 'Healthy Homes' in April 2024, a dedicated damp-and-mould and disrepair-reduction service, delivered with its own labour, with six customers already signed. Wates says it manages repairs for 500,000+ homes across 65 social landlords. The gate and the dossier both missed this. Mears has said regulation is creating new damp, mould and condensation service lines [S], and it is winning 10-year total-asset deals: Rooftop is GBP15M a year for 7,000 units (about GBP2,143 a home) and Birmingham is GBP45M a year for 11,500 units (about GBP3,913 a home), both covering far more than repairs. The hot-spot fee the idea wants (about GBP2,200 per costly home) is about what Mears is already paid per average home for everything. A landlord on price-per-property can tell its prime to fix the root causes at no extra fee. A landlord on visit-priced contracts can write a hot-spot lot or pain/gain schedule into its next tender or an amendment. The likely incumbent response is cheap: add a gain-share schedule, bundle sensors and analytics, and price it as part of the existing contract. The incumbent wins because it already has the job-level data, the vans, the tenant access and the landlord relationship. The entrant would depend on a landlord handing over data and a cohort carved out of the incumbent's contract, and on the incumbent not objecting. Scattered single homes across a patch give the entrant no route density, so the likely end state is subcontracting to the prime at prime-level margins (Mears guides to 5.0-5.2% for FY26, 5-6% medium term) rather than 12-18%.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (fatal): NO EVIDENCE ANYONE WILL BUY THIS, AND BUYER BEHAVIOUR POINTS THE OTHER WAY. Landlords are bundling everything into whole-stock deals and insourcing for control, not carving out their worst homes for a risk-taker. Bundling: Livin (about GBP200M, 10 years, single lot, TAC-1 alliance covering responsive, voids, adaptations, compliance, planned, retrofit, cyclical; award notice 27 Feb 2026), Thurrock (GBP240M), Winchester (GBP56.7M, scope names disrepair and damp-and-mould work types), PfH GBP480M and Riverside GBP657M frameworks. Insourcing: Teign Housing is ending a 10-year Ian Williams contract in April 2027 so it has 'more control over how we work' [V]; Grand Union, Uttlesford, Basildon, Ashford, Cheshire West. The signer (FD/Director of Assets, then board) would be paid roughly a 20% saving on the cohort (about GBP560 a home, about GBP1.1M on 2,000 homes, under 0.5% of a 40,000-home landlord's turnover [A]) while the landlord stays the s10A duty holder with the full legal, Ombudsman and reputational tail on its most vulnerable, most litigated homes. The Ombudsman's Hyde investigation blamed failure to oversee contractors. That is a bad risk/reward for a career-risk-averse buyer. The fee also draws on three budget owners (responsive, disrepair/legal, capital) [A], so there is no single owner with a budget line and a burning need. Demand shown in the dossier is for works and software (Mobysoft GBP97-276k a year, Wigan GBP2M, InCommunities GBP3M), never for capitation. Absence of any carve-out precedent after about 10 targeted searches is consistent with buyers not wanting it, not only with a gap.
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (fatal): A newco cannot get through the procurement door, and the scope is already taken. Landlords are bundling disrepair and damp-and-mould work into single long-term repairs partners, so a carve-out of the worst homes sits inside someone else's exclusive scope. Winchester (tender published 15 Oct 2025, go-live 1 Aug 2026, GBP56.7M, 4+3 years) puts day-to-day repairs, disrepair and damp-and-mould work in one contract. It requires turnover of at least 1.5x annual contract value, EL GBP10M / PL GBP10M / PI GBP5M, and a Dun & Bradstreet failure score of 51 or more because it is rated a high-risk procurement. Livin Housing, with only about 8,500 homes, is tendering one fully integrated repairs, maintenance and compliance partner: GBP200M, 10 years from April 2026, turnover of at least GBP40M against GBP20M a year. A 500-home pilot at about GBP1.1M a year needs about GBP1.65M of trading history and a D&B score that a company with no accounts cannot produce. So the realistic entry is as a subcontractor to the prime, who is then both the channel and the competitor. The notice-to-go-live gap at Winchester was about 9.5 months, so a contract signed from a start now goes live in late 2027. The 2026-2031 round is already awarded: Mears-Thurrock 5+5 years, Cardo-Dacorum 10 years, the PfH GBP480M framework, Riverside to about 2029.

## Second-round critic: wounded
- **Is the upside believable?** No. The upside needs six conditions to hold together: a 25%-plus cost share at the top 5-8% with 60%-plus persistence, a causal cut of 45%-plus after a winter, a new outcome-priced hot-spot lot appearing in 2029-33 tenders, GBP20-30M of fund capital raised on a thin evidence base, four primes choosing to subcontract rather than build in-house, and 60-70% renewal with no insourcing. Even generous independent odds (0.5 on the cut, 0.25 on category creation, 0.6 on capital, 0.5 on primes, 0.7 on renewal) put the conditional probability near 2-3%, not 10-15%, and overall under 1% against the stated 3-5%. The revenue arithmetic itself checks out (GBP49.7M + 15.2M + 16.5M + 1M = GBP82.4M, about $107M at 1.3), but it requires about 27% of reachable counterparties by year 7, which the rewrite itself calls heroic. It prices cohort homes at a per-home fee that assumes near-total persistence while the gate allows 60%, and it counts about 20% of revenue as GBP5,500-a-home pass-through works with blended EBITDA of only 8-12%. The credible residual is a small specialist niche: rungs one to three plus a few prime or insourcing fix-only deals, plausibly low single-digit GBP millions, in line with the dossier's $13M median and $58M upside rather than $100M.
- **Remaining holes:** Pricing contradicts the pitch. The headline says we are paid against an untreated control group, but the rung-four fee is 85% of the cohort's own 36-month trailing cost, rebased and multiplied by the landlord's rest-of-stock cost index. That is the trailing-baseline pricing the rewrite says it dropped, with an inflation index added. Once a landlord hands over its whole cohort there is no untreated same-profile group left, so a concurrent control cannot exist at the capitated rung. | The claim that the indexed fee does not pay us for what analytics vendors deliver only holds inside the randomised trial. Mobysoft-style repeat-visit cuts land mostly in the hot homes, not in a rest-of-stock average, so the index does not strip them out. Critic 1's cheap-25% point is answered at rung three and not at rung four. | Per-home fee and persistence are inconsistent. Upside revenue uses about GBP2,300 per home, which assumes the cohort stays near GBP2,700-2,800 a year. The pass gate only requires 60% persistence, which implies a counterfactual near GBP1,700. A control-based fee at 85% of that is about GBP1,430 a home, roughly 38% less recurring revenue. The GBP2,800 cohort cost is also self-described as unsourced. | Randomised waitlist controls collide with Awaab's Law. Phase 2 commentary (Clyde & Co and others; I did not read the gov.uk text) says supplementary works to prevent recurrence must start within 5 working days of the investigation, with 12 weeks as the backstop. Withholding root-cause works from control homes for a winter is legally exposed wherever a qualifying hazard is reported, and invites disrepair claims. The rewrite does not address this. | Rung three has no procurement route. 400 homes at about GBP3,000 each is about GBP1.2M, well above the roughly GBP207k-213k services threshold the rewrite relies on for the 50-home sprint. The capitated rung is a full flexible-procedure tender with turnover tests of about 1.5-2x annual contract value, which Critic 3 raised; the rewrite answers only with 'parent-guarantee provider'. Below-threshold relief is real (s.85 Procurement Act bars suitability or turnover gates on regulated below-threshold contracts) and helps rung two, but does not extend to rungs three or four.

## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** That the same 5-8% of homes, selected on repeat same-fault jobs, carry at least 25% of repair-plus-disrepair spend and still cost at least 60% of their prior level the following year (persistent and therefore fixable, not regression-to-the-mean noise), and that a real landlord will hand over the ledger to find out. Everything downstream (margin, landlord value, the case for a fund, a priced tail) depends on it, nobody has measured it, and it is cheap to test. The next-riskiest assumption, a 40%+ causal cut against a concurrent control, cannot be tested without a winter.
- **2-week test:** Build the x-ray (top-5% share, year-to-year persistence, same-fault share, cluster index, data-quality score) in days 1-2. On day 1 file an FOI to 25 councils for anonymised per-property totals for the 100 costliest homes in 2023/24 to 2025/26 (answers arrive after the window, for the 8-week gate). Days 2-4 contact 40 named targets: 15 Directors or Heads of Asset or Repairs at 25k+ associations and ALMOs, 10 council housing directors, 8 contract directors at second-tier PfH-lot primes, 7 at insourcing landlords. Offer a free 48-hour x-ray under NDA with hashed property IDs. Ask three questions: does your repairs contract allow a cohort variation, which budget line pays for supplementary root-cause works, and would you fund a 50-home sprint from it if the x-ray justifies it. Day-14 readout: at least 8 of 40 reply, at least 2 ledgers promised in writing, at least 1 received and analysed, at least 1 verbal willingness to fund a sprint. Eight-week pass: at least 3 ledgers covering 40k+ homes, top-5% share of 25% or more in at least 2, persistence of 60% or more. Kill: top-5% share under 15% in two of three, persistence under 40%, or fewer than 2 ledgers by day 28 (nobody will even share data, which answers the demand question). Cost: analyst time and about GBP15-25k of counsel.

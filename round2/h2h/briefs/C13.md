# C13: Run-Base Shrinker v2: funded retirement of application run cost, paid per invoice line that drops

**One-liner:** A vendor-neutral company that funds and runs the retirement of unused application run cost (apps, licences, hosting, third-party support, AMS contract lines) and is paid a share only when a third-party invoice line actually falls, on a ledger the client's Internal Audit signs.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $17M/yr; upside $100M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C13_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C13_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (serious): The incumbents are already conceding the savings the Shrinker would bill for, and the cheapest fix for the 'billed on volume' problem is a contract change, not a 35% third party. Vendors and rivals are moving to fixed-price, outcome-based and productivity-commitment terms. Cognizant says fixed-bid and outcome work is now over 50% of revenue. Rimini Street sells Rimini Manage on a fixed-price, unlimited-ticket model. Rimini also argues in public that time-and-materials AMS has 'no provision for root cause problem management', which is this idea's exact thesis, voiced by a vendor with 3,100 clients. Sourcing advisers are telling buyers to demand front-loaded productivity commitments and a share of the gain at renewal. ISG's Q2 2026 data shows Americas managed-services ACV down 12% to $5.2B, with IT outsourcing down 23% 'led by' application development and maintenance. Industry AI deflation is quoted at 2-5% a year, and a TCS leader reportedly says 10-15% can be committed from day one (unattributed). The gate defines the baseline as 'contract-guaranteed AI pass-through', so the Shrinker only earns beyond what the vendor already concedes. Its 6-12% headroom over 3 years is the same size as the discount the client wins anyway by renegotiating. Under fixed-bid contracts a ticket cut raises the vendor's margin, not the client's saving, until the baseline resets. On the vendor-labor lever the client's real alternative is 'renegotiate or rebid to a fixed-price vendor', which costs 0% of savings.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (serious): The $100M volume math contradicts the 36-month fee structure, using the gate's own inputs. The offer takes 30-35% of savings for 36 months and then the client keeps 100%. That makes each client a three-year annuity, so concurrent payers equal 3 times new clients per year. The gate says 79 clients is reachable at '12-15 new clients a year'. That produces only 36-45 paying clients, or about $45-57M a year at the gate's $1.26M per client, not $100M. Holding 79 payers needs about 26 new signings every year, sustained. Against the dossier's 300-600 reachable US payers, that is 4-9% of the whole market per year, or roughly 145 cumulative logos (about 25-50% of reachable payers) by year 7. Repeat sales are unlikely to fill the gap because retirements and deletions are one-time savings. On the dossier's evidence-anchored inputs ($0.45M a year per client), $100M needs 222 concurrent payers, about 74 new clients a year. That is 12-25% of the entire market annually, which is not reachable. What survives is a $10-25M project business with a revenue cliff at month 36 on every client. The gate's 'strong' verdict and $110M upside never test this.
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (serious): PRICE AND TIMING: the vendor-labor savings pool is already being harvested, one time and cheaply, by a channel the pitch undervalues. The 30-35% for 36 months has no market support. A sourcing adviser's published Cognizant SAP AMS renewal reports about 16% off contracted fees, a roughly 9% rate-card cut, year-over-year productivity savings layered on top, and more than 40x return on advisory spend (my inference: $19M claimed savings / 40 is about $0.5M of fees, roughly 2.5% of savings). Redress Compliance charges 25% of savings beyond the vendor's best-and-final, invoiced once after signature, no savings no fee, but its page covers software-publisher deals and does not name IT services. HFS says 75% of enterprises want to renegotiate IT services contracts, up from 40% in 2020. By the time the Shrinker has three design partners and a Big-4-style protocol (the gate's own test is 30-60 days, then a 36-month tail), the 2025-27 renewal wave will have taken the first 15-20% of the vendor-labor line, and what is left is the residual 'beyond contract AI pass-through' that the gate's own baseline rule defines. The 'we fund the engineering' justification for the high share is the input losing value fastest: SAP ships a free Custom Code Migration app plus Joule/ABAP AI assistance, and AWS Transform custom and GitHub Copilot app modernization are generally available. The CFO is being asked to pay a third of the savings, for three years, for capital the CFO already has and labor that is getting cheaper.

## Second-round critic: wounded
- **Is the upside believable?** No. The $100M upside needs several assumptions at once, and the author's own sensitivities show how fragile it is. About 700 reachable payers is unsourced, though the definition is broader than the dossier's 300-600 offshore-labor count, so it is not absurd. Accounts must land at $40M and double to an effective $80M within 3 years. Verified reduction must reach 9% at year 3 and 11.5% at year 5, and the rewrite concedes documented per-client savings of $1-5M a year do not support that. Blended share must be 28%, engineering cost 0.30x, and signings must climb from 3 to 20 a year via channels that are asserted, not owned. Tech E&O and cheap receivables financing must also be available. Any one adverse change drops year-7 revenue below $100M by the rewrite's own numbers: signings down 30% gives $70M, and a $60M effective base gives $76M. These assumptions are correlated, not independent, because they all depend on owner follow-through plus CFO willingness to pay a top-of-range share for a long tail. The 2-4% odds come from the author's own priors and carry no validation. Peak burn of about $32M for the upside versus about $28M for the median looks too low for 94 logos funding roughly $2.8M of engineering each. The gate-and-kill structure is honest and cheap, so the idea is worth testing as an option, but it is not a believable path to a large business as written.
- **Remaining holes:** The core 'why pay us' question is unanswered. The pitch sells 'execution with the CFO behind it', but the CFO is the client, so the missing ingredient is one the buyer already owns. Wave 1 also leads with low-engineering lines (licence, SaaS, hosting, support), where our funding adds nothing. The real comparators are a flat-fee diagnostic from ISG, UpperEdge or Flexera, or Redress at 25% invoiced once. The rewrite's own value claim is 'roughly parity with DIY at observed 25-30% realization'. Parity is not a reason to switch, and the edge depends on realization well above 50%, which is untested. | The price concession is cosmetic. Critique 3 said 30-35% for 3 years (about 0.9-1.05x a year's saving in total fees) was unsupported. v2 charges 30% for 3 years plus 10% for 4 more, which is 1.3x at headline, 1.1x at the 26%/8% median blend and 1.24x at the 28%/10% upside blend. The headline share fell a few points while the buyer's total take stayed the same or rose. The tail was added to fix the volume math, not the buyer's problem, and the 'adds only 12%' framing measures company revenue, not what the buyer pays. The tail is also a fee for keeping a ledger of the client's own invoices. | The dated-event wedge contradicts the fee definition. Verified reduction is the lesser of units x price and the actual net fall in that vendor's invoice. SQL Server 2016 support ended 14 Jul 2026 and Windows Server 2016 ends 12 Jan 2027. I confirmed both dates, and also that these are price-up events: SQL ESU year 1 is about 75% of licence cost, then 150% and 300% (secondary sources, unverified against Microsoft). Retiring a server there avoids new spend, so no invoice line falls. VMware repricing has per-CPU core floors and bundling (consultancy sources), so removing VMs can leave the invoice flat or higher. Under v2's own definition the fee on these triggers is near zero. The post-acquisition trigger has a similar problem: synergy targets are already in the deal model, so baseline fights return. | No new evidence was gathered. Every 'what would change my mind' item from the critics is converted into a plan. The 700-payer count, 9-11.5% verified reduction, CFO acceptance of 26-30% for 3 years plus a tail, tech E&O availability, lender terms at 12% or less and client acceptance of a liability cap all remain unverified. The rewrite's own sizing, 'if under 500, the upside fails', rests on a number it admits it made up. | The 14-day test is weak and does not test the riskiest assumption. It uses a convenience sample of friendly IT-finance and ITAM contacts, and getting three companies to hand over AP exports and SSO logs by cold outreach within 7 days is unlikely. A non-sharing outcome is relabelled 'inconclusive' and a 'demand signal'. The pass metric is business owners emailing back 'agree' to a contact with no budget authority, which is stated intent, not an invoice line falling within 12 months. The real evidence (20% targeted, 5-6% realized) is about follow-through, which a 14-day email poll cannot measure.

## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** Once a CFO backs the program, business owners will release at least half of the identified retirable run spend, so that third-party invoice lines actually fall within 12 months. Everything downstream (share, funding, tail, financing) needs a fee base that exists. Price has precedent (SaaS-buying share models of 15-30%, recovery audit, cloud savings share); realization has contrary evidence (20% targeted, 5-6% realized in a health-system panel; $1M of labor to save $200k of licences).
- **2-week test:** 14 days, about $0 cash, no system access, only data the owner is authorised to share under NDA. Days 1-7: recruit one insider advisor (ex-AMS delivery head, ITAM or IT-finance lead); reach out to about 40 people in IT finance, ITAM, vendor management and PE portfolio operations at companies with an estimated $40M+ of external application run spend; offer a free 'retirable spend' list from one export in 48 hours; goal three companies. Days 5-10: from each company's exports (AP lines for software, hosting and support with renewal dates; SSO or usage report; application inventory) build a ranked list of at least 15 units of $25k+ a year with renewal date, usage evidence and named owner; record identified dollars as a share of the base supplied. Days 8-14: the company contact (not us) emails each unit's owner 'we plan to retire or downsize this on [renewal date] unless you object by [date]'; count responses by dollars (written agree, silent, object). In the same window show the CFO or Controller the one-page term sheet (share, tail, buyout, sweep) and record the maximum share they would sign, the paying budget line, the alternative they would use (Redress, in-house, vendor) and which event would make them sign within 90 days. PASS (all three): M1 identified dollars at least 6% of the supplied base in at least 2 of 3 companies; M2 written agreement on at least 50% of identified dollars and objection on no more than 25%; M3 at least 2 of 3 finance contacts state a maximum share of 25% or more on invoice-evidenced lines and name the paying budget. KILL: identified under 4% in two of three, or agreement under 25% of dollars, or finance caps at 15% or prefers in-house or Redress. INCONCLUSIVE: fewer than three companies agree to share data in 7 days (itself a demand signal); then run the fallback of 12 calls with people who ran decommissioning programs (dollars identified, retired and actually dropped from invoices; median realization under 30% means kill) plus two independent counts of qualifying US companies from former AMS delivery heads. Later gates, kill not shrink: day 60 two paid X-rays at $50k+; day 120 one signed program agreement at 25%+ share on a $40M+ base signed by a CFO or Controller; month 6 tech E&O quote of $5M+ with no AI exclusion on human-led work; month 12 first audited invoice drops with at least 50% of Wave 1 dollars realized; miss any two by month 12 and stop.

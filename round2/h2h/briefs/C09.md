# C09: Stafette: Meister-partnered service hubs that absorb the customer books and crews of closing Handwerk firms (replaces Meisterwerk)

**One-liner:** A Meister-partnered network of local building-service hubs (electrical and heating/plumbing service first) that buys the customer books, crews and vans of retiring and closing owner firms into dense hubs, each led by an equity-earning Meister, financed in stages by steward-owned capital.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $27.6M/yr; upside $108M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C09_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C09_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (serious): The exact model already exists at the target scale, with deeper capital, and micro-tier entrants sit on the same channel. HomeServe Deutschland (a Brookfield portfolio company) buys SHK and electrical firms, keeps their names, leaves management in place and centralises admin, digital, purchasing and recruiting. It went from 8 firms at the start of 2024 to 21 by March 2025 and 24 firms with about 1,300 staff by Oct 2025. German revenue was reportedly about EUR 50M in 2023 and about EUR 100M in 2024, which is the $100M milestone the idea is aiming for. In Mar 2026 it bought Scheer (about 50 staff) with the stated plan of 'long-term growth with the existing management team'. Around it: VINCI Energies agreed to buy R+S Group (EUR 191M revenue, 1,200 staff, electrical/HVAC) in 2025; Konzmann/KLAR (21 firms, about EUR 180M); Builtech/VDK (about EUR 2B, 150+ businesses). At the sub-EUR 2M tier, MEISTEREHRE (founded by an SHK Meister and a Betriebswirt) is advertising for firms with 5-20 staff in Altbayern on the HWK Muenchen Betriebsboerse, the same chamber channel and region as the idea's first-10-deals wedge. SHK Group/SHK Ventures (Berlin) buys micro firms, leaves the owner as Geschaeftsfuehrer, and offers back office. If this entrant gets traction, the response is cheap: HomeServe or VINCI lower their size floor or raise the cash bid, using reference sellers, existing back office and Brookfield or VINCI cost of capital against a EUR 15M seed.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (serious): The 'customer' premise, at least 50% recurring statutory service revenue, is unsourced, and the evidence I found points to commoditised, tendered, cancellable revenue. (a) A 2023 Karlsruhe DGUV V3 testing tender: 22 firms downloaded the documents, 11 bid, 63 offers across 6 lots, award on price alone, 4-year frameworks, EUR 1.26M in total, with lots of roughly EUR 215-363k. Vendor guides put testing at EUR 3-8 per device (another page says 5-15). Testing needs an Elektrofachkraft, not a Meister, so there is no licence barrier and a customer can switch on price. (b) For consumer maintenance contracts on standard terms, BGB 309 Nr. 9 (in force since 1 Mar 2022) allows silent renewal only on an indefinite basis with at most one month's notice, so a bought 'contract book' of private customers can leave monthly. B2B contracts are not directly covered. (c) The dossier's own split is about 39% private and 35% commercial customers, and about a third of acquired firms fail to hold prior revenue (VDB). (d) Where recurring work is real (fire alarm, fire protection), PE and strategics already buy: svt/Ergon, Waterland, and VINCI Energies (EPS Hamburg, 22 staff, EUR 4.6M revenue, March 2025). 'Buy the contract book' is therefore either unproven for installers or crowded for service specialists.
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (serious): CAPITAL: the entity as specified cannot be financed, and its 'hold forever' promise has no legal backing. The dossier itself says EUR 15M seed is 3-6x too small and the real need is EUR 50-100M of sponsor equity. The debt channels are thinner than the gate assumed: Buergschaftsbanken cap at EUR 2M per borrower, a guarantee 'is no substitute for missing equity', KfW 077 is for natural persons (not a holding), and trade press describes 2026 acquisition financing as still 'selective'. The only legal vehicle for a binding hold-forever structure, the GmgV, is still just a BMJV/BMF Rahmenkonzept (4 Mar 2026), no draft bill. Its core feature is a non-amendable profit-distribution ban, which contradicts the pitch to permanent-capital LPs of a 7-10% unlevered cash yield. Without it, 'hold forever' is a side-letter that LPs or a later GP can undo, so sellers taking half their price as a deferred note are trusting a promise nobody can enforce. The German PE pool is flat (EUR 13-15B a year for seven years, 68% buyouts), and funded AI roll-up vehicles (Generation Tech Partners EUR 50M+, Tenet EUR 80M target) are now chasing the same owner-managed SME sellers and the same LP attention (not Handwerk-specific, but the same pool of sellers and LPs). A first-time operator with no track record raising EUR 50M+ of no-exit equity at a 7-10% yield is the likeliest cause of death in a 2028 pre-mortem.

## Second-round critic: wounded
- **Is the upside believable?** No. The upside ($108M, EUR 94M, 35 hubs, EBIT EUR 6.2M) is arithmetically consistent: I recomputed the cohort revenue and EBIT and both tie out. But the six conditions are conjunctive and mostly unsourced: a first-time operator raising EUR 26M of no-exit equity by month 48 with no LP identified; 70% retention on books bought from owners who could not sell; a 19% contribution invented from nothing; prices held at or below 0.4x with incumbents and GREENOX/MEISTEREHRE active; 6-7 Meister-partners a year on an unprecedented equity ladder; HWK acceptance of mobile-crew hubs; and demand recovery after 2027. The rewrite's own unconditional probability is 3-5%. I would shade that lower, nearer 1-2%, because the capital condition alone is the pre-mortem's likeliest cause of death and the median trajectory (about 4% yield) makes tranche B less likely, not more. Even the upside only just clears $100M in year 7, so there is no margin for any single assumption to slip. Honest framing: this is a small regional service group with a low-probability path to a EUR 6M-EBIT business, and the rewrite says so. It earns 'wounded' rather than 'dead' because it is falsifiable in two weeks for near-zero cash and no killing evidence was found, but the test as written is too weak to justify capital.
- **Remaining holes:** The core economic claim (EUR 155k of EBIT per extra EUR 1M, 19% contribution, EUR 240k fixed overhead) is invented. The refined file labels these 'assumptions, all to be replaced by test data' and says no margin benchmark was found. The model is reverse-fitted to give a viable hub: at EUR 1.5M it gives -0.5%, the same result the critics computed for a stand-alone micro firm. So the 'fix' is simply 'be a 25-technician firm'. EUR 240k of overhead for a 25-technician hub (one Meister, two admin/dispatch staff, premises) is about 7% of revenue, which looks lean against typical Handwerk overhead. The two-week test cannot validate any of it. | The marginal-economics argument undermines the platform. If an extra EUR 1M adds EUR 155k because Meister, dispatch and premises are already paid for, then every existing local firm, HomeServe unit or neighbour with spare capacity absorbs a closing owner's book at lower cost than a new hub. The hub loses money for about 2 years, pays a 3.5% fee and carries a central team. The rewrite lists 'neighbour sale' as a free substitute and never explains why a firm with sunk overhead would not outbid or out-service the platform on books. Falling demand (ZDH turnover -12, orders -15) means incumbents have more idle capacity right now. | The seller-side price gap is relabelled, not closed. Books and anchors are bought at 0.25-0.45x, with 40-50% paid at closing, against the 0.87x owners expect. The pool is restricted to 'closers with no alternative'. Adverse selection follows: owners who cannot sell are the ones with owner-dependent, low-profit, consumer-heavy books, which is exactly where the 70% retention and 55% B2B-repeat assumptions are weakest. The anchors are 45% of year-7 upside revenue (EUR 42M of EUR 94M). They are 1.2M-revenue firms with a Meister in place, the highest-quality targets with the best alternatives (KBG/KfW employee buyout, HWK Betriebsboerse, a bid from HomeServe or GREENOX). The rewrite admits it does not plan to compete for those firms, yet the anchors carry the model. | The recurring/B2B-book premise is still unsourced. The bottom-up funnel multiplies a stock distribution (59.8% under 5 staff) onto a closure flow, even though closures skew micro, and then applies an invented 30-35% 'B2B-heavy book' share. The critique's best evidence (price-only tenders, cancellable consumer contracts) is answered by excluding those books, not by showing Hausverwaltung or housing-company work is sticky. My WEG search shows administrators must usually obtain about 3 comparative quotes for larger jobs. The only exception is a provider already 'bewaehrt' for that community. The rewrite's own 'no old name' rule (to avoid Sec. 25 HGB) removes the brand continuity that exception relies on. The 24-month continuity letters are non-binding. | Crew value and attrition are not modelled. 'A crew that cannot easily find an employer' conflicts with a persistent electrical/SHK labour shortage: about half of SHK firms report vacancies, and Bauelektrik is the top shortage occupation. Crews have outside options, so they can leave after absorption or after a Sec. 613a objection. Crew retention is as much a risk as customer retention and appears nowhere in the 70% / 19% assumptions. A 60-minute-radius hub also eats productive technician time, which the 19% contribution ignores.

## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** That enough owner-led Elektro/SHK firms with real, retainable B2B service books and no successor exist in one cluster and will accept the book formula (0.25-0.40x retained revenue, 40-50% at closing, the rest on retention, jobs guaranteed). If not, there are no deals, no hubs and no capital. The second-biggest unknown is the 19% contribution on absorbed crews.
- **2-week test:** Seller-ledger sprint in one metro cluster, near-zero cash, run by a German-speaking operator with Handwerk credibility. Days 1-3: build a list of 120 Elektro/SHK owners aged about 58+ (Handwerksrolle and Impressum data, HWK Betriebsboerse, nexxt-change) and draft a one-page formula offer. Days 3-10: 60 owner calls (cold plus two Innung or HWK Nachfolgemoderator introductions) and 10 Hausverwaltung interviews asking which vendors are aging out and whether they would sign a 24-month continuity letter. Ask interested owners for a 30-minute call and a redacted three-year ledger under NDA (revenue by customer type, share B2B repeat, top-10 customer share, headcount, owner hours). PASS: at least 10 of 60 take the call; at least 4 send ledgers within 14 days; at least 2 say yes in writing, non-binding, to the formula; at least one ledger shows 55%+ B2B repeat revenue with top-10 customers under 40%; at least 3 of 10 property managers name an aging vendor and would sign a continuity letter. FAIL (stop or downgrade): under 2 ledgers, every owner insists on 0.7x or more in cash, or B2B repeat share under 40% in every ledger.

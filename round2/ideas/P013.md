# P013 - US HOA, condo and co-op associations: assessments, capital repairs, reserves (round 2 ideas)

Written 2026-10-14. Eight ideas, two per lens. Every number is tagged: (S) = sourced, (A) = my arithmetic or assumption, (U) = unverified claim from a secondary source or an insider hypothesis I could not confirm.

## Pool facts used (all re-checked this session)

- (S) FCAR/CAI 2025 Statistical Review: 373,000 associations, $124.2B assessments collected in 2025, $31.1B of it into reserves; 9-10k management companies; 30-40% self-managed. 29.6M housing units and 78.1M residents come from a secondary summary of the same review. (A) That is about $4,200 per unit per year and about $250k of operating spend per association ($93.1B / 373k).

- (S) Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 (Mar 18 2026): reserve floor 10% to 15% of budgeted assessment income for applications dated on/after Jan 4 2027; (U) a reserve study no older than 3 years can replace the flat test if the budget funds the study's highest recommended allocation; Limited Review ends Aug 3 2026; (S) $50,000-per-unit cap on master-policy deductibles for applications on/after Jul 1 2026; GSEs dropped the 50% investor-concentration cap. (U) CAI CEO: about 40% of mortgaged condo purchases used Limited Review. (S) A project that fails one criterion is non-warrantable for every unit.
- (S) NAR: 373k condo/co-op existing-home sales in 2025 (about 9% of 4.06M).
- (S) Florida: HB 913 (signed Jun 2025) means reserves for structural items must be fully funded from Jan 1 2026, no waiver; associations may fund reserves with loans/lines by majority owner vote (secondary summaries). ## 1. Capital Projects Desk: the financeable-package standard (INSIDER: structural engineer who did milestone/SIRS inspections, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** Turn a liability-driven engineering report into a vote-ready, lender-ready, bid-leveled package and run the project, so boards stop buying repairs from one bid and a PDF.
- **Customer:** Boards of condo/co-op associations with Phase-2 repairs, roofs, envelope, elevators, paving. First 10: 10 Florida buildings (100-400 units) with a fresh Phase-2 report, referred by 2 engineering firms and 2 mid-size managers.
- **Payer and signer:** The association, from the project budget (an owner's-rep/soft-cost line, 3-5% of construction cost); signer is board president or treasurer; lender or credit platform adds a package/underwriting fee. This line already exists: Stone Building Solutions, Souffront and DSS Condo sell owner's-rep services in Florida.
- **Problem:** The Phase-2 report is written to limit the engineer's liability, not to win an owner vote. Volunteer boards get one or two bids, cannot compare scopes, pay large deposits to contractors with no lien-waiver control, and cannot hand a lender a draw schedule. Engineers cannot procure or guarantee price (independence); contractors cannot be neutral. Capacity is scarce: an AKAM Southeast executive cites a shortage of engineers and restoration contractors, and one inspection quote went from $32k to $50k in six months.
- **Solution:** (1) Re-scope into phased options with three vote-ready budgets; (2) 3-5 leveled bids from pre-qualified contractors, priced against a growing cost database (restoration, waterproofing, roofs, balconies); (3) lender-ready package: draw schedule, lien-waiver gating, retainage rule, independent monthly verification; (4) owner's rep through completion. Revenue is a percentage of construction value plus package fees from lenders; the cost database and contractor performance scores are the scalable assets.
- **Why now:** Florida SIRS full funding from Jan 1 2026 with no waiver; Phase-2 pipeline of at least 1,575 buildings in two years (undercount); Fannie 15% from Jan 2027 forces budget decisions; PE is buying restoration contractors (Fortify Restoration, KLH/CPR), which will make contractor-side bundling the default unless an independent standard exists.
- **Wedge:** Phase-2 package for Florida buildings, priced at 2.5-3.5% for the first 10, in exchange for the right to keep the anonymized cost data and a lender referral right.
- **Path to $100M (A):** 4% x $2.5B of construction value managed per year = $100M. At an average $3M project that is about 830 projects a year (Florida structural alone is about $2.4-3B/yr, from 1,575 Phase-2 inspections in 2 years x $3M avg, undercounted, so Florida alone cannot carry it). Nationally it needs about 8% of the $31.1B a year associations put into reserves (a proxy for capital spend, A) across roofs, paving, elevators and envelope, i.e. the desk must go multi-state and multi-category. Share needed: about 8% of national association capital projects; heroic for one state.
- **Who does it today:** Owner's-rep firms (Stone Building Solutions, Souffront Engineering, DSS Condo); engineering firms doing construction administration; management companies' project departments (Associa, FirstService Residential); PE-backed restoration contractors that bundle scope (Fortify Restoration/Osceola Capital, KLH/CPR); lender-side draw software that is not association-focused (Built Technologies, Rabbet, Oracle Textura, Handle, CoFi). No national association-specific cost benchmark found.
- **Why they lose:** Owner's reps are local and relationship-based with no shared data; engineers cannot procure; contractors cannot be neutral; lender draw software serves lenders on new construction, not volunteer boards.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (cost and performance database that compounds with each project) plus process power; network effect with lenders if they accept the package as a standard.
- **Earned secret (U):** The 12-24 months between a Phase-2 report and a shovel are almost all owner vote, money and bid leveling, not engineering; the same building can yield a "do now" list priced well below the full-scope number. Validate on 10 real reports before believing it.
- **First 90 days:** Take 5 Phase-2 reports pre-bid with 2 engineering partners; run 5 competitive bids; build the first 200-line cost table; sign one Florida bank and one title underwriter for draw administration; hire an ex-GC estimator and an ex-bank construction lender.
- **Legal premise to check:** Florida rules on engineer/contractor independence, owner's-rep licensing, and the competitive-bidding requirement attributed to HB 913 (stated on one vendor site, U).

## 2. Living reserve study (INSIDER: reserve specialist, 15+ years) - weakest of the eight, flagged

- **One-liner:** An annual, lender-accepted, machine-readable reserve record sold as a subscription, because Fannie only trusts a study less than three years old.
- **Customer:** Condo/co-op boards below 15% reserve allocation; managers with 50-300 associations; later lenders and insurers. First 10: 25 Florida/California associations with a loan or sale event in the next 12 months.
- **Payer and signer:** Board treasurer, from the reserve-study/professional-fees line ($2.5-20k+ per traditional study every 3-5 years, $5.5-16.5k+ for a SIRS; vendor-blog figures, U).
- **Problem:** From Jan 4 2027 projects must show 15% or a current independent study whose highest recommended allocation is funded (U). A study older than three years is worthless to a lender; traditional firms deliver one PDF every few years with long lead times; credentialed engineer capacity is thin; Florida's Dec 31 2026 SIRS deadline for coordinated buildings lands in the same quarter.
- **Solution:** Annual field refresh by local technicians with drone/photo capture and LLM-assisted component quantification, signed each year by a credentialed professional; cost-indexed funding plan; automatic 15% test and "cheapest compliant budget" what-if; API and signed letter for lenders and insurers. About $2-3k per association per year.
- **Why now:** The three-year clause turns a decade-cycle purchase into an annual one; Limited Review ends Aug 3 2026; Florida SIRS cycle.
- **Wedge:** 15%-test plus funding what-if as a free tool for managers; paid annual refresh for associations with a pending sale or loan.
- **Path to $100M (A):** About 40,000 subscribers x $2.5k = $100M, i.e. about 10.7% of all 373k associations, or a much larger share of the condo/co-op subset. Heroic.
- **Who does it today:** Association Reserves and Reserve Advisors (incumbent study firms, decades of data); engineering firms; SmartProperty (formerly eReserve, San Diego) launched "Atlas" in June 2026, described as a living reserve intelligence engine on 20 years of study data, which is near-identical positioning (aggregator reports only a tiny raise, U); management software modules. Hidden-incumbent warning is live.
- **Why they lose:** Study firms are paid per report and carry E&O per signature; software vendors do not hold the credential. Honest note: SmartProperty may simply sell the "living" layer to the same study firms, so this idea survives only if lender acceptance plus owned credentialed signers is the differentiator.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs (annual record and history) and a cornered resource (credentialed signers plus a component database); weak.
- **Earned secret (U):** Most of a study is desk work (quantities, unit costs, schedules); lenders need independence, currency and machine-readability, not precision. The three-year clause is the tell.
- **First 90 days:** Get 3 lenders to confirm in writing that an annual signed record satisfies the reserve test; produce 25 studies at half cost to prove margin; hire 2 RS/PE signers.
- **Legal premise to check:** Whether the three-year study substitution is in the final Fannie text (secondary sources only; Fannie PDF returned 403 earlier); Florida SIRS requires an engineer/architect visual inspection.

## 3. Ledger-underwritten association credit (PATTERN: "control the repayment channel", imported from Brazil's CondoConta)

- **One-liner:** A non-bank lender-of-record for association repair loans, underwritten from live management-company ledgers and funded by capital markets, with a partner bank keeping the deposits.
- **Customer:** Boards of associations facing $1M-$20M projects. First 10: 10 Florida Phase-2 buildings introduced by 3 managers and 2 engineers, 100-400 units, $1.5-6M loans.
- **Payer and signer:** The association, from assessments (debt service replaces a special assessment); board president/treasurer signs after the owner vote where documents or statute require (majority per HB 913 summaries; a law-firm page says 75% is the default for special assessments when documents are silent, U).
- **Problem:** Associations can borrow almost only from banks, which lend to win the deposit and which the manager, not the board, usually picks. Bank appetite is limited by concentration caps; paper is short, floating or balloon; there is no secondary market. Champlain-style gaps (repairs of $16.2M against $0.7M cash) are now routine in Florida.
- **Solution:** Standard 10-20 year fixed, amortizing, assessment-pledged term loan with a construction-draw mode (draws via idea 1 or 4); underwriting from ledger feeds (delinquency, budget, reserve study, SIRS, insurance); warehouse line and insurance-company private placements; partner banks take deposits and participations. Platform earns interest on a retained slice, origination fees and servicing.
- **Why now:** Florida full funding from Jan 1 2026 and loans explicitly allowed; Phase-2 pipeline; Vantaca now holds the ledgers of 550+ management companies and 6.5M units and just launched vendor payments (Jun 2026), proving ledger integrations are open; CondoConta shows the model works abroad.
- **Wedge:** Phase-2 repair loans in Florida, then roofs/elevators/paving nationally.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Revenue here is mostly interest, so count it gross: a $1.3B book at about 7.5% is about $98M plus fees, i.e. about 650 loans at $2M average; at maturity that is about 120 originations a year (WAL about 5.5 years). Against an assumed $4B a year of national association project borrowing (Florida structural about $1B at a 40% debt share, plus about $2.5-3B elsewhere; unsourced) that is about 6% share. A fee-only version would need about $3B a year of originations, which is not viable. Net interest after funding is only about $30-40M and the book needs roughly $150-200M of equity and first-loss; the real constraint is capital, not demand.
- **Who does it today:** Banks: Western Alliance (Alliance Association Bank), Axos (receivables-secured 3-20 year loans), City National Bank of Florida (500+ associations), Columbia Bank ($100k+), Leader Bank, North State, American Momentum, U.S. Century (per pool file); public programs for owners (Miami-Dade, North Bay Village); Endeavor Financial markets HOA lending (page unreadable, U). No venture-backed US association lender found. Closest analog: CondoConta (Brazil): about 400k condo users, over R$150M of credit, US$13.2M Series A, loans secured by receivables, integrated with condo ERPs. Risk: Vantaca partners a bank and bundles lending before an independent lender scales.
- **Why they lose:** Banks sell loans to buy deposits and are gated by manager referrals and concentration limits; they hold, they do not syndicate. Vantaca is software without a balance sheet.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource and switching costs (ledger access, servicing relationship, operating accounts pinned by loan covenant) plus scale economies in servicing and funding cost.
- **Earned secret (U):** Association loans are bought on the deposit, not the credit; bankers are paid for deposits and the manager picks the bank. The credit is statutory (power to levy and lien), so the real risks are a failed owner vote and a project overrun, not default. I could not find published loss rates; verify with call-report history before believing "near zero".
- **First 90 days:** Lock one Florida community bank (deposit and participation partner) and one insurer or credit fund for $25M forward flow; build ledger ingestion from Vantaca/CINC exports; get a legal opinion on pledge and lien enforceability and Florida owner-vote rules; pilot 5 loans only after the warehouse line is committed.
- **Legal premise to check:** State commercial-lending licensing, usury, and whether a pledge of future assessments is enforceable in each target state.

## 4. Association treasury: project-funds escrow, draw and instant-pay (PATTERN: "float and spread without credit risk")

- **One-liner:** A custody rail for special-assessment and loan proceeds that releases money only against lien waivers and inspector sign-off, and earns the float and a contractor instant-pay fee.
- **Customer:** Boards with a funded repair project; owner's reps; lenders. First 10: the idea-1 pilot buildings.
- **Payer and signer:** Association pays 0.3-0.5% escrow/draw fee from the project budget; contractors pay a 1-1.5% discount for instant pay; the bank partner shares float. Signer is board treasurer.
- **Problem:** Special assessments of $8k to $300k per unit sit in operating accounts for 12-24 months; volunteers pay deposits of 30-50% and release retainage by feel; the manager both controls the account and pays the contractor. Fraud and contractor-failure losses land on owners.
- **Solution:** Per-project trust account at a partner bank, dual board approval in an app, lien-waiver-gated pay applications, independent inspection (from idea 1), retainage rules, instant pay for verified draws, unused balance in T-bills or insured sweeps; add a reserve ladder for idle reserves.
- **Why now:** More assessments and loans in 2026-27; Vantaca just proved association payments rails are open.
- **Wedge:** Draw administration for the 10 pilot projects at 0.3%.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Assume $5B a year of project flows (about 16% of the $31.1B reserve inflow, a proxy): escrow fee 0.4% = $20M; instant-pay 1.25% on 30% of draws = $19M; float on an average $2B balance at 4% with a 50% bank share = $40M; reserve ladder on $4B at 0.6% = $24M; total about $103M. Needs about 16% of project flows plus about 6% of the $63B of association deposits. Heroic, rate-sensitive (float shrinks if rates fall), and probably not big standalone; it is a feature of 1 and 3.
- **Who does it today:** Built Technologies ($137M+ raised, lienwaivers.io), Rabbet, Oracle Textura, Handle, CoFi (all new-construction/lender-side); title agencies and banks running disbursements on large loans; association banks (Western Alliance with ICS/CDARS and HOA money market, Axos, American Momentum); the managers themselves.
- **Why they lose:** Draw software is sold to lenders and developers, not volunteer boards; banks do not ladder reserves or police contractor payments; managers are conflicted.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs and counter-positioning against manager-controlled accounts; weak without 1 and 3.
- **Earned secret (U):** The float and the fraud exposure are the same dollars; nobody is paid to protect them.
- **First 90 days:** Sign a bank trust-account partner; license check (escrow/disbursement agent, money transmission); run 3 pilot projects with manual lien-waiver gating.
- **Legal premise to check:** Florida escrow/disbursement licensing and construction trust-fund law (Ch. 713); stored-balance rules.

## 5. Fee-only board CFO (DILEMMA: managers, brokers and banks are structurally conflicted)

- **One-liner:** A conflict-free cost-recovery service for associations that audits insurance, reserve cash, vendors and utilities, paid a share of verified savings by the association only.
- **Customer:** Managed associations with $150k-$2M budgets. First 10: 10 boards from one friendly management company, flat-fee audit at $1,500.
- **Payer and signer:** The association, from professional fees; signer is the board treasurer; fee is 20% of verified first-year savings or $2-4 per unit a year.
- **Problem:** Managers hold affiliated insurance agencies, lockbox/bank arrangements, resale-document fees and vendor programs (e.g. RealManage Insurance Services exists; Florida added kickback prohibitions with criminal penalties and manager conflict disclosures in 2024). Boards are volunteers: CAI reports 91% of associations saw premium increases, 17% above 100%, 20% lost carriers, and one source calls idle reserves at 0.5% the largest opportunity cost.
- **Solution:** Annual audit and rebid: insurance to 30+ markets through a licensed broker-of-record partner with a flat fee (no commission to the platform), reserve ladder under an investment policy, vendor bid-leveling (landscape, pool, elevator, trash), utility and tax audits, management-contract benchmarking. Benchmark data across thousands of associations is the asset.
- **Why now:** Florida's 2024 conflict and kickback law; hard-market insurance renewals; reserve inflows rising ($31.1B a year) with higher yields; E&S property pricing began softening in 2026, which helps rebids but also makes savings attribution noisy.
- **Wedge:** Insurance renewal rebid plus reserve-cash yield for 10 boards.
- **Path to $100M (A):** $250k operating spend per association x 5% savings = $12.5k; 20% fee = $2.5k; 40,000 associations = $100M, about 10.7% of all associations (or about 20% of managed associations above 50 units). Heroic and savings attribution is hard.
- **Who does it today:** Management-company programs (Associa, FirstService Residential, RealManage), brokers (Brown & Brown, Acrisure, local agents), Condo Control and Spectrum (RFP and vendor portals), CAI business partners; no fee-only venture-scale board advisor found (not proof).
- **Why they lose:** Managers cannot give up ancillary income; software vendors sell to managers; banks and brokers are paid on the thing being optimized.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning (cannot be copied without cutting manager revenue) and a benchmark data asset; switching costs are low.
- **Earned secret (U):** The board rarely sees more than two or three insurance quotes and the manager's affiliate often holds the placement; the biggest savings are insurance renewals and idle reserve cash, not vendor rebids.
- **First 90 days:** 10 flat-fee audits; two insurance renewals through a partner broker; measure realized savings against a documented baseline; legal review of Florida s. 468.4335 and insurance licensing.
- **Legal premise to check:** Whether a fee-only advisor can legally rebate commissions and whether payments from vendors to the platform would themselves look like kickbacks (so: association pays only).

## 6. Manager-of-record for small and self-managed associations (DILEMMA: incumbents cannot serve below about 75 doors) - weak, flagged

- **One-liner:** A licensed management firm that runs 3x the associations per licensed manager through automation, priced per door-month with an SLA, for the 110-150k associations the big managers decline.
- **Customer:** Self-managed or small associations (<75-100 units); 30-40% of 373k (about 112k-149k). First 10: 10 Florida associations of 20-60 units.
- **Payer and signer:** The association, from the management-fee line; signer is board president; $12-20 per unit per month or $400-1,200 flat.
- **Problem:** A licensed manager needs a portfolio of 8-12 associations; below about 75 doors the per-door fee does not cover their time, so volunteer boards run everything alone just as Florida and Fannie add websites, SIRS, conflict disclosures and 15% budgets.
- **Solution:** AI-assisted AP/AR, notices, violation letters, budget drafts, board packets, compliance calendar, with a licensed CAM supervising 25-40 small associations and local technicians for site work.
- **Why now:** Compliance load jumped in 2024-26; LLMs cut the routine work.
- **Wedge:** Florida small condos and HOAs.
- **Path to $100M (A):** 14,000 associations x 40 units x $15 x 12 = $100M (about 9-12% of self-managed associations). Heroic and it looks like an AI services roll-up.
- **Who does it today:** PayHOA ($27.5M Series A, software for self-managed), Assembly HOA, Bluejay, Vantaca (sold to management companies), CINC, Condo Control; about 9-10k management firms including Associa and FirstService (not for small); thousands of small local managers; free: QuickBooks and spreadsheets.
- **Why they lose:** Their cost structure is licensed headcount per door; cutting headcount cuts their billing logic; regional offices cost too much.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies in staffing ratio; weak.
- **Earned secret (U):** The work is license- and meeting-bound, not software-bound, so productivity accrues to whoever holds the license and the routine.
- **First 90 days:** Acquire or partner for a Florida CAM firm license; sign 10 associations at SLA pricing; measure doors per licensed manager.
- **Legal premise to check:** State CAM licensure and unlicensed-management rules; AI cannot sign.

## 7. Assessment deferral for unit owners (SHOCK: special assessments of $10k to $300k per unit)

- **One-liner:** Pay the association in full on day one and collect from the owner at sale, refinance or death, secured by a junior lien and underwritten on the building.
- **Customer:** Unit owners, especially retirees on fixed incomes and absentee investors, in buildings with large assessments; boards benefit by being paid at once. First 10: 20-40 Florida buildings with $20k-$100k per-unit assessments.
- **Payer and signer:** The owner signs (the alternative is lien and foreclosure; no budget line exists, the shock creates the need); the association signs a participation agreement and needs no loan vote.
- **Problem:** About 2 in 5 Florida condo owners got a special assessment in three years (U); lenders will not lend into non-warrantable buildings with critical repairs; reverse mortgages need FHA-approved condos; public help is capped and income-tested (Miami-Dade up to $50k, owner-occupants, income under 140% AMI, $15M a round); association loans make solvent owners co-obligors for others.
- **Solution:** Funder pays the assessment to the association; owner repays on a trigger event (accruing at about 7-9%) or monthly; junior recorded lien; combined LTV cap about 50-60%; underwrite the building (SIRS, scope, reserve funding, delinquency, litigation, insurance) and the unit, not just the borrower; exclude buildings under vacate or unsafe orders.
- **Why now:** Funding of structural reserves is mandatory from Jan 1 2026; Fannie rules shrink bank appetite; a lien-based deferral is the only workable product when income-based underwriting fails.
- **Wedge:** Owner-occupied seniors in 20-40 buildings with board endorsement.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Gross interest and fees on about a $900M book (about 30,000 loans at $30k average, WAL about 4-5 years, about 5,000-6,500 originations a year). Against roughly 213k Florida condo owners a year facing assessments (A, from the 2-in-5 claim over three years on about 1.6M condo units, unverified) that is about 3% penetration, and other states add volume. Net interest after funding is only about $30-40M and it needs about $150M of equity; the constraint is capital and regulation, not demand.
- **Who does it today:** Miami-Dade and North Bay Village programs; bank HELOC/second liens; home-equity-investment firms and HECM lenders (condo access limited, U); unsecured personal-loan fintechs; no private lien-secured special-assessment product found.
- **Why they lose:** Banks cannot price building-condition risk at the unit level and are boxed in by ability-to-repay rules; HEI firms avoid condos with big assessments; public programs are small.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (building-level underwriting data from idea 1/8) and scale economies in servicing and ABS funding.
- **Earned secret (U):** Most owners who "cannot pay" are equity-rich and income-poor (seniors, estates) or absentee investors, not insolvent; the blocker is the building, not the borrower.
- **First 90 days:** Counsel opinions on Reg Z/ATR, Florida ch. 494 mortgage licensing and any lien assignment; $2M pilot warehouse; participation agreements with 3 boards.
- **Legal premise to check (decisive):** A junior lien on a dwelling is a mortgage for TILA/RESPA purposes; deferral and reverse-style structures have different rules; whether the funder can take an assignment of the association's lien is unknown. If ability-to-repay forces income underwriting for seniors, the product dies.

## 8. Neutral association data exchange with an indemnified lender desk (SHOCK: Fannie full review, 15% rule, $50k deductible cap)

- **One-liner:** Managers connect once and get paid; lenders, title agents and insurers buy a verified, living eligibility record per building, and lenders can buy the determination with a repurchase indemnity.
- **Customer:** Mortgage lenders (condo project review), title/closing agents (resale, estoppel, Florida disclosure packages), insurers, servicers and investors. First 10: 5 independent mortgage lenders in Florida/California plus 5 managers.
- **Payer and signer:** Head of secondary marketing or production operations at the lender (per-loan cost and QC line); title operations VP; fee per determination or per package.
- **Problem:** Limited Review ends Aug 3 2026, moving about 40% of mortgaged condo purchases to full review (U); from Jan 4 2027 the 15% test; $50k deductible cap; one failed criterion makes the whole project non-warrantable. Lenders chase questionnaires from managers who owe them nothing, charge for them, and answer from memory; Fannie cannot verify. Data sits in 9-10k management companies and at least six software systems, so no vendor can aggregate rivals.
- **Solution:** Neutral exchange with API/export connectors, a living eligibility record (reserve %, study date, delinquency, insurance and deductible, litigation, investor %, special assessments, SIRS status), automatic questionnaires, and a lender desk offering a determination with an insurer-backed repurchase indemnity; revenue share to managers.
- **Why now:** Aug 3 2026 change and Jan 4 2027 date; 64% of boards whose building was deemed ineligible say it hurt sales or values (survey of 700+, U); Florida disclosure packages.
- **Wedge:** Lender desk for Florida condo loans, then resale/disclosure packages.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Lender project-review wallet about 300-375k loans x $150-300 = $45-110M; resale/estoppel/disclosure vendor share about 1.5M packages x $40-80 = $60-120M (gross fee pool about $450M, mostly kept by managers; Florida caps estoppel at $299 plus $119 expedited); insurer and investor data about $40-110M. Total addressable vendor wallet about $350M, so $100M needs 25-30% of it. Heroic; this is a standard-setting bet, not a mid-cap one.
- **Who does it today:** RealPage/HomeWiseDocs (Thoma Bravo, resale and disclosure leader), CondoCerts, Fannie's own free Condo Project Manager and PERS service, condo review vendors with unpublished prices, UWM's condo eligibility tool for brokers, InspectHOA (HOA database startup, $3.1M seed, date U), Vantaca (system of record for 550+ companies).
- **Why they lose:** System-of-record vendors cannot aggregate rivals; managers profit from per-document fees; the GSEs cannot verify; HomeWise sells documents, not determinations with liability.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (supply: managers connect once; demand: lenders) and counter-positioning against single-vendor records; Race-the-bundle risk is the main threat.
- **Earned secret (U):** Lenders do not need perfect data; they need someone else to own the repurchase risk. Managers resist standardizing because the questionnaire fee is a profit center, so pay them to participate.
- **First 90 days:** Connect 5 managers (100+ associations); run 100 determinations for 2 lenders at cost; find one admitted insurer to reinsure the indemnity.
- **Legal premise to check (decisive):** A repurchase indemnity may be regulated insurance (round 1 died on this); structure as an admitted-carrier policy with the platform as MGA. Owner-level delinquency data could trigger FCRA, so keep it association-level.

## Ideas killed or not written up

- Standalone reserve treasury (HOA money-market pool): $63B of deposits x about 12-20 bps of platform fee is $75M even at 100% share; folded into idea 4.
- Insurance MGA/reciprocal for condo master policies: CORE (HCI) premium appears to have fallen from about $81M to about $31M in 2025 (columns unverified), E&S property rates began softening in 2026, Corgi is entering, capital-heavy.
- Condo terminations/bulk-sale brokerage: only 18 buyouts in Miami-Dade and Broward in 2023; too small.
- Restoration contractor platform: PE roll-ups already exist (Fortify, KLH/CPR); a startup would be an acquirer or a target, not an edge.
- GPO for association vendors: managers hold the vendor relationships and rebates; needs about 12% of addressable spend; services do not aggregate like drugs.
- Collections as a service: Axela ($4M Series A) and LM Funding already there; fee-shifted pool about $300M.
- Turf-to-xeriscape conversion: Nevada deadline is Dec 31 2026 (too late), California HOA ban is Jan 1 2029 and conversion is not mandated; sizes unverified.
- Closing-doc rail alone: HomeWise (RealPage) owns it; folded into idea 8.

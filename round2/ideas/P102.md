## 1. Shop Ledger: cross-shop carrier-health network, paid in 24 hours (INSIDER: independent heavy-duty shop owner / controller, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** A neutral credit network across independent shops that sees every shop's invoices to a carrier plus the carrier's ELD activity, so shops get paid in 24 hours and healthy carriers get net-45 terms.
- **Customer:** Independent heavy-duty shops with 5-30 technicians and more than $250k a year of fleet receivables, and the carriers with 5-200 trucks they bill. First 10: shops in one metro (for example Dallas-Fort Worth) that already run Fullbay or ShopView.
- **Payer and signer:** The shop pays a 2.0% discount on guaranteed invoices (replaces fleet-card merchant fees, bad debt and its own factoring line); the carrier pays 1.0% for net-45 (replaces fleet-card fees and late-pay penalties). Signers: shop owner or controller; carrier owner or maintenance manager. The budget line exists: ShopView describes shops fronting about $15k for 60 days and fleets on net-30 to net-90.
- **Problem:** Shops are the unsecured lenders to trucking in the third year of a freight recession. Each shop sees only its own invoices, so it cannot tell whether a carrier is slow everywhere. Existing tools move money (Fullbay Payments, RoadSync) or buy invoices on application-based credit (cbCharge), none watches the carrier.
- **Solution:** (1) Shops connect their shop software; carriers consent to ELD data (miles, engine hours, idle days) through Samsara or Motive APIs. (2) A cross-shop payment-behavior ledger plus ELD activity yields a daily carrier-health score. (3) The network guarantees payment in 24 hours on invoices to carriers above a score threshold. (4) Carriers get net-45 and repay by sweep from their factor, fuel card or settlement (this is the repayment channel in Idea 3). (5) Later, dealers and distributors add their receivables from small fleets.
- **Why now:** Third year of the freight recession and R&M up 8.6% in 2025 (ATRI); the Fullbay/Stripe survey shows 31% of operators delay repairs for cash-flow reasons; ELD vendors expose APIs and just added maintenance products (Motive Aug 2026, Samsara), so the data to see a carrier exists for the first time; Fullbay's acceptance rails are built but its survey shows credit is not.
- **Wedge:** 10 shops, one metro, invoices under $5k from carriers with 12+ months of ELD history, 2% for 24-hour pay, free to join.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Blended take 3.0% (2.0% shop side plus 1.0% carrier side) needs $3.3B of financed invoices a year. Outsourced repair and parts spend is about $50B (A), so that is about 6.6% of it. Credit losses must stay under about 1% of volume. Fullbay's claimed $5.04B of shop billing shows a single network can carry that scale of flow, but this must sit across many shop systems. Share needed: about 6-7% of outsourced spend, heroic but within what freight factoring achieved with small carriers (not sourced).
- **Who does it today:** Fullbay Payments (Stripe; ACH, cards, Affirm/Klarna BNPL; next-day funding); RoadSync (embedded fleet-card and ACH acceptance, partnered with Fullbay in 2022); cbCharge / Corporate Billing (pays the shop within 24 hours on its own credit decision, takes new invoices as its liability); TruckSuite / Repair ACES (2021 "no recourse" customer repair financing; no scale numbers published); fleet-card networks (WEX, EFS, Comdata, T-Chek); freight factors serve carriers, not shops (from memory: Triumph, RTS, Apex). Hidden-incumbent warning: Fullbay (Mainsail/JMI) owns the shop record and can add credit; if it does, this idea shrinks to the shops outside Fullbay.
- **Why they lose:** Fullbay sees only its own shops and is a software vendor avoiding balance-sheet risk (U); cbCharge and TruckSuite underwrite on a credit application, not live ELD; fleet cards give payment but at merchant fees and no credit judgment; factors finance freight bills, not repair bills.
- **Moat:** Data network effect (cross-shop payment behavior plus ELD), scale economies in funding cost, and switching costs once a shop's cash flow depends on the 24-hour pay.
- **Earned secret (U):** A shop knows a carrier is dying three weeks before the bank does: the PM visits stop, the trucks stop moving, and the last invoices get paid on a spouse's card. One shop sees one slice; a network that joins invoice behavior with ELD miles sees the whole curve. Backtest on 50 carriers that failed before believing it.
- **First 90 days:** Sign 10 shops; get 24 months of anonymized AR history from each to backtest the score; get ELD consent from 100 carriers; open a $3-5M warehouse line; check commercial-finance licensing and disclosure laws (California, New York and others) with counsel.
- **Legal premise to check:** Commercial-finance licensing and disclosure rules by state, and true-sale versus loan treatment of purchased invoices (U).
- **Outside-box:** 5. The "BNPL for auto repair" cliche applies to consumer-style products; this is a B2B receivable network with live carrier data, not a checkout button.

## 2. Overnight Yard Crews: Level-1 PM and tire crews with remote master techs (INSIDER: diesel technician instructor plus shop service manager, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** Fix the technician shortage by changing the job, not just the headcount: train hourly Level-1 crews in weeks to do the checklist work (PM, inspections, tires, brakes, lights) in the fleet's own yard at night, with a Level-3 master tech on video for escalations.
- **Customer:** Fleets with 50-2,000 trucks and a yard (private fleets, food distributors, waste, school and transit) that cannot staff their in-house shops, plus OEMs that need recall and campaign capacity. First 10: yards within 60 miles of one metro hub with PM backlogs.
- **Payer and signer:** The fleet's PM, inspection and tire budget at about $115 an hour (A) against dealer shop rates in the $140-$195 range (U); signer: director or VP of maintenance. Second payer: the OEM warranty and campaign budget (the pool's claims are $3.94B a year; Daimler already pays Love's for campaign work).
- **Problem:** ATRI: 65.5% of shops are understaffed and 19.3% of positions are unfilled. Dealer technicians are paid flat-rate, so low-ticket PM and tire work is what they avoid and what backs up. Truck stops run fixed locations on fixed hours. Apprentices are given sweeping and lube jobs, so only 38% enter with formal training and 44% of techs consider leaving.
- **Solution:** Standard-work crews of two Level-1 workers in a branded van or at the customer's bays, run to a checklist per service, with a Level-3 mentor on video and a parts kit pre-loaded from the ELD's PM schedule. Level-1 hires train 4-6 weeks (357 hours and $8,211 is what a shop spends today to bring in an untrained entry tech, ATRI). Pay Level-1 hourly, not flat-rate. Apprenticeship ladder to Level-3 so the crew is also the talent pipeline. Partner with community colleges so short programs can qualify for Workforce Pell from 2027 (not a day-one revenue source).
- **Why now:** The 2025 ATRI shortage data; Workforce Pell (July 2026) and the apprenticeship order (Apr 2025) put public money behind short programs; data-center generator work pays $37-$42 an hour (one posting), pulling experienced technicians away; Daimler's Love's program proves OEMs buy non-dealer capacity.
- **Wedge:** Overnight PM and DOT annual inspections for 10 yards, then tires and brakes, then OEM campaigns.
- **Path to $100M (A):** A crew-year bills about $345k (2 techs x 1,500 billable hours x $115). $100M needs about 290 crews, roughly 600 field staff and 60 master-tech mentors. If PM, inspection, tire, brake and lighting labor is about $35B (A, roughly 40% of the outsourced and in-house pool), that is about 0.3% of it. Share is small; the constraint is hiring and route density, not demand.
- **Who does it today:** Ryder (Torque by Ryder mobile PM in six states, SelectCare), Love's Speedco (1,500+ bays), Penske contract maintenance (133,700 customer-owned vehicles), dealer mobile units (Allegiance, Bergey's, Vanguard), Valvoline's heavy-duty fleet service center, FleetPride and TruckPro service centers and mobile units, TDI Fleet Services (Texas), Diesel Laptops (remote virtual diesel tech since 2019), True North Fleet Services (PE-backed shop roll-up). Assume all of these.
- **Why they lose:** Dealers' flat-rate pay plans make PM the work no technician wants (U); OEMs cannot run service themselves (franchise laws, U); truck stops cannot move to the customer's yard; Ryder's mobile offer is retail-priced and in six states. No one has made Level-1 work a trained, hourly, standardized job with a ladder.
- **Moat:** Process power (training standard, checklists, QA), cornered resource (the apprentice pipeline and instructor bench), route-density scale economies. Weak against Ryder and Love's scale, so the standard must be licensable to shops.
- **Earned secret (U):** The shortage is partly a task-allocation problem. Maybe half of the billable hours at a typical shop are checklist work (the Fullbay line shares suggest it but do not prove it; hours, not lines, matter). The pipeline fails because apprentices get the junk work; make that work the first job, and the best apprentices self-select upward.
- **First 90 days:** Hire two ex-instructors and a recruiter; write the 20 standard-work cards (PM-A, DOT annual, brake relines, tires, lights); pilot with 3 yards of one regional fleet; apply to a community college partner for Workforce Pell eligibility.
- **Legal premise to check:** Hourly versus flat-rate and state technician certification rules; OEM authorization needed for warranty work; Workforce Pell requires a 12-month track record plus governor and federal approval.
- **Outside-box:** 4. Good business, crowded field, thin moat; included because the labor model is the part others skipped.

## 3. Pay-by-the-Mile: major-repair credit repaid through the factor and the odometer (PATTERN: control the repayment channel plus remote-lock collateral)

- **One-liner:** Finance the $2,500-$40,000 engine, transmission and aftertreatment repairs that owner-operators and small fleets cannot pay for, collect in cents per mile through the freight factor or settlement they already use, with the odometer as the schedule.
- **Customer:** Owner-operators and sub-20-truck fleets (97% of fleets) with 3+ years in business and thin credit. First 10: 100 borrowers from 5 independent shops that already use Fullbay Payments.
- **Payer and signer:** The borrower pays about 12-18% APR-equivalent plus fees, the shop pays a 2% merchant fee to be paid at completion. Signer: the owner-operator or small-fleet owner; budget line is the repair reserve they do not have (31% delay repairs for cash flow).
- **Problem:** Fullbay/Stripe: 61% of operators would likely use pay-over-time and interest is 61% for major engine repairs, but only 30% of shops offer a plan and 13% can use third-party financing. BNPL tickets are small, and consumer-grade underwriting declines thin-file commercial borrowers. A parked owner-operator loses about $700 a day (U).
- **Solution:** (1) Underwrite on ELD miles, tenure and repair history, not FICO. (2) Repay by sweep from the borrower's factor or broker settlement before the remainder is released, with a per-mile accrual from the ELD as the trigger. (3) Collateral: a lien on the truck and the replaced component, and a start-inhibit only when the truck is parked, never while moving. (4) Shop is paid at completion. (5) Later, finance donor-truck engine swaps from Idea 7.
- **Why now:** R&M +8.6% (ATRI 2025); tariffs add 20-30% to the heavy parts; trucks held longer; the Fullbay/Stripe survey quantifies demand for the first time; Motive and Samsara have made ELD data portable.
- **Wedge:** One freight factor with 500 small-carrier clients as the sweep partner; 50 pilot loans up to $10k.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Revenue is about 14% of originations (11% interest plus 3% fees), so $715M of originations. Pool: about 1.2M trucks in small fleets x 0.35 repairs over $2,500 a year x $8,000 average = $3.4B, so the plan needs 21% of all such repairs financed. Heroic. Credit losses of 5-8% of originations (A) must be absorbed. This is a companion to Idea 1, not a standalone venture-scale bet.
- **Who does it today:** Fullbay Payments with Stripe (Affirm/Klarna), TruckSuite / Repair ACES (non-recourse customer financing), cbCharge, freight factors (from memory: Triumph, RTS Financial, Apex Capital, Thunder Funding, OTR Solutions) that already hold the borrower's cash flow and advance fuel, general SMB lenders, and Mudflap's fuel card (Series B $50M in 2022; unverified later rounds) that already has small-operator credit data. A factor adding repair advances is the most likely competitor.
- **Why they lose:** BNPL has consumer-grade underwriting and small limits; factors do not finance repairs (U) and do not talk to shops; shops cannot underwrite; no one sits in the repayment waterfall and holds the ELD feed at once.
- **Moat:** Cornered resource (sweep agreements with factors and brokers), data, and scale economies in funding cost.
- **Earned secret (U):** Repayment is the product. A trucker who gets paid through a factor repays the factor first; a lender inside that waterfall sees losses near factor loss rates, while an unsecured small-business loan to the same person loses several times more.
- **First 90 days:** Sign one factor and 5 shops; build the ELD mileage trigger; $5M warehouse line; counsel on state commercial-financing disclosure laws (California, New York and others) and on remote start-inhibit liability.
- **Legal premise to check:** State commercial-financing disclosure and licensing, usury, UCC lien priority against the factor, and wrongful-immobilization risk; do not disable a moving truck.
- **Outside-box:** 6.

## 4. Warranty Wrapper: the OEM-paid multi-brand network for non-dealer warranty and campaign work (PATTERN: wrapper intermediary on pass-through flows)

- **One-liner:** Be the contract vehicle between OEM warranty budgets and the thousands of independent shops, fleet in-house shops and mobile crews that can do warranty and recall work faster than dealers, carrying authorization, training, prior approval, parts return and claims compliance.
- **Customer:** OEM warranty and aftermarket directors (Daimler Truck NA, Volvo/Mack, PACCAR, Navistar/International and component makers). Fleets use the network free. First: one OEM that lacks a Love's-style partner.
- **Payer and signer:** The OEM warranty administration budget; signer: VP of warranty or customer quality. The line is real: US heavy-duty manufacturers paid $3.94B of claims in 2025; PACCAR alone paid $772.1M.
- **Problem:** Dealers' service bays are full, so recalls, campaigns and light warranty work wait while the truck sits. Daimler's answer was a single exclusive partner (Love's, 400+ locations, truck-stop sites only). OEMs cannot own service (state franchise laws, U) and fleets with in-house shops cannot get paid for warranty work they could do.
- **Solution:** Onboard shops and fleet shops as authorized providers (OEM-specific training and tooling), digital prior approval, standard three-C narrative and photo capture, parts-return logistics, first-pass claim filing and appeals. The OEM sets labor rates; the platform keeps about 10% of the pass-through. Fleet-side software (Samsara drafts claims) pushes more claims in; this is the provider-side rail.
- **Why now:** The Daimler/Love's precedent is proven since Jan 2024; WARRANTYWEEK shows claims at $3.94B; the EPA proposal keeps emissions warranty at 5 years / 100,000 miles, so OEMs keep the cost-per-claim pressure; telematics vendors are adding claim drafting that raises claim volume from fleets.
- **Wedge:** One OEM campaign, 200 trucks at five fleet yards in one region, with the local dealer signing off and keeping parts and claim filing as in the Love's model.
- **Path to $100M (A):** $1.0B of pass-through (25% of the $3.94B claims pool) at a 10% net take rate is $100M; gross booking would make $100M only 2.5% of claims. Net basis is heroic and depends on at least two OEMs opening their programs. Revenue overstates economics if booked gross; report net.
- **Who does it today:** Franchised dealers (primary), Love's Truck Care / Speedco for Freightliner (Freightliner ExpressPoint), OEM warranty portals, Samsara (fleet-side claim drafting, flagged as depending on OEM terms), dealer-side warranty administrators (from memory). Love's said it was not approved for warranty and campaign work before the Daimler deal.
- **Why they lose:** Dealers' territorial franchises and full bays cap capacity; Love's covers truck stops and one OEM; OEMs cannot run service; fleets cannot get paid. No one runs a multi-brand authorization-and-claims rail for non-dealer providers.
- **Moat:** Switching costs (OEM system integration and authorizations), process power (first-pass acceptance rate), and network coverage.
- **Earned secret (U):** A dealer warranty administrator knows that 8-12% of claims get short-paid or charged back and that campaign work is scheduled last because it pays flat time with no upside; OEMs measure campaign completion rate and will pay for speed. Whoever gets clean claims accepted on first pass becomes the preferred vendor.
- **First 90 days:** Hire an ex-dealer warranty administrator and an ex-OEM field-service manager; pitch Volvo/Mack and PACCAR with the Love's precedent; run the 200-truck pilot; build the claim-acceptance dashboard.
- **Legal premise to check:** State dealer-franchise laws on OEMs using non-franchised providers for warranty work, OEM authorization of non-dealers for recall remedies, and parts-return rules (U).
- **Outside-box:** 6. The buyer is the OEM, not the fleet, and the OEM already shows it buys outside the dealer channel.

## 5. Uptime Plan: cost-per-mile maintenance for the fleet the lessors and OEMs ignore (DILEMMA: the lessor, the OEM and the telematics vendor each cannot do it)

- **One-liner:** A fixed price per mile for PM, wear items and covered repairs at any shop in a network, for mixed-make trucks in fleets of 3-50, underwritten from ELD data and repair history and backed by an insurer.
- **Customer:** Owner-operators and fleets of 3-50 trucks running 6-15-year-old, mixed-make equipment. First 10: fleets of 10-30 trucks in one metro, sourced from independent shops and one ELD vendor's partner list.
- **Payer and signer:** The fleet's maintenance budget (R&M plus tires averages 26.5 cents a mile in ATRI 2025; an HDMA study reports about 25 cents for owner-operators, definitions differ). Signer: owner or fleet manager. A fixed price replaces a variance they carry now.
- **Problem:** OEM programs (Volvo Cost Per Mile, Daimler Extended OPTIMUM) are single-brand and route to dealers. Ryder SelectCare and Penske contract maintenance need their own shop density and are priced for larger accounts. Third-party plans (National Truck Protection) cover breakdowns on used trucks (under 10 years, under 300,000 miles on one retailer's page), not PM, and the OEM-sponsored view is that claim approvals add 1.4 days (marketing, U). Telematics vendors will not carry risk.
- **Solution:** Price each truck by age band, duty cycle and ELD fault history; network of independent shops and mobile crews paid in 24 hours (pairs with Idea 1); PM compliance and fault alerts as a condition; insurer or reinsurer holds the risk; a roadside desk. Plans from PM-only to PM plus covered repairs.
- **Why now:** Fleets are holding trucks longer and R&M rose 8.6% in 2025; the EPA July 2026 proposal keeps emissions warranty at 5 years / 100,000 miles, so fleets carry aftertreatment risk after 100,000 miles; tariffs added 20-30% to heavy parts; ELD vendors now expose data and shops are on Fullbay; and the 2021-23 truck cohort (2022 Class 8 retail was 309,615, ACT) has left basic coverage.
- **Wedge:** PM plus roadside plus tires for 6-15-year-old trucks in fleets of 10-30, with a capped repair add-on, at 14 cents a mile.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Average price 17 cents x 75,000 miles = $12,750 a truck-year; the platform keeps 22% (fees, shop spread, float) = $2,805. $100M needs about 35,600 trucks, 0.9% of the 3.8M Class 8 population or about 2% of the roughly 1.7M trucks in small fleets (A). That is 27% of what Penske has under contract maintenance today (133,700). If booked gross as obligor, $100M is only 7,800 trucks. Pay-for-risk needs reinsurance capital and a loss ratio near 78% (A).
- **Who does it today:** Penske (133,700 customer-owned vehicles), Ryder SelectCare and Torque, NationaLease (140+ independent lessors, 900 locations), Volvo Cost Per Mile, Daimler Extended OPTIMUM and other OEM plans, National Truck Protection and similar third parties (3,000+ garages), Love's/Speedco, and ELD vendors that give alerts only. Hidden-incumbent risk is high: a lessor offering a mixed-make, any-shop tier is the obvious counter.
- **Why they lose:** OEMs earn about 24% pretax on parts (PACCAR) and a multi-brand independent-shop plan cannibalizes it; Ryder and Penske profit from shop utilization and cover only where they own bays; NTP covers breakdowns, not maintenance, on one segment; telematics vendors do not carry risk or run shops.
- **Moat:** Counter-positioning against OEM and lessor economics, scale economies in shop rates and parts, and a cornered data set (ELD plus repair history per truck).
- **Earned secret (U):** Maintenance cost per truck is far more predictable than small fleets think once age band and duty cycle are controlled; they pay a no-scale premium (HDMA's 25 cents versus ATRI's 21.5 cents suggests roughly 15%, on different bases) because no one will take the variance.
- **First 90 days:** Hire an actuary who priced contract maintenance; backtest 5,000 truck-years from Fullbay or ELD partners; get an MGA or fronting arrangement and a reinsurer; sign 20 independent shops and a mobile partner; enroll 10 fleets.
- **Legal premise to check:** Classification as a service contract or insurance in each state, and who must hold a reimbursement policy. California's service-contract definition reportedly excludes vehicles of 30,000 pounds GVWR or more (secondary; verify); FleetOwner quotes Mack's warranty manager that few rules govern who can sell extended plans and that some providers have gone bankrupt. Use insurer-backed paper from day one.
- **Outside-box:** 5.

## 6. Public Fleet Bench: fixed-fee maintenance for fleets that cannot pay market wages (DILEMMA: civil-service pay scales and procurement)

- **One-liner:** Sell school districts, transit agencies, counties and refuse haulers a fixed per-vehicle maintenance contract delivered by market-wage crews inside their own garages, because their pay scales guarantee they cannot hire.
- **Customer:** School transportation directors (about 480,000 buses), county and municipal fleet managers, refuse and public-works fleets. First 10: districts of 300-1,500 buses with mechanic vacancies above 30%, for example in North Carolina, Georgia and Texas.
- **Payer and signer:** The district or county operations budget (fleet maintenance and contract services); signer: chief operations officer, fleet director, or the board through a cooperative purchasing contract. Many districts' diesel costs run 6-20% over budget (2026 AASA survey), which squeezes headcount.
- **Problem:** Guilford County Schools is about 14 mechanics short, roughly half strength, with 600 buses; Charlotte-Mecklenburg had 17 vacancies of 47 and 27% of 108 inspected buses had serious defects (2023). Districts cannot raise a single job class without ripple effects and union scales (U). Turnkey transport contractors want the whole route contract, which districts resist for labor-politics reasons (U).
- **Solution:** A fixed annual fee per vehicle for PM, state inspections and repairs, delivered by Level-1/Level-3 crews (Idea 2's model) using the district's bays, parts contracts and a data dashboard. Sell PM and inspection first, then full repair.
- **Why now:** Persistent mechanic vacancies (36% of surveyed districts short of maintenance staff); diesel overruns; retirements (trade commentary says boomers are 40-50% of technicians, retiring by 2030); safety and liability exposure after failed inspections.
- **Wedge:** PM and inspection contracts under the district's competitive-bid threshold, delivered in the district garage.
- **Path to $100M (A):** School bus maintenance is about $5,500 a bus (STN survey), so $100M is about 18,200 buses, 3.8% of 480,000. Adding transit, refuse and public-works vehicles (refuse is the costliest per vehicle) widens it. Roughly 45 districts of 400 buses each. Public pool (A) about $5B a year (school buses $2.6B plus the rest), so $100M is about 2% of the pool or about 8% of an assumed 25% contracted share.
- **Who does it today:** The districts' own garages; turnkey transport operators (First Student, Student Transportation of America, National Express, from memory) that bundle drivers and maintenance; transit operators and contract managers (Transdev, MV, from memory); dealer service departments; Ryder's public-sector fleet business (from memory). Maintenance-only outsourcing is rarely sold; I found no named maintenance-only specialist.
- **Why they lose:** Districts cannot pay market wages or hire seasonally; turnkey operators want the whole route; dealers are capacity-bound; no one sells a maintenance-only fixed fee with an SLA.
- **Moat:** Government switching costs and past performance (procurement incumbency), cooperative contract vehicles, process power.
- **Earned secret (U):** A district fleet director would rather outsource PM and inspections than drivers; the vacancy is permanent, the board cannot pay more for one job, and every failed inspection is a headline.
- **First 90 days:** Hire a retired district fleet director to sell; answer 3 live RFPs; offer a free fleet-condition audit to 10 districts; hire 6 technicians in one county; register on the Sourcewell and NASPO-style vehicles (U; verify).
- **Legal premise to check:** State procurement thresholds, prevailing-wage rules and union no-subcontracting clauses; school-bus inspection regulations by state.
- **Outside-box:** 6.

## 7. Second Source: graded, warrantied used and reman power-train parts plus a core exchange (SHOCK: Section 232 tariffs and the used-truck glut)

- **One-liner:** Become the default 30-60%-below-new answer to the tariff: dismantle donor trucks, buy cores from shops with instant pay, test and grade every engine, transmission, turbo, ECM and injector set to a published standard, and sell with a 12-month warranty.
- **Customer:** Independent shops and small fleets facing 20-30% price increases on heavy components. First 10: 10 shops within 100 miles of the first donor yard.
- **Payer and signer:** The shop or fleet parts budget; signer: shop owner or fleet maintenance manager. This is a price-for-quality swap inside an existing line.
- **Problem:** Reman parts run 30-70% below new and have outperformed the broader parts market (FreightWaves quoting Trucks, Parts, Service 2026); independents lack trusted grading and warranties on used units, OEM reman is dealer-routed with OEM-controlled cores, salvage yards are local and ungraded, and shops sell cores for scrap or sit on them. Distributors reprice preemptively and do not itemize tariffs.
- **Solution:** (1) Buy donor trucks (auctions, failed carriers, total losses) and cores from shops at grade-based prices. (2) Bench or dyno test, grade A/B/C, publish the standard. (3) Reman high-value units. (4) A catalog and API into shop systems. (5) Core exchange with instant credit. Aftertreatment is covered separately in Idea 8.
- **Why now:** 25% Section 232 tariffs from Nov 2025; injectors, turbos, EGR, DPF assemblies and transmissions +20-30% (Decisiv/TMC); steel and aluminum at 50%; the USMCA exemption for parts is conditional and the agreement was not renewed on July 1 2026; recession-stressed small carriers and 2025 new-truck sales down 13.4% (208,155) keep older trucks running and produce donors. Cummins paying $3.7B for Meritor and Dorman paying $338M for Dayton Parts show HD aftermarket assets are being re-rated.
- **Wedge:** Five SKU families: ECMs, injector sets, turbochargers, EGR coolers, transmissions and axles.
- **Path to $100M (A):** If used plus reman is about 8% of the $37B parts pool today ($3.0B) and rises to 12% on the tariff ($4.4B), $100M is 2.3% of that. Operationally, about 83,000 units at $1,200, from about 5,500 donor trucks at about $18k of sellable parts each plus cores bought from shops. Share needed: low single digits; the constraint is core supply and test capacity.
- **Who does it today:** OEM reman programs through dealers (Cummins, Detroit, PACCAR, Volvo/Mack, from memory), Dorman (Dayton Parts), Cummins-Meritor, independent rebuilders, salvage yards such as TLG Trucks (25 acres, 200+ salvage trucks per its site), FleetPride and TruckPro reman lines, HDA Truck Pride members. I did not find a national graded-component standard.
- **Why they lose:** OEMs and dealers earn about 24% pretax on parts (PACCAR) and run closed core loops; distributors push private label and import lines; salvage yards cannot warrant or grade; no one holds both supply (cores and donors) and trust (grade plus warranty).
- **Moat:** Cornered resource (core and donor supply contracts), brand and standard (grading data and warranty experience), scale economies in test equipment.
- **Earned secret (U):** Cores, not demand, are the scarce input; every shop has a core pile out back. A dyno-tested, warrantied used unit is worth about 70% of reman price; the missing thing is a grade the shop trusts.
- **First 90 days:** Lease a yard; buy 20 donor trucks; hire a dyno-capable test lead; publish the grading card; sign 10 shops on core exchange credit.
- **Legal premise to check:** EPA remanufacturing and emission-certification rules for rebuilt engines (40 CFR 1068.120, U), salvage-title and hazardous-waste rules, and warranty reserve accounting.
- **Outside-box:** 6.

## 8. Aftertreatment Exchange: pooled, multi-brand DPF/DOC/SCR swap units (SHOCK: EPA's 2027 package, an aging parc and platinum at $1,793)

- **One-liner:** A national pool of cleaned, flow-tested aftertreatment units shipped to any shop in 4 hours at a flat exchange price, so the truck leaves in hours instead of waiting two days for cleaning.
- **Customer:** Independent shops, mobile mechanics and fleet in-house shops. First 10: 10 shops near Dallas and Atlanta with 20+ aftertreatment events a month.
- **Payer and signer:** The shop or fleet aftertreatment budget; signer: shop owner or maintenance manager. A cleaning costs $350-$600 and a replacement $4,800-$8,200 (vendor sources); the swap sits between them.
- **Problem:** OEM exchange programs (Detroit, 800+ locations; Volvo/Mack reman) are brand-specific and dealer-routed. Independent cleaners work one unit at a time and the truck waits. The EPA's 2027 standard stays at 35 mg and the July 2026 proposal keeps emissions warranty at 5 years / 100,000 miles instead of extending it, so the cost stays on fleets. Platinum, palladium and rhodium prices (Aug 21 2026: $1,793, $1,299, $8,550 an ounce) make failed units valuable and theft attractive.
- **Solution:** Regional hubs with bake, air, aqueous cleaning and flow-bench testing; a rotating inventory per hub; exchange at a flat price with a 12-month warranty; telematics regen data to trigger swaps before failure; PGM recovery on failed units; ash disposal.
- **Why now:** 2027 NOx standard unchanged; July 2026 NPRM leaves warranty at 100,000 miles; 6.6-year Class 8 trade cycles and a 12.8-year average fleet age (KPMG) put more trucks in the aftertreatment failure window; metals prices. Counter-risk: the proposal also drops DEF derates, which may cut forced shop visits.
- **Wedge:** DPF and DOC swaps for the top 20 engine families in two metros.
- **Path to $100M (A):** About 5.3M DPF-equipped Class 6-8 trucks x 0.35 cleanings a year x $450 = $0.83B, plus 4% replacements x $5,500 = $1.17B, about $2.0B total (A). $100M is 5% of that. Pool capital: about 20,000 units in circulation x $1,500 = $30M (A).
- **Who does it today:** OEM exchange and reman programs (Detroit at 800+ locations, Volvo/Mack), Cummins/Fleetguard (not verified), local DPF cleaning shops, mobile cleaners, FleetPride/TruckPro and HDA members selling new and reman units (not verified). No national multi-brand pooled exchange found, but the search was thin.
- **Why they lose:** OEMs sell new aftertreatment at $4.8k-$8.2k and run exchange through dealers; local cleaners have no pooled inventory; distributors sell units, not turnaround time.
- **Moat:** Scale economies in cleaning labs, pooled-asset density (delivery time), core supply.
- **Earned secret (U):** The money is turnaround time, not cleaning: fleets pay thousands to avoid two days of downtime, and a swap takes cleaning off the critical path.
- **First 90 days:** Build one lab; buy 2,000 cores; sign 10 shops; log flow-bench data on every unit; check the EPA rules on aftermarket aftertreatment and CARB approvals.
- **Legal premise to check:** EPA and CARB treatment of remanufactured aftertreatment (CARB Executive Order requirements, U), hazardous-waste rules for ash and catalyst, and tampering risk.
- **Outside-box:** 5.

## Considered and killed (so the tournament does not re-propose them)

- **Parts marketplace or "PartsTech for trucks":** a five-minute idea. PartsTech (225+ suppliers, shop integrations) is light-duty, Fullbay integrates distributors, FleetPride has a 1M-part e-commerce site, HDA Truck Pride and its 750+ locations are a member-owned alternative.
- **Group purchasing for independent shops:** HDA Truck Pride already runs it; share needed for $100M exceeds the independent channel's size.
- **AI diagnostic copilot for technicians / predictive maintenance:** on the cliche list and being bundled by Motive (Aug 2026), Samsara and Fullbay-Pitstop.
- **Free technician tool paid by suppliers (OpenEvidence pattern):** failed the pool-size check. HD supplier marketing aimed at technicians is (A) a few hundred million dollars; $100M would need a third or more of it.
- **Drive-through AI inspection portals:** UVeye launched a Class 6-8 platform in Sept 2025 with an MVT rollout in June 2026 and Amazon as a customer.
- **Heavy-duty collision / insurer repair network:** Sedgwick launched a heavy-equipment direct-repair program in July 2026.
- **CARB Clean Truck Check compliance rails:** about 750,000 vehicles registered and $32 state fee; testing vendor pool is under $100M.
- **Tariff-compliance data layer for parts importers:** crowded general customs software, small HD slice, and 232 duties are not refundable.
- **USMCA-native private-label parts brand:** the exemption is conditional on a Commerce process and the US declined to renew USMCA on July 1 2026.
- **Tire casing bank:** needs more than half of all casings for $100M.
- **Per-diem diesel technician marketplace:** GigSmart and LAUNCH TransTechs exist; needs about 13% of unfilled technician slots.
- **Shop back-office agent (voice/AI service writer):** cliche list and Fullbay bundle risk.
- **Lease-purchase maintenance escrow trustee:** a real FMCSA task-force issue but a pool well under $100M.

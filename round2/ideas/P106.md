# P106 - State Medicaid and SNAP agencies' eligibility and administration spend (round 2 ideas)

Written 2026-10-18. Eight ideas, two per lens. Tags: (S) = sourced in this session, (A) = my arithmetic or assumption, (U) = unverified claim, recollection or insider hypothesis. Search depth: about 25 searches/fetches. Competitor checks were light for ideas 2 and 8 (flagged there).

## Pool facts used

- (S) Rule: CMS interim final rule 91 FR 33348 (published 2026-06-03). Community-engagement (CE) requirement starts 2027-01-01 for expansion adults. States must try ex parte verification first (payroll, claims/encounter data, SNAP/TANF, education, incarceration, federal hub). Self-attestation allowed through 2027-12-31; from 2028-01-01 documentation is required where reasonably available. A failed check triggers a notice and 30 calendar days to show compliance. 26 states sued CMS on 2026-06-29 (case status unchecked).
- (S) Losses: CMS RIA says 2.3M fewer enrollees in FY2027 and 3.1-3.3M later. Manatt models 8.2M average annual losses under the IFR (6.4M under the statute), assuming a 40% loss rate among those subject (New Hampshire experience); 2.8M in FFY2027 rising to 9.2M by FFY2034. (A) Manatt's numbers imply roughly 20M adults subject.
- (S) Vendor response: ten integrators pledged $600M+ of free/discounted services (Jan 2026). Deloitte's NextGen360 Verification Hub free for year one; Maximus tracking tool free for current clients; Conduent AI fraud tool free. CMS dropped its Emmy API into the Federal Data Services Hub (only school enrollment via National Student Clearinghouse and disabled-veteran status). Pennsylvania, North Dakota, Vermont, Missouri, South Dakota, New York and Illinois say they will not use Emmy. States estimated $3-10M each to build.
- (S) Incumbent timing: Maximus U.S. Services ran $416-418M a quarter in FY2026 (down year on year). It calls FY2027 a "building year": semiannual redeterminations start July 2027, full run rate FY2028; state talks slower than expected.
- (S) Georgia Pathways (the only live precedent): GAO (Sep 2025) found $54.2M administrative spend vs $26.1M health care since 2021, $45.1M of it to contractors. 42% of interested applicants never filed a report showing any qualifying hours; 19% of complete applications were denied for under 80 hours or weak verification (CBPP).
- (S) Medical frailty: states must check 12 months of claims/encounter data first; a diagnosis alone is not enough (must also impair ability to comply); code lists must be auditable and regularly revised; provider confirmation allowed; reverify at least every 12 months; self-attestation only once per enrollment period from 2028. Code for America (via Nextgov) calls frailty the largest exemption category and says no good free data source exists. KFF notes New Hampshire providers resisted completing exemption forms. Connecticut's early estimate: 12% likely exempt on medical frailty, 8% on SSI/disability.
- (S) Managed care: §438.58 conflict-of-interest rules bar MCOs and entities with financial ties to MCOs from determining compliance; plans may play "a supporting administrative role" (Mintz). 71% of unwinding disenrollments were procedural (Unite Us/Fortuna release). Medicaid managed care enrollment 60.4M in March 2026 (-3.1M year on year, HMA). Big Five plans 34.0M at 2026-06-30, about half of all Medicaid enrollees (Georgetown CCF). Shares in 34 states: Centene 17.9%, Elevance 10.6%, UnitedHealth 8.4%, Molina 6.0% (HMA). Elevance said members subject to CE are about 20% of its Medicaid business; Centene is exiting Arkansas expansion in 2027. Post-unwinding acuity shift cut Centene's 9-month 2024 Medicaid medical margin 27%.
- (S) Income data: Equifax said it expected about $800M of government revenue in 2025 (Q1 2025 call); The Work Number holds 209M active records; about half of states use it. Senators (Feb 2026) cite per-query hikes over four years of 126% (CO), 120% (KS), 95% (NC), 69% (CT) and allege exclusive multi-year deals with payroll providers. Private antitrust suit Greystone v. Equifax (E.D. Pa., 2:24-cv-02260): motion to dismiss denied Feb 2025; plaintiffs allege exclusive control of 40%+ of payroll records through ADP, Intuit, Walmart, Home Depot; the court ordered some VOIE terms rewritten.
- (S) SNAP: FY2025 national payment error rate 10.62% ($10.1B improper); 41 states + DC above 6%. From FY2028 states pay 5% (6-8%), 10% (8-10%), 15% (10%+) of benefit costs; FY2028 may use the FY2025 or FY2026 rate, then the third preceding year. States at 13.34%+ get a delay (AK, DE, GA, IL, NM, OR, DC); FL, MD, MA, NJ, NY lost delay eligibility. CBPP/FFIS: about $9.0-9.4B a year at FY2025 rates (CA $1.9B, FL ~$0.9B, KS ~$60M at 10%+). Senate S.5526 (not law) would delay a year. Error dollars by element: wages 34% (NM, half year) to 59% (FL); shelter 14-28%; household composition 13-17%. USDA OIG found some third-party consultants "eliminated errors" with unwarranted improvements; USDA is reviewing consultant contracts; PCG took 15% of Wisconsin's $2.86M bonus and a DOJ probe followed. (S pool file) SNAP federal admin share fell 50% to 25% on 2026-10-01 (about $16.9B shift over FY2027-31).
- (S) Other: retroactive Medicaid coverage falls from 3 months to 1 (expansion adults) and 2 (others, including nursing home) for applications from 2027-01-01. §71103 requires a CMS-approved address-verification process by 2027-01-01 (USPS returns, MCOs, NCOA); HHS duplicate-enrollment system by 2029-10-01; §71104 quarterly Death Master File checks from 2028. Medicaid eligibility error above 3% already means federal recoupment; H.R.1 narrows good-faith waivers (about 2030); Maine estimates $65M at a 5% rate. Community service per 42 CFR 435.552(b): unpaid, structured, for public/nonprofit benefit; need not be voluntary; court-ordered counts. SNAP E&T 50/50 spending was about $550M in 2022.
- (S) Startups already here: Fortuna Health (a16z; $18-22M reported, figures conflict; Unite Us partnership Nov 2025 incl. CE-hour verification), Reema Health ($7.6M), Develop Health, Argyle, Propel (company says 5.5M monthly users, 1M+ work-requirement screener uses, now selling to states), Better Impact and Helper Helper (verified community-service hours for state Medicaid).
- (A) Incremental recurring Medicaid admin from H.R.1 is roughly $1.5-2B a year (about 20M expansion adults x one extra renewal x ~$50 + CE verification + outreach + frailty). One-time system spend is small ($3-10M per state).

## 1. Second Look: independent per-case SNAP accuracy layer (INSIDER: SNAP quality-control reviewer, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** Recompute every SNAP certification before benefits issue, sample the caseload the way a federal re-reviewer does, and give the state an evidence trail FNS and the OIG will accept, priced per case with a refund guarantee.
- **Customer:** State SNAP directors and human-services CFOs in states between the 6% and ~13% tiers, starting with the five that lost delay eligibility (FL, MD, MA, NJ, NY), plus KS, VA, LA and large county operators in county-administered states. First 10: those five states, three large counties, two mid-size states.
- **Payer and signer:** State human-services commissioner with the budget director. Budget lines: SNAP payment-accuracy/QC unit plus the new state-only benefit cost share from FY2028 (state general fund, so federal cost principles do not bind). SNAP director signs; CFO moves the money. Price about $25-35 per case-year, fixed, with refund if audited correction rate misses target. Never contingent on the official QC rate.
- **Problem:** A state's liability steps 0/5/10/15% on a sampled statistic (S). A state at a true 9.4% can land in the 15% tier by luck. Wages are the largest error element (34-59% of error dollars in two states' reports, S). States cannot prove an improvement is real: OIG found consultants who "eliminated errors" with unwarranted gains and USDA is reviewing those contracts (S).
- **Solution:** (1) Pre-issuance recompute on every certification, recertification and interim report, from the case file plus third-party wage, shelter and household data. (2) Shadow QC: replicate the FNS re-review protocol on a sample 10-50x the state's own, with confidence intervals, so the state knows which side of 6/8/10 it is on in real time. (3) Immutable evidence ledger of what was known when and what was corrected, designed with an FNS regional office. (4) Worker-level coaching analytics. Later: Medicaid eligibility (PERM) audit-readiness, child-care and UI integrity.
- **Why now:** FY2027's sample year began 2026-10-01 and (A, third-preceding-year rule) sets the FY2030 share; FY2026 closed 2026-09-30 and FY2028 can use FY2025 or FY2026 (S). Five states lost their delay (S). If S.5526 passes, the first cost-share year uses the lower of FY2026/FY2027 (S, not law), which raises the value of FY2027. State SNAP admin cost rose to 75% state-funded on 2026-10-01 (S).
- **Wedge:** Wage-income recompute on earned-income cases in two or three states; free shadow QC on 400 closed cases for each of the five lost-delay states; one paid county pilot ($250-400k).
- **Path to $100M:** (U) about 22M SNAP households x $30 = ~$660M pool (A); $100M is 3.3M household-years, 15% of the pool. Cross-check on exposure: $9.0-9.4B a year x a 4-7% price-to-exposure ratio = $360-660M, so 15-28% share. Three to five big states do it (CA's $1.9B exposure alone supports $76-133M at 4-7%). Heroic only if states will pay under 4% of exposure.
- **Who does it today:** Deloitte (SNAP payment accuracy, 25+ states, self-reported reductions up to 35%), Public Consulting Group (SNAP accuracy improvement; the Wisconsin contingency deal), Human Services Group (Kansas, since Oct 2025), Code for America, state QC units, FNS/APHSA assistance. No per-case independent assurance product found (light check).
- **Why they lose:** Deloitte and PCG sell hours, often build the systems they would audit, and cannot take a contingency without recreating the Wisconsin problem. Self-reported improvement is no longer bankable after the OIG finding. Independence plus a ledger plus a refund guarantee is something they cannot copy without cannibalising consulting.
- **Moat:** Counter-positioning (independent, fixed price) + cornered resource (labelled corpus of FNS re-review findings across states) + process power.
- **Earned secret:** QC's "truth" is only what a federal reviewer can reproduce from third-party evidence for the sample month, so most error dollars are discoverable at certification time with the same sources. States do not query every case because per-hit income-data prices keep rising (S, idea 5). Liability is a cliff on a noisy sample, so measuring the true rate is worth as much as lowering it. The legal fee structure is per-case, never a share of the measured rate.
- **First 90 days:** Pull FNS FY2025 state tables and public state QC reports; build an error-element model for three states; email the five lost-delay SNAP directors a free shadow-QC offer; have an FNS regional office review the ledger design; close one county pilot; get a design-partner state to sign a letter of intent.

## 2. Medicaid-Pending Bridge for nursing homes (INSIDER: county long-term-care eligibility supervisor, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** File every Medicaid long-term-care application in the admission month, assemble the five-year evidence file in parallel, and optionally advance cash against the pending receivable, so operators stop carrying Medicaid-pending residents as free loans.
- **Customer:** Revenue-cycle leaders at nursing-home and assisted-living operators with high Medicaid mix (chains and large regionals). First 10: operators with 20-100 facilities in slow-processing states.
- **Payer and signer:** Operator CFO or VP revenue cycle. Budget: business-office labor, outsourced eligibility contingency fees, bad-debt write-offs. Fee: $750-1,250 per filed-and-approved application (A) plus a financing spread.
- **Problem:** From 2027-01-01 nursing-home applicants get at most two months of retroactive coverage (S). Coverage still starts in the application month, so the exposure is filing delay. Families need five years of bank records and weeks to assemble them (S). Medicaid pays for over 60% of residents and LeadingAge warns providers may limit admission of Medicaid-pending residents (S).
- **Solution:** Day-one skeleton filing; consented bank connections and document capture; automated 60-month transfer screen with penalty-period modelling and a cure playbook (return of funds, exempt transfers) with partner elder-law attorneys; state-specific packet generator; up to 80% advance against expected Medicaid receivables priced on approval probability.
- **Why now:** The retro cut starts 2027-01-01 (S); operators will tighten admissions of pending residents; the process gap becomes a cash gap.
- **Wedge:** Ten facilities of one chain in two states; filing-in-month plus evidence packet only; financing after 90 days of approval data.
- **Path to $100M:** (U) about 1.2M nursing-home residents; (A) 600-800k new Medicaid LTC applications a year (nursing facility plus home-and-community waivers). At $750-1,250 the pool is $450M-1B; $100M is 100-130k applications, about 15%. Financing alternative: $0.6-0.8B average advances at 12-15% = $75-100M. All volume numbers here are unverified and must be checked first.
- **Who does it today:** Regional eligibility specialists and elder-law firms (U), chains' in-house business offices (U), hospital-oriented eligibility vendors extending into LTC (trade press names Change Healthcare, now Optum; old), bank-statement analytics such as Ocrolus, which started on Medicaid eligibility in 2018 (pool file). Competitor check was one search.
- **Why they lose:** Contingency vendors are paid on approval and work in batches; none sells filing-in-month certainty plus bridge financing; in-house offices are labour-bound; attorneys price at a family level.
- **Moat:** Process power in state-specific rules + a cost-of-capital edge + switching costs once embedded in admissions workflow.
- **Earned secret (U):** The retro cut does not move the coverage start date. It makes the application month the only variable that matters, so the process to optimise is time-to-filing, not time-to-approval. Denials and long pends come mostly from unexplained transfers in the look-back, not income; a transfer narrative prepared before the caseworker's first request is what shortens the process. The real asset is a state-by-state map of what triggers a pend letter.
- **First 90 days:** Interview 15 LTC eligibility supervisors and business-office directors; build five states' checklists; run 50 live filings with a design-partner chain; line up one elder-law partner; test price.

## 3. Continuity Manager: PMPM carve-out for the top Medicaid plans (PATTERN: concentrated operators + NEMT/dental-style carve-out)

- **One-liner:** A coverage-continuity carve-out that plans buy per member per month, the way they buy transportation and dental managers, covering outreach, evidence assembly, cure tracking and reinstatement for expansion adults, with a firewall from eligibility determination.
- **Customer:** COO, chief growth or chief medical officers at Centene, Elevance, UnitedHealthcare, Molina and CVS/Aetna, which hold about half of managed Medicaid (S). First 10: about ten state markets across three plans.
- **Payer and signer:** Plan COO/CFO. Budget: administrative load already in capitation rates and member-engagement/retention. Price $1.25-2.50 PMPM on subject expansion adults (about $30 per member-year, 0.4% of a $7k capitation, A).
- **Problem:** Loss is mostly procedural (71%, S). Manatt: 8.2M average annual losses (S). Plans may only "support" (S, §438.58). A plan funding retention captures only part of the gain because retained members can re-enrol elsewhere after a lapse, so every plan under-invests (A).
- **Solution:** A delegated-entity service, structurally independent of plan affiliates: ingest plan data (claims, pharmacy, ADT, care-management contacts, address changes) as supporting evidence under member consent; contact members on plan-permitted channels; pre-fill and submit as authorised representative where the state allows; run the 30-day cure clock; manage reinstatement within the reconsideration window (U, 90 days, my recollection of 42 CFR 435.916) and hearings; report outcomes only. No determinations. Later a cross-plan statewide utility funded pro rata.
- **Why now:** Start 2027-01-01; semiannual renewals from July 2027 (S); managed Medicaid already shrinking 3.1M a year (S); acuity-driven margin squeeze (S); Elevance and Centene statements (S). Why it has not happened: plans had no forcing event, unwinding outreach was state-led, and the plan-state firewall needs legal design.
- **Wedge:** One plan in one state with an early requirement (Georgia) plus one plan running six-month-renewal pilots elsewhere; sell the pilot on cost per retained member.
- **Path to $100M:** (A) about 16M expansion adults in managed care (20M x 80%) x $30 = $480M pool; $100M is 21%, which is heroic but alternatively 0.2-0.6% of the $16-57B of capitation revenue at risk (CMS vs Manatt, x $7k). Five buyers means five enterprise sales.
- **Who does it today:** Fortuna Health (a16z, Unite Us partner, sells to plans and states), Unite Us, Reema Health, plans' in-house engagement teams, Maximus/Conduent outreach to states, Propel on the SNAP side. Nobody found offering a PMPM-priced accountable carve-out.
- **Why they lose:** Fortuna sells software and engagement, not a risk-bearing PMPM service; Maximus and Conduent are paid by states and cannot sit on the plan side; plans cannot coordinate with each other or with the state without a neutral party.
- **Moat:** Switching costs (embedded in plan data flows) + scale economies (50-state compliance map) + network effects (cross-plan utility).
- **Earned secret:** The plan's best asset, member-level claims and contact data, is exactly the evidence the IFR names as ex parte sources, but plans may not determine anything. The winning design ships evidence packets under consent and leaves determination to the state. Retention carve-outs get bought like NEMT, not like software.
- **First 90 days:** Pitch the three largest plans; legal memo on §438.58, marketing and inducement rules, and authorised-representative rules in five states; sign one design-partner plan with a PMPM pilot on 20,000 lives.

## 4. Hours Marketplace: verified shifts for the 80 hours (PATTERN: instant-pay spot-labor marketplace, Timee-style)

- **One-liner:** A marketplace of 2-4 hour community-service shifts at pre-vetted public and nonprofit hosts, with instant verified hours delivered to state and plan systems, funded by the plans and programs that lose money when an enrollee fails.
- **Customer:** Hosts (parks departments, school districts, food banks, hospital auxiliaries, faith networks) and enrollees who cannot reach 80 hours of work; buyers are plan community-engagement leads and state SNAP E&T directors. First 10 hosts: anchor food banks and cities in two states; first payers: two plans.
- **Payer and signer:** Plan member-engagement/value-added-services budget (state approval needed) and state SNAP E&T programs (50% federal reimbursement of non-federal spend, S, about $550M nationwide in 2022). Signer: plan COO or state E&T director.
- **Problem:** Community service is a statutory path and the rule is broad (S, 42 CFR 435.552(b)). There is no registry of volunteer hours. In Georgia 42% of interested applicants never filed a report with any qualifying hours (S). Existing tools verify hours but nobody supplies capacity.
- **Solution:** Hosts post shifts; members book by text; geo/QR check-in and check-out; hours post to a ledger with host attestations and go to the state or plan by API; no-show handling, host liability cover, referral to job training and E&T.
- **Why now:** CE starts 2027-01-01 and self-attestation ends 2028-01-01 (S). Why not yet: no forced demand existed; volunteer platforms serve volunteers, not compliance; Georgia's programme was too small.
- **Wedge:** One metro, one plan: 50 hosts, 5,000 member-months a month; expand to SNAP-overlap members for E&T reimbursement.
- **Path to $100M:** (A) 20M subject adults; 12-15% need non-job hours = 2.4-3.0M; serve 0.3-0.45M member-years at $300-350 = $90-160M, i.e. 10-15% of that need. Capacity check: 0.3M x 80 hrs x 12 is about 290M hours a year against (U) roughly 5B hours of formal volunteering in the US, about 6%. Host supervision is the weak link: it must be in-kind, because paid supervision costs more than the fee.
- **Who does it today:** Better Impact (verified hours for state Medicaid), Helper Helper, volunteer platforms such as Galaxy Digital and VolunteerMatch (U), Maximus tracking tool (free to clients), Deloitte hub, Fortuna + Unite Us (hour verification), E&T providers.
- **Why they lose:** Verification vendors sell attestation, not supply; volunteer platforms have no state-verified record; state-owned trackers cannot run a marketplace.
- **Moat:** Network economies (hosts x members) + cornered resource (anchor host contracts).
- **Earned secret:** The scarce thing is host capacity, not software. Friction is in the first hour, as Georgia shows. Public hosts count under the rule, and court-ordered hours count, so municipal anchors are available. Legal check required before building: wage-and-hour and workers' compensation treatment of compelled service (U; workfare participants have been treated as employees in other programmes).
- **First 90 days:** Map anchor hosts in two states; talk to three food-bank networks; legal memo on FLSA and workers' comp; one plan funds 1,000 slots; ship a text-based MVP.

## 5. Open Verification Exchange (DILEMMA: Equifax cannot discount, pay suppliers more, or reach gig work)

- **One-liner:** A cheapest-first router that sends each state verification to free and low-cost sources before The Work Number, backed by a payroll-data exchange that pays payroll providers a real revenue share.
- **Customer:** State Medicaid, SNAP, TANF and child-care agencies that have already suffered Work Number price hikes (NC, KS, CO, CT, SD) and states shopping contractors (Missouri uses SteadyIQ, Equifax and ID.me).
- **Payer and signer:** State Medicaid director or human-services CIO/procurement chief. Budget: eligibility verification line (50% federal admin match; systems 75-90% where built into eligibility systems, U). Today this is paid per transaction to Equifax.
- **Problem:** Equifax's government revenue was guided to about $800M in 2025 and grew low double digits; per-query hikes of 69-126% in four years; exclusive supply deals now in antitrust discovery (S). The IFR forces ex parte checks first, so volume rises.
- **Solution:** Phase 1 a savings-share router: query NDNH, state UI wages and the CMS Hub first, then consented payroll and gig connections, then The Work Number last; the state pays a share of savings. Phase 2 a non-exclusive payroll exchange: 30-40% of verification revenue to payroll vendors and employers, with consented gig/1099 connections (Argyle, Truv, Pinwheel as suppliers, not rivals). Same API for SNAP error prevention (idea 1) and renewals (idea 6).
- **Why now:** Court denied dismissal (Feb 2025) and ordered some terms rewritten; Senate letter Feb 2026; Medicaid verification volume jumps in 2027 (S).
- **Wedge:** Router sold to three states on shared savings; first 10 customers: states named in the senators' letter.
- **Path to $100M:** At a 35% price discount, $100M of revenue equals $154M of Equifax-equivalent volume, about 19% of an $800M government book (A). Waterfall mechanics mean the exchange need not cover everyone to be primary: it needs about 60% hit rate to cut Work Number queries by more than half (A). Adjacent programmes (SNAP, UI, housing, student-loan income-driven repayment) lift the ceiling.
- **Who does it today:** Equifax Workforce Solutions; Experian and TransUnion employer-verification products (U); Argyle, Truv, Pinwheel, Atomic (consented payroll); ID.me; SteadyIQ; Deloitte's free year-one hub; CMS Hub (student, veteran); free public sources NDNH and state UI wages (U on access terms).
- **Why they lose:** Equifax cannot pay suppliers more without repricing its whole book (mortgage verification is its profit pool, U), cannot discount to states without inviting price-discrimination claims in a live antitrust case (A), and its data come from payroll contracts, so it cannot reach gig and self-employed work. Deloitte's hub is free for one year then a lock-in.
- **Moat:** Network economies (two-sided data) + scale economies (connector fixed costs) + counter-positioning (revenue share).
- **Earned secret:** Equifax's moat is contractual, not technical, and it is under challenge; and a router does not need coverage parity to win, only to answer the cheap-first fraction.
- **First 90 days:** Request state price histories (public records) from five states; build the router with free sources only; sign one state on shared savings; meet five payroll providers not named in the complaint (status unchecked).

## 6. Renewal Autopilot (DILEMMA: integrators and BPOs earn more when volume of touches rises)

- **One-liner:** Run the new semiannual Medicaid renewal and the SNAP recertification as a managed service priced per case completed without a caseworker touch, so the vendor earns more only when the state's touch volume falls.
- **Customer:** State Medicaid and SNAP operations chiefs and county human-services directors; first 10: states with six-month renewals and ex parte rates under about 60% (U).
- **Payer and signer:** State Medicaid director / COO with the CFO. Budget: eligibility operations and vendor contracts (50% federal admin match). Staff still make every decision.
- **Problem:** Expansion adults must renew every six months from 2027, ex parte first (S). Maximus's own guidance ties growth to redetermination volume starting July 2027 (S), so its incentive points the other way.
- **Solution:** Assemble data from every permissible source before the renewal date; send determination-support packets to state staff for approval; contact members only for the residual; track cures; pay per auto-completed renewal plus shared savings against an agreed baseline.
- **Why now:** July 2027 redetermination start; 75%-state-funded SNAP admin from 2026-10-01 (S).
- **Wedge:** One state's renewal queue for SNAP plus Medicaid; pre-populated renewals first.
- **Path to $100M:** (A) 40M Medicaid renewals + ~15M SNAP recertifications = 55M a year; manual cost per case $30-100 vs ~$5 automated, so a ~$55 saving. At $12 per auto-completed case, $100M is 8.3M cases: 30% of national volume at 50% auto-completion, or 50% of volume if paid only on lift above the state baseline. Weakest path with idea 8.
- **Who does it today:** Maximus, Conduent (work-requirements suite), GDIT, Deloitte hub, Gainwell, Accenture, state staff.
- **Why they lose:** Their revenue grows with volume and staff-hours; a vendor that cuts touches shrinks their line. Their free-year pledges are land-grabs, not outcome contracts.
- **Moat:** Counter-positioning + process power.
- **Earned secret:** The incumbent's growth story is the number of redeterminations (S), so no incumbent will take an outcome contract on reducing them.
- **First 90 days:** Collect ex parte rate and cost-per-renewal data from five states; build one pre-population pipeline; sign a pilot priced against a measured baseline.

## 7. Exemption Evidence Utility, starting with medical frailty (SHOCK: the IFR's frailty rules create a standards-and-audit workflow)

- **One-liner:** The audited source for exemption evidence: version-controlled medical-frailty code sets, claims-based acuity scoring on the 12-month lookback, and a paid clinician channel for cases data cannot resolve.
- **Customer:** State Medicaid directors, MMIS and analytics leads; plan compliance officers. First 10: six large-exposure states and four plans.
- **Payer and signer:** State Medicaid agency (admin match 50%, systems up to 75-90%, U) co-funded by plans; signer is the state Medicaid director with CMS advance-planning approval; plans' chief compliance officer.
- **Problem:** Frailty needs a diagnosis plus functional impairment; code lists must be auditable; claims older than 12 months do not count; reverify yearly; New Hampshire providers refused unpaid forms; no good free source exists (S). Manatt says the narrow frailty rules drive extra coverage loss (S).
- **Solution:** (1) Versioned code-set library with a clinical advisory panel; (2) acuity engine run inside the state's warehouse (claims, encounters, pharmacy, DME); (3) an explanation record per exemption for audit; (4) a clinician channel: short standard form plus telehealth functional assessment, paid per completion; (5) reverification scheduler. Extend to caregiver, student, treatment-participation and veteran exemptions.
- **Why now:** Start 2027-01-01; documentation tightening 2028; Emmy and the Hub do not cover frailty (S).
- **Wedge:** Code set plus scoring in three states already running claims analytics; clinician channel in the same states; one co-funding plan.
- **Path to $100M:** (A) 12% of 20M = 2.4M frailty claimants (CT, S, for the share); 40-60% unresolved by claims = 1.0-1.4M x $45 per packet = $45-63M; licences $0.8-1.2M x 40 states = $32-48M; total $77-111M with near-total adoption, or about 60% of the frailty workflow. Adding the other exemption categories (roughly 35-45% of subject adults, A) roughly doubles volume and brings it to about 25-30% share.
- **Who does it today:** State analytics teams, MMIS vendors (Gainwell, Conduent), Optum, risk-grouper vendors (U), CHCS guidance; no audited frailty product found (light check).
- **Why they lose:** Integrators bundle "claims match" as a feature but do not carry clinical governance; risk-grouper vendors skip the functional-impairment step; plans cannot determine.
- **Moat:** Standard (audited code sets) + cornered resource (clinician network) + switching costs.
- **Earned secret:** Frailty is an audit problem, not a matching problem. CMS says lists must be auditable and weakly supported determinations can cost the state; the winner sells defensibility, and the cheapest way to resolve the residual is to pay a clinician for ten minutes.
- **First 90 days:** Recruit a five-clinician advisory panel; build a v1 code set mapped to the five statutory categories; test on public claims files; interview six state policy directors; draft advance-planning language.

## 8. Rules Twin: versioned rules and a caseload digital twin (SHOCK: rule volatility becomes a permanent data layer)

- **One-liner:** A machine-readable record of every Medicaid and SNAP rule, waiver and exemption by state and date, plus a twin that replays each state's de-identified caseload through a proposed rule to forecast churn and error before go-live.
- **Customer:** State Medicaid and SNAP policy and IT directors; plan strategy and finance teams; navigator networks; integrators as OEM. First 10: four states, three plans, two networks/integrators, one OEM.
- **Payer and signer:** State advance-planning budgets (testing and independent verification lines, U); plan actuarial/strategy; signer the state Medicaid IT director with CMS approval.
- **Problem:** The IFR rewrote dozens of rules, 26 states are suing, the SNAP cost-share start may move, and waivers vary. Eligibility logic sits in proprietary rule engines changed by change order (U). Nobody can tell a legislature or court how many people a rule version affects.
- **Solution:** Rules-as-code library with citations; digital twin with synthetic and de-identified households; regression tests against the production system; drift monitoring; auditable code-set registry (links to idea 7).
- **Why now:** IFR amendments, litigation and the pending SNAP delay bill (S); auditable-list requirement (S).
- **Wedge:** Forecast and regression test for one state's CE release; sell the same forecast to its plans.
- **Path to $100M:** (A) 43 Medicaid states x $1.2M + 50 SNAP/TANF programs x $0.5M + 25 plans/systems x $0.6M + integrator OEM $15M = about $107M, which needs ~70% of states. Weakest path; kill if four states will not share de-identified caseloads.
- **Who does it today (light check):** Nava and PolicyEngine (nonprofit/open source, U), integrators' proprietary engines (Merative/Curam, Deloitte), Salesforce public-sector (U), forecasting consultancies (Manatt, Milliman, Mercer).
- **Why they lose:** Integrators earn on change orders and do not simulate against real caseloads; nonprofits lack procurement staying power.
- **Moat:** Process power + cornered resource (cross-state rule library) + switching costs.
- **Earned secret (U):** Integrators are paid to deliver to specification, not to ask what a specification does to a real caseload; the question the legislature and CMS now ask is exactly the twin's output.
- **First 90 days:** Encode the CE rule as code with citations; run a synthetic-household test on one state's published rules; offer plans a free state-by-state forecast; line up one state data-sharing agreement.

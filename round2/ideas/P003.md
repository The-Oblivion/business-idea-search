## Pool facts used across ideas

- Repairs and maintenance by private registered providers: GBP10B in the year to 31 Mar 2025, of which GBP3.9B capitalised, up 13%. Providers forecast GBP10.9B a year over five years. The 19 largest providers hold 42% of units; 200 groups with 1,000+ homes hold over 96% of stock. [V: gov.uk RSH 2025 global accounts]
- Council HRA repairs about GBP2.5B a year (2024/25 budget, 2022/23 prices). [V-weak: CIH-hosted MHCLG table, not confirmed]
- About 4.4M social homes in England. [M]
- Awaab's Law phase 1 live 27 Oct 2025. Phase 2 goes live 30 Nov 2026 and adds excess cold, excess heat, falls (level, stairs, between levels), structural collapse, fire and explosion (including boiler failure), electrical hazards and domestic hygiene (pests). Same clocks as phase 1: emergency hazards 24h; significant hazards investigated within 10 working days, written summary within 3 working days, safety works within 5 working days, supplementary works started within 12 weeks. Alternative accommodation at the landlord's expense if the home cannot be made safe. Tenants can sue for breach of the implied term (s10A Landlord and Tenant Act 1985): damages and specific performance in the County Court. Phase 3 (2027) takes in the remaining HHSRS hazards except overcrowding. [V: Clyde & Co Aug 2026, JMW]
- Awareness trigger: the clock runs from the landlord becoming aware, including through its own inspection, staff or contractors, not only a tenant report. [V-secondary: law-firm commentary on MHCLG guidance]
- Lambeth's first 3 weeks: 347 cases (34 potential emergency, 313 potential significant), about 94% in time, on a stock of roughly 33,000 homes. Lambeth disrepair legal costs plus damages GBP15M in 2024/25 (about GBP600 per tenant). [V: Brixton Buzz Jan 2026; Plentific for stock size]
- Social landlords now owe five-yearly electrical inspections. For existing tenancies the duty runs from 1 May 2026, with inspections and landlord-supplied equipment checks due by 1 Nov 2026 per one secondary source. [V-secondary: SI 2025/1043, gov.uk guidance]
- MEES: EPC C by 1 Apr 2030 for social homes, government impact assessment over GBP16B, GBP10k per-property spend exemption. New Decent Homes Standard from 2035. Warm Homes Plan GBP15B public total, of which GBP4.4B grant plus GBP0.6B finance via the Social Housing Fund. [V: Inside Housing, Marley, Talan]
- Voids: about 300,000 social homes void in 2023-24; over GBP1B spent on repairs, GBP220M on re-letting, GBP1.6B all-in with lost rent. Average re-let 74 days (about 50 for standard voids) against under 30 five years earlier. [V-secondary: HouseMark figures quoted by a supplier CEO]
- Temporary accommodation: councils spent GBP2.84B in 2024/25 (+25%), over GBP1B of it on nightly-paid self-contained; 134,210 households. [V: Shelter/LGA]
- Software price points are tiny: Mobysoft RepairSense is GBP109,800 a year to Flagship Group; Switchee has raised GBP11.5M in total (2023, 2024). Software alone cannot reach $100M in this pool. [V]
- Incumbent contractor scale: Mears FY25 revenue GBP1,135.5M, maintenance-led GBP620.4M, adjusted operating margin 5.7%, order book GBP4.0B, 100% retention on 2025 maturities. [V]

Currency: GBP. $100M is about GBP77M at 1.30.

## INSIDER 1. One Knock: the annual home check that replaces six statutory visits and fixes what it finds

**One-liner:** A two-ticket technician team does the gas check, electrical test, alarm test, a 29-hazard screen and small fixes in one 75-minute visit a year, so the landlord pays for one access attempt and starts no legal clock it cannot meet.

**Insider:** Head of compliance / asset safety at a 40,000-home landlord, 15+ years.

**Earned secret:** The cost of compliance is the knock on the door, not the engineering. A gas service is about 40 minutes of work behind a door that does not always open on the first try; a failed visit costs GBP25-100 (Gateshead estimate via a vendor blog [V-weak]); and six separately tendered contractors each knock, fail and chase. Since Awaab's Law each knock is also an inspection: whatever a contractor sees counts as the landlord becoming aware, and the 5-day safety-works clock starts. Landlords are paying six visitors to find hazards they have not budgeted to fix in time.

**Customer:** Mid-size and large social landlords (housing associations, ALMOs, councils) with 10k-60k homes that tender gas, electrical, alarms, stock-condition and fire-door work as separate single-service lots.

**Payer and signer:** Director of Assets / Compliance (or COO). Budget lines: gas servicing, electrical testing, alarms, stock-condition surveys and cyclical fire-safety checks, normally 4-6 separate contracts. The CEO and board sign because the executive team owns the liability under the safety standard and Awaab's Law.

**Problem:** Statutory visits are bought as separate lots, each with its own no-access rate and paperwork. Mears itself calls UK housing compliance "largely single service led" and "fragmented" [V]. From 30 Nov 2026 phase 2 adds electrical, fire and explosion (boilers), falls, excess cold and heat, structural and hygiene hazards to the same clocks, and social landlords now owe five-yearly electrical inspections.

**Solution:** A crew of two: a technician with Gas Safe and electrical-inspection tickets, plus a hazard screener with a fix kit. One visit produces the gas safety record, the EICR when due, alarm tests and replacements, a photo-and-sensor screen against the 29 HHSRS hazards (a humidity and temperature logger is left for 14 days), a stock-condition and asbestos-register refresh, and on-the-spot fixes under about GBP150 (handrail, extractor fan, door undercut, smoke alarm, tap). Anything bigger goes to the landlord with evidence attached and the clock already started. One home record feeds the landlord's housing system. One access process: tenant-chosen windows, evening and weekend slots, and a single forced-entry route. Gas checks can be done up to two months before the anniversary without losing the due date [M], which gives scheduling room.

**Why now:** Phase 2 on 30 Nov 2026; the awareness trigger; the new electrical duty with a 1 Nov 2026 deadline for existing tenancies; the 2035 Decent Homes Standard being condition-based; compliance contracts that come up for renewal in 2027-29.

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** Start as a subcontractor to a landlord's existing gas contractor for 1,500 homes: gas plus alarm test plus hazard screen. Customers 2-10 are mid-size housing associations and ALMOs (8-25k homes) whose gas and electrical lots expire in 2027-28, the size the primes pursue least aggressively. [A]

**Path to $100M:** Visit-based compliance pool [A, no compliance-only tender price found in searches]: gas 3.0M homes x GBP85 = GBP255M; electrical 4.4M / 5 x GBP200 = GBP176M; alarms GBP40M; stock condition 20% a year x 4.4M x GBP70 = GBP62M; other cyclical (fire doors, FRA, legionella, asbestos re-inspection) GBP100M. Total about GBP633M. Add on-the-spot fixes at 30% of homes x GBP120 = GBP158M. Pool about GBP790M. Revenue per home-year about GBP180 (check GBP144, fixes GBP36). GBP77M needs about 430,000 homes a year, roughly 10% of stock, or about 19 landlords of 22,000 homes. About 10% of the pool; no heroic share. Reachable payers: about 200 large groups plus about 150 stock-holding councils.

**Who does it today:** Mears (Pennington Choices bought Sep 2025 for GBP9.5M, GBP17M revenue; manages about 450,000 homes) [V]; Sureserve (3,700 staff) [V]; Aspect, Gas Call, Equans and in-house teams [M]; partial linking by individual landlords (Darlington tests alarms with the gas service; North Northamptonshire tests alarms at the EICR) [V]; sensors from Switchee and Aico [V]. Four searches found no landlord running a single multi-skilled annual visit for gas, electrical, alarms and stock condition.

**Why they lose:** The primes sell compliance as per-visit, single-service lots. A combined visit cuts their visit revenue by roughly a quarter to a third [A] and needs dual-ticket staff they do not employ. Landlords split gas, electrical, fire and survey budgets across different owners, so no one is paid for fewer visits.

**Moat:** Counter-positioning against per-visit revenue; scale economies (route density lowers cost per home); cornered resource (dual-ticket technicians, see the Guild idea); switching costs (five-year cycle plus the home record).

**First 90 days:** (1) Sign one mid-size landlord for a 1,500-home pilot due Jan-Mar 2027. (2) Hire and cross-train 6 technicians. (3) Measure visits per home, no-access rate, findings per visit and minutes from finding to clock start against the incumbent's baseline. (4) Before 30 Nov, publish a phase-2 "clock map" showing which visit finds which hazard. (5) Price against the sum of the separate lots.

**Outside-the-box score:** 6/10.

## INSIDER 2. Hot-Spot Contract: capitated root-cause care for the worst 5-8% of homes

**One-liner:** Take a fixed annual fee per home for every repair, disrepair claim and root-cause fix on a landlord's highest-cost homes, guarantee the fix for five years, and keep the savings.

**Insider:** A contractor operations director with 15+ years, plus a housing-association finance director. Each holds half the secret.

**Earned secret:** Repair spend is skewed; a few homes get patched 5-8 times a year while the root-cause fix (a ventilation system, a roof, a leak) is a capital item nobody proposes. The responsive contractor is paid per visit and never recommends the fix. The landlord's capital programme is built from five-year-old sample surveys. Accounting rewards the fix: replacing a component is capitalised, patching is revenue, and capitalising lifts the EBITDA-MRI interest-cover covenant. Evidence is partial: a 2022 Journal of Building Engineering case study of one housing association found heavily skewed repair costs; the Ombudsman's repeat-failure findings cite temporary fixes and poor diagnosis; per-home spend varied from about GBP1,579 on average to about GBP7,000 at the top landlord in an older analysis [V-weak]. No published Pareto split was found.

**Customer:** Landlords with 25k+ homes and a visible disrepair or damp-and-mould problem.

**Payer and signer:** Finance Director and Director of Assets. The fee is funded by responsive repairs plus the disrepair, compensation and legal budgets, with root-cause works moved to capital. The CEO and board approve a multi-year contract.

**Problem:** Per-visit contracts reward repeat visits. Damp and mould is the sharpest case: the English Housing Survey puts damp in 7% of social homes (broader measures 11%) [V], and the legal clocks and the right to sue now punish patching.

**Solution:** Per landlord, rank homes by the trailing three-year cost of repairs, disrepair and compensation, then contract for the top 5-8%. Diagnose with sensors and a competent assessor. Do the root-cause works (ventilation, heating controls, gutters, leaks, insulation). Take over all repairs on those homes for a capitated fee of about 80% of trailing cost, with a five-year guarantee that pays for any repeat visit. Reinsure the tail once 12-24 months of loss data exist. This is the high-cost-patient programme and accountable-care shared-savings model from managed care, moved to buildings.

**Why now:** The right to sue (phase 1 live, phase 2 on 30 Nov 2026) turns repeat failures into claims. Lambeth's disrepair bill alone was GBP15M in 2024/25. Fixed recoverable costs for disrepair are exempt until Oct 2028, so claimant costs stay uncapped. [V]

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** A 500-home pilot at one mid-size housing association, then landlords already burned by disrepair claims (London boroughs, large ALMOs). Data access is the gate; landlords own their job data. [A]

**Path to $100M:** Average responsive-plus-void cost is about GBP600-800 per home a year in council benchmarks (2016-21, probably higher now) [V-weak]; assume hot-spot homes cost about four times that, about GBP2,800 [A]. A 5% cohort is about 220,000 homes, a pool of about GBP616M a year. At a GBP2,200 capitated fee, GBP77M needs about 35,000 homes: 16% of the cohort, 0.8% of all stock, about 15-20 landlords handing over 1,500-2,500 homes each. Revenue is gross of crew costs; net margin is a fraction (assume 12-18%) plus shared savings and guarantee premium. **Kill test:** if the top 5% of homes carry under about 15% of repair spend in three landlords' data, drop it.

**Who does it today:** Mobysoft RepairSense (repeat-repair analytics, 108 staff, GBP109,800 a year at Flagship) [V]; Switchee Mould iQ (monitor and treat, 92,000 homes, 3 housing associations) [V]; Aico HomeLINK [V]; the primes' own total-asset contracts [M]; Plentific [V]; Made Tech [V]. None was found taking capitated risk.

**Why they lose:** Primes earn on visits and have 5.7% margins that cannot absorb outcome risk. Analytics and sensor vendors cannot do the work or carry a guarantee. Insurers exclude gradual damp as a maintenance matter and no damp-and-mould cover product was found. [V]

**Legal premise to check:** whether an uncapped repeat-repair guarantee is a "contract of insurance" under FSMA. Keep it a service warranty from the party that does the work and put the reinsurance on the provider's own account; get FCA perimeter advice. Multi-year capitation above the threshold needs a Procurement Act 2023 route.

**Moat:** Cornered resource (a cross-landlord loss dataset that prices building risk, which nobody has); counter-positioning against visit-based pay; switching costs of 5-10 year contracts; reinsurance relationships.

**First 90 days:** Get job-level data from three landlords and measure concentration (the kill test). Pick one 500-home cohort and set a baseline. Fix the top 100 homes, instrument them, and write the guarantee wording with a broker and counsel.

**Outside-the-box score:** 8/10.

## PATTERN 1. Clock Desk: pay per closed Awaab case, not per hour

**One-liner:** A vetted assessor network and a case desk run the entire statutory clock (intake, 10-day investigation, 3-day summary, 5-day make-safe, 12-week works, early settlement of any claim) for a fixed price per closed case, with a pooled fund behind the deadlines.

**Pattern transferred:** Outcome-priced replacement of a high-volume measurable function (Sierra), combined with selling scarce credentialed expertise to a new concentrated buyer class (Mercor, Surge). The buyers are about 200 large landlords and 150 councils with legal clocks. Why it has not happened here: the duty is under 13 months old, and no competent-person scheme or standard exists yet. [V]

**Customer:** Landlords whose cases overwhelm in-house surveyors; Lambeth's 347 cases in three weeks is the signal.

**Payer and signer:** Director of Housing Services or Repairs; budget is a new line carved from damp and mould, disrepair and legal budgets. The Chief Operating Officer signs.

**Problem:** Landlords are hiring surveyors, letting damp-and-mould frameworks (Riverside's GBP657M framework across 12 regional lots) and bolting software on. Winter brings long lead times. [V] The new right to sue makes missed clocks costly: Clyde & Co expect a "significant increase in claims activity" from phase 2. [V]

**Solution:** (1) A credentialed network of assessors (environmental health, building surveyors, damp specialists) with our own protocol, QA and professional-indemnity cover; this protocol is the standard. (2) Remote-first triage using tenant video and sensors, with a site visit only when needed. (3) A case desk that issues the written summary, builds the evidence file, scopes works, books crews, triggers decant, and settles claims early using fixed-fee panel counsel. (4) A pooled liability fund, reinsured, that pays when the Desk misses a clock.

**Why now:** Phase 2 on 30 Nov 2026 multiplies hazard types beyond what damp surveyors cover; phase 3 follows in 2027; Ombudsman repair complaints are up sharply [V]; Renters' Rights Act powers let councils fine up to GBP7,000 from 22 Jun 2026 and councils inspect only about one in three complaints [V].

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** One council or housing association with 100+ cases a month. Run the clock for 60 days for the hardest 20% of cases. Customers 2-10 are London boroughs and large ALMOs with disrepair pain.

**Path to $100M [A throughout]:** 8% of 4.4M homes = about 350,000 cases a year by 2028 (Lambeth's first three weeks annualise to about 18% but include backlog). Fee GBP450 per case. Own works on 40% of cases x GBP1,100 average. Revenue about GBP890 per case. Claims: 25-40k a year nationally (claims against councils rose 392% from 2020 to 2024 in FOI data [V]) at about GBP2,500 per claim handled. GBP77M = 64,000 cases (GBP57M) + 8,000 claims (GBP20M). That is **about 18% of cases and about 25% of claims, which is heroic.** If case volumes are half my estimate it needs 35% and the idea fails. Extension if Awaab's Law reaches private renting (no date; 2027+ at the earliest [V]) would halve the needed share.

**Who does it today:** Landlords' in-house surveyors (Karbon, Lewisham hiring) [V]; damp specialists such as Insite Group (Scotland framework) [V]; frameworks (Riverside, LHC) [V]; Plentific [V]; Alphatec ClaimControl (claims software, HHSRS-categorised) [V]; panel law firms (Ward Hadaway, Clyde & Co, Devonshires publish guidance) [V]; Zurich Municipal for liability cover [V]; the primes. No competent-person scheme or managed case service found.

**Why they lose:** Panel solicitors bill by the hour, so fast resolution cuts their revenue. Primes cannot sign for deadlines that depend on landlord-controlled steps. Marketplaces carry no liability. Insurers exclude gradual damage.

**Legal premise to check:** the liability fund is insurance, so use a regulated carrier or reinsurer and act as TPA/MGA at first. Litigation runs through SRA-regulated counsel only. No statutory competent-person scheme exists, so the credential is contractual.

**Moat:** The standard and registry (if a landlord consortium or regulator adopts the protocol); network effects in assessor supply; claims data that prices the fund.

**First 90 days:** Sign the pilot, recruit 25 assessors, run 100 cases through the clock, publish on-time rate and cost per case, and take reinsurance quotes on the fund.

**Outside-the-box score:** 5/10.

## PATTERN 2. Home Safety Technician Guild: the scarce input for every other idea

**One-liner:** A landlord- and levy-backed guild trains career changers into dual-ticket home-safety technicians (gas plus electrical plus hazard screening) and bills hours under take-or-pay offtake from landlords and primes with funded backlogs.

**Pattern transferred:** Capex-supercycle pass-through to scarce physical trades. A founder enters through the scarce input and sells to owners with a funded backlog.

**Customer:** The 20-40 landlords and primes that hold phase 2/3, electrical, MEES and adaptations backlogs and cannot hire.

**Payer and signer:** Director of People or COO plus Director of Assets; the budget is agency and sub-contractor labour (already paid) plus unspent apprenticeship levy.

**Problem:** CITB's 2026-2030 outlook needs about 41,000 extra construction workers a year (206,000 over five years) [V]. Training providers estimate 27,000 extra gas engineers by 2028 and 15,000 electricians in five years [V-weak, commercial sources]. Single-trade routes take 3-4 years. Employers train for their own contracts and lose staff to rivals.

**Solution:** A 24-month blended route for career changers (ex-forces, retail and logistics leavers aged 25-40) combining Gas Safe, electrical inspection and testing, hazard screening and small repairs, with paid work from month 4. The guild is the employer of record. Landlords commit to hours at about GBP48 an hour for trainees and GBP62 for qualified staff. Training is funded by apprenticeship levy transfers (employers can transfer up to 50% of unspent levy; 83% of councils in an LGA survey did not use their full allowance; from 1 Aug 2026 the 10% top-up ended and new funds expire after 12 months, which pushes employers to place it [V-secondary]). A twist: landlords pay partly in housing, offering key-worker tenancies from stock in their own areas, which removes the biggest barrier in high-cost regions. [A]

**Why now:** The shortage is documented (CITB), the levy rules changed on 1 Aug 2026, and phase 2/3, MEES and the electrical duty all draw on the same technicians.

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** A 40-person first cohort for two landlords or primes. Customers 3-10 are the same landlords' neighbours in each region.

**Path to $100M:** GBP77M = about 1,250 technicians billed at about GBP62k. With 60% completion that is about 420 starters a year for three years. Gross margin is about 12-18% [A], so this is revenue-rich and profit-thin; its value is as the supply engine for One Knock, Void Clock and the adaptations idea. Share of roughly 90,000 social-housing operatives [A]: about 1.4%.

**Who does it today:** Prime-run academies (Mears, Wates, Kier [M]); colleges; recruiters (Hays social housing [V]); housing associations' own placements (one has launched a 20-place scheme [V]); private Gas Safe training providers [V]; CITB.

**Why they lose:** Each employer trains for its own contracts and fears poaching; colleges teach single trades; nobody sells a multi-ticket route with guaranteed employment.

**Moat:** Weak and honest: a cornered resource (a trained cohort bonded to offtake) that lasts only while the shortage lasts. The standard (an employer-group-led occupational standard) is the only durable moat and needs an approval process of 12-18 months [A]. **Dies if** the trade shortage eases or offtake commitments are not take-or-pay.

**First 90 days:** Sign two take-or-pay offtakes; secure two levy transfers; contract a training provider; form the employer group for the standard; recruit the first cohort.

**Outside-the-box score:** 4/10.

## DILEMMA 1. Void Clock: the one party paid for the days between tenants

**One-liner:** A fixed price per void, a 35-day target against today's 74, and a payment that moves with the days, with the provider owning the whole chain: compliance certificates, asbestos, works, clear, clean, inspection, keys and lettings handover.

**Structural conflict:** Primes are paid per work package on schedule-of-rates and control only the works phase. The days sit in steps the landlord controls (allocations, compliance sign-off, keys). Landlords' departments are siloed and no one owns end-to-end days.

**Insider:** Voids and lettings director, 15+ years.

**Earned secret:** Average re-let time rose from under 30 days to 74 in five years while the works themselves did not change. The added days are queues between 6-9 hand-offs: the new electrical duty, gas, asbestos, damp checks under Awaab's Law, smart-meter and carpet requirements (Cardiff attributes longer turns to these [V]), tenant rubbish, utilities. Nobody is paid for fewer days.

**Customer:** Landlords with 10k-40k homes and 600-2,500 voids a year.

**Payer and signer:** Director of Assets or Housing Operations. Budget: the voids line in responsive repairs. The rent line (about GBP105 a week, so about GBP15 a day [A]) justifies a bonus.

**Problem:** About 300,000 voids a year, GBP1.6B all-in [V-secondary]. Milton Keynes loses 4-5 weeks of rent per void, around GBP440-550, and spends about GBP8,590 on works per void [V]. Council re-let times run from 51 days (Anglesey target) to 87 days (Cardiff, standard voids) [V].

**Solution:** A void cell per region run as a joint P&L with the landlord: a pre-void inspection and materials order during the tenant's notice period, parallel booking of the gas, electrical, asbestos and damp checks, one crew sequence, a lettable-standard definition agreed up front, a hand-off API to lettings, and a fixed price by archetype (about GBP4,000 [A, HouseMark: over GBP1B repairs / 300,000 voids = GBP3.3k, plus about GBP0.7k lettings]) with a per-day malus and bonus. Void homes that sit idle between steps can feed the Decant Reserve.

**Why now:** The sector's void times doubled; the electrical duty and Awaab checks add more steps; and rent caps and the new rent settlement make rent loss visible.

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** Take the "tail" voids (over 60 days) of one mid-size landlord as a managed pilot, then the full void flow. Customers 2-10: landlords whose repairs contract is expiring or who already run voids as a separate lot. [A]

**Path to $100M:** GBP77M / GBP4,000 = about 19,000 voids a year, about 6% of 300,000. About 14 landlords with 1,400 voids each. Landlord value: 39 days x GBP15 x 19,000 = about GBP11M a year in rent recovered plus a works saving of 5-10%. [A]

**Who does it today:** The primes' bundled voids work (Mears, Wates, Morgan Sindall [M]); Made Tech voids prioritisation software (vendor claims GBP573 saved per void and 21% shorter re-let) [V-weak]; councils' in-house teams. No public contract with a guaranteed turn time was found.

**Why they lose:** A prime cannot sign for days it does not control, and bundling voids with responsive work hides the pricing. A landlord cannot restructure its allocations and compliance teams quickly. Software vendors cannot deliver the work.

**Moat:** Process power (the cell design and step-level data); switching costs (embedded in lettings workflow); regional purchasing scale.

**First 90 days:** One pilot landlord; log days by step for 200 voids; take 100 tail voids and cut them below 45 days; agree the price book.

**Outside-the-box score:** 6/10.

## DILEMMA 2. Decant Reserve: idle void days and block-leased units as the supply landlords and councils cannot trade

**One-liner:** A licensed, furnished stock of void-gap homes and leased apartments that landlords call on for Awaab decants, and councils call on as temporary accommodation overflow, at a price under nightly-paid hotels.

**Structural conflict:** Housing associations hold idle homes (300,000 voids x 74 days / 365 is about 61,000 homes vacant at any moment [A]) but cannot sublet short term because of nominations agreements, tenancy law, insurance and VAT. Councils spend GBP2.84B a year on temporary accommodation and cannot build supply fast. Neither can trade across. Awaab's Law now forces landlords to buy decant at short notice.

**Insider:** Director of housing options at a London borough, or a housing association asset director.

**Earned secret:** Many void days are not safety days. They are waiting for allocation, viewings or keys. A share of the 74 days (my guess is 10-25) is "lettable but unlet". [A, not sourced]

**Customer:** Housing associations needing decant capacity; councils needing temporary accommodation.

**Payer and signer:** Council Director of Housing/Homelessness (TA budget); housing association Director of Assets (new Awaab alternative-accommodation line, regeneration decants).

**Problem:** The decant market today is tiny: the Procurement for Housing decant framework was tendered at GBP25M over about four years, Places for People ran a GBP2M tender, ICAB is a named supplier [V]. Awaab's Law adds a new forced, unbudgeted cost. The government will consult on applying the law to temporary accommodation. [V]

**Solution:** (1) Licence void-gap homes of 4+ weeks from housing associations, furnish them, and manage them under occupation licences. (2) Block-lease serviced apartments for longer decants. (3) A 24/7 booking desk that handles tenant support, storage, pets and moving. (4) Sell the same inventory to councils as temporary accommodation overflow. Price about GBP80 a night [A; no decant nightly rate was found, Aberdeen's B&B average of GBP37 is not comparable].

**Why now:** Phase 2 widens the hazards that can force a decant; temporary accommodation spend is up 25% in a year; HA void times are at their worst in years.

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** One housing association (1,500 voids a year) and one London borough; pilot 40 units; run one Awaab decant. [A]

**Path to $100M:** GBP77M / GBP80 = about 960,000 nights, about 3,100 units at 85% occupancy. Against nightly-paid self-contained TA spend of over GBP1B, that is about 7.7% share. **The Awaab decant market alone is far too small; only the temporary-accommodation bridge makes this big**, and TA is an adjacent pool, not this one.

**Who does it today:** ICAB and the other PfH framework suppliers [V]; councils' own leasing schemes and hotels/B&Bs [V]; housing associations' own TA leases [M]; nightly-paid TA providers [M].

**Why they lose:** Framework suppliers lease commercial stock at market cost and cannot reach void-gap supply. Housing associations cannot run a hospitality-style operation. Councils pay hotel rates because they have no alternative.

**Legal premise to check (all five):** short-let VAT treatment (sleeping accommodation can be standard-rated); nominations agreements and allocation policy; licence versus tenancy status; insurance; planning use class. Verify with counsel before any pilot.

**Moat:** Balance sheet (leased inventory); privileged supply (void pipelines of partner landlords); approvals as a barrier; booking data.

**First 90 days:** Sign one housing association and one borough under an MoU; get a VAT and licence opinion; furnish 10 units; run 5 Awaab-style decants and measure cost per night against hotel rates.

**Outside-the-box score:** 7/10.

## SHOCK 1. Falls on a Clock: standard adaptations delivered inside the Awaab deadline

**One-liner:** From 30 Nov 2026 a handrail, stair, bath or level-access failure for an older tenant is a clocked hazard: a make-safe in 5 working days and fuller works inside 12 weeks. A phone-based "trusted assessor" protocol and standard kits replace an occupational-therapy queue that runs to 8 months.

**Customer:** Housing associations and councils with older tenants; councils administering Disabled Facilities Grants.

**Payer and signer:** Director of Assets/Housing (landlord adaptations budget, Awaab clock budget); council DFG manager (DFG budget). Funding bodies: Integrated Care Boards later.

**Problem:** DFG is GBP761M in 2025-26 after a late top-up, about 60,000 grants a year averaging about GBP10k. OT assessment waits reach 8 months (Salford, longest of 89 councils), and completion takes almost two years in several areas. [V] 56% of properties needing adaptations lack them; the share of adapted homes in the housing association sector fell by about a fifth in a decade. [V] Phase 2 turns falls (level, stairs, between levels) into a legal clock. [V]

**Solution:** (1) A phone/video "trusted assessor" protocol run by OT assistants and technicians for simple works (rails, steps, ramps, half-steps, toilet frames), with OT review only for complex cases. [A: the 2022 DFG guidance allows trusted assessors; verify] (2) Standard kits (grab and stair rails, step conversions, level-access shower pods) installed by the same crews that do One Knock. (3) A fixed price per case and a 5-day make-safe commitment.

**Why now:** Phase 2 on 30 Nov 2026; a one-off DFG top-up shows political pressure; Ombudsman findings on OT delays (Redbridge, 8 months). [V]

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** Two mid-size housing associations (rails and handrails are the commonest adaptation) plus one council's DFG minor-works stream. [A]

**Path to $100M [A]:** In-pool social adaptations about GBP150-250M (Leicester's council stock spends about GBP1.1M a year on about 33,000 homes, about GBP33 a home, x 4.4M = about GBP145M). Adjacent DFG pool GBP761M. Combined about GBP0.95B. GBP77M = 8% of the combined pool, e.g. 15% of the in-pool market (GBP30M) plus 6% of DFG (GBP46M). **In-pool alone it is too small; the DFG bridge is needed.**

**Who does it today:** Millbrook Healthcare (home improvement agency for many councils; FY25 turnover GBP114M, EBITDA GBP3.8M; owned by Cairngorm Capital; a Lichfield contract drew an improvement notice) [V]; councils' own agencies; Foundations; stairlift and bathroom suppliers (Stannah, Premier Care in Bathing [M]); housing association in-house teams. Millbrook proves a $100M-revenue adaptations business exists, but at thin margin.

**Why they lose:** Millbrook is paid to administer grants and supervise contractors, and the OT assessment queue is the council's, not theirs. Landlords treat adaptations as a minor operational issue. [V]

**Moat:** An assessor protocol approved by councils; kit standardisation; contract position (5-7 year council contracts); case data.

**First 90 days:** Agree the protocol with one OT service; run 100 rail and step cases against the 5-day clock; publish waiting-time reduction; open DFG minor-works talks with one council.

**Outside-the-box score:** 6/10.

## SHOCK 2. Whole-Home Once: one disturbance per home instead of five component programmes

**One-liner:** A factory-made "interior service core" (kitchen and bath, ventilation, electrics, heating controls, alarms, handrails) fitted in 5 working days with the tenant in place, sold at a fixed price per home archetype and capitalised.

**Shock:** The forced capital programme: EPC C by 2030 (over GBP16B), a condition-based Decent Homes Standard from 2035, phase 2 excess-cold and fire hazards, and a Warm Homes Plan with GBP4.4B of social-housing grant. [V]

**Insider:** Planned maintenance director.

**Earned secret:** Tenants are disturbed 4-6 times in ten years because kitchens, heating, windows and electrics are bought as separate component programmes from separate lots. Each mobilisation carries access, scaffold or set-up, asbestos survey, tenant-liaison and scheduling overhead of an estimated 15-25% [A]. Capitalising a bundled renewal also helps the interest-cover covenant.

**Customer:** Housing associations and councils with large capitalised programmes (GBP3.9B capitalised repairs a year plus council major works [V]).

**Payer and signer:** Executive Director of Property/Assets; capital programme budget; Board approval for multi-year deals.

**Problem:** Component-by-component procurement multiplies disruption and cost. Deep retrofit has been slow: London's retrofit innovation partnership had 61 deep retrofits in the pipeline against a target of 65 in Jan 2024, versus over 1,800 contracted; Mears has done about 5,000 homes of fabric and ventilation work. [V]

**Solution:** Factory-built kits matched to the top 8 archetypes in a landlord's stock; interior-only (no scaffolding or planning, which de-risks it); fixed price about GBP14-18k per home [A]; a 12-year performance guarantee on ventilation and moisture; asbestos survey and tenant liaison included; capital-eligible by design. Energy measures follow PAS 2035 where included.

**Why now:** MEES 2030 and DHS 2035 force the volume. Phase 2's excess-cold and fire hazards add urgency.

**Wedge and first 10 customers:** One housing association's worst 300 homes from its own damp and disrepair list (a feed from Hot-Spot). Then neighbours with identical stock archetypes.

**Path to $100M:** GBP77M / GBP17k = about 4,500 homes a year. If about 35% of capitalised spend (about GBP1.4B) is interior component work [A], that is about 5.5% of that spend. No heroic share, but working capital and bonding needs are heavy.

**Who does it today:** Mears (about 5,000 retrofits; further 2,329 planned by 2028) [V]; Wates, Kier, Morgan Sindall, Willmott Dixon [M]; Energiesprong UK and Melius Homes (63 Nottingham homes) [V]; London retrofit innovation partnership [V]; Sero and kitchen/bathroom specialists [M, not verified].

**Why they lose:** Separate lots by component and trade, schedule-of-rates pricing per component, and landlord asset teams that buy from sample surveys. Nobody tenders by home.

**Moat:** Scale economies in the factory kit; process power; switching costs of the home record from One Knock. **Execution risk is high** (UK deep-retrofit delivery is slow; Ilke and Legal & General Modular failed offsite [M]).

**First 90 days:** Define the 8 archetypes from one landlord's stock data; prototype one kit in three real homes; test asbestos and tenant-in-situ logistics; obtain a capitalisation opinion from the landlord's auditor.

**Outside-the-box score:** 5/10.

## Paths considered and killed

- **Contractor-cost audit on savings share:** overbilling evidence is anecdotal (Brighton found over GBP300k in 17 months on one subcontractor [V]); even at 2.5% leakage on the whole GBP14B pool and a 25% fee, GBP77M needs near-total penetration.
- **Awaab clock software / hazard ledger:** about GBP3-4 per home a year (Mobysoft); at 100% share about GBP20-40M.
- **Subcontractor instant-pay and financing:** at most about GBP66M a year even if every subcontractor invoice were financed at 10-12%; dead.
- **Defence-only disrepair service or defect recovery from housebuilders:** pools of GBP150-250M and GBP45M respectively; folded the claims piece into Clock Desk.
- **Repairs data co-operative / group purchasing:** fee pool about GBP45M; existing non-profit consortia (LHC, PfH, Fusion21 [M]) hold it.
- **AI tools for operatives (Rilla-style capture), voice repairs reporting:** seat maths tops out around GBP45M and it is a listed cliche.
- **Heating-as-a-service, pest control, asbestos register:** crowded (primes, boiler makers, Rentokil [M]) or would need 25%+ share.

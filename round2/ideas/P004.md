# P004 Germany Mittelstand ownership succession: 8 ideas (round 2)

Written 2026-10-18. About 24 searches and fetches (the brief said 8-15; I went over because several first answers were thin). FX: 1 EUR = 1.15 USD, so $100M = EUR 87M.

## Pool facts used (all checked this run unless marked)

- KfW Nachfolge-Monitoring 2025 (Fokus 526, 9 Jan 2026, read in full): 545,000 of 3.87M Mittelstand firms plan succession by 2029 = about 109,000 a year. About 114,000 a year plan closure with no successor (first time closures exceed successions). Average intended price EUR 499,000 (+34% vs 2019), but the median is only about 0.6x annual revenue. FINANCE multiples for firms under EUR 20M revenue: Bau/Handwerk 0.25-0.50x revenue, Maschinen- und Anlagenbau 0.46-0.69x, Fahrzeugbau/-zubehoer 0.38-0.57x, beratende Dienstleistungen 0.56-1.03x.
- KfW hurdles: bureaucracy is #2 (45%), legal/tax complexity 32%, financing only 18%. EBO considered by 28%, MBO by 24%. Investment falls about 32% in firms with near-term succession plans. 186,000 firms plan succession by end-2026, of which 119,000 are already agreed or in concrete talks.
- IfM Bonn (Nov 2025): about 186,000 handovers in 2026-2030 = 37,200 a year (my division), covering about 3M employees (about 16 per firm). This is the realistic count; 109,000 is the owners' wish.
- nexxt-change (free, run by BMWE/KfW with DIHK/ZDH/BVR/DSGV, 431 regional partners): 22,300 handovers "initiated" since 2006 (about 1,100 a year). KfW is relaunching it as an aggregating, independent 2.0.
- KfW ERP-Foerderkredit Gruendung und Nachfolge (077, since 1 Nov 2024): up to EUR 500,000 per applicant, max 35% of eligible costs, 100% Buergschaftsbank guarantee (80% federal counter-guarantee), bank carries no credit risk, applicant stays personally liable, natural persons within 5 years of starting self-employment, "taetige Beteiligungen" eligible. Buergschaftsbank Baden-Wuerttemberg logged only 123 applications for it in 2025.
- Cautionary: creditshelf (Mittelstand credit marketplace) went into protective-shield proceedings Feb 2024; its loan business went to Teylor AG.
- [M] = from memory, not re-verified this run.

## 1. Werkhof (lens: insider, a Steuerberater with 20 years of Mittelstand files)

- **One-liner:** A net-lease landlord that buys the owner's privately held operating building at the moment the company is sold and leases it back to the successor for 15-20 years, so the owner exits clean and the buyer needs a third less financing.
- **Customer:** Owner-managers aged 58-68 who hold the Betriebsimmobilie personally or in a Besitz-KG and rent it to their own GmbH, and the successors who cannot fund company plus building.
- **Payer and signer:** The successor's operating company pays rent from operating costs (it replaces debt service). Signers: the retiring Inhaber (sale), the successor Geschaeftsfuehrer (lease). The Steuerberater recommends. Capital side: a Pfandbrief or insurer lender's credit committee plus equity LPs.
- **Problem:** The building is the second-largest asset and the hidden deal-killer. A building a shareholder rents to his GmbH is usually a Betriebsaufspaltung (the BFH treats it as an essential operating asset). When the shares go to an unrelated buyer the link ends, and the hidden reserves in the building can become taxable (CMS Law, Steuerschroeder). The owner is left with three bad choices: sell building with company (successor cannot fund both), cash it out taxed, or stay landlord to a stranger (and heirs fight later).
- **Solution:** A standardised, notarised sale (of the building, or the Besitz entity where that is tax-better) to Werkhof at closing, with a triple-net 15-20 year indexed lease to the new owner and a tenant buy-back option. First product is a tax-first term sheet built with the owner's Steuerberater, not a price quote.
- **Why now:** Prices are up 34% since 2019 (EUR 499k average), 077 funds only 35% of costs up to EUR 500k and only for natural persons, so the building is the cheque that breaks the financing stack. 119,000 short-term successions are already in talks. E&Y found 11.6% of Mittelstaendler could imagine selling real estate (via kreditwesen.de).
- **Wedge:** Buildings of EUR 0.5-2.5M (Handwerk, light industry, wholesale) tied to an identified successor. First 10 customers via 10-15 Steuerberater kanzleien in Bavaria and NRW (largest absolute handover counts per IfM).
- **Path to $100M:** Rent of EUR 87M at a 7% net initial yield (my assumption for thin-credit single tenants) = EUR 1.24B of buildings. At EUR 1.2M average that is about 1,030 assets, built at 225 a year over 4.6 years. If 25% of the 37,200 actual handovers a year have an owner-held building (my assumption; no national statistic found, only that Mittelstand firms own about 75% of used property), that is 9,300 a year, so Werkhof needs about 2.4% of them. Capital: about EUR 370-430M equity plus EUR 800-870M secured debt. It needs a real-estate sponsor, not a seed round.
- **Who does it today:** DAL Deutsche Anlagen-Leasing (Sparkassen-Finanzgruppe) markets Immobilienleasing for succession; Nord Leasing does machinery sale-and-rent-back; Sparkassen sell Immobilien-Leasing. A leasing executive says they are often called "as an emergency anchor" late. Free substitute: the owner keeps the building and rents it to the successor (the default today). I did not find DAL's minimum ticket.
- **Why they lose:** Leasing arms are project-driven and sold at the Berater's discretion, not a standard small-ticket product with a Steuerberater channel. Sparkassen want the building loan and the deposits, not a buyer that removes both. Nobody owns the tax-first workflow.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies (cost of capital, standard documents) plus switching costs (15-20 year leases); counter-positioning against bank-owned leasing arms.
- **Earned secret:** In small-company sales the argument is rarely about price. It is about what happens to the building after tax. A buyer of the building that lets the owner choose the cheapest tax path unlocks deals that otherwise die in the Steuerberater's office.
- **First 90 days:** Get a written tax memo on the three exit paths from two Steuerberater firms. Sign 10 kanzleien for a free "Immobilien-Check". Underwrite 5 live buildings. Get one Pfandbrief-bank term sheet on a pooled facility. Draft the notarial template.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 6.
- **Risks to kill it:** Lender appetite for small single-tenant assets; tax structure turns out expensive for most owners; yield for weak tenants is above 7%.

## 2. Verkaufsreif (lens: insider, a Unternehmensmakler)

- **One-liner:** A 6-24 month "make it sellable" programme for owners 58-66 that is paid only at closing (about 9-10% of price), producing audited normalised earnings and the first price database for sub-EUR 2M firms.
- **Customer:** Owner-managed firms with 3-30 employees, 1-3 years before the planned exit.
- **Payer and signer:** The seller-owner, from sale proceeds at closing (a transaction cost netted from the price). Signer: the Inhaber; the Steuerberater introduces. Later buyers (holding vehicles, lenders) pay for data access.
- **Problem:** Deals die 24 months before the negotiation: owners stop investing (KfW: about -32% investment), the owner is the only relationship holder, books are tax-minimised so buyers cannot see earnings, and expectation meets reality only at LOI (owner median 0.6x revenue; Bau/Handwerk market 0.25-0.50x). KfW itself says there is no generally accepted pricing scheme. The 42% of closure planners who cite bureaucracy never start.
- **Solution:** A fixed programme: normalised-EBITDA package; install a Prokurist or Betriebsleiter and move key customer relationships into the firm; e-invoice/GoBD/ERP clean-up; capture the owner's tacit knowledge; then run a buyer process into a pool of pre-qualified buyers (Meisterwerk and Teamkauf below, PE platforms, strategics, individuals). The platform fronts the cost.
- **Why now:** First year closures exceed successions (KfW), 186,000 firms plan to hand over by end-2026, 11% see modernisation cost as a hurdle, nexxt-change 2.0 will make listings free and commodity, so the paid value moves to readiness and certainty.
- **Wedge:** Handwerk and small trade/wholesale firms with EUR 0.8-4M revenue; first 10 from a Steuerberater panel and an HWK Nachfolgemoderator in one region.
- **Path to $100M:** 9-10% of an average EUR 450,000 price = EUR 40,500-45,000 a closing. EUR 87M needs 1,930-2,150 closings a year = **5.2-5.8% of the 37,200 actual handovers** a year (about 2x the roughly 1,100 a year that nexxt-change has initiated). Staffing: about 300 transaction managers at 7 closings each. Not heroic, but it is the only fee model here that gets near $100M, and it needs a 45-55% close rate.
- **Who does it today:** Unternehmensmakler and M&A boutiques (e.g. Nachfolgekontor), IHK/HWK Nachfolgemoderatoren (free), nexxt-change (free), Steuerberater with DATEV Vermoegensnachfolge and wevalue valuation on the DATEV Marktplatz, Sparkassen advisers.
- **Why they lose:** Brokers are paid only at closing, so they cannot fund 12-24 months of preparation; chambers are free and cannot operate; Steuerberater are barred from referral shares (§9 StBerG, "sonstige Vorteile") and from most success fees (§9a, narrow exceptions [M]), and the sale may cost them the client. DATEV's valuation is a tax valuation, not a market price.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (the only dataset of normalised earnings to realised prices below EUR 2M) plus process power.
- **Earned secret:** The price gap and the owner-dependence are curable, but only if someone is willing to work for free for two years and be repaid out of the closing. Nobody in the chain has that incentive.
- **First 90 days:** Sign 10 owners with a 24-month exclusivity and a tail clause (to stop disintermediation). Build the normalisation template with two Steuerberater. Price 20 closed comps from advisers. Recruit 5 buyers.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5.
- **Risks to kill it:** Disintermediation after the free work; programme cost per closing is higher than EUR 15k; the legal fee premise needs counsel.

## 3. Meisterwerk (lens: pattern, roll-up multiple arbitrage in recurring, compliance-mandated services)

- **One-liner:** A steward-owned, permanent-capital buyer of micro Handwerk and technical-service firms (electrical testing, fire protection, heating and water service, lift maintenance) priced below what private equity can touch, with a Meister equity ladder for the people who run them.
- **Customer:** Owners 58-68 of firms with EUR 0.8-2.5M revenue and recurring inspection and service contracts.
- **Payer and signer:** The customers of the firms (building owners, property managers, small industry) pay from maintenance budgets. Capital from permanent-capital LPs (Versorgungswerk, family office CIO). Signer on the deal: the seller-owner.
- **Problem:** A-trades in Germany need a Meister as owner or Betriebsleiter on the Handwerksrolle [M: HwO §7, verify]. When the owner retires the licence leaves with him. PE platforms want EBITDA above EUR 1M and a sale in 4-6 years; the micro firms are below that and sellers fear a flip.
- **Solution:** Buy at 0.25-0.50x revenue (FINANCE range) with half deferred against retention of service contracts. The old Meister stays 2-3 years as Betriebsleiter (a Ludwig-Froehler-Institut finding). The firm sponsors a Geselle to a Meister, who earns equity in the local firm over 5 years. Central: back office, procurement, compliance scheduling, insurance. No AI claim.
- **Why now:** HWK Dortmund spring 2026: 33% of Handwerk firms plan a handover within 5 years. About 100,000 unfilled Handwerk positions (KOFA 7/2026). KfW: owners value continuity (EBO 28%, MBO 24% considered). The GmbH mit gebundenem Vermoegen has BMJV/BMF key points (Feb-Mar 2026), though no draft bill is confirmed.
- **Wedge:** Elektro and SHK firms with periodic-inspection books in two regions; the first 10 acquisitions from HWK Nachfolgemoderatoren.
- **Path to $100M:** 60 firms x EUR 1.5M = EUR 90M ($103.5M). Purchase price EUR 22-45M in total plus working capital; 12 deals a year over 5 years. This needs about 0.03% of handovers: the limit is Meister supply and integration, not market share. Revenue is bought; expect a 6-9% operating margin. This is the least original idea here and the surest path to $100M.
- **Who does it today:** HWP Handwerkspartner (Adiuva to Goldbeck Robens), Builtech Group, Konzmann Gruppe, TBS Brandschutz (DBAG's Fire Holding), FOGTEC (Deutsche Private Equity), svt (Ergon). Free substitute: the owner closes.
- **Why they lose:** Fund economics cannot buy EUR 100-200k EBIT firms or promise to hold forever. Banks cannot hold equity. Steuerberater cannot take a commercial role.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning against PE (a hold-forever promise), scale economies in back office and procurement, a Meister pipeline as a cornered resource.
- **Earned secret:** In Handwerk the Meister is the asset, not the building or the customer list. Whoever solves Meister succession can buy the rest cheaply.
- **First 90 days:** Sign term sheets with 3 sellers. Line up EUR 15M of seed permanent capital. Draft the Meister-equity ladder. Check DGUV V3 and other contract recurrence on real customer books (DGUV V3 intervals are risk-based, not fixed).
- **Outside-the-box score:** 4.
- **Risks to kill it:** Customers drift away on the owner's exit; Meister-ladder equity design; PE moves down-market.

## 4. Netzplan (lens: pattern, capex pass-through to scarce trades plus outcome-priced labour)

- **One-liner:** A group of retiring owner-led planning, survey and permit offices that sells fixed-price "planned and permitted kilometre of route" to distribution-grid operators and Stadtwerke.
- **Customer:** Distribution-grid operators and municipal utilities with rate-base-funded grid expansion.
- **Payer and signer:** The DSO's approved investment budget (capital costs recognised in the revenue cap). Signer: head of grid construction or of procurement. Municipal Tiefbauamt for Sondervermoegen work.
- **Problem:** The bottleneck of the money is planning and supervision, not shovels. IW: about 10,000 planning and supervision experts missing; 81% of planning vacancies unfilled (Oct 2025); Tiefbau needs about 18% more capacity. BDEW: DSO investment rises from EUR 8.6B (2024) to EUR 15.4B a year by 2030. Small owner-led offices that hold local permit knowledge are retiring.
- **Solution:** Buy the offices (keep owners as employees; Aktivrente is tax-free to employees, not to the self-employed). Install one toolchain (digitised route data, standard permit packs). Sell per-route-kilometre or per-connection-batch fixed prices instead of hours.
- **Why now:** BDEW grid-investment path; the EUR 500B Sondervermoegen (about EUR 30B a year of construction orders per IW); Aktivrente from 2026; the 2025-26 shortage of planners.
- **Wedge:** Hausanschluss and Trassen planning for 3 mid-size Stadtwerke; first acquisitions are 2 offices with EUR 2-3M revenue.
- **Path to $100M:** Planning/survey/permit/supervision at about 10-12% of grid capex (my assumption) = EUR 1.5-1.85B a year by 2030, plus about EUR 1.9B on Sondervermoegen Tiefbau. EUR 87M is **5-6% of the DSO pool alone, or about 2.5% of both**. Build: 25-30 offices at EUR 3M. Purchase at 0.56-1.03x revenue = EUR 50-80M.
- **Who does it today:** Triton's formicon (Jan 2026), XPartners (Axcel, employee-majority; bought Corall Ingenieure in Aug 2026), plus large engineers (Sweco, Arcadis, Ramboll [M]) and DSO in-house teams.
- **Why they lose:** Generalist engineering roll-ups sell hours across all building types; none is organised around DSO outcome units. DSOs are rate-regulated and want predictable unit costs.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Process power (standard toolchain, permit templates), switching costs through framework contracts.
- **Earned secret:** Permit knowledge sits in the head of a 62-year-old. It is the scarce input, and the DSO will pay for certainty.
- **First 90 days:** 3 DSO pilot conversations; identify 10 retiring offices through Ingenieurkammer listings; test unit-price quotes on 5 past projects.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 6.
- **Risks to kill it:** An Aug 2026 survey says the Sondervermoegen "barely arrives" at engineering offices; a VKU poll says only 8% think the DSO target is reachable under current rules; PE is already buying.

## 5. Teamkauf (lens: dilemma, the employee-buyout umbrella)

- **One-liner:** A packaged team buyout for 3-8 employees, using the 100% state-guaranteed 077 loan per partner, a vendor note and a regional participation, plus a shared back office that stays with the firm for a fee on revenue.
- **Customer:** Retiring owners who want continuity and teams that lack equity, collateral and management bandwidth.
- **Payer and signer:** The firm pays a service fee out of operating costs; the origination fee is financed inside the purchase. Signers: the seller-owner (mandate) and the lead Geschaeftsfuehrer.
- **Problem:** KfW says EBO (28%) and MBO (24%) are considered by many but get little attention. Hausbanken lend against collateral and personal guarantees; the Steuerberater cannot take equity or a commercial role; chambers cannot charge; PE cannot do the tickets; 077 is per natural person and covers 35% of costs. Nobody puts the stack together or stays on as the finance function afterwards.
- **Solution:** A standard vehicle (a holding of employee-partners), a template financing stack (077 per partner as a "taetige Beteiligung", Buergschaftsbank-backed bank loan, MBG participation, vendor note), governance covenants, and a back office (invoicing, dunning, payroll prep, e-invoice compliance, purchasing, insurance, financing) for about 1.2% of revenue. Right of first offer on partner exit.
- **Why now:** KfW Jan 2026 (closures exceed successions; EBO and MBO rising). 077 live since Nov 2024. GmbH mit gebundenem Vermoegen key points in Feb-Mar 2026 (no draft bill confirmed).
- **Wedge:** Firms with 6-20 employees and a clear No. 2 and No. 3 in the team; first 10 via HWK Nachfolgemoderatoren and Steuerberater who already see the employee option.
- **Path to $100M:** Origination 2.5% of a EUR 400k price (EUR 10k) plus 1.2% of EUR 1.8M revenue (EUR 21.6k a year). A portfolio of about 3,900 firms gives EUR 87M of recurring fees. That is about 650 closings a year for 6 years = **1.7% of the 37,200 actual handovers** a year. The honest caution: only 123 applications for 077 were logged in Baden-Wuerttemberg in 2025, and applicants stay personally liable, which is the real reason employees say no.
- **Who does it today:** I found no dedicated German EBO packager in this run. Pieces exist: Hausbank + Buergschaftsbank + MBG + KfW 077, Steuerberater, and steward-ownership investors (Purpose Evergreen Capital [M]). UK EOT advisers and US ESOP administrators are analogues.
- **Why they lose:** Each incumbent holds one piece and a conflict (collateral culture, no equity role, no fee mandate).
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs (embedded back office, covenants, right of first offer), scale economies in shared services, counter-positioning against Steuerberater.
- **Earned secret:** Employees do not fail at buying; they fail at the 18 months after, when nobody is the finance function.
- **First 90 days:** Sign 5 sellers with a visible No. 2; write the stack with one Buergschaftsbank and one MBG; legal template; price the service fee against a part-time Buchhalter.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 7.
- **Risks to kill it:** Personal liability keeps employees out; low demand behind "considered".

## 6. Aufloesungsboerse (lens: dilemma, the closure exchange)

- **One-liner:** An orderly-closure service that sells the bundle a closing firm leaves behind: its staff, its customers, its equipment and its premises, to neighbours who are short of all four.
- **Customer:** Owners of the roughly 114,000 firms a year that plan to close with no successor; on the other side, growing firms with open positions.
- **Payer and signer:** The owner pays a wind-down fee; hiring firms pay placement fees (personnel budget); buyers pay for customer books and equipment. Signers: the closing owner and the Inhaber of the hiring firm.
- **Problem:** No incumbent earns anything on a firm worth under EUR 100k. M&A boutiques have minimum fees, Steuerberater bill hours, chambers are free and cannot operate, Sparkassen see no credit, and nexxt-change matches going concerns. Yet Handwerk has about 200,000 open positions (ZDH) and roughly 100,000 unfillable ones (KOFA 7/2026).
- **Solution:** A fixed wind-down package (Schlussbilanz and archive with a partner Steuerberater, Gewerbeabmeldung, notices, GoBD archive, run-off cover) that the owner can fund from selling the bundle. Selective hiring of staff by neighbours (not an entity transfer, to stay outside §613a BGB), customer-by-customer consent for the customer book, and an equipment/vehicle auction channel.
- **Why now:** 2026 is the first year closures exceed successions; KfW says 47% of closure planners cite no family interest and 42% cite bureaucracy.
- **Wedge:** Handwerk closures in regions with the most open positions; first 10 via HWK Betriebsberater and Steuerberater who tell clients "it is not worth anything".
- **Path to $100M:** Per client: EUR 2.5k wind-down fee (all clients) plus, for the 45% with a bundle, about EUR 13.5k (1.8 hires x EUR 5k, EUR 3k customer-book commission, EUR 1.5k equipment) = about EUR 8.6k average. EUR 87M needs about 10,100 clients a year = **about 9% of the 114,000 planned closures (about 12% of the 80,000 that actually close, my assumption)**. That is close to heroic: it works only with strong software and as the feeder for Meisterwerk and Teamkauf.
- **Who does it today:** Nobody as a bundle. Pieces: nexxt-change, HWK Betriebsboersen [M], Surplex/Troostwijk machinery auctions [M], Kleinanzeigen, the Arbeitsagentur, Steuerberater for the wind-down.
- **Why they lose:** Each earns nothing on its piece and none can sell the whole.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (closing supply meets hiring demand in the same region), plus data on regional vacancies.
- **Earned secret:** A closing firm's value is not in its balance sheet. It is its Gesellen and its customer list, and the buyers are the neighbours who cannot hire.
- **First 90 days:** Run 20 closures end to end in one region; get a labour-law opinion on selective hiring versus §613a; sign 15 hiring firms.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 8.
- **Risks to kill it:** §613a reading; customers don't follow; closures are mostly solo operators with no employees (I found no headcount data for closers).

## 7. Zulieferwerk (lens: shock, rearmament plus the auto-supplier crisis)

- **One-liner:** An acquirer-modernizer of owner-led precision-machining shops that qualifies them as second-source suppliers for defence primes.
- **Customer:** Primes and tier-1s that need machined parts (Rheinmetall says it has about 23,000 suppliers).
- **Payer and signer:** Strategic procurement at the prime, through multi-year framework supply contracts. Signer: head of purchasing or supply chain. On acquisition: the seller-owner.
- **Problem:** The constraint is qualification, not orders (quality systems such as AS/EN 9100, security clearance, traceability, first-article approval, export controls). A 58-year-old Zerspanungsunternehmer with idle 5-axis capacity cannot afford 18-36 months of qualification and has no successor.
- **Solution:** Buy the shop at 0.38-0.69x revenue, fund the qualification, bring the prime's framework contract before closing where possible, and keep the owner on.
- **Why now:** German rearmament, the auto-supplier job losses, VW Osnabrueck in talks with defence firms, Rheinmetall selling its auto division to Aequita for EUR 350M to focus on defence, IHK Stuttgart procurement seminars up from "a few" to 300-400 attendees.
- **Wedge:** Turned-part and 5-axis shops with EUR 4-8M revenue and an existing prime as customer.
- **Path to $100M:** 15 shops x EUR 6M = EUR 90M. Cost EUR 35-50M to buy plus EUR 10-15M qualification. Execution-limited, not share-limited.
- **Who does it today:** Mutares (turnarounds, defence units such as Magirus), Pinova Capital (industrial tech, EUR 10-75M revenue), Aurelius, Rheinmetall itself.
- **Why they lose:** Mutares and Aurelius buy distress, not healthy owner-led shops. Primes cannot buy suppliers without a conflict. Banks hesitate on defence exposure [M].
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (qualified capacity) and switching costs once qualified.
- **Earned secret:** Spare capacity is worthless until it is certified. Certification is what the buyer adds.
- **First 90 days:** Sit with 3 primes' purchasing teams; get a qualification-cost estimate from a lead auditor; find 5 shops.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5.
- **Risks to kill it:** The sources do not show Mittelstand gains yet (produktion.de: value distribution not clear before 2027; Stuttgarter Zeitung: auto jobs are not absorbed). This idea fails the digest's gate unless a prime commits.

## 8. Anschluss (lens: shock, the grid-connection queue meets the closure wave)

- **One-liner:** Buy the sites of closing Mittelstand plants whose value to the owner is a building, but to a battery, charging or data-centre developer is an existing grid connection.
- **Customer:** Closing manufacturers, bakeries, cold stores and workshops with a medium-voltage connection; on the other side, storage, truck-charging, light-industry and defence tenants.
- **Payer and signer:** Tenants and developers from their site budgets; signer: their head of grid or real estate.
- **Problem:** E.ON reports more than 10,000 large-battery connection requests (about 500 GW) and more than 500 data-centre projects (about 70 GW). Oldenburg IHK reports delays of years. The cabinet adopted the Netzpaket on 29 Jul 2026 (priority and reservation rules). An existing connection is the scarce input, and the owner prices a building.
- **Solution:** Buy or option the site at a price above building value, secure the Anschlussnutzung, and re-let or develop.
- **Why now:** The Netzpaket, the queue, and 114,000 closures a year.
- **Wedge:** Sites with 0.4-2 MVA connections in grid-constrained regions; first 10 via HWK, IHK and local Steuerberater.
- **Path to $100M:** 150 sites a year at EUR 1.2M = EUR 180M a year; after 6 years about EUR 1.08B at 8% yield on cost = EUR 87M. Assumption: 3-5% of the 114,000 closers have a connection of 400 kVA or more, so 150 a year is 3-4% of that group.
- **Who does it today:** Developers buy land directly; DSOs; real-estate developers. I found no one systematically buying closing SMEs for connection rights (negative result).
- **Why they lose:** Developers look at greenfield; closers don't know what they have; DSOs cannot trade capacity.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (connection rights), speed.
- **Earned secret:** The queue is long, and a closing neighbour has already waited in it.
- **First 90 days:** A written opinion from a grid lawyer on whether capacity survives owner change and change of use under the Netzpaket; survey 50 closures for connection size; sign a BESS developer for 3 sites.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 9.
- **Risks to kill it:** The legal premise: whether connection capacity can be kept, transferred or reused. If it lapses on closure or on change of use, the idea is dead. Overlaps with Werkhof as a real-estate model.

## Ideas I considered and dropped

- A KfW-guaranteed acquisition lender or mezzanine bank: the 077 loan carries about a bank margin only, a EUR 87M revenue line needs a EUR 1.7B+ book, and creditshelf failed in this exact segment. Dead as a startup.
- A valuation/readiness score on DATEV data: wevalue and DATEV Vermoegensnachfolge are already inside the Steuerberater workflow.
- A listing marketplace: nexxt-change is free and being rebuilt by KfW.
- Pension-liability transfer for owner-managers: the pain is real (Rentner-GmbH works since BMF 2017) but I found no count, and fees would need heroic share.
- Inheritance-tax rush: BVerfG 1 BvR 804/22 is pending and the SPD paper targets firms above EUR 5M, which is above the typical Mittelstand deal.

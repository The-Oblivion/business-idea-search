# P007 Japan contracted road freight (~$110B): 8 ideas (round 2)

Written 2026-10-18. About 42 searches and 7 fetches (the brief said 8-15; I went over because this pool is contested and first answers on size, competitors and legal premises kept coming back thin). FX: 1 USD = 150 JPY, so $100M = JPY 15B. Evidence tags: [V] read in a primary document this run, [S] search-result summary only (not opened), [M] from memory, [D] my derivation.

Reality check that applies to every idea: all eight need a Japanese operating partner or co-founder (permits, labour law, customer trust); none can be run from outside Japan.

## Pool facts used

- Size: about 63,000 operators x JPY 264M average revenue (JTA FY2023 analysis, n=2,451) = about JPY 16.6T ($110B). FY2023 operating margin 0.6% (1.0% in FY2024); 51% of operators profitable at operating level; 52% of operators with 10 or fewer vehicles lose money [S].
- Standard fare revised 22 Mar 2024, effective 1 Jun 2024: +~8% on average; a separate 10% subcontract fee may be collected; loading/unloading fee for medium trucks JPY 2,180 (machine) or 2,100 (manual) per 30 minutes; +50% once waiting plus loading exceeds 2 hours [V, lnews.jp].
- MLIT FY2025 survey (published Jul 2026, typical day Apr-Aug 2025): average duty time per run 10h13m; waiting plus loading and ancillary work 2h02m (waiting 1h00, loading 41m, ancillary 21m [S]); down 1h16m from FY2024; government target is 2 hours or less [V, MLIT press release].
- Toriteki Act (in force 1 Jan 2026): shipper-to-carrier transport entrustment now covered; 60-day cash payment from receipt; notes banned (and receivables that cannot be cashed in full by the due date); consultation required before a unilateral price; fines up to JPY 500k; MLIT/JFTC joint shipper patrols Oct-Nov 2025 [S].
- Trucking Business Act (Acts 60 and 61 of 2025) [V, MLIT page]: written contracts and the actual-carrier register since 1 Apr 2025; from 1 Apr 2026 they extend to all forwarders using trucks as prime, plus a duty of EFFORT (not a ban) to stop at two tiers; white-truck use by shippers punishable from 1 Apr 2026; 'appropriate cost' floor and permit renewal take effect by cabinet order within three years of promulgation (mid-2028). Expert panel opened 17 Jul 2026; trade press expects the notification in 2027 with a one-year grace [S].
- Logistics Efficiency Act: about 3,200 designated shippers (3,299 notified), CLO at executive level, first mid/long-term plan due end Oct 2026, periodic reports from Jul 2027, fine up to JPY 1M for failing to appoint a CLO [S]. CLO software and consulting is already a red ocean (Hacobell dashboard 28 Aug 2026, Transcosmos from JPY 400k/month, ABeam, Sagawa Global Logistics, Logisteed) [S].
- Multi-tier survey (METI/MLIT/MAFF via JTA, 2023, n=4,401): 74% re-subcontract; pass-down rates 4% at 100%+, 28% at 95-99%, 42% at 90-94%, 14% at 85-89%, 7% at 80-84%, 5% below 80% (my reading of a chart) [S].
- Capacity: NX Research Institute shortfall 14.2% (2024), 34.1% (2030) without action [S]. Load factor 39.1% in FY2025, down 2.3 points [S]. Trucking bankruptcies 328 (FY2024) and 321 (FY2025, 4th highest ever, TDB) [S].
- Competitors: Hacobu (MOVO Berth at 37,000+ sites, 57.2% berth-management share, JPY 5.6B raised; no revenue disclosed), Hacobell (owned by Seino HD, Fukuyama, Sankyu and others), Trabox (Visional; 20,000+ carrier members; Finto factoring at 2-9.5%), CBcloud PickGo (~70k light vans, ~40k motorbikes, ~3,400 general-freight partners), T2 (autonomous trucking) [S].


## 1. Dock Crews with a Turn-Time Guarantee (lens: insider, outside-the-box 6/10)

- **One-liner:** Sell distribution-center docks a guaranteed 30-minute truck turn, delivered by trained loading crews that cost under half the standard-fare price of the same minutes and give the driver his capped hours back.
- **Customer:** Distribution-center and plant docks of the ~3,300 designated shippers and their big receivers (food and daily-goods wholesalers, building materials, retail DCs) where drivers still hand-load.
- **Payer and signer:** Shipper or consignee logistics budget (outsourced dock operations, or the loading fee they now owe carriers under the 2024 standard fare). Signer: DC director (butsuryu center-cho) up to roughly JPY 20M a year per site; the CLO (executive-level by law) above that.
- **Problem:** MLIT's FY2025 survey: the average driver spends 1h00 waiting plus 41 min loading plus 21 min on ancillary work every duty day (2h02 total, target <=2h), inside a hard hours cap. A driver-hour earns the truck about JPY 5,400 of revenue (my arithmetic: JPY 16.6T / ~300M duty days / 10.2h), while a loader-hour costs about JPY 2,500. The 2024 standard fare prices loading at JPY 2,100-2,180 per 30 min (about JPY 4,300 an hour), so every minute a driver loads is the most expensive labour in the building, and free driver loading is increasingly a violation.
- **Solution:** A crew company contracting (as ukeoi, a work contract, not worker dispatch) to turn trucks at a named dock inside an SLA: loaded or unloaded within 30 min of dock-in, or the next turn is free. Crews are cross-trained (forklift, hand-load, scan, pallet build), carry handhelds, and are priced per truck-turn (about JPY 3,000). Turn time is verified from the carrier's digital-tachograph or GPS timestamps, the same data the shipper must now report to MLIT. Over time we sell ASN/pallet-ready loading standards that cut crew minutes per turn. Legal premise to clear first: ukeoi versus dispatch (sham-contract risk) and the dispatch ban at port docks [from memory, verify with a labour consultant].
- **Why now:** MLIT's FY2025 survey (published Jul 2026) shows waiting+loading fell 1h16m in one year to 2h02: enforcement works and the last hour is now the shipper's problem. The Toriteki Act (1 Jan 2026) added shipper-to-carrier transport entrustment; MLIT and JFTC ran joint shipper patrols in Oct-Nov 2025; JFTC warned 779 shippers in FY2025; a revised logistics special designation takes effect 1 Apr 2027 [secondary source]. The FY2026 minimum wage (JPY 1,177 average, Tokyo 1,280) is still far below the JPY 4,300/hour fare benchmark.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** One Kanto food or daily-goods wholesale DC with chronic >90-minute turns: 8-10 crew on one shift for a 90-day pilot, turn time measured from carriers' tachograph timestamps. First 10 customers come from 3 mid-size carriers whose drivers lose the most hours at those docks (the carriers champion it; the DC pays).
- **Path to $100M:** JPY 15B / ~JPY 2,500 per crew-hour = 6.0M crew-hours a year (~3,100 FTE) = ~1.9% of the ~310M driver-hours spent on loading and ancillary work (62 min x ~300M duty days). The 300M duty-day count is my estimate (about 1.5M commercial trucks from a secondary source x 80% active x 250 days). Equivalent to ~5M truck-turns at JPY 3,000, i.e. 1.7% of duty days. Price build: JPY 1,481 average warehouse/factory wage (Mynavi, Aug 2026) +25% on-costs +15% supervision and equipment, 15% margin = ~JPY 2,500. Caveat: revenue is gross labour pass-through, so gross profit is ~$15M at $100M revenue.
- **Who does it today:** Timee (spot work: JPY 21.0B revenue in the six months to Apr 2026, logistics GMV +22.9%, 12.7M registered workers); warehouse staffing agencies (Recruit Staffing, Pasona; from memory); 3PL in-house labour (Logisteed, Sagawa Global Logistics, NX); shipper DC staff; and drivers doing it unpaid. I found nobody selling a per-truck-turn SLA.
- **Why they lose:** Timee and agencies sell hours or take-rate, so their incentive is more hours, not fewer minutes per turn. 3PLs bundle labour into warehouse contracts and will not price it per truck for other firms' carriers. Shippers' own DC staff are fully loaded with picking.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Process power (turn-time SLA backed by tachograph timestamps, a crew training standard) plus scale economies from crew density (a shared bench across neighbouring DCs). Counter-positioning against hourly staffing. Honest rating: modest; the moat is operating density, not IP.
- **Earned secret:** A dispatcher prices a driver-hour at what the truck earns (about JPY 5,400), not at the driver's wage, and since April 2024 that hour cannot be bought back (960-hour overtime cap, duty-day limits). Every dispatcher already routes around bad docks and lets drivers load for free. The standard fare quietly put a price (JPY 2,100-2,180 per 30 min) on the one thing nobody sells: a cheaper hour. The buyer who benefits is the shipper, who otherwise owes the carrier ~JPY 4,300/hour or loses the truck.
- **First 90 days:** Find a Japanese operator co-founder with a Kanto DC contact (a foreign team cannot run this alone). Weeks 1-3: collect tachograph dwell data from 3 carriers and rank 20 docks by loading minutes. Weeks 3-6: sign one wholesale DC to a 90-day ukeoi pilot at ~JPY 3,000 per turn, with a labour consultant (sharoushi) checking ukeoi versus dispatch. Weeks 6-13: 8-10 crew on one shift; publish the before/after turn-time as the evidence for that shipper's CLO plan.

## 2. Open-Book Tier-1: Floor Plus Ten Percent, Carriers Free, Paid in Three Days (lens: insider, outside-the-box 4/10)

- **One-liner:** A registered forwarder that contracts directly with shippers as the single legal tier-1, posts its margin at the statutory 10% fee, and pays the actual carrier within 3 days, so the layered 5-10% per-tier markup disappears and the two-tier rule and register are satisfied by design.
- **Customer:** Mid-size shippers (JPY 0.3-5B freight spend, mostly below or near the 90,000-tonne designated line) and the 63,000 carriers who do the actual hauling.
- **Payer and signer:** Shipper freight budget (unchanged line, fewer layers). Signer: logistics manager or procurement head; the CLO at designated shippers.
- **Problem:** 74% of operators re-subcontract (METI/MLIT/MAFF survey of 4,401 JTA members, 2023). Of those passing work down, 42% pay 90-94% of what they received and 28% pay 95-99%; only ~12% pay below 85%. A shipper paying through two intermediaries pays ~19% over what the actual carrier receives, while the actual carrier is squeezed (SME Agency: trucking price pass-through 36.5%, 27th of 30 industries). From 1 Apr 2026 every forwarder must issue written terms and keep the actual-carrier register, with a duty of effort to stop at two tiers; the legally binding 'appropriate cost' floor follows by mid-2028.
- **Solution:** Register as a Type-1 freight forwarder using trucks (a registration, not a trucking permit; 1,671 such road forwarders were registered in Kyushu alone at Mar 2025 [S]; verify the exact requirements with a gyoseishoshi). Shipper pays the cost-based rate (the standard fare now, the MLIT formula when it lands) plus an 8% posted fee (the 2024 standard fare itself allows a separate 10% subcontract fee). Carriers join free, see the price before accepting, and are paid in 3 days from our balance sheet (asset-based line; the shipper pays in 30-60 days). The platform is the compliance file: e-contract with itemised charges, register entry, permit verification (white-truck liability for shippers began 1 Apr 2026), and a delivery-receipt timestamp that starts the 60-day clock.
- **Why now:** Register and written-contract duties extend to all forwarders on 1 Apr 2026; 2-tier effort duty same date; white-truck use is punishable for shippers from 1 Apr 2026; Toriteki (1 Jan 2026) made 60-day cash payment and price consultation enforceable for shipper-to-carrier work; notes are ending (most banks stopped issuing them 30 Sep 2026, the clearing house closes 31 Mar 2027); the appropriate-cost floor will make 'cost plus a posted fee' the only defensible price.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** Two lanes where shippers visibly pay three tiers (e.g., Kanto-Kansai building materials, Hokkaido-Tohoku food). Target 10 mid-size shippers that received JFTC warning letters (779 shippers in FY2025) and want a clean tier-1, and pre-recruit ~300 carriers on those lanes with the 3-day-pay promise.
- **Path to $100M:** At an 8% posted fee, $100M (JPY 15B) of revenue = JPY 187.5B of freight moved ($1.25B) = 1.1% of the JPY 16.6T pool (0.9% at 10%). Scale reference: Trabox reports 230k+ cases a month. Unit economics are thin: 8% fee less ~0.3% float cost (45 days at 2.5%), ~0.3% bad debt, ~0.5% claims and ~3% operations leaves ~4% contribution, so $4M at $100M revenue. Digital freight brokers lost money in the US; this only works if shippers actually collapse tiers and we keep claims and credit tight.
- **Who does it today:** Hacobell (shareholders include Seino HD, Fukuyama Transporting, Sankyu, Nippon Logitem, Japan Post Capital, SOMPO Growth Partners; bought the Truck-bo reservation business in 2024; CLO dashboard launched 28 Aug 2026); Trabox (Visional group: 20,000+ carrier members, Finto factoring at 2-9.5%); CBcloud PickGo (~70k light-van, ~40k motorbike, ~3,400 general-freight partners; raised again Apr 2026); thousands of registered forwarders; the big 3PLs (NX, Logisteed, Sagawa).
- **Why they lose:** Hacobell's owners are carriers and forwarders whose margin is the layering, so an open-book posted fee cannibalises them. Trabox's model rests on carrier-paid membership [from memory] and on lending to the same carriers at 2-9.5%; a carrier-free, 3-day-pay model attacks both. CBcloud is built for light vans and last mile. The big 3PLs own trucks and cannot be neutral.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (carrier liquidity from free membership and 3-day pay), switching costs (register, contracts and receipt timestamps become the shipper's legal compliance file), counter-positioning (open-book fee versus unposted layers). Honest: thin contribution, credit and claims sit on our balance sheet.
- **Earned secret:** A forwarder's pricing desk knows the margin is not for matching. It buys two risks the shipper cannot see: the forwarder pays the carrier whether or not the shipper has paid, and it quietly re-tenders failed pickups down the chain. The 2024 standard fare legitimised an explicit 10% fee for exactly this. A shipper paying through two intermediaries is paying about 19% above what the actual carrier gets (92% pass-down compounded); selling the same credit and failed-tender cover at a posted 8% beats the unposted 15-20%.
- **First 90 days:** Find a Japanese forwarder-licence holder as co-founder or acquire a small registered forwarder (registration takes months). Weeks 1-4: interview 15 pricing desks and 30 carriers on two lanes; get an asset-based credit line term sheet from a regional bank or lease company. Weeks 4-13: sign 3 shippers on a pilot tender (cost-plus-8%), pay 50 carriers in 3 days, and produce each shipper's register and itemised-charge file as the deliverable.

## 3. The Anchor Roll-Up: Buy the Permits the Cost Floor Will Strand (lens: pattern, outside-the-box 3/10)

- **One-liner:** Acquire 50-60 successor-less regional carriers as permit renewal, the cost floor and the register duties hit all at once, merge them into a few 80-plus-truck 'anchor' operators that can be tier-1 on their own, and lift margin by collapsing tiers and re-pricing to the legal floor.
- **Customer:** Owners of sub-scale carriers (mostly under 20 trucks) with no successor; the demand side is shippers that need a compliant tier-1 inside two tiers.
- **Payer and signer:** Capital from independent-sponsor or regional-bank-linked funds (the shareholder lists of Hacobell show JA Mitsui Leasing, SOMPO Growth Partners and Japan Post Capital already invest in this pool) plus senior debt from regional banks and shinkin. Signer: the fund's investment-committee partner; each seller's president signs the share transfer.
- **Problem:** The pool is pulverised: 63,000 operators, FY2023 operating margin 0.6% (1.0% in FY2024), only 51% profitable at operating level, 52% of operators with 10 or fewer vehicles lose money (JTA analysis, n=2,451). TSR 2025 puts the successor-absent rate in transport at 57.3%. Since Oct 2015 only 711 trucking corporations disappeared by merger and 267 passed to third parties, and acquirers were far bigger (median 80 vehicles versus 12 for the industry). Now permit renewal and the appropriate-cost floor arrive together (by mid-2028), on top of written-contract, register and hours-cap burdens.
- **Solution:** Buy at net assets plus about one year of EBITDA (a 2026 listing for a JPY 250M-revenue Kanto carrier asks net assets plus 0.9x EBITDA, roughly 0.3x revenue), keep the permits, drivers and shipper relationships, and merge into regional anchors with their own tier-1 contracts and a shared back office. Margin levers: collapse tiers on ~20-30% of volume (7-8% retention per tier), re-price to the floor, backhaul density, group purchasing of fuel, tyres and insurance, and one back office. Adjacent businesses in this file (dock crews, the open-book tier-1) can run on the anchor's permits and trucks. This is not an AI-margin thesis.
- **Why now:** Permit renewal and 'appropriate cost' take effect by cabinet order within three years of promulgation (mid-2028); MLIT's expert panel opened 17 Jul 2026 and trade press expects the notification in 2027 with a one-year grace period; the 1 Apr 2026 register and 2-tier-effort duties; 321 trucking bankruptcies in FY2025 (4th highest ever, labour shortage cited in 55) show the stress.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** One region (Tokai or Tohoku) where a mid-size shipper cluster already complains about tiers. First deal: a 20-40 truck carrier at about 0.3x revenue; second and third within six months. First 10 sellers come from broker listings (Batonz, Tranbi, Nihon M&A Center) and regional trucking-association contacts.
- **Path to $100M:** JPY 15B = ~57 average-size operators (JPY 264M each) = 0.9% of the JPY 16.6T pool. Purchase at ~0.3x revenue = ~JPY 4.5B ($30M) plus ~JPY 1.3B working capital; if ~1% of the 63,000 firms sell each year (my assumption, ~630), we need ~9% of annual sellers. Honest flag: the revenue path is easy, the profit path is thin. At 0.6-1% EBIT, $100M of revenue earns $0.6-1.0M; it is worth buying only if the levers lift EBIT to about 5% ($5M), and there is no evidence yet that they do.
- **Who does it today:** Strategic buyers: SBS Holdings, Hamakyorex, Trancom, Seino, Fukuyama and big 3PLs (the acquirers were mostly trucking companies; listed parents directly took over only 22 of the 711 disappearing firms). Intermediaries: Nihon M&A Center, Batonz, Tranbi. I found no private-equity trucking roll-up. 'AI roll-up of a legacy services industry' is on the cliche list, so the angle must be regulation and tier collapse, not software.
- **Why they lose:** Strategics buy for geography or fleet and integrate slowly; they do not want 0.6%-margin 10-truck firms. PE cannot underwrite 1% margins at their usual leverage. Brokers sell but do not operate.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies (purchasing, back office, density) and a cornered resource (permits become scarcer once renewal begins). Honest: weak and execution-dependent; the edge is buying before the floor is priced in.
- **Earned secret:** The owner is not selling a trucking business; he is selling a permit, a Safety Excellence mark, trained drivers and shipper relationships that shippers need to stay inside the two-tier effort rule. Prices are low because buyers capitalise a 0.6% margin, not because the permits are worth little. Renewal plus the floor is a forced re-rating that a patient buyer with a balance sheet can underwrite.
- **First 90 days:** With a Japanese co-founder and an independent-sponsor structure: weeks 1-4 list every listed carrier for sale in one region and screen by permit status, vehicles and customer concentration; weeks 4-8 meet five owners and agree one non-binding offer at net assets plus 0.9x EBITDA; weeks 8-13 sign a regional-bank facility term sheet and begin legal and MLIT transfer-approval diligence on the first target.

## 4. The Unit-Load Loop: RFID Rental Pallets for the Decade the Government Mandates T11 (lens: pattern, outside-the-box 6/10)

- **One-liner:** Build the second national rental-pallet pool, with every pallet tagged so its location and last handler are known, priced per use, with collection on trucks' empty backhauls, to meet the FY2030 standard-pallet targets.
- **Customer:** Food, daily-goods and beverage manufacturers and the wholesaler or retail DCs that receive from them; secondarily produce shippers (MAFF's produce guideline names T11 rental pallets).
- **Payer and signer:** Shipper logistics or packaging budget (rental per use). Signer: logistics manager; CLO at designated shippers, where palletisation is a named plan lever.
- **Problem:** Hand-loading is why loading plus ancillary work still takes ~62 minutes per duty day. Japan's pallets are ~500M, of which T11 is only ~30% (JPR's president); the government FY2030 targets are T11 share of pallet production 26% to 50%+, T11 share of the rental fleet 76% to 85%+, and handling time per worker-year 375h to 315h. Palletisation is a duty of effort for all shippers since April 2025.
- **Solution:** A rental pool of T11 pallets tagged with RFID or BLE, per-use pricing, a receiver-confirmation scan at each DC dock, and collection loops routed on carriers' empty return legs (carriers are paid per pallet returned). Tags convert the pool's biggest cost, unreturned and mis-exchanged pallets, into a billing event, and give each shipper pallet-use data for its CLO plan.
- **Why now:** Revised Logistics Efficiency Act: shipper duty of effort on palletisation and ASN from Apr 2025; designated shippers must file mid/long-term plans (first due end Oct 2026) and periodic reports from Jul 2027; FY2030 numeric pallet targets; the instant-noodle industry (2022) and MAFF produce guideline already name T11/T12 standards; MLIT's FY2025 survey shows loading time is the remaining hour.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** Segments with an existing standardisation plan: instant-noodle makers and produce shipping hubs (JA). First 10 customers: 3 produce hubs, 2 instant-noodle makers and the DCs they deliver to, so both ends of each loop are on the pool.
- **Path to $100M:** At JPR's yield (about JPY 30B revenue / 12M pallets = JPY 2,500 per pallet-year, or ~JPY 540 per supply across 55.6M supplies in FY2025), JPY 15B needs ~6M pallets, i.e. half of JPR's fleet or 114% of UPR's 5.28M pallets, or ~28M uses a year. Capex is ~JPY 24B ($160M) at JPY 4,000 a pallet, financeable as asset-backed leases. Unverified: the national rental-fleet size, so I cannot compute the share of market growth required.
- **Who does it today:** JPR (Japan Pallet Rental; ~JPY 30B revenue, ~12M pallets, ~60 depots, shared-collection system, supplies +4.7% in FY2025) and UPR (TSE 7065; 5.28M pallets as of Nov 2025), plus shippers' own pallets and white wooden pallets. [JPR figures are from a search summary; the primary PDF returned 403.]
- **Why they lose:** The incumbents earn on pallet-days and depot service and, I suspect, cannot see where a pallet is between depots, so they price in loss [unverified; the first thing to test]. They are capex-constrained and conservative, while the targets roughly require the rental pool to grow severalfold.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies (depot density, procurement), network economies (loop density: a pallet is only valuable where both ends are on the pool) and a cornered resource (pallet positions inside DCs). Honest: the capital need is the real barrier, and incumbents can add tags.
- **Earned secret:** A berth manager sees that docks do not jam on slots; they jam on mixed, hand-stacked loads and the receiving check. The quickest way to cut minutes is a unit load that arrives identified. And a pallet pool lives or dies on whether the pallet comes back: the real loss is the mis-exchanged pallet at the receiving dock where nobody is accountable. Whoever can name the last handler of every pallet wins the loop. [Insider claim; verify with two DC managers before building.]
- **First 90 days:** Weeks 1-3: interview 10 DC receiving managers and the JPR/UPR customer experience on losses and exchanges. Weeks 3-8: price a 20,000-pallet tagged pilot with a pallet maker and RFID supplier; secure a lease partner. Weeks 8-13: sign a closed loop (one instant-noodle maker, its two DCs) with receiver-scan billing and a loss-rate guarantee.

## 5. The Carrier's Advocate: Contingency Recovery of Price, Waiting and Handling Charges (lens: dilemma, outside-the-box 7/10)

- **One-liner:** A contingency firm that builds the evidence file, sends the legally required price-consultation requests and keeps 20% of the verified uplift: the one product every shipper-paid platform in this pool is structurally unable to sell.
- **Customer:** Carriers with 20 or more trucks (0-1% margin, shippers paying below the standard fare), later forwarders caught in the same squeeze.
- **Payer and signer:** The carrier's president (daihyo torishimariyaku), paid out of recovered money; no budget line is needed because the fee is contingent on a verified uplift.
- **Problem:** MLIT's FY2025 fare survey (2026): ~90% of operators negotiated, ~70% got some understanding, only ~35% collected 80% or more of the 2024 standard fare. SME Agency: trucking price pass-through 36.5% (27th of 30 industries). A 2017 MLIT finding: ~30% of operators could not collect waiting and accessorial fees. The FY2025 MLIT survey shows ~1h00 of waiting and ~62 min of handling per duty day. Since 1 Jan 2026 the Toriteki Act bars setting price unilaterally once the carrier asks to consult, requires cash payment within 60 days of receipt, bans notes, and now covers shipper-to-carrier transport directly; JFTC warned 779 shippers in FY2025.
- **Solution:** (1) Ingest the carrier's own data (tachograph, invoices, delivery slips, fuel and tolls). (2) Build lane-level cost evidence against the standard fare and, later, the appropriate-cost formula, and compute dwell and unbilled handling per shipper site. (3) Generate time-stamped consultation requests and rate-card amendments for the carrier to send, and track every response. (4) If refused, assemble the complaint file for MLIT's Truck/Logistics G-men or the JFTC. (5) Charge 20% of verified uplift for 24 months, then an automatic annual cost escalator. Legal premise: the carrier sends and negotiates itself (no legal representation, Attorneys Act art. 72) and drafting government filings for a fee may touch the Administrative Scriveners Act; partner with a law firm and a scrivener [from memory, verify].
- **Why now:** Toriteki in force 1 Jan 2026 (price-consultation duty, 60 days, notes banned, specified transport entrustment); revised logistics special designation effective 1 Apr 2027 [secondary source]; the 2024 standard fare gave loading and waiting a price; the appropriate-cost floor is coming by mid-2028; capacity is short (34.1% gap by 2030), so carriers hold more leverage than at any time since deregulation.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** Carriers of 30-100 trucks serving the 779 shippers who got JFTC warning letters. First 10 customers via trucking-association chapters and one trade-press launch; start with unbilled waiting and handling (the evidence is already in the tachograph).
- **Path to $100M:** A 20% fee on a 3-8% uplift earns 0.6-1.6% of client revenue a year, so JPY 15B needs clients representing JPY 0.94-2.5T of freight revenue (5.6-15% of the pool; central case 5% uplift = JPY 1.5T = 9%), i.e. roughly 3,600-9,500 average-size operators. That is heroic: even the central case needs ~9% of the entire pool's revenue as paying clients (about 1,500-2,500 carriers if we focus on JPY 500M-1B-revenue firms). Treat as a high-upside, high-variance idea.
- **Who does it today:** Consultancies (KPMG sells a one-off 'fair freight rate' tool), the Japan Trucking Association (a free standard-fare calculator and rate-notification support), law firms, the free MLIT/JFTC hotlines, and Trabox (added compliance features for the new law in Nov 2025). Shipper-paid platforms (Hacobu MOVO, Hacobell CLO dashboard) serve the other side. I found no contingency-priced carrier-side service.
- **Why they lose:** Hacobu and Hacobell are paid by shippers and cannot invoice against their own customers; Trabox needs shipper postings on its boards; JTA is a collective with shipper-side members; consultancies sell hours and avoid contingency; a law firm cannot economically handle a JPY 300M-revenue carrier.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning (channel conflict), a cornered-resource database of what shippers actually pay by lane and what they concede when pushed, and evidence-process power. Honest: switching costs are low once the uplift is booked, so churn after 24 months must be offset by the escalator.
- **Earned secret:** Carriers do not under-bill because they cannot compute; they under-bill because the shipper's account manager is also their only source of loads. A third party that carries the evidence and takes the fight removes the carrier's personal exposure. The old Act already banned retaliation for reporting [verify that it carried over], and with 34% capacity missing by 2030 the carrier holds real leverage; he just does not want to be the one using it.
- **First 90 days:** Weeks 1-3: recruit 5 friendly carriers and ingest 12 months of tachograph and invoice data. Weeks 3-8: build the lane-level evidence file and the standard-fare gap; send the first consultation requests (carrier-signed). Weeks 8-13: book the first uplift and one dwell-charge settlement; write the playbook with a law firm that blesses the contingency structure.

## 6. The Neutral Clean Room for Competing Shippers' Joint Trucks (lens: dilemma, outside-the-box 6/10)

- **One-liner:** A trusted third party that holds lane-level shipment data from competing shippers, finds shared loads, books the trucks and settles the savings 70/30, with antitrust-safe aggregation.
- **Customer:** Designated shippers in food, beverage, daily goods, paper and building materials whose rivals ship similar goods on the same lanes.
- **Payer and signer:** The CLO of each participating shipper; gainshare from verified savings (so no new budget line is needed). Subsidy support is available for certified joint-transport plans (verify current MLIT/METI programmes).
- **Problem:** The commercial-truck load factor was 39.1% in FY2025 (down 2.3 points as relay transport and fewer backhauls pushed it lower); designated shippers must file plans showing efficiency gains. F-LINE (Ajinomoto, Kagome, Nisshin Oillio, Nisshin Foods, House, Mizkan) cut deliveries 16% and about 1,000 four-tonne trucks in its first three months in Hokkaido (per an excerpt of a paywalled Toyo Keizai article) and reports 15-19% CO2 reduction (19.1% in an MLIT award document), but it took a bespoke multi-year joint venture per region. I found no neutral shipper-to-shipper matching service.
- **Solution:** A clean room run by a neutral operator: shippers upload lane-level shipments (origin, destination, weight, time window) under a data charter; the operator publishes only aggregated lane-level opportunities, never customer-level volumes or prices; it books carriers, runs the consolidated truck and settles savings 70/30 (shipper/operator) after audit. Start with pre-consultation with the JFTC and a signed data-governance charter.
- **Why now:** Load factor fell in FY2025 as the hours cap forced relay; designated shippers must file mid/long-term plans (first due end Oct 2026) and annual reports from Jul 2027; joint transport is a named CLO plan lever; the JFTC's own older guidance treats logistics collaboration as low antitrust risk unless it restricts price, quantity or customers.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** Hokkaido, Kyushu and Tohoku mass-market food and daily goods lanes where F-LINE-type savings are proven and 3-4 rivals already sit on the same lane. First 10 customers: the 6 F-LINE members' competitors plus beverage and paper makers recruited through their CLOs.
- **Path to $100M:** JPY 15B at 30% of verified savings = JPY 50B of savings; at an F-LINE-like 15% saving that is JPY 333B of jointly shipped spend = 2.0% of the JPY 16.6T pool. Plausible only if shared lanes become standard for the top 3,300 shippers; the savings rate on non-food lanes is unproven.
- **Who does it today:** F-LINE (six-company JV since 2015-16, expanded to Chugoku and Tohoku), shipper-formed co-delivery JVs, LTL consolidators (Seino, Fukuyama and other tokutsumi networks), 3PL pooled-distribution products (Logisteed, NX, Sagawa). No neutral matching service found.
- **Why they lose:** 3PLs and LTL carriers sell capacity, not neutrality; no shipper will upload a rival-sensitive lane to a competitor's 3PL. Consultancies design but do not operate. Each JV takes years and serves only its members.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (partner shippers per lane), counter-positioning (neutral operator), cornered resource (JFTC pre-consultation clearance plus a signed data-governance charter), and lane data that improves matching.
- **Earned secret:** The blocker is not the Antimonopoly Act; it is that no neutral party holds both rivals' lane data and a settlement mechanism. A dispatcher on the ground sees rivals' half-empty trucks passing each other on the same lane every night; the information exists but nobody is trusted to join it.
- **First 90 days:** Weeks 1-4: JFTC pre-consultation design and a data charter; recruit 3 shippers (two rivals plus one neutral category). Weeks 4-8: aggregate 6 months of lane data in a clean room and find the 10 best shared lanes. Weeks 8-13: run one shared lane with a single carrier and audit the savings.

## 7. The Capacity Reservation Exchange for Shippers Who Cannot Afford a Failed Tender (lens: shock, outside-the-box 7/10)

- **One-liner:** A reservation book where carriers sell committed truck-days on defined lanes for a retainer and shippers buy assured capacity with a substitution guarantee, priced from the platform's own tender-acceptance data.
- **Customer:** Designated shippers and large mid-market shippers with peak-season exposure (food, retail, consumer goods, building materials).
- **Payer and signer:** Freight budget (a new reservation-fee line inside the same total). Signer: the CLO or head of logistics, who now has a legal duty to plan for sustainable transport capacity.
- **Problem:** NX Research Institute estimates a 14.2% capacity shortfall in 2024 and 34.1% by 2030 without action; Hokkaido METI's survey (Feb 2025) found more than 30% of firms already feel the shortage and more than half say hours-cap-driven shortage has materialised. In shortages, failed tenders are re-tendered down the chain at spot premiums, and a missed peak (December, March, Obon) costs a lost sale, not just a higher rate. Carriers, on a 0.6% margin, carry idle truck-hours as pure fixed cost.
- **Solution:** A pooled book of carriers' committed truck-days per lane and week, paid a retainer for availability; shippers buy assured volume with an SLA; a defaulting carrier is replaced from the pool (substitution, not cash payout). The premium is priced from the platform's own tender-acceptance history. Legal premises to clear: our pricing is set unilaterally by the platform (carriers must not collectively fix prices, Antimonopoly Act); the contract is an availability service, not insurance (Insurance Business Act); every price stays at or above the future appropriate-cost floor; 60-day payment.
- **Why now:** CLO appointment and first plans due end Oct 2026 make capacity a board-level item; the 2024 hours cap is permanent; the appropriate-cost floor (mid-2028) removes the cheap-capacity option; capacity gap forecasts of 34.1% by 2030.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** Peak-sensitive shippers on 5-10 trunk lanes (Tokyo-Osaka, Fukuoka-Osaka, Hokkaido-Tohoku). First 10 customers: shippers that had service failures last December and whose CLO needs a documented fix; pre-recruit carriers with idle weekday capacity.
- **Path to $100M:** At a 4% net take on reserved fare (about 1.5% premium margin plus 2.5% handling), JPY 15B needs JPY 375B of reserved fare = 2.3% of the pool. Willingness to pay is untested: I found no survey of shipper tender-refusal rates (the closest evidence is the forecast gap and the Hokkaido survey). Treat as speculative and sensitive to a loosening market.
- **Who does it today:** Big 3PLs (NX, Yamato, Sagawa, Seino, Fukuyama) provide capacity assurance implicitly inside bundled contracts; spot and matching platforms (Hacobell, Trabox, CBcloud) sell no commitment; consultancies advise on contracts. I found no exchange-style instrument.
- **Why they lose:** 3PLs sell assurance only bundled with their warehouses and trucks; spot boards have no commitment mechanism; carrier associations cannot coordinate prices without antitrust risk.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (a committed-capacity book gets more valuable with each lane), a tender-acceptance data advantage, and trust. Honest: the model can be copied by a 3PL once WTP is proven.
- **Earned secret:** The CLO's pain is not the average rate; it is the one failed tender at peak that stops a production line or empties a shelf, and he will pay to remove variance. Carriers will take a discount for guaranteed utilisation because the hours cap turned idle truck-hours into pure fixed cost. A retainer is the cheapest working-capital product a 0.6%-margin carrier can get.
- **First 90 days:** Weeks 1-4: interview 20 CLOs on last year's failed tenders and what they paid to fix them (this tests WTP before building). Weeks 4-8: recruit 40 carriers on 3 lanes, set retainers by data. Weeks 8-13: run one peak-week pilot with two shippers and measure tender acceptance versus spot.

## 8. The Relay Exchange: One Contract, One Settlement, Three Carriers (lens: shock, outside-the-box 6/10)

- **One-liner:** A neutral relay operator that standardises hand-offs between competing carriers: one shipper contract, leg-by-leg carriage terms, liability at the swap point and instant fare splitting, built on existing DCs and truck stations so the shipper-facing chain stays legal.
- **Customer:** Shippers and carriers on trunk lanes of 300 km and up (Tokyo-Nagoya-Osaka-Kyushu, Tohoku-Hokkaido) where the hours cap blocks the one-driver overnight run.
- **Payer and signer:** Per-relay fee paid out of the freight budget by the shipper or the tier-1 carrier. Signer: operations director of the tier-1 carrier or the shipper's logistics manager. Offsetting subsidy: MLIT-certified relay hubs get five years of property-tax relief and planning-cost subsidies.
- **Problem:** The 2024 overtime cap (960 hours) and duty-day limits broke the one-driver long haul; relay is the answer, but each relay is a multi-party contract problem: whose truck, whose driver, who is liable for damage between hubs, who pays if the second driver is late, and how the fare is split. Load factor fell to 39.1% in FY2025 partly because of relay. A Univearth survey (Aug 2025) found 38% of shippers have introduced relay on long hauls but only 13% see an effect.
- **Solution:** A relay protocol and clearing layer: standard relay contract, hand-off checklist with photo and seal capture, leg-level liability and insurance, instant fare splitting, and a slot calendar at member DCs and truck stations (no new concrete). The shipper sees one tier-1; the platform is the register for every leg. Legal premise to verify with MLIT: whether relay legs count against the 2-tier effort duty (the MLIT page I read is silent) and whether the written-contract and register duties need leg-by-leg entries.
- **Why now:** Hours cap (since Apr 2024); register and 2-tier effort duty (1 Apr 2026); revised Logistics Efficiency Act (May 2026) certifies relay hubs near interchanges, target 20 by FY2030, with tax relief; 38% of surveyed shippers have already tried relay; the 34.1% capacity gap forecast for 2030.
- **Wedge (first narrow product, first 10 customers):** One trunk lane (Tokyo-Nagoya or Osaka-Fukuoka) with 3-4 carriers who already do informal relay. First 10 customers: carriers on that lane plus two shippers; use an existing DC yard as the swap point.
- **Path to $100M:** Assume 8% of ~300M duty days are long-haul (my guess, unverified) = ~24M runs; at JPY 6,000 per relay (platform fee including settlement and liability coordination) the pool is JPY 144B, and JPY 15B is ~10% of long-haul runs. That is a heroic share; MLIT's own certified-hub target (20 hubs by FY2030) caps policy-backed physical nodes, so the platform must be asset-light.
- **Who does it today:** Fukuyama Transporting with Logisteed (inter-company trailer-swap relay from Apr 2025); Coca-Cola bottlers with Tomakomai Port (trailer-swap pilot, Jan 2025); MLIT's relay-hub certification and NEXCO truck-parking expansion (520 bays across 23 sites in FY2026). All operating relays are bilateral or group-internal [S].
- **Why they lose:** Each incumbent runs relay inside its own group or with one partner because its contracts, liability and settlement are bilateral; none can be neutral between rivals, and none wants to give a rival's trucks a fair slot.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (relay nodes and carrier pairs on each lane), process power (a hand-off standard), and a standard-setting position in contract, liability and settlement terms. Honest: copyable by Fukuyama/Logisteed if they open their network.
- **Earned secret:** Relay fails at the hand-off, not at the hub. The hard part is a contract-and-settlement template that makes three carriers act as one, plus a way to show regulators the chain still has one tier-1. The winning standard is paper and money, not concrete.
- **First 90 days:** Weeks 1-4: interview 20 dispatchers on a single lane about their informal relays and the last three disputes. Weeks 4-8: draft the relay contract, insurance and settlement terms with a transport lawyer and an MLIT pre-consultation on tier counting. Weeks 8-13: run 200 relays between 3 carriers at one existing yard and measure late hand-offs and damage claims.

## Honest ranking (share of the pool each needs, and the main way each dies)

| # | Idea | Share needed for $100M | Main way it dies |
|---|------|------------------------|------------------|
| 1 | Dock crews with a turn-time guarantee | ~1.9% of driver loading hours; ~5M truck-turns | Thin gross margin (~$15M GP at $100M revenue); Timee or a 3PL copies; ukeoi-vs-dispatch ruling |
| 2 | Open-book tier-1 | 0.9-1.1% of pool freight | Contribution ~4%; credit and claims on our balance sheet; Hacobell/Trabox respond |
| 3 | Anchor roll-up | 0.9% of pool revenue; ~9% of yearly sellers | Margin never leaves ~1%; capital and integration; cliche-adjacent |
| 4 | Unit-load pallet loop | ~6M pallets (half of JPR) | Capex ~$160M; incumbents add tags; loss data may not support the secret |
| 5 | Carrier's advocate | ~9% of pool revenue as paying clients (heroic) | Churn after 24 months; legal structure; shippers retaliate despite the ban |
| 6 | Joint-shipment clean room | ~2.0% of pool spend in shared lanes | Antitrust comfort; shippers will not share lane data; savings rate off food lanes unproven |
| 7 | Capacity reservation exchange | ~2.3% of pool as reserved fare | Willingness to pay untested; market loosens; a 3PL copies |
| 8 | Relay exchange | ~10% of long-haul runs (heroic); unverified base | Incumbents open their own networks; relay legs may count as tiers |

Best risk-adjusted: 1 (cleanest arithmetic, smallest share), 6 and 7 (largest structural novelty, WTP still to be tested), 2 (largest pool access, thinnest economics). Weakest on size: 5 and 8, which I include because the upside is large if the assumptions hold; I would drop them first.

## Killed in this run (and why)

- **CLO software or consulting** for the ~3,200 designated shippers: Hacobell, Transcosmos, ABeam, Sagawa and Logisteed all launched in 2025-26.
- **Actual-carrier register or e-contract as a product**: MLIT leaves the format free and free Excel templates and whitepapers exist.
- **Remote roll-call (tenko) centre**: only 197 inter-operator filings (159 trucks) by end-Dec 2025; same-type-operator and per-office permit rules; the priced pool needs ~26-50% of all drivers.
- **Freight factoring and early-pay rail**: Trabox Finto launched 2-party factoring in Sep 2025 (2-9.5%); the 60-day cap shortens the financed window, so the fee pool is a few percent of flows.
- **Foreign driver pipeline**: the specified-skilled-worker cap for auto transport is 22,100 over five years and only 151 were working at end-2025.
- **Rate or cost index**: MLIT will publish the appropriate-cost formula and JTA already hosts a free standard-fare calculator.
- **Samsara-for-Japan**: digital-tachograph shipments are only ~79k units a year (FY2024, Yano) and the installed base is OEM- and dealer-channelled; no mandate extension is decided.
- **Driver super-app funded by suppliers**: supplier marketing pool and fuel margins are too small.
- **Truck-parking and relay hubs as real estate**: MLIT's certification target is 20 hubs by FY2030.

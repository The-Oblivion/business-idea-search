# P021 US personal-auto physical-damage claims ($92B): 8 ideas (round 2)

Written 2026-10-18. About 35 searches and fetches (the brief said 8-15; I went over because the pool file's "nothing funded on ADAS calibration" turned out wrong and several first answers were thin). [M] = from general knowledge, not re-verified this run. "My arithmetic" = a number I derived, not a sourced figure.

## Pool facts used (checked this run unless marked)

- Pool: about $92B incurred losses (pool file: $154.3B premiums x 59.9% loss ratio, my arithmetic). Claim count is not sourced anywhere I found. I use about 15M collision-plus-comprehensive claims a year (my arithmetic: $92B over roughly $6K a claim; HLDI puts collision frequency at 5.9 claims per 100 insured vehicle years, average collision loss $10,267 for MY2022-24).
- Total loss (TL): CCC says 23.1% of claims in 2025 (22.6% through Sept); Copart said 24.2% in Q4 2025; J.D. Power says 27% in 2025, up from 16% in 2022. At 23.1% of 15M that is about 3.5M TLs a year (my arithmetic). Verisk: personal-auto claim volume fell about 5% in 2024 and 3% in 2025. Copart US insurance units fell 9.5% (Q1 2026) and 10.7% (Q2 2026): fewer claims, higher TL share.
- Shops: North America collision market $55-60B (Boyd investor summary). Big 4 MSOs (Caliber, Crash Champions, Gerber, Classic) about 4,019 locations and 31.7% revenue share (Matthews, end 2025). Gerber 2025 revenue $3.1B and bought Joe Hudson's (258 locations, $1.3B) in Jan 2026. 130+ PE firms active. About 800 independent single-shop closures in 2024. Technician supply gap 57% (TechForce 2026). Customer-pay is about 20% of repair-order count (MSO Symposium, secondhand).
- Estimate fight: insurer-side automation is real. CCC Estimate-STP was used by 15 insurers incl. 7 of the top 10 by premium, about half of US claims volume (CCC, early 2023); Mitchell Intelligent Estimating; Audatex Qapter; CCC Intelligent Reinspection (Jul 2024). Supplements: Mitchell Q2 2018, 38.99% of estimates supplemented, average variance $948; CCC (older) 52% of appraisals with at least one supplement, about $500 per appraisal. Shop-side tools are tiny: Mitchell Estimate Review (self-audit inside UltraMate), GuideCoat (BainbridgeAI), VaraFix (launched 25 Aug 2026). No funding found for any of them.
- Parts: PartsTrader Spring 2026: OEM new parts +4.3% since the tariffs, aftermarket under 1%; Enlyte Trends 2026: OEM +4.21%, aftermarket +3.89% (2025). About 44% of OEM collision parts sold in the US are made overseas. Median delivery: OEM 8.7-9.0 days, aftermarket 2.9-3.1, recycled 3.4-3.8, price-matched 4.9-5.6. Order mix: OEM 52%, aftermarket 20%, price-matched 18%, recycled 6%. Taiwan 232 rate cut from 25% to 15% on 1 May 2026.
- Calibrations: CCC says 28.3% of repairable estimates carry one (21.8% a year earlier). KPMG: North American calibration services about $1.2B in 2024, US about $3.0B by 2030; over 90% of mechanical shops and 70% of collision shops lack in-house capability (KPMG); Revv survey: 57% of shops do some in-house, 44% fully equipped. Missed-calibration suits: 3 in 2018 to 61 in 2024 (an analyst's count); settlements $200k-$1M+ (analyst estimate). NY signed a glass calibration disclosure law 19 Dec 2025; IL HB 4373 and a MD anti-denial bill are pending. Players already here: Safelite (TruRoad), LKQ (Elitek), Caliber (Protech), Repairify asTech, AirPro, Opus IVS, Driven Brands. **The pool file's "nothing funded on calibration" is wrong.**
- TL speed and cost: FNOL to release about 19-22 days (LexisNexis 2022; Hi Marley says 20); lienholder details confirmed around day 13 in a LexisNexis timeline; storage plus rental about $54 a day (Hi Marley) to $105 a day (Canadian Underwriter 2019). Enterprise US collision length of rental: 15.1 days in Q2 2026.
- Salvage: Copart plus IAA about 80% of US salvage (one investor write-up; other sources split it 40-45% and 30-35%); insurers supply 80-85% of units; Copart owns 275+ yards. A major carrier moved volume to IAA in 2025 (carrier not verified).
- Litigation and regulation: Progressive CO projected-sold-adjustment settlement $15.24M (opt-out deadline 5 Nov 2026), Progressive NY $48M (93,000 drivers, 2024), State Farm AR $15.6M (an Audatex 9% "typical negotiation adjustment"). Condition-adjustment (CCC) classes keep failing on appeal (9th Cir. Liberty Mutual; 3rd Cir.). NAIC 12-state AI examination (pilot 2 Mar to Sep 2026) covers TL thresholds, damage assessment and parts valuation; vote on nationwide adoption at the Nov 2026 NAIC meeting; insurers are responsible for vendor AI.
- CCC itself: 2026 revenue guide $1.158-1.164B; AI revenue over $120M annualized; owns Safekeep (subrogation, 2022) and EvolutionIQ (2025); runs CCC Payments (Nvoicepay), Total Loss Care, and a Zag-powered replacement-car program. **Anything an insurer-paid platform can build, CCC is already building or buying. Every idea below therefore sits where CCC's customer base blocks it, or where it needs a yard, a van, a balance sheet or a licence.**

## Dead ends found this run (so the next reader does not redo them)

- ADAS calibration vans/network as a plain network: crowded (Safelite, LKQ, Caliber, Opus IVS, Repairify, AirPro, PE roll-ups). Only the proof/authorisation layer (idea 7) is still arguable.
- TL replacement-car leads to dealers: already run through CCC and Mitchell with TrueCar/Zag (3,000+ dealers, free to insurers). A dealer or administrator guaranteeing the ACV gap is unlicensed insurance in NY (DFS opinions 2004, 2009).
- Subrogation: CCC bought Safekeep and a top-5 insurer adopted it in Q2 2026. Arbitration Forums handled about 1.2M disputes and 2.4M subro demands in 2024.
- Claims payment rails: CCC Payments exists; I found no evidence that insurers force 3% virtual-card fees on collision shops (that is a medical-billing story).
- Policyholder-side contingency TL audit: 30+ states license public adjusters, NY's 2004 opinion excludes auto, Michigan says a contingency adjuster cannot be an independent appraiser; fee pool is small and the legal premise is a patchwork. Not worth an idea slot.

## 1. Counterweight (lens: insider, a collision estimator who became an MSO VP of operations)

- **One-liner:** A revenue-cycle outsourcer for multi-shop repairers that does for body shops what R1 does for hospitals: it fights insurers' automated estimate-stripping with OEM-backed documentation, learns what each carrier pays for, and takes a share of the money it recovers.
- **Customer:** PE-backed and independent MSOs with 8-300 locations (the Big 4 are 31.7% of revenue; Focus Advisors tracks 13 independent non-dealer MSOs with 8+ shops still adding stores).
- **Payer and signer:** The MSO pays from recovered insurer dollars (gain-share) and from the office/estimating labour line it replaces. Signer: CFO or SVP operations; the VP of insurance relations is the champion.
- **Problem:** Insurers now run estimates through straight-through and rules-based review (CCC Estimate-STP covered about half of US claims volume in early 2023; CCC Intelligent Reinspection since Jul 2024). Operations get flagged, reduced or stripped by rules the carrier configured; shops report rewriting estimates three or four times (a Collision Repair survey of senior Canadian industry figures, so not a US sample). Supplements run on 39-52% of estimates. The BainbridgeAI author (a vendor) says estimating systems default blends to 50% of a full refinish while the SCRS blend study found 31.59% more time than a full refinish. Shops have no counter-automation.
- **Solution:** Four layers, sold as one service. (1) Pre-submission completion: every estimate checked against OEM procedures and carrier rules, with citations attached. (2) A carrier-rule engine trained on every submission, approval and denial across customers (carrier x adjuster x operation x document -> approval odds). (3) A supplement desk (people plus software) that argues in carrier portals and on recorded calls, so verbal approvals become a ledger. (4) Back-office: payment posting, parts credit and return reconciliation, deductible collection.
- **Why now:** Insurer automation crossed half of claim volume. Repairable claims are down about 10% so shops are fighting for margin per job. PE platforms (130+ firms) are centralising back offices and want a measurable line. The NAIC examination makes insurers answerable for vendor AI, which gives documented disputes more weight.
- **Wedge:** Start with the three operations that are both frequently stripped and OEM-documented (calibrations/scans, adjacent-panel blend, coating removal) for 3 MSOs, charging only 12% of verified incremental approved dollars. First 10 customers: PE-backed platforms of 20-80 shops reached through Focus Advisors-type advisers.
- **Path to $100M:** Insurer-paid repair revenue is about $49B (my arithmetic: $57.5B midpoint of Boyd's range x 85%). If documented incremental operations are 2.5% of that (my assumption; the supplement data suggest $200-450 per repair order gross, of which I assume a quarter is the contestable part) that is $1.2B a year; a 12% fee is $147M, plus a platform fee of $8 on about 8.8M insurer-paid repair orders ($70M) = about $217M at full coverage. **$100M needs about 46% of insurer-paid repair orders. That is heroic.** It only works if the top 15-25 consolidators (45%+ of revenue) all adopt, which is how MSO consolidation is heading (Romans: top three at up to 37% by 2028).
- **Who does it today:** Mitchell Estimate Review (a shop self-audit tool inside UltraMate that imports carrier requirement profiles, undated); GuideCoat (BainbridgeAI); VaraFix (Aug 2026); in-house estimators and MSO central teams. CCC, Mitchell and Audatex all serve the insurer side. No funded shop-side revenue-cycle company found.
- **Why they lose:** CCC, Mitchell and Audatex are paid per claim by insurers and cannot ship a product designed to raise claim payments. Mitchell frames its tool as avoiding rejection, not as recovering money. GuideCoat and VaraFix are small founder-led tools with no carrier-behaviour dataset, no desk and no MSO distribution.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning against insurer-paid platforms, plus a cornered resource (carrier x operation approval data nobody else can see) and switching costs once payment posting runs through it.
- **Earned secret:** Shops do not lose most on the lines a carrier denies. They lose on the three rewrites it takes to win them, and on the lines the estimator never writes because denial is expected. A system that knows what a given carrier pays with which document converts "expected denial" into automatic inclusion and removes the rewrite.
- **First 90 days:** Sign 3 MSOs for no-fee pilots on gain-share only. Ingest 6 months of their estimate and supplement history to price the leakage baseline. Build the carrier-rule dataset from their submissions. Hire 5 former DRP managers and estimators as the desk.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 6.
- **Risks to kill it:** Carriers retaliate by deselecting DRP shops that use it; recovered-dollar attribution is disputed; the 2.5% assumption is much lower in practice.

## 2. Severity Pool (lens: insider, a DRP manager at a tier-1 carrier)

- **One-liner:** A purchasing pool for tier-2 and tier-3 personal-auto carriers and MGAs that guarantees MSOs a share of claim volume in return for big-carrier repair terms, taking an admin fee and a slice of the savings, with the guarantee backed by pooled volume rather than any single carrier.
- **Customer:** Regional mutuals, MGAs and insurtech carriers (Root, Branch, Clearcover class) that write personal auto but cannot move a labour rate or a parts discount on their own volume.
- **Payer and signer:** The carrier, from loss-adjustment expense and claims vendor budget; the savings show up in loss. Signer: chief claims officer (MGAs: COO). Success fees are booked against realised severity.
- **Problem:** The big carriers get volume-tiered labour rates, parts discounts, SLAs and warranties from MSOs. Small carriers pay more for the same repair and are on skeletal networks. State Farm has trimmed its DRP from about 20,000 shops to about half, so MSOs are choosing whom to serve.
- **Solution:** Contract once with the Big 4 and 15-20 other MSOs for a pooled-volume tier (labour rate, P&M rate, parts discount, calibration rate, cycle-time SLA, warranty). Route each member carrier's claims into it. Take $30 a claim plus 10% of verified savings, and carry the performance guarantee on severity per claim. Add rental (days), salvage and glass legs once the repair leg is running.
- **Why now:** MSOs are consolidating (Big 4 at 31.7%) and want guaranteed volume while repairable claims fall 10%. Carriers are under pressure on severity (average repair $4,818 per CCC; tariffs add about $100 of parts per repair order per PartsTrader). A Branch/CrashBay network launched in under three weeks in Jul 2026, so network setup is no longer the barrier; volume leverage is.
- **Wedge:** One state, 6-10 member carriers and 2 MSOs, repair leg only, with a published cost-per-claim guarantee. First 10 customers: MGAs and regional mutuals that already outsource claims (they have a vendor budget).
- **Path to $100M:** Assume carriers outside the top ~10 write 35-40% of personal-auto claims (my assumption; I did not pull NAIC market-share data), so about 5.5M of the 15M claims, 70% repairable with a shop (3.9M), half DRP-eligible (1.9M). Fee per claim $30 plus 10% of $330 savings (6% of $5,500) = $63. Pool revenue is $120M at 100% of the addressable base. **$100M needs about 80% of the addressable base unless the TL, rental, salvage and glass legs are added (another $40-60 a claim). With all five legs it still needs 40-50% of tier-2/3 volume. That is heroic.**
- **Who does it today:** Innovation Group (managed network of 2,500+ shops for tier 2/3 carriers), Sedgwick managed DRP, CrashBay (Branch, Jul 2026), CCC's DRP and program guidelines, Driven Brands (1,000+ DRPs), carrier in-house shop-management teams.
- **Why they lose:** Innovation Group and Sedgwick manage network quality but do not pool volume across carriers or carry a savings guarantee. CCC sells to the carriers and cannot commit one carrier's volume against another's. Top carriers will never join a pool that dilutes their own tiers. Nobody else has the cross-carrier volume.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies (pooled volume lowers every member's rate) plus a cornered resource (MSO volume commitments).
- **Earned secret:** MSOs already price by volume tier, not by who the carrier is. A 2%-share carrier cannot promise a 25% share of a market, but forty of them can, and the MSO only needs one counterparty that pays in 10 days.
- **First 90 days:** Get 3 MSOs to quote a pooled tier in one metro. Recruit 4 MGAs. Build the severity-per-claim baseline from their last 12 months. Settle the antitrust and insurance-regulatory advice (buying-group rules) before any term sheet.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 4.
- **Risks to kill it:** Antitrust treatment of a carrier buying group; MSOs unwilling to hold the tier when a member carrier's volume falls; incumbents copy the pooling.

## Pool facts used

- Stock of gold loans: INR 19.4 lakh crore at Mar 2026 (Experian) [S]; CRIF POS INR 15.6 lakh crore at Nov 2025 [S]; the pool file uses INR 18.6-20 lakh crore, about $200-215B. Definitions conflict (Experian also quotes AUM of INR 11.9 lakh crore).
- CRIF CreditScape, Nov 2025 [S]: 9.03 crore active accounts (+10.3% y/y) while POS grew 41.9%; originations in 8M FY26 were INR 17.4 lakh crore (+111%) across 10.5 crore loans. Annualised, that is about INR 26 lakh crore and about 15.8 crore loans a year [A]. Public-sector banks hold 59.9% of POS and 46.6% of accounts; loans above INR 2.5 lakh were 59.1% of origination value.
- Experian [S]: average ticket INR 0.98 lakh (FY23) to INR 1.96 lakh (FY26); repeat borrowers about 75% of new sourcing; net 90+ delinquency 0.2%. ICRA via Kotak Neo [S]: quantity of gold pledged with large lenders rose only 3-4% over FY22-26 while books grew about 24% a year. Growth is revaluation, not new borrowers.
- Price [S]: record INR 1,80,779 per 10g on 30 Jan 2026; about 23% below peak on 12 Oct 2026 (CreditSights). Average portfolio LTV 66-70% at Muthoot, Manappuram, IIFL (Muthoot alone 58%). Muthoot FY26 gold yield about 20% (Q4 20.8% with one-offs).
- SIZE TRAP: the pool file's "NBFC share of retail gold loans 28% to 40%" is a flow share. RBI's own series [S] shows NBFC gold lending of INR 3.41 lakh crore (about $37B) at Jun 2026 and banks' retail gold loans of INR 5.1 lakh crore (about $55B) at May 2026. Any fee tied to "the NBFC book" is a $40B pool, not $85B.
- RBI Lending against Gold and Silver Collateral Directions 2025 [S, notification Id 12859]: para 17 value = lower of 30-day average close and previous-day close (IBJA or SEBI exchange price); para 19 LTV 85/80/75%; para 20 LTV maintained throughout tenor; para 21 uniform assay procedure; para 23 borrower present at assay; para 27 assay certificate with image; para 22 publish net-weight methodology; para 30 gold "handled only in its branches and only by its employees"; para 34 periodic surprise verification; paras 37-43 auctions (reserve 90% of current value, 85% after two failed; first auction physical in the branch district; lender and related parties may not bid; surplus refunded in 7 working days); para 35 release within 7 working days, INR 5,000 a day for delay. The Directions are silent on BIS hallmarking and on whether assayers may be outsourced. Bullet consumption loans are capped at 12 months.
- Entrants and base rates [S]: Rupeek, the best-known gold-loan startup, had AUM of INR 1,864 crore at Sep 2024, of which only INR 236 crore was its own book (the rest was off-book as business correspondent), and FY23 revenue of about INR 89 crore (third-party compilation). Tata Capital is buying about 88.6% of Yogakshemam Loans (Jul 2026); Aditya Birla Capital NBFC entered in Aug 2026 (200-300 branches by Mar 2027); Godrej Capital took over Kanakadurga Finance; Bajaj Finance has 1,507 gold branches and AUM of INR 17,831 crore (+115%). Jefferies and HDFC Securities expect 3,000-3,700 new gold branches in FY27.
- Axis Bank + Freecharge launched a gold-backed credit line on UPI in Sep 2025 (overdraft, interest only on the drawn amount) [S].
- 2026 appraiser-linked frauds [S]: ICICI Nagpur over INR 23 crore (159 accounts, 9 branches; valuer allegedly paid INR 5,000 a report); NBFC Delhi INR 3.81 crore (683 fake accounts, 2022-25); Bank of Baroda Jangareddigudem about INR 10 crore (gold swapped); Union Bank Telangana INR 2.91 crore (96 accounts). Indian Bank added 982 empanelled appraisers in six months (panel 3,538); Indian Overseas Bank's panel is 2,910.
- Scrap [S]: India recycled about 125-150 t in 2025; industry estimates 200-250 t in 2026; households sold nearly 50 t in Apr-Jun 2026 (+43%, IBJA via ET). At about INR 1.39 lakh per 10g that is roughly $150M per tonne [A], so about $30-37B a year.
- Informal share: KPMG (2017, citing WGC) put informal lenders at 60-70% of the market at 25-50% interest. This is dated and the least reliable number in the file.

## 1. Girvi Formalizer (lens: insider, a small-town jeweller and pawnbroker with 25 years of tickets)

- **One-liner:** Buy the books, vaults and people of retiring pawnbrokers in Tier-3 towns, re-paper their customers into one RBI-regulated NBFC at roughly half the informal rate, and run each shop as an employee-staffed branch with an XRF reader and a shared ledger.
- **Customer:** Small-ticket borrowers (INR 10,000-1.5 lakh) who pay a pawnbroker 24-48% a year, and the retiring pawnbroker who wants a clean exit.
- **Payer and signer:** Borrowers pay interest at 18-22% (my price target; Muthoot's yield is about 20%). Signers: the pawnbroker (sale and employment contract), the borrower (new loan agreement), and the bank credit committee that funds the NBFC at about 10-11% [A].
- **Problem:** Informal lenders still hold a large share at two to five times the formal price because they are 200 metres away, accept anything (stones, utensils, partial pieces) and ask nothing. Formal greenfield branches need a pipeline of INR 20 crore or more; a lane with INR 4-6 crore of books never clears the hurdle. The owners are ageing and their children do not want the trade [A, insider claim].
- **Solution:** A conversion kit. (1) Diligence the ticket book: redemption rate, auctions, realised loss. (2) Buy goodwill and lease the vault premises. (3) Re-paper every live pawn ticket as a new NBFC loan that pays off the pawn ticket the same day, so the gold never leaves the room. (4) The pawnbroker becomes a salaried branch head with equity upside, which satisfies the employee-handling rule in para 30. (5) Install XRF, scale and photo capture so each pledge gets a para 27 certificate. (6) Shared loan system, central audit, bank-line funding.
- **Why now:** Licences and branches are being bought, not built: Tata Capital via Yogloans, Godrej via Kanakadurga, with 3,000-3,700 new branches expected in FY27. Gold 23% below peak squeezes pawnbrokers who financed themselves at peak prices, which should create willing sellers [A].
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** One district cluster, ten pawnbrokers in two or three adjacent towns with books of INR 3-8 crore each. Use an acquired dormant small gold NBFC as the vehicle (the route Tata and Godrej took) rather than a fresh licence.
- **Path to $100M:** About 1,000 converted shops x INR 6 crore average book [A] = INR 6,000 crore ($645M) of loans x about 20% yield [S] = about $130M a year. That is 0.3% of the $215B stock and about 1.5% of an informal pool that is at least $42B even if informal is only 20% of today's stock [A]. It needs about $95M of equity (15% of loans) and about $550M of bank debt over five years. No share above 2% is required; the constraint is execution speed (about 200 conversions a year) and capital.
- **Who does it today:** I found no pawnshop-conversion programme. Greenfield: Muthoot (more than 7,500 branches per one aggregator, weak source), Manappuram, IIFL, Bajaj Finance, ABCL, Tata Capital, Godrej. Fintech: Rupeek set out to fix the unorganised market and is still under INR 2,000 crore of AUM; IndiaGold got an NBFC licence (weak source).
- **Why they lose:** Greenfield economics skip lanes that cannot reach INR 20 crore. A formal lender cannot rent a relationship, and a compliance-first culture does not know how to take over a cash-economy owner. Rupeek's app-led attempt shows that persuading the borrower alone fails; buying the shop and the person is a different bet.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource. The sourced and vetted shops, plus pawnbroker-turned-branch-heads with their customers' trust, are scarce and can be bought only once. Scale economies in funding and audit are secondary.
- **Earned secret:** A pawnbroker's real asset is not the gold but his memory of whom to trust and his tolerance of the long tail (stones, partial pieces, late payment enforced socially). His loss rate on gold is tiny, which is why he can charge 3% a month and still sleep: the 30%+ price is for liquidity and privacy, not risk. [Insider claim; test with loan-level data in the first 90 days.]
- **First 90 days:** Map one district's licensed pawnbrokers from the state licence list (Tamil Nadu licences come from the tahsildar under the 1943 Act; other states differ). Meet 30, obtain loan-level ticket books from 10 under NDA, and test two claims: realised loss under 0.5% and annual redemption above 85%. Shortlist two dormant small NBFCs for acquisition and ask the RBI regional office about the change-of-control route.
- **Legal premise to check:** State pawnbroker and moneylender acts differ (only Tamil Nadu's was read: rate ceiling set by the state, at most bank rate plus 5 points per the 1992 amendment). RBI prior approval for a change of control. Off-book cash in the sellers' accounts. If the books fail diligence, the idea is dead.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 6.

## 2. Bullion-Bid (lens: insider, a lender's auction and collections head who also knows what refiners pay)

- **One-liner:** A lender-neutral national bid layer that sells pledged gold when a loan goes bad, before or inside the Directions' auction, and lets stressed borrowers sell part of their gold to renew a loan instead of losing all of it.
- **Customer:** Lenders' heads of collections and recovery; stressed borrowers at renewal; refiners and bullion dealers who want scrap.
- **Payer and signer:** The lender pays a seller fee out of the auction and recovery budget; the winning bidder pays a buyer premium. Signer: the NBFC or bank Head of Collections and Recovery, with the Chief Risk Officer approving the auction SOP (para 9 requires an SOP).
- **Problem:** Para 41 forces the first auction to be physical in the branch district, so bidding is thin exactly when the lender is most exposed. Para 42 bars the lender and related parties from bidding, which closes the old private sale to a group company. Reserve falls from 90% to 85% of current value after two failed auctions, so the rules themselves bake in a 5-point haircut.
- **Solution:** (a) A para 39-compliant empanelled auction service with live remote bidding by 20+ refiners and dealers, and structured post-auction reports for audit. (b) A borrower-initiated pre-auction sale: a tri-party release in one branch visit where the buyer pays the lender, the lender releases only the sold items, and the borrower keeps the surplus (paid within the 7-working-day limit of para 43). (c) Sell-to-renew: at renewal, sell the surplus pieces to bring LTV within the cap and keep the family's best pieces. (d) Triage by item using the para 27 certificate and images: melt lots go to refiners, lots with maker or stone value go to a jeweller marketplace intact.
- **Why now:** The paras 37-43 SOP is new (effective Apr 2026). Gold is 23% below its January peak (CreditSights expects asset quality to weaken over the next few quarters), and the 12-month bullet cap makes maturities hard dates. Households are already selling gold: nearly 50 t in Apr-Jun 2026, +43% [S].
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** Second-attempt lots, which para 41 allows to move online or to an adjoining district. Pilot with a mid-size NBFC or small finance bank, then add three refiners; target ten lenders in year one.
- **Path to $100M:** Three layers, each in a stress-year regime [A]. (A) If 3% of the $215B stock reaches the para 37 notice stage and half is sold, that is about $3.2B of loans and about $4B of gold; at a 40% share ($1.7B) and a 2.5% blended net take (seller fee plus buyer premium plus uplift on intact lots) = $42M. (B) Borrower part-sales and sell-to-renew: $3B x 1.5% = $45M. (C) 5% of the $30-37B scrap flow ($1.7B) x 0.8% = $14M. Total about $100M, which needs about 40% of the distress flow plus a start in scrap. In a normal year layer A is about a quarter of that. I call this heroic, and it depends on gold staying low.
- **Who does it today:** Local auctioneers and the jewellers who bid at them (the "ring" is an industry-folklore claim I could not verify); lenders' own staff-run auctions; refiner scrap networks and cash-for-gold shops; Tanishq-style exchange schemes (nearly 80% of jewellery sales in Ahmedabad went through exchange schemes, and Tanishq ran a zero-deduction scheme) [S]. I found no national lender-neutral bid layer.
- **Why they lose:** Lenders cannot bid and cannot run a national bidder pool. Local rings cannot scale beyond their town. Refiners lack local origination and lender integrations. Jeweller exchange schemes only work against a purchase of new jewellery.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (more refiners raise lot prices, which attracts more lenders) plus process power from audit-ready SOP evidence that lenders will not rebuild.
- **Earned secret:** A quarter or more of pledged pieces (temple sets, Kundan, polki, antique) carry maker's or stone value that a melt-priced auction hands to local bidders for free, and the first-auction-in-district rule guarantees those same bidders face no competition. [Hypothesis from lender and refiner practice; test on 100 lots.]
- **First 90 days:** Run 100 second-attempt lots for one lender. Get at least three refiner bids per lot via a simple API, compare clearing price with the 85-90% reserve and with the lender's historical realisation, and report the uplift by lot type.
- **Legal premise to check:** Empanelment as auctioneer (para 39); whether remote bidders can participate in the physical first auction (para 41); the platform must have no lender-affiliated owner (para 42); GST on repossessed gold; AML and KYC for buyers.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 7.

## 3. Karat Ledger (lens: pattern, Persona/Socure-style per-event verification rail created by a liability-shifting mandate)

- **One-liner:** An independent verification and record layer for pledged gold: instrument-backed assay evidence at the counter, surprise re-assay for the lender's own audit team, a shared appraiser track record, and a live collateral ledger that holds each pledge's LTV through its tenor.
- **Customer:** Chief Compliance Officers, heads of internal audit and gold-loan business heads at NBFCs and banks; funders of NBFCs.
- **Payer and signer:** The lender pays per pledge and per active loan per year from its compliance and fraud-loss budget; the signer is the Head of Internal Audit or Chief Compliance Officer, with the Gold Loan Business Head co-signing. At public-sector banks the borrower already pays "appraiser charges" per pledge; I believe they are about 0.25% of the loan but did not verify this, so the pool is an unverified lever.
- **Problem:** Para 21 demands a uniform assay procedure, para 27 an assay certificate with image, para 34 periodic surprise verification, and para 20 ongoing LTV. The party being audited (branch or appraiser) writes the evidence. In 2026 fraud cases the unit of damage was the appraiser, not the gold: 44, 96, 159 and 683 accounts per case. Appraiser panels are growing fast (Indian Bank +982 in six months to 3,538) and nobody tracks an appraiser who is fired from one lender and empanelled by another.
- **Solution:** (1) A handheld XRF, scale and camera kit with an app that writes the para 27 certificate and signs it. (2) Surprise verification as a service for the lender's own audit employees (the devices stay in lender hands, consistent with para 30). (3) An appraiser registry with outcome scores. (4) A collateral ledger that holds weight, purity, images and LTV for every pledge using the para 17 price rule, with margin-call and release workflows. (5) Later, a fraud warranty backed by reinsurance.
- **Why now:** The Directions turned assay evidence from a branch habit into a regulated record. RBI's Sept 2024 notice flagged third-party sourcing and appraisal as a deficiency, and a string of 2026 frauds followed.
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** Sell para 34 surprise verification to the internal audit head of one NBFC or small finance bank, because audit has a mandate and no tool and does not need IT sign-off. Add the ledger as the second product.
- **Path to $100M:** Per pledge INR 60 ($0.65) x about 158M pledges = $102M at 100% share. Per active loan INR 100 ($1.08) x 90M = $97M at 100%. A fraud warranty at 5 bps of INR 26 lakh crore of originations = $140M at 100%. To reach $100M you need roughly 35% of all three modules together, or 50% of the first two. That is heroic, and I treat this as a wedge to Idea 6 rather than a stand-alone $100M business. Pricing per account is capped by the small ticket: INR 76,500 average for loans up to INR 2.5 lakh.
- **Who does it today:** In-house systems at Muthoot and others; core-banking vendors (item-level collateral images are not their strength); XRF makers selling hardware; Rupeek and IndiaGold claim AI-assisted assessment (their claims, not tested). Credit bureaus cover the borrower, not the appraiser or the collateral. I found no lender-neutral appraiser registry or independent assay-evidence company.
- **Why they lose:** The auditee writes the evidence. Each lender keeps its fraud data private. Public-sector core systems cannot store spectra and images per item. Bureaus do not touch collateral.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs once the ledger is the system of record, plus a cornered data asset: cross-lender spectra and appraiser outcomes that no single lender can assemble.
- **Earned secret:** Gold-loan fraud is mostly collusion with a handful of appraisers who originate dozens of accounts each, not clever fake gold. Catching one appraiser's pattern is worth more than a better assay for every pledge. The borrower-present, image-certificate rule (paras 23, 27) means every pledge now produces structured evidence a neutral party can score.
- **First 90 days:** Rent five handheld XRF units. With one internal audit head, re-assay a 2% sample of packets in 20 branches, compare to the certificates, and deliver an appraiser-level anomaly report. Price per branch from that.
- **Legal premise to check:** Para 30 (our people never touch gold; the lender's employees use our devices); the DPDP Act and competition law on pooling appraiser data across lenders (consent and anonymisation design needed); an insurer must front any warranty.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5.

## 4. Gold Float (lens: pattern, Fuliza-style auto-overdraft plus control of the repayment channel)

- **One-liner:** A revolving credit line on UPI secured by gold that already sits in a lender's vault, charging interest only on the days money is used, and sweeping UPI inflows to repay it.
- **Customer:** Lumpy-cash-flow gold borrowers (shopkeepers, farmers, traders) who today take a lump sum and pay 10-20% on money sitting idle, and the banks and small finance banks that want gold assets without more branches.
- **Payer and signer:** The lender shares interest on the drawn balance with the program manager (my assumption: 150 bps). Signer: the bank's Head of Digital Lending or Chief Business Officer. The borrower pays interest on use only.
- **Problem:** Gold loans are priced and built as bullet lumps. NBFC revenue depends on interest on the full balance, so pay-on-use cuts their own income; public-sector systems cannot run a limit.
- **Solution:** A white-label line manager: limit set from LTV on the para 17 value, daily revaluation to keep LTV within para 20, UPI draw and auto-repay from inflows, and partial top-up or release flows.
- **Why now:** Axis and Freecharge proved the product in Sept 2025 under NPCI's secured-credit-line framework, and tiered LTV up to 85% for small tickets raised limits.
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** One small finance bank or private bank already live on UPI credit lines. Pre-approve 5,000 existing gold borrowers by collateral headroom and measure draw frequency.
- **Path to $100M:** 5% of the 9.03 crore active accounts = 4.5M lines x INR 1.4 lakh average drawn [A] = $6.8B of drawn balance x 150 bps = $102M. That is 5% of all gold borrowers on a line with this platform as program manager on every one, or 10% of borrowers if the platform has half. I call that heroic.
- **Who does it today:** Axis Bank with Freecharge (existing Axis customers; MSME and self-employed focus) [S]. Banks' gold overdrafts for MSMEs [M]. No NBFC or multi-lender manager found.
- **Why they lose:** NBFCs cannot cannibalise lump-sum interest. Public-sector systems cannot run a limit. Axis's line is a closed product for its own customers.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning against NBFC lump pricing; switching costs are low, so the position is weak.
- **Earned secret:** Fuliza worked because wallet inflows repaid the overdraft automatically. Gold has no such rail yet: the unlock is sweeping UPI merchant and wage inflows, and the collateral is already locked, so the line needs no new vault visit.
- **First 90 days:** Sign one live UPI-credit-line bank, build the gold limit adapter, run 5,000 pre-approved borrowers, and compare their effective interest cost with a bullet loan.
- **Legal premise to check (make or break):** I found no RBI statement permitting revolving consumption lines against gold, and the Directions cap bullet consumption loans at 12 months. UPI credit lines are bank-led; NBFC access is unverified. If a consumption line is not permitted, only the MSME and income-generating version survives, which cuts the pool.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5.

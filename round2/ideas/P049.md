# P049 - Indonesia: the free-meals programme (MBG) money flowing from BGN through ~27,800 community kitchens to ingredient suppliers (round 2 ideas)

Written 2026-10-14. Eight ideas, two per lens. Every number is tagged: (S) = sourced in a news report or official statement I saw in search results (most primary pages returned 403, so "S" means a news outlet states it), (A) = my arithmetic or assumption, (U) = unverified, single-source, conflicting, or an insider hypothesis I could not confirm. FX is Rp16,000 per $ (A; the pool sheet implies Rp15.9k).

## Pool facts used (re-checked this session)

- (S) 2027 draft budget Rp240T = $15.09B for 60.6M beneficiaries (finance minister, 14 Aug 2026, investortrust.id, from the pool sheet). The 2026 pagu was trimmed from Rp268T to Rp229T (infobanknews headline, U on the exact figure); Rp177T had been spent by 27 Aug 2026 (Katadata headline). A KabarBursa headline says the finance minister is watching BGN and the budget could be cut below Rp200T (U).
- (S) Governance shock: on 29 Sep 2026 the Attorney General put state loss from MBG governance in 2025-26 at about Rp10T (BPKP-calculated); former BGN head Dadan Hindayana and two former deputy heads are among six suspects. Sudaryono (ex-deputy agriculture minister) was sworn in as BGN head on 22 Jul 2026 after Nanik S. Deyang resigned. Three heads in one year: policy volatility is a first-order risk for every idea below.
- (S) Kitchen economics: Rp15k per portion = Rp10k ingredients (Rp8k for toddlers to grade 3) + Rp3k operations, reimbursed "at cost" (power, water, gas, volunteer pay, BPJS premiums) + Rp2k facility incentive, now a flat Rp6M per day per kitchen at a 3,000-recipient standard. The incentive is suspended the same day a kitchen fails the readiness standard, and (SE 12/2026, 17 Jun) is not paid on school holidays, weekends or national holidays (BGN's Rp3T saving over 18 days = 27,820 kitchens x Rp6M x 18).
- (A) Pool arithmetic at 220 operating days (BGN has not published a number; its budget implies about 260-270 days, the holiday letter implies fewer): ingredients $8.3B a year (60.6M x 220 x Rp10k; the pool sheet's ~$10B is the upper case), operations $2.5B, facility incentive $2.3B (27,820 x Rp6M x 220), 13.3B meals. Per full-size 3,000-portion kitchen: ingredients $412k, incentive $82k, operations $124k. Average kitchen in the 2027 draft serves about 2,180 portions (60.6M / 27,820).
- (S) Money rail: Treasury (KPPN) pays directly into a virtual account (VA) per kitchen at the five state banks (BRI, BNI, Mandiri, BSI, BTN); Rp39T had flowed through VAs by end-2025. Spending needs approval from the yayasan/mitra representative and the Kepala SPPG (a BGN-appointed civil servant). One source says funds are released every two weeks against a budget plan (RAB) in BGN's format (U). BGN owes about Rp1.6T to construction contractors (Rp800B unpaid since March, 74 contractors) (S).
- (S) Rules now in force. Peraturan BGN 10/2026 (stipulated 27 Jul, in force 18 Aug): eligible suppliers are micro and small businesses, one-person companies, cooperatives, Koperasi Desa/Kelurahan Merah Putih (Kopdes) and BUMDes; they need NIB and NPWP; processors need food-safety and halal certificates; sourcing runs kecamatan, then nearest kecamatan, then kabupaten, then province, then outside; kitchen managers may not mark up, take commissions or block eligible sellers. At least 15 suppliers per kitchen is widely reported (U, no primary text seen). Perpres 115/2025 threatens suspension for kitchens that refuse farmer/UMKM supply. The 4-hour rule (6-11 Aug 2026): food must be eaten within four hours of cooking, with a consumption-deadline label; the Kepala SPPG must be in the kitchen 02:00-08:00 (26 Aug).
- (S) Portal: on 12 and 17 Sep 2026 BGN said all kitchens must buy seven commodities (rice, cooking oil, chicken, eggs, beef, fish, milk) through a SIPLah-style procurement portal, that conceptualising it takes three to four weeks, that it is open to marketplace partners, and that sellers will be "verified" through associations. SIPLah (school procurement) has 15 marketplace operators, 223k schools and 100k vendors (S, July 2025); Telkom's Xooply serves 84.8k schools and 9.7k vendors; Telkom's consumer marketplace Blanja.com shut in 2020.
- (S) Compliance wave: SLHS (hygiene certificate) deadline 10 Aug 2026; 504 kitchens closed permanently by 13 Aug; 1,999 closed on 7 Sep for not registering (Katadata says temporary, JawaPos does not); 833 closed permanently in July with no further payment; 1,780 suspended in April for lacking IPAL (wastewater plant) or SLHS; 654 more classed "critical". IPAL is mandatory from 2026 under Kementerian LH rules (Kepmen LH 276/2025), cost Rp18-70M in one region (U).
- (S) Capital: about Rp54T of private money sits in kitchen buildings; about 13,000 kitchens could stall with Rp8.7T at risk (partner-association figure); new registrations are permanently closed; a yayasan is capped at 10 kitchens with Polri exempt (ICW claim, U). Bank exposure: BNI Rp1.5T across 577 debtors (Jan 2026), BSI Rp181B, BTN Rp53.5B for 24 kitchens, OJK Rp1.21T across 1,373 kitchens (Jan 2026); Kontan expects bad loans to rise from September and notes the SPPG permit is part of the collateral.
- (S) Mitra friction: the mitra association threatened a national "padlock" (14 Jul) and complained again on 1 Oct; on 2 Oct Sudaryono said some yayasan collect a fee per tray while doing nothing, opened the way for mitra to switch yayasan, and said cooperation agreements are being renewed.
- (S) Kopdes Merah Putih: Inpres 9/2025 makes them the main MBG supplier; every coop must run six business lines including a warehouse with cold storage and a logistics vehicle; Himbara banks lend up to Rp3B per coop (PMK 49/2025, six years); first instalments fell due 25 Sep 2026 and the finance minister says there will be no default, with a village-fund backstop of up to 30% (Permendes 10/2025). Bisnis (Jul 2026): 14,442 gerai complete and 20,915 still being built; only 1,061 coops had been inaugurated as operating in May 2026 (U). Telkom launched Digi Koperasi (cashier, accounting, dashboards) for 80,081 coops.
- (S) State IT already bought: BGN's IT budget is Rp1.2T, of which SIPGN (national app) Rp550B and an IoT managed service Rp199B with Peruri as strategic partner; kitchens report through a free Aplikasi Pelaporan Keuangan SPPG v4 integrated with the finance ministry.
- (S) Eggs and poultry: farm-gate eggs collapsed in May-June 2026 (Blitar farmers gave away 1M eggs); reference purchase price (HAP) Rp26,500/kg versus Rp22,644/kg on 14 Jul; Kementan projects 2026 output of 7.3 Mt, about 13% above need (U); BGN circulars threaten suspension of kitchens that do not buy local eggs, and SE 14/2026 (12 Aug) directs kitchens to buy poultry products from micro, small and medium farmers and cooperatives; menus now include eggs at least three times a week. Per full cook BGN cites 3,000 eggs, 350 chickens, 350 kg of vegetables and 450 L of milk (S, BGN statement). Bisnis (Feb 2026) headlines a 1.1 Mt chicken opportunity for Japfa and Charoen (U, headline only).
- (S) Workforce: about 47 local workers per kitchen (BGN deputy head); Keputusan Kepala BGN 401.1/2025 requires BPJS Ketenagakerjaan through the yayasan; in April 2026 BGN said 278,614 workers in 5,322 kitchens were not covered; Komnas HAM (15 Jun 2026) said they are workers, not volunteers. BPJS premium Rp16,800 per worker per month (S, 2025). No official daily pay rate found.
- (S) 3T (frontier) kitchens: 480 activated from 2 Oct 2026 (211 independent mitra plus 269 remote units) across 16 provinces and 116 districts; a ministry source says 1,703 3T kitchens are being tidied up while another says 352 (conflict, U); BGN has said Papua costs about three times Java (U, undated); the government is preparing an ingredient cost-of-living index and a rent incentive for remote areas (S, undated).
- (S) Energy: the 3 kg subsidised LPG ban was reiterated on 27 Jul; an LPG shortage halted kitchens in NTT in May; PGN's CNG to 46 kitchens is claimed to save 15-20%; one Magelang kitchen burns 800-1,000 m3 of CNG a month (about Rp12M) at 2,179 portions a day.
- (S) What actually poisons children, from BGN's own analysis of 70 incidents: E. coli in water, rice, tofu and chicken; Staph aureus in tempeh and meatballs; Salmonella in chicken, eggs and vegetables; B. cereus in noodles. Semarang 2026: E. coli, 707 victims (Menkes). The incident that triggered the 4-hour rule involved food cooked at 19:00 and served at 11:00 next day.

Per-kitchen service layers (compliance, evidence capture, accounting, payroll fees, consultancy) top out at about $4-5k a year per kitchen, i.e. roughly $110-140M at 100% of 27,820 kitchens (A), so none of them is a $100M business alone in Indonesia. Only ideas that sell or move the $8.3B ingredient flow (1, 2, 4, 7) reach $100M of revenue with a modest share (1-12%), and even there gross revenue is not profit: expect 8-15% gross margin. Ideas 5, 6 and 8 need 15-17% of their sub-pool and are flagged. The pool itself can shrink (budget cut talk, 60.6M versus the 82.9M target, Kejagung, three BGN heads), so ideas that cut cost per portion (1, 2, 4, 7) survive a cut better than ideas that ride the incentive (6). Strongest pairing: 2 + 1 (the hub feeds the plant), with 4 as the eastern extension. Weakest: 6 and 8, flagged. ## 1. Siap Masak: certified cook-ready prep plants for 4-hour kitchens (INSIDER: Puskesmas sanitarian / Dinkes food inspector, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** Move the riskiest, most labour-heavy step (raw chicken and vegetable handling) out of 27,820 volunteer-run kitchens into audited plants that deliver portion-weighed, chilled, cook-ready kits timed to the new 4-hour rule.
- **Customer:** Kepala SPPG and mitra/yayasan of full-size kitchens (2,000-3,000 portions) within about a 2-hour chilled-delivery radius of a plant. Start in dense Java and Sumatra clusters (Bogor-Bekasi-Karawang, Sidoarjo-Mojokerto, Medan-Deli Serdang).
- **Payer and signer:** The kitchen's ingredient line (Rp10k per portion, $300-410k a year per kitchen) paid from its VA, which needs two approvals: the yayasan/mitra representative and the Kepala SPPG. For cluster deals the signer is the chair of a mitra group (a Kadin, Muhammadiyah or NU unit) with a written no-objection from the BGN regional coordinator.
- **Problem:** The rule changes of August squeeze a 3,000-portion cook into a three-hour night window (cook from about 02:00, eat within four hours). Washing, cutting and portioning about 350 chickens and 350 kg of vegetables per cook day is done by volunteers in that window, which is where cross-contamination and time-temperature errors happen. BGN's own pathogen list (Salmonella on chicken, eggs and vegetables; E. coli on water, rice, tofu and chicken) points at handling, not chemical poison. BGN's response is test kits for arsenic and spoilage (still only "considered" on 17-18 Aug), plus closures.
- **Solution:** NKV-registered (poultry), halal-certified, HACCP plants that deliver raw-but-prepared kits: portion-cut and weighed chicken, washed and cut vegetables, graded and sanitised eggs, in coded trays, arriving 00:00-01:30 on our reefer vans so cooking starts at 02:00. Every batch carries a QR with farm, plant, temperature and weigh data. Kitchens still cook, so the 4-hour rule is untouched; we sell only uncooked inputs. Price sits inside the Rp10k cap by capturing the distribution spread between farm-gate and the wet-market price kitchens pay now. Plants buy from local farmer cooperatives so the sellers of record stay eligible.
- **Why now:** 4-hour rule and label (6-11 Aug); Kepala SPPG on site from 02:00 (26 Aug); 1,999 + 504 + 833 closures; Peraturan BGN 10/2026 names certified processors as eligible suppliers; poisoning in Berastagi (13 Aug) and Jayapura (Aug) cost kitchen heads their jobs.
- **Wedge:** One plant (leased, not built: an existing NKV poultry cut-up room plus cold store) serving 25-40 kitchens in one regency with chicken and vegetable kits only, which BGN's per-cook numbers show are the two biggest handling items. First 10 customers: kitchens that were suspended or "critical" (they have the most to lose), plus the kitchens of one mitra group.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Chicken plus vegetables per full-size kitchen is about $136k a year (420 kg of chicken on 88 days at Rp40k/kg = $92k; 350 kg of vegetables on 220 days at Rp9k/kg = $43k). Cross-check: 27,820 kitchens x $92k = $2.6B, close to Bisnis's 1.1 Mt-of-chicken headline at Rp38k/kg = $2.6B. A plant serving 100 kitchens is about $13.6M of revenue, so $100M is about 7.4 plants and about 740 kitchens, i.e. 2.7% of kitchens and 2.4% of the $5.6B chicken-plus-vegetable flow. Gross margin 12-15% means about $13-15M of gross profit at $100M revenue; capex about $4-6M per leased-and-fitted plant (A). Reaching $100M of gross profit would need roughly 20% of kitchens.
- **Who does it today:** Japfa (15 poultry slaughterhouses, 78 breeding farms, national cold storage; says it supplies some kitchens), Charoen Pokphand (processed chicken about 15% of H1 2026 sales), Malindo; wet-market slaughterhouses and traders; Eden Farm and Sayurbox (Jabodetabek B2B fresh, no MBG role found); Chickin (said it hopes to join MBG, U); each kitchen in-house; Polri's own warehouses and farms for its kitchens. No supplier of cook-ready kits timed to SPPG schedules found.
- **Why they lose:** Integrators sell standard SKUs through distributors and are not the micro, small or cooperative sellers the rules favour; none runs per-kitchen night delivery windows. B2B startups have no plants and focus on Jabodetabek. Traders have no cold chain, NKV or paperwork. Kitchens cannot afford to fix handling with volunteers on a 3-hour clock.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Process power (HACCP discipline and time-temperature data that the kitchen cannot replicate) plus cornered resource (certified capacity near clusters and farmer-cooperative offtake). Scale economies in routes once density reaches 30+ kitchens per plant.
- **Earned secret:** In outbreak investigations the culprit is almost never poison; it is time, temperature and raw handling. Test kits catch arsenic and spoilage, not Salmonella, B. cereus or E. coli, and a kitchen is inspected once for its SLHS while a plant can be audited daily. The new rule makes it worse: more raw handling by tired volunteers in less time. Move the raw step upstream.
- **First 90 days:** Recruit a retired Dinkes/BPOM food-safety lead and a HACCP auditor. Lease one NKV poultry room and cold store near a dense cluster. Run chicken and vegetable kits for five kitchens, logging time and temperature and counting incidents. Ask the BGN deputy for food safety and a regional coordinator for written confirmation that certified uncooked inputs are acceptable under Keputusan 401.1/2025 and the 4-hour rule. Sign a supply MoU with one poultry farmer cooperative.
- **Legal premise to check:** That certified processed inputs from a plant count as an eligible supply (Peraturan BGN 10/2026 lists processors with food-safety and halal certificates, which suggests yes, U) and that the 4-hour label rule applies only to cooked food. No ban on prepared inputs was found (S: searches), but no primary text was read.

## 2. Satu Faktur: kecamatan consolidation hubs in idle Kopdes cold stores (INSIDER: village cooperative / BUMDes manager or SPPG accountant, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** Run the state-built Kopdes cold-store warehouse in each kecamatan as the one place that turns 15-40 eligible micro-suppliers into one purchase order, one invoice, one delivery and one payment per kitchen, with the cooperative as the legal seller.
- **Customer:** The 27,820 kitchens (3.9 per kecamatan on about 7,200 kecamatan, A) and the Kopdes boards that own an empty warehouse, a logistics vehicle and a Rp3B Himbara loan.
- **Payer and signer:** Kitchens pay through the VA (service fee carved from the same Rp10k, not added); signer is the Kepala SPPG plus the yayasan representative. The Kopdes board chair signs the management contract; the Himbara regional commercial/micro head is the sponsor (protecting a Rp3B loan); at scale the Kemenkop deputy minister for cooperatives.
- **Problem:** Rules now require a kitchen to buy from at least 15 eligible suppliers, local-first, with NIB and NPWP, tax withholding, BGN-format budget lines, no mark-ups, no commissions, on pain of suspension or dismissal; the Attorney General has just named the former BGN leadership as suspects. The Kepala SPPG's problem is paperwork and personal liability, not price. Meanwhile 14,442 Kopdes buildings with a mandated cold-store and vehicle exist, most idle (only 1,061 inaugurated as operating in May, U), and their loan instalments fell due on 25 Sep.
- **Solution:** A hub operated under a management contract inside the Kopdes warehouse: receive from local farmers, millers and processors; weigh, grade, temperature-check and document; consolidate per kitchen per day; invoice once in e-faktur form; print BGN-format budget lines; pay suppliers within 48 hours (fed by early-pay discount); keep price-benchmark logs that make a mark-up impossible and a dispute short. The cooperative is the seller of record, so Peraturan BGN 10/2026 eligibility holds. The hub is also the physical node of the BGN portal for the seven mandated commodities.
- **Why now:** Peraturan BGN 10/2026 (18 Aug); portal announcement (12 and 17 Sep) with marketplace partners wanted; SE 14/2026 (12 Aug) pushing poultry through farmer cooperatives; Kopdes instalments due 25 Sep; Kejagung (29 Sep) making defensible buying more valuable than cheap buying.
- **Wedge:** Three districts with a finished gerai, a working cold room and four or more kitchens (a Pamekasan-type Kopdes already supplies a kitchen with eggs and sembako). Run eggs, chicken and vegetables first. First 10 customers: Kepala SPPG in those kecamatan; first sponsor: the Himbara branch holding the Kopdes loan.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Average hub gross merchandise value is 3.9 kitchens x $300k = $1.16M a year. At a 6% consolidation fee that is $69k of revenue per hub, so $100M needs about 1,440 hubs (20% of kecamatan, 20% of the $8.3B ingredient flow). Adding supplier early-pay discount and documentation fees takes the take to 8.5% = $98k per hub and about 1,020 hubs (14%). If the cooperative books sales and we take management fee plus margin, the same $100M is about 85 hubs of principal sales but only 6-8% net. Share needed is 14-20%: heroic, so say so. The portal and mandatory seven commodities are the reason it is not hopeless: the share comes from a mandate, not from persuading each kitchen.
- **Who does it today:** Telkom Digi Koperasi (software for 80,081 coops, BRILink for coop banking), Agrinas Pangan with TNI (builds gerai), ID FOOD (dry goods to 108 coops), Bulog (rice), the BGN portal itself (SIPLah model; 15 SIPLah operators including Telkom/Xooply), Sayurbox, Eden Farm, Chickin, farmer associations' direct-sale deals (East Java eggs), informal middlemen (tengkulak), and Pintek-style supplier financing on SIPLah (2021 precedent). No operator running Kopdes warehouses as MBG hubs found.
- **Why they lose:** Telkom sells software to bookkeepers, not field operations; Himbara cannot operate what it lends to; marketplaces cannot hold cold chain; integrators are not eligible sellers and conflict with their own distributors; tengkulak cannot produce NIB, NPWP and e-faktur without exposing margin; Kopdes boards were appointed in a rush and have six business lines to run.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (management contracts in every kecamatan where the cooperative is the eligible seller; Himbara sponsorship) plus switching costs (a kitchen's budget-line history, supplier network and payment record live in the hub).
- **Earned secret:** A Kepala SPPG will pay for the most defensible purchase, not the cheapest one: after the Attorney General's announcement nobody wants to sign an invoice they cannot explain. A coop manager knows that village cooperatives only survive with a guaranteed anchor buyer (the old KUDs lived on sugar mills and milk processors) and MBG is the only anchor on offer; the Rp3B loan clock is the lever.
- **First 90 days:** Sign management MoUs with three Kopdes boards and their Himbara branches; recruit a Kopdes veteran and an ex-BGN accountant; start egg, chicken and vegetable lanes for six kitchens; produce BGN-format lines and pay suppliers T+2; measure rejected lines before and after.
- **Legal premise to check:** Whether a private operator may run a Kopdes warehouse with the coop as seller of record under Peraturan BGN 10/2026 and the portal terms (not read, U), and whether a hub's fee counts as an allowable "at cost" line or as a prohibited commission.

## 3. Franchisor-in-a-box for the institutional operator groups (PATTERN: "concentrated-operator vertical platform", EliseAI shape)

- **One-liner:** Sell the HQ functions a franchisor would have (central lab, procurement master agreement, treasury and lender reporting, training) to the handful of institutional groups that run hundreds of kitchens under one chain of command.
- **Customer:** Yayasan Kemala Bhayangkari/Polri (1,179 kitchens in Feb 2026, 1,415 built and 828 operating by June, target 1,500), Kadin (its 742nd kitchen opened 6 Mar 2026, target 1,000), TNI (2,000 targeted), BIN (1,000), NU (1,000) and Muhammadiyah (500) (the last four are March 2025 targets, U).
- **Payer and signer:** HQ operations/logistics heads under a master services agreement, paid per kitchen per month out of the "at cost" operating line and the facility incentive: for Polri the logistics assistant to the Kapolri and the chair of Yayasan Kemala Bhayangkari; for Kadin the chair and the MBG task force head.
- **Problem:** BGN capped each yayasan at 10 kitchens (Polri exempt, per ICW), so about 13,000 mitra are too small to buy anything; the only owners with command-and-control are institutional. They are franchisors without a franchise toolkit. Polri builds warehouses (18, rising to 28 of 1,000 t), farms and a feed mill, yet purchases are still kitchen by kitchen from selected suppliers; their kitchens carry Himbara and cooperative loans that need covenant-grade reporting; ICW has asked the KPK to oversee them.
- **Solution:** (1) A central accredited lab and retention-sample programme; (2) master supply agreements with Kopdes and farmer coops for the seven commodities, executed through hubs (idea 2) and plants (idea 1); (3) lender reporting from VA data, compliance (SLHS, IPAL, BPJS) and incident dashboards; (4) a training academy for the 47-person crews. Pricing: about $1k per kitchen-month plus a 2.5% procurement margin share and lab fees.
- **Why now:** Polri and Kadin only reached 1,000+ kitchens in 2026; the mark-up ban, 4-hour rule and Kejagung case make unmanaged purchasing a career risk for generals and chairs; moratorium on new kitchens means growth must come from running the existing ones better.
- **Wedge:** Kadin's 742 kitchens in 3T and provincial clusters (business-led, less politically charged than Polri). Build a central lab and master supply agreement for 50 kitchens in one province, with a lender dashboard for the Himbara bank behind them.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Per kitchen $12k fee plus 2.5% of about $300k = $19.5k a year, so $100M is about 5,100 kitchens. Institutional groups hold about 4,000-5,000 kitchens if targets are met (U), so this needs nearly all of them: 18% of all kitchens but only 4-6 signatures. If the master agreement is a principal supply contract, $100M of revenue is about 25% of Polri-plus-Kadin ingredient flow ($0.6B + $0.2B at 2,200 portions, A). Heroic and politically exposed; flagged.
- **Who does it today:** In-house HQ logistics (Polri's food-security warehouses, farms and feed mills), BGN's SIPGN and Peruri-managed IoT, Telkom, IDSurvey (Sucofindo and Surveyor Indonesia offered QA support in April 2025), consulting firms. No private franchise-toolkit vendor found.
- **Why they lose:** In-house HQ units move at command speed, not lab speed, and attract ICW and KPK attention; BUMN IT sells software; Sucofindo-type inspectors audit but do not procure or train.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs (embedded in an HQ's operating manual and loan covenants) plus scale economies in procurement; counter-positioning against in-house build (they would have to admit failure).
- **Earned secret (pattern):** The cap of 10 kitchens per yayasan, not the market, fragmented the operator base; the only enterprise accounts in this industry are institutions. Win two signatures and you cover 8-10% of the programme; the pattern says widen to the system of record after.
- **First 90 days:** Warm introduction through Kadin's MBG team; offer a 50-kitchen pilot with central lab plus lender dashboard at cost; do a reputational screen before any Polri discussion; recruit a former police logistics officer and a former bank covenant manager.
- **Legal premise to check:** The Polri exemption from the 10-kitchen cap (ICW claim only), conflict-of-interest rules for foundations led by officers' spouses, and whether service fees can sit in the "at cost" line.

## 4. Java-to-3T protein corridor (PATTERN: "marketplace-owned logistics where infrastructure is weak", Mercado Libre)

- **One-liner:** Own the chilled lane that ships Java's egg and chicken surplus to the 1,700-plus frontier kitchens where BGN says the same programme costs up to three times as much.
- **Customer:** 3T kitchens in Papua, Maluku, NTT and Sulawesi: 480 activated 2 Oct 2026 (211 independent mitra, 269 remote units), 16 provinces; ministry sources cite 1,703 (or 352, conflict) 3T kitchens in the pipeline; a presidential target of 2,500 kitchens in Papua was reported (U).
- **Payer and signer:** The kitchen's VA with an indexed budget (the cost-of-living index BGN is preparing), signed by the Kepala SPPG and mitra; at scale a master supply agreement with BGN's 3T programme office, a Danantara-financed kitchen programme, or Polri/TNI units building kitchens in Papua.
- **Problem:** Eligible local sellers barely exist in remote kecamatan, so the local-first rule falls through to "outside province" with a trader taking a 2-3x mark-up; LPG shortages halted NTT kitchens in May; poisoning in Jayapura and Depapre cost kitchen heads their jobs; Java has an egg and chicken surplus that the state is begging kitchens to absorb.
- **Solution:** Contract reefer slots on Pelni/Tol Laut from Surabaya and Makassar to cold-store hubs in Jayapura, Sorong, Ambon, Kupang and Merauke; feed them from Blitar egg cooperatives and East Java slaughterhouses; run last-mile trucks and boats on a published delivery calendar (kitchen menus and school holidays are public); sell frozen chicken, graded or pasteurised eggs, and dry goods at the indexed price with batch traceability. Later add local fish and sago.
- **Why now:** 3T activation on 2 Oct; moratorium elsewhere pushes the growth to the east; 12 Aug poultry circular and surplus eggs; the cost index and rent incentive are being set now; kitchens in the east cannot meet the 4-hour rule without reliable inputs.
- **Wedge:** One lane, Surabaya to Jayapura/Sorong, for 20 of the newly activated independent kitchens, with frozen chicken and eggs only. First 10 customers: independent mitra in Papua and NTT plus Polri's Papua kitchens.
- **Path to $100M (A):** 1,703 kitchens x 1,800 portions x 220 days x an indexed Rp20k per portion (about 2x Java) = $0.84B a year. $100M of revenue is 12% of that flow, at 15-20% gross margin (logistics premium). If only 352 3T kitchens materialise the pool is about $0.17B and the idea fails; that is the main uncertainty (U).
- **Who does it today:** Makassar/Bugis and Surabaya trading networks, Pelni and Tol Laut (shipping only), Bulog (rice), Pertamina (LPG), Japfa and Charoen through distributors, provincial BUMD logistics, Polri's food-security warehouses, TNI/Agrinas. No cold-chain operator contracted to 3T kitchens found.
- **Why they lose:** Traders have no cold chain, paperwork or price index; shipping lines do not run door-to-door or quality control; Polri warehouses serve Polri; integrators sell at Java prices to distributors.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies in lane utilisation (menu calendars make volume predictable) plus cornered resource (contracted reefer slots and Papua cold stores that cannot be copied quickly).
- **Earned secret:** Where logistics is weak the platform that owns it wins. A 3T kitchen is a captive, price-indexed buyer with a published calendar (SE 12/2026), and Java's surplus makes the backhaul-free outbound cheap; the real cost is empty containers and cold-chain gaps, which a scheduled lane converts into a utilisation problem.
- **First 90 days:** Secure reefer slots Surabaya-Jayapura on Tol Laut/Pelni; contract a Blitar egg cooperative and a Jawa Timur slaughterhouse; lease a cold store in Jayapura; get the draft cost index from BGN's 3T office; pilot 10 kitchens and compute landed cost against the trader price.
- **Legal premise to check:** The local-first sequence (Article 5) allows out-of-province purchase when no local supplier qualifies; who is the eligible seller of record for imported-from-Java goods in 3T (coop or UMKM wrapper).

## 5. Kitchen workforce employer-of-record: the liability BGN cannot take (DILEMMA: BGN cannot reclassify 1.3M workers without exploding its budget)

- **One-liner:** Become the legal employer, payroll and BPJS provider for the roughly 1.3M "volunteers" so the yayasan chair stops carrying employer liability and the at-cost wage claim becomes audit-proof.
- **Customer:** About 13,000 yayasan/mitra responsible for 47 workers per kitchen (S).
- **Payer and signer:** The yayasan chair, from the Rp3k-per-portion operating line (BGN already allows BPJS premiums and wages to be reimbursed at cost); Kepala SPPG co-approves in the VA.
- **Problem:** Komnas HAM (15 Jun 2026) says cooks and drivers are workers, not volunteers; Keputusan 401.1/2025 makes the yayasan responsible for BPJS, yet 278,614 workers in 5,322 kitchens were unprotected in April; pay is cash, below minimum wage in places, with a worker in Langkat hurt commuting to work and uncovered. At-cost wage claims are also an audit risk: ghost volunteers are the classic leak and the Attorney General is looking.
- **Solution:** An employer-of-record plus instant-pay rail: contract workers, pay daily to bank or e-wallet, face-and-QR attendance matched to 02:00 starts, BPJS JKK/JKM (Rp16,800 a month), PPh 21, injury response, and an at-cost invoice pack (hours, pay slips, bank proofs) that a Kepala SPPG and KPPN can accept. Optional earned-wage access and micro-insurance on top.
- **Why now:** Komnas HAM statement; BPJS mandate; Kejagung; 02:00 attendance rules; cooperation-agreement renewal this month.
- **Wedge:** Yayasan with 5-10 kitchens in one province, introduced by BSI/BNI relationship managers worried about their loans and by the mitra association.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Wage pool = 47 x Rp100k a day (assumed; derived from the Rp3k operating line, no official rate) x 220 days x 27,820 = $1.8B. As an employer-of-record booking gross billings (like a staffing firm), $100M of revenue is 5.5% of the wage pool, about 1,530 kitchens. Net take is about 6% of payroll: $108M net only at 100% share. Payroll gross revenue is easy, profit is not; flagged.
- **Who does it today:** BPJS Ketenagakerjaan (insurance only), alih-daya outsourcing firms, payroll software (Mekari Talenta, Kledo), earned-wage-access fintechs (not verified for this niche), yayasan cash payroll.
- **Why they lose:** Payroll software does not take employer liability; outsourcers do not know at-cost reimbursement, VAs or BGN reporting; BPJS covers accidents but not payroll.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Process power (compliance know-how under changing BGN rules) plus switching costs (payroll history, BPJS records, attendance data usable for credit).
- **Earned secret:** BGN's refusal to say "employee" is a budget decision: paying 1.3M workers a monthly minimum wage (about Rp3M, A) would cost about Rp50T against roughly Rp29T now, i.e. about Rp21T more in a budget that is being trimmed. The ambiguity therefore persists until a court or labour inspector forces it, and the first yayasan sanctioned will buy cover overnight.
- **First 90 days:** Build with a labour lawyer and a BPJS Ketenagakerjaan partner; pilot ten kitchens in one regency with daily pay through a Himbara bank VA; get a KPPN/Kepala SPPG to accept the audit pack; approach the mitra association.
- **Legal premise to check:** Whether an EOR is allowed to employ workers whose status BGN calls volunteers, whether the employer fee is reimbursable at cost, and Indonesian alih-daya licensing limits for core-business workers.

## 6. Mitra resolution operator: the special servicer banks and BGN cannot be (DILEMMA) - weakest of the eight, flagged

- **One-liner:** Take management contracts over stalled, suspended and non-compliant kitchens from their owners and their banks, fix them, get them reinstated and share the restored incentive.
- **Customer:** Passive mitra (facility owners) and the special-assets units of Himbara banks. Pool: about 13,000 stalled kitchens (Rp8.7T at risk), about 4,500 suspended in May, 1,999 closed on 7 Sep for not registering, 504-plus closed permanently.
- **Payer and signer:** The mitra owner (fee from the restored incentive: Rp6M a day) with the bank's head of special assets co-signing a workout letter; BGN's regional coordinator accepts the resubmission.
- **Problem:** Bank collateral is a building with no use except a kitchen and a permit that lapses when the kitchen is closed; foreclosure recovers cents, and Kontan expects bad loans to rise from September. BGN suspends on any poisoning report without a hearing (mitra association complaint), promised a settlement scheme on 27 Jul but has not defined it, and cannot run kitchens itself.
- **Solution:** A hotel-management-style contract: we manage the non-BGN half of the kitchen (facility, volunteer workforce, compliance, utilities, supply) for a fee, fix SLHS/IPAL/gas/BPJS, and resubmit. Use BGN's new yayasan-switch route (2 Oct) to place kitchens under a clean foundation. Stack ideas 1, 2, 5 and 8 as paid modules.
- **Why now:** SLHS closure wave; bank NPL fear; cooperation-agreement renewal; the yayasan fee exposure; moratorium makes each existing permit scarce.
- **Wedge:** Ten suspended kitchens referred by two banks' special-assets teams, paid 15% of restored incentive for 24 months.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Per kitchen: 15% of $82.5k restored incentive = $12.4k, plus about $5k compliance/utility margin and $4k workforce fee = $21k a year. $100M needs about 4,700 kitchens (17% of all kitchens, maybe 30% of reinstatable distressed ones). Heroic; and the pool is one-off until BGN allows steady-state management contracts.
- **Who does it today:** Bank special-assets units, the mitra association (lobbying only), crowdfunding platforms Danamart and Bizhare (one has said it will only monitor existing projects), BGN's pending settlement scheme, Danantara's asset management MoU (new builds). No private resolution operator found.
- **Why they lose:** Banks cannot operate or foreclose a permit; BGN cannot restructure 27k private contracts or run kitchens; the association cannot provide services; passive owners lack expertise.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Process power (a standard turnaround playbook) and, if bank-sanctioned, a cornered resource (referral flow).
- **Earned secret (bank officer):** The collateral is the permit and the VA stream, not the building. Banks see VA inflows daily and know which kitchens fail 60-90 days ahead, but have no operator to hand them to.
- **First 90 days:** Meet special-assets heads at BSI, BNI and BTN; write a standard turnaround checklist from the SLHS/IPAL/4-hour requirements; take three owners on 24-month contracts; clear the legal route with BGN.
- **Legal premise to check:** Transfers of operation without a handover document are disputed (Yayasan Garuda Muda Dharmagati reported an account transfer to the Attorney General); kitchens closed permanently receive no payment; the 10-kitchen cap per yayasan means ownership roll-up is blocked, so only management contracts are possible and BGN may refuse them.

## 7. Telur Koperasi: farmer-cooperative egg grading, pasteurising and glut-buffer plant (SHOCK: HAP price, SE 14/2026 and the holiday calendar)

- **One-liner:** Build the processing plant that turns the state's forced egg demand into a stable business: grade, sanitise and pasteurise local eggs for kitchens, and freeze or dry the glut for industry.
- **Customer:** Kitchens in egg-producing provinces (East Java, Lampung, North Sumatra, South Sulawesi), plus bakery and noodle makers who now import egg powder or frozen liquid egg.
- **Payer and signer:** The kitchen VA for graded and pasteurised egg (HAP-based price plus a service premium); industrial buyers for powder and frozen product; a farmer-cooperative chair signs as seller of record; Bapanas/Kementan are policy sponsors, not payers.
- **Problem:** Eggs are the mandated protein (three times a week) but the programme pauses on weekends and school holidays while hens do not; in June farm-gate fell below cost and Blitar farmers gave away a million eggs; the response was decrees (HAP Rp26,500/kg, circulars threatening suspension, SE 14/2026) that force kitchens to buy but do not create storage, grading or Salmonella control (Salmonella was found on eggs in BGN's analysis).
- **Solution:** A cooperative-owned plant: washing, UV sanitising, grading and coding of shell eggs; pasteurised liquid egg for omelettes and scrambles (labour-saving, Salmonella-safe); frozen liquid and spray-dried powder to soak up troughs and supply industry; cracks and downgrades (3-5% of volume, A) become product instead of waste. Offtake contracts follow the BGN holiday calendar so farmers know the next six weeks.
- **Why now:** Egg price collapse (May-June); HAP and circulars (June-August); 13% surplus forecast (U); SE 12/2026 publishes the pause calendar; kitchens ordered to serve eggs three times a week.
- **Wedge:** One Blitar-Kediri-Tulungagung plant (leased breaking line plus grading) supplying 10-20 local kitchens (the local-first rule favours them) and one bakery buyer.
- **Path to $100M (A):** MBG eggs: 60.6M x 132 egg-days = 8.0B eggs = 480 kt, worth $0.79B at HAP. At about Rp34k per kg of liquid-egg equivalent, $100M is 47 kt of liquid egg = 0.89B eggs = 11% of MBG egg demand (0.7% of national output), two 100k-egg-per-hour lines at two shifts. Capex about $12-20M (A). Gross margin about 10-12% ($10-12M); the HAP price floor limits the premium kitchens will pay (about +16% over shell egg, A), so adoption depends on safety and labour savings.
- **Who does it today:** Intan Kenkomayo (Japfa joint venture; pasteurised liquid egg, mayonnaise), Japfa and Charoen's own egg operations, imports of frozen liquid egg (441 t in 2021) and egg powder (flagged by parliament in 2023), Pinsar and cooperative direct-sale deals in East Java, and government decrees. No cooperative-owned processing at MBG scale found.
- **Why they lose:** Integrators compete with independent farmers and are not the micro, small and cooperative sellers SE 14/2026 favours; Intan Kenkomayo serves bakeries and horeca; farmers lack processing assets and credit.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning against integrators (cooperative ownership qualifies for the mandate) plus cornered resource (offtake contracts and processing assets).
- **Earned secret (egg trader, 15+ years):** The price signal is a WhatsApp rumour that moves farm-gate in hours; cracks and downgrades are where the margin lives; and the programme's pauses are scheduled, so the glut is predictable. Only someone who can store (frozen, dried) can profit from a predictable glut.
- **First 90 days:** Form a cooperative-of-record with Blitar egg cooperatives; lease a used 30k-eggs-per-hour breaking line; sample 10 kitchens with graded and liquid egg; test acceptance of liquid egg by kitchen heads; confirm the SE 14/2026 text on processed forms.
- **Legal premise to check:** Whether processed egg from a cooperative plant satisfies SE 14/2026 and the HAP rule (it targets farmers and cooperatives); halal and food-safety certification for processors; BGN menu acceptance of liquid egg.

## 8. Dapur Utilitas: IPAL, gas and water as a per-portion service (SHOCK: IPAL mandate, 3 kg LPG ban, SLHS closures) - flagged

- **One-liner:** Finance, own and operate each kitchen's wastewater plant, bulk gas and water treatment, and bill it per portion inside the "at cost" operating line.
- **Customer:** Mitra/yayasan of full-size kitchens, especially the ones that were suspended for IPAL or lack gas supply.
- **Payer and signer:** The mitra, from the Rp3k operating line (power, water, gas are reimbursable at cost); the yayasan chair signs a 5-7 year service contract; infrastructure funds and Danantara/Himbara are capital providers.
- **Problem:** IPAL is mandatory from 2026 and cost Rp18-70M in one region, with substandard units common; 1,780 kitchens were suspended in April for IPAL or SLHS; 3 kg LPG is banned and 50 kg cylinders must be pre-ordered, with NTT kitchens halted in May; mitra capital is exhausted and banks will not finance single-purpose assets.
- **Solution:** Bundle wastewater (IPAL, grease trap, sludge removal, quarterly effluent tests), water treatment, bulk CNG/LPG skids with telemetry and induction/steam retrofits into one per-portion fee (Rp400-600, A), with a savings share against current energy cost. Each kitchen gets compliance evidence that Dinkes and the Environment Ministry accept.
- **Why now:** IPAL enforcement; LPG ban (27 Jul); SLHS closures; PGN's CNG pilot (46 kitchens, claimed 15-20% savings) proves the product.
- **Wedge:** Two districts with PGN CNG access: sign ten kitchens for IPAL-as-a-service with CNG supply, no capex for the mitra.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Gas about $10.3k per full-size kitchen (from the Magelang CNG example scaled to 3,000 portions and 10 operating months), power about $7k (assumed), IPAL/water/waste about $5k: about $22k a year, so a $610M pool across 27,820 kitchens. $100M is about 4,500 kitchens, 16% of the pool, with capital of about $110-180M ($25-40k per kitchen, A). Heroic.
- **Who does it today:** Pertamina Patra Niaga (Bright Gas 50 kg/12 kg), PGN/Pertagas (CNG, pipeline), PLN, local IPAL contractors, PDAM and depot water vendors, Aneka Gas and Samator, regional task forces pushing IPAL. No bundler found.
- **Why they lose:** Each incumbent sells one commodity through a network monopoly and none finances on-site assets or carries compliance; mitra cannot capex.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs (installed assets, 5-7 year contracts) plus scale economies in procurement and maintenance.
- **Earned secret:** The cost of non-compliance (a Rp6M-a-day incentive) dwarfs the capex of compliance ($1-4k for an IPAL), so the binding constraint is cash and know-how, not willingness to pay. A cheap gas price is worthless if the cylinder does not arrive.
- **First 90 days:** Sign PGN/Perta Samtan as CNG supplier; design a standard IPAL module; fund ten kitchens; measure savings; approach an infrastructure fund.
- **Legal premise to check:** That utilities and service fees are reimbursable at cost, that LPG distribution outside Pertamina channels is permitted, and Kementerian LH effluent standards (Kepmen LH 276/2025).

## Ideas killed or not written up

- Kitchen OS / accounting SaaS: BGN and the finance ministry ship a free reporting app (Aplikasi Pelaporan Keuangan SPPG v4) integrated with SIPGN (Rp550B); free state substitute kills it.
- Telemetry, CCTV and evidence capture for kitchens: BGN has an IoT managed service (Rp199B, Peruri), plans CCTV to head office, IFSR-KBI sells AI CCTV at Rp30 per portion and Indosat offers Vision AI; BGN's whole IT budget (Rp1.2T, about $75M) caps all vendors.
- Per-meal assurance or test-kit rail: test kits are only "considered"; Polri develops its own; even Rp100 per meal over 13.3B meals is $83M and needs 100% adoption.
- SLHS/IPAL consulting alone: $3-4k per kitchen means $100M at 100% share; folded into idea 8.
- Supplier finance and VA split-payment lending: a 14-day float on $8.3B is about $320M outstanding, about $58M of interest at 18% and 100% share; the five state banks own the VAs and KUR offers up to Rp500M; Pintek-style supplier finance on SIPLah was tried in 2021.
- Kitchen roll-up or step-in ownership: 10-kitchen cap per yayasan, registrations permanently closed, disputed account transfers (Garuda Muda Dharmagati); only management contracts survive (idea 6).
- Portal marketplace-partner business alone: 15 SIPLah operators exist and Telkom Xooply is the incumbent; a 1.5% fee on $7B (A) is about $105M to split; folded into idea 2.
- Dairy and school-milk plants: BGN told kitchens in July not to use branded packaged milk, but milk is not mandatory, the budget cannot carry it and the 20% to 80% milk-share rule I saw in one summary could not be confirmed.
- Trays: a one-off purchase (70-82.9M units), imports and counterfeit scandals.
- Time-temperature label rail: BGN's consumption-deadline label is a printed label; $0.01 a meal is not a venture.
- Mitra insurance: most suspensions follow compliance failures, which are not insurable.
- Incentive-stream securitisation and crowdfunding: fee pool about $30M; Danamart says it will only monitor existing projects.
- Idle-day catering marketplace: institutional catering demand is tiny compared with 81M idle meals a day.
- Tray-wash hubs and kitchen automation: BGN forbids laying off volunteers; labour is the point.
- Claims aggregation for Rp8.7T of stalled kitchens and Rp1.6T of contractor arrears: no scheme yet, one-off, roughly $10-30M of fees.

# Gate report: P137 (US graduate borrowing displaced from Grad PLUS, ~$8B a year of loan volume)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: adversarial. Default assumption was that someone already does each idea and the market is smaller than claimed. About 40 searches and fetches. Two pages returned 403 (cuinsight.com and creditunions.studentchoice.org), so CU Student Choice facts come from search summaries. Evidence is tagged [READ] where I read the page, [S] where it is a search summary, and [M] where it is from memory or my own arithmetic.

**Result: 0 picks.** Two weak (Seasoning Factory is the best of the pool; Offer-Backed is second), six kills. The pool is real ($8B a year of displaced loan volume, a 6-7x market expansion), but the volume is not revenue. The school-side ideas (software, desk, score, test replica) sit on fee pools of single-digit to low-tens of millions. The only ideas that reach $100M do it by counting gross interest on a $1B book funded with roughly $0.9B of borrowed money. That is a lending business competing with Sallie Mae-KKR, Nelnet, Ascent and the ABF desks, and none of the eight shows an edge over them.

## Sizing convention (important)

- **Net revenue** = fees plus net interest income (interest earned minus funding cost), not gross interest on a financed book. This matches the P104 gate ("not pass-through"). Several ideas in this pool quote *gross* interest on a $1B balance as "revenue". I show gross in brackets where it differs, so the orchestrator can compare either way. Gross interest on money you borrowed is not comparable to a software fee.
- Median = 5-7 years, competent execution. Upside = 5-7 years, top-decile execution.
- Rule used: **kill** = already done, false premise, or small even in the upside; **weak** = real pain but upside under $100M or a major unresolved defect; **strong** = upside at least $100M, median at least $20M, and a defensible edge. Nothing reached strong.

## Pool-level arithmetic (the reason nothing is strong)

- Total displaced volume is ~$8B a year, but lender revenue accrues on a *book* that builds over many years (in-school deferral, then 10-15 year amortization). By years 5-7 of the ramp, balances reach roughly $30B ([M]: about $9B a year of private graduate originations times 3-4 years of build), so the whole pool's net interest pot is on the order of $1B a year at a ~3.5% net spread ([M]). That pot is shared with Sallie Mae (about 64% of private student lending, [S] NBER), SoFi, Citizens, Navy Federal, Nelnet/Navient and Ascent. A new entrant needs about 10% of the pool to reach $100M *net*. The brief's own rule flags more than 20% share as heroic, and this is half that, but it is still heroic for a startup.
- Calibration for the lender upside: College Ave took about 8 years to reach $1B a year of originations, and CommonBond funded more than $5B and still closed in 2022 [S]. A top-decile lender reaching a $1.5B balance in 6-7 years is plausible if the market really grows 6-7x. That is about $150M gross interest and about $50-55M net.
- School-side fee pools are small. NBER cites school contributions of 5% (Kansas Law) and 7.5% (WashU Law) on school-sponsored loans, and the idea assumes 4 points. If school-sponsored loans reach 10% of the gap (~$0.8B), the entire fee pool is about $32-60M ([M] arithmetic), and a neutral intermediary keeps a slice of it.

## Idea-by-idea evidence

### 1. Gap Ledger (kill)

**Competitors and substitutes**
- Lenders already give schools the gap math for free. Ascent publishes a Grad PLUS calculator "built for institutions and financial aid professionals", and a funding calculator with GMAC (19 May 2026) [S]. [Ascent](https://www.ascentfunding.com/grad-plus-calculator/), [P&Q](https://poetsandquants.com/2026/05/19/mba-students-face-an-8-94-reality-gmacs-new-financing-deal-is-a-sign-of-whats-coming/).
- Schools run the lender process in house. Columbia and Pratt ran RFPs, Georgetown names six preferred lenders, and Harvard received 22 lender submissions to its 2026 RFI and said it would consider risk-sharing [S]. [Columbia](https://sfs.columbia.edu/content/university-disclosure-private-lenders), [Pratt](https://www.pratt.edu/admissions/graduate-admissions/finance-your-education/financial-aid-options/graduate-loans/), [Harvard](https://sfs.harvard.edu/resource/harvard-university-preferred-lender-selection-process).
- Juno is a free buyer-side intermediary (negotiates with competing lenders "at no cost to schools or students"), claims 14-15% of private in-school graduate lending in 2025 (company claim) and is hiring a financial-aid partnerships team. [Juno job post](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/juno/jobs/4016498009), [AAPA](https://www.aapa.org/news-central/2026/07/aapa-juno-announce-strategic-partnership-to-expand-student-loan-resources-for-pa-students-and-practicing-pas/).
- SIS vendors are shipping the cap logic. Workday's 2026R2 release lists the new annual, aggregate and lifetime limits and an override for NSLDS aggregates [S]. I found nothing for Ellucian or Anthology (absence of evidence only).

**Revenue math**
- Leg 1 (software): the idea concedes 1,000 programs is "most of that universe". A realistic median is about 150 programs at $25k.
- Leg 2 (desk): $2B a year of loans is 25% of the *entire* gap. At 10% of the gap, 4 points and a 15% take, revenue is about $5M.
- Leg 3 (held book): this is the Seasoning Factory's balance sheet, so it is idea 2's number, not this idea's.
- Median ~$5M, upside ~$35M (500 programs at $40k plus the full $12M desk number the idea itself claims).

**Legal or premise check.** The HEOA rule (at least three unaffiliated lenders on a preferred-lender list) is [S] from NBER. It does not stop a lender-owned calculator, but it does not create a buyer budget either. No school has been shown to pay a fixed per-loan fee through a third-party agent. Kansas and WashU fund it themselves.

**Deciding reason.** A feature with a free substitute, and a fee pool smaller than the target. Not a company.

### 2. Seasoning Factory (weak, best in pool)

**Competitors and substitutes (the premise "no multi-originator aggregator" fails)**
- Nelnet agreed in Feb 2015 to buy up to $150M of CommonBond's private graduate loans ($64.2M bought by 30 Jun 2015), put an ABS together from Truist- and Cognition-originated loans, and in 2021 sponsored four ABS for $8.7B of loans sold by Wells Fargo (its own JV interest was about 8%) [S, from Nelnet filings]. [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1258602/000125860221000076/nni-20210930.htm), [ASR](https://asreport.americanbanker.com/news/nelnet-prepared-to-raise-358-9-million-in-abs).
- Ascent has already shown the chicken-and-egg problem can be crossed. It issued a $287M rated ABS (Ascent Education Funding Trust 2024-A, DBRS AA/A/BBB(L), Atlas SP and Barclays) in 2024, and the DBRS review in May 2025 says losses are higher than expected because delinquent loans were in the pool at closing. MPOWER also marketed an ABS [S]. [Ascent](https://www.ascentfunding.com/blog/ascent-completes-first-public-securitization-of-college-loans-in-287-million-offering/).
- Ascent funds originations with bank partners (Bank of Lake Mills, DR Bank), not from an unfunded gap [S].
- CU Student Choice is a credit-union platform. Langley FCU and Elements Financial launched professional-degree lines in Aug 2026 and "a dozen more" were expected within 45 days [S, page 403]. [Press release](https://www.cuinsight.com/press-release/cu-student-choice-launches-new-professional-degree-financing-solutions-as-grad-plus-loans-phase-out/).
- Sallie Mae is its own conduit. KKR buys a seed portfolio plus at least $2B a year for at least three years, as a true sale with full risk transfer, and a second loan-sale partner is "advancing" [S]. [SLM Q2](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/slm-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-07-23/), [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/sallie-mae-joins-forces-with-private-equity-giant-kkr).
- Forward flow is broadly available. A Morgan Lewis partner says forward flow is "an option for just about any lender" (Jan 2026) [READ, 15-word summary page]. Other consumer-credit forward flows are large (TPG-OneMain $2.4B). [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/news/2026/01/abf-enthusiasm-opens-forward-flow-channel-to-smaller-sellers), [SCI](https://www.structuredcreditinvestor.com/news/asset-backed-finance/84512/abf-deal-digest:-tpg,-onemain-extend-forward-flow-agreement-to-us$2.4bn).
- Carlyle/KKR (Discover book) and Apollo/Blackstone (Wells book) already hold the legacy books and have servicing through Nelnet/Firstmark. They are the Factory's would-be capital, and also its natural competitors for the same originator flow.

**Revenue math**
- The idea's $100M is gross interest: $1B balance times 10%. After roughly 6-7% funding cost and losses, net revenue on $1B is about $35-40M plus structuring fees.
- Median: a $300M balance by year 6-7 gives about $30M gross and about $12M net (including fees).
- Upside: a $1.5B balance (about 8% of a $9.4B private graduate market) gives about $150M gross and about $55M net. Reaching $100M *net* needs about $3B, which is not a 5-7 year outcome.

**Legal or premise check.** Reg B's final rule (effective 21 Jul 2026) removed the federal disparate-impact test, but state attorneys general kept enforcement authority (Earnest paid Massachusetts $2.5M) [S, via NBER and Husch Blackwell]. Pricing grids that vary by program within a field remain a state-law risk. [Husch Blackwell](https://www.huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/cfpb-finalizes-major-regulation-b-overhaul-disparate-impact-out-discouragement-narrowed-and-spcps-restricted). The First Marblehead/TERI failure lessons (single guarantor, chain-of-title gaps) are valid and sound design advice. [PBN](https://pbn.com/teri-collapse-unionfed-cash-drainbrtake-toll-on-student-lender-fmc36487/).

**Upside assumptions and plausibility**
- Raise about $100M of first-loss capital for an unproven conduit: low to moderate.
- Sign 8-10 originators who would rather sell to KKR or Nelnet and keep their brand and servicing: low to moderate.
- Insurers take about $0.9B of 10-15 year notes: moderate (insurers like the duration).
- Outcome-based loans perform: unknown. Ascent's own ABS shows higher-than-expected losses on an early pool.

**Deciding reason.** A real gap (data and first-loss capital for new outcome-based loans) but no verified edge over the capital that is already moving, and the net upside is about half the bar. It stays "weak" rather than "kill" because nobody runs a neutral multi-originator conduit for graduate loans (I found none), and that is the one thing worth testing.

### 3. Offer-Backed (weak)

**Competitors and substitutes**
- Clasp is the incumbent on the employer side. Its employers have allocated over $130M, with offers such as Allegheny Health Network up to $110k over three years and OhioHealth and MSK up to $75k, and Clasp's CRNA page advertises up to $100k [S]. Clasp says payments start after hire, nothing is clawed back if a student leaves, and employers generally defer repayment until retention milestones [S, company claims]. [Clasp](https://www.clasp.com/post/press-release-top-u-s-health-systems-commit-more-than-usd100m-to-tackle-healthcare-staffing-and-student-debt-crises), [Becker's](https://www.beckersasc.com/quality/allegheny-health-network-goes-national-in-the-hunt-for-crnas/).
- Hospitals also fund tuition directly (Temple Health Nursing Scholars, Rochester Regional, Penn Highlands PT up to $65k with a three-year commitment) and there is the federal NURSE Corps. [Temple](https://templehealth.org/about/news/game-changing-partnership-temple-health-nursing-scholars-program-transforms-rn-and-crna-recruitment), [WXXI](https://www.wxxinews.org/local-news/2026-06-22/nurse-anesthetists-are-in-high-demand-rochester-regional-is-trying-a-new-tactic-to-recruit-them).
- I found no source of any lender underwriting a student loan off an employer job commitment.

**Internal contradiction.** The idea says lenders cannot underwrite an employer's promise, then makes the promise non-binding with no clawback so it passes California AB 692 and the New York law, and then says the lender prices off it. A pledge with no recourse is not credit enhancement. A binding one is the stay-or-pay structure the idea is trying to avoid. [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/labor-days/californias-ab-692-the-state-puts-stay-or-pay-agreements-under-greater-scrutiny).

**Revenue math**
- The 9,200 financed students a year is the idea's own unsourced number (7-8% of "~120,000 completers", also unsourced). Clasp's cumulative $130M allocation across all employers is far below the 8,000 hires a year needed for the $40M fee line.
- Section 127 helps only at the margin ($5,250 a year, now permanent for loan repayment [S]). [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/obbba-makes-tax-free-student-loan-reimbursements-permanent/).
- The addressable gap depends on a court stay. Under the Howell stay (late June 2026) the interim list treats nursing, PT, OT, PA and several other fields as professional ($50k a year), which shrinks the nursing and PT gap that this idea targets. I found no post-July status, so the volume is unverified. [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/court-temporarily-blocks-ed-dept-regulations-limiting-professional-degree/823837/), [PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/doe-scales-back-lower-student-loan-limits-for-several-degrees/).
- Median ~$4M. Upside ~$45M net (3,000-8,000 hires a year at $5k plus a $0.5-1B book at 2% net spread); about $125M gross if the idea's own volumes all arrive.

**Deciding reason.** A real insight (carrots, not sticks, are legal) but the lending half rests on an unsupported credit claim, and the employer half already belongs to a funded incumbent.

### 4. Operator Rail (kill)

- The anchor operator is already contracted. On its 28 Jan 2026 call, Adtalem's CEO said the company is working with Sallie Mae on definitive documentation for a loan-cap partnership, that Sallie Mae is assembling a syndicate of capital sources, and that supplemental lending is mainly for the medical and veterinary segment [READ]. [Transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/01/28/adtalem-atge-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/).
- The exposure is concentrated. Medical and Veterinary revenue was $102M in the quarter. Walden had 52,400 students and Chamberlain is a pre-licensure nursing school [READ]. I assume most of their tuition sits under the $20,500 cap, but I did not verify tuition levels [M]. The call did not quantify students affected [READ].
- Grand Canyon, Strategic Education and Touro: I found no private-lender deal tied to the caps (absence of evidence).
- The $100M path needs about 70,000 graduate starts a year at the anchors, and the idea says it "cannot show" this. Adtalem's 97,359 includes undergraduates.
- Legal: the 90/10 treatment of an operator guarantee is unresolved by the idea's own admission. [M]
- Median ~$3M, upside ~$20M.

### 5. Program Score Exchange (kill)

- Free substitute: ED's Office of the Chief Economist published a program-level dataset (PPD:26, 5 Jan 2026) from IPEDS, Scorecard, ACS, NSLDS, FAFSA and IRS data; ED estimates about 6% of programs fail; Scorecard earnings were updated in March 2026; AIR published a guide to the data in Aug 2026 [S]. [ED](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/2025-ahead-results-of-earnings-test-and-ge-changes-112932.pdf), [AIR](https://www.airweb.org/article/2026/08/12/air-publishes-guide-to-higher-education-federal-earnings-data).
- I found no Gray, Lightcast or National Student Clearinghouse risk score for lenders (absence of evidence only); NBER says program-level pricing is feasible in law and MBA but flat in social work, counseling and PT. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35683).
- Revenue math: the $92M assumes 5 rating agencies at $1.5M, 40 lenders at $0.4M, and 1,500 schools at $30k (near-full adoption). There are roughly a dozen active graduate lenders, and a data sale to rating agencies at that price is [M] unsupported.
- Premise: lenders will not pool loan-level tapes with competitors (the idea admits legal exposure). The pooled dataset is also exactly what the Seasoning Factory would hold, so this is a feature of idea 2.
- Median ~$4M, upside ~$30M.

### 6. Employer-Cohort Cap-Fit (kill)

- The price anchor already exists at scale. WGU charges a flat rate per six-month term (graduate portion of RN-to-MSN $5,050 in 2026-27), so the programme costs about $20-21k and has hospital partnerships (University of Vermont Medical Center reportedly pays 100% of WGU tuition; other partners give discounts; the ReNEW Fund with Social Finance covers prelicensure BSN) [S]. [WGU tuition](https://www.wgu.edu/financial-aid-tuition/2026-2027-tuition.html), [WGU healthcare](https://www.wgu.edu/partnerships/healthcare.html).
- The OPM model is wrecked (2U Chapter 11 in July 2024, Minnesota's restriction) but the survivors and alternatives are Guild, InStride, Risepoint, Noodle and Coursera degrees; I found no 2026 revenue figures for them. [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/how-2us-bankruptcy-shed-light-company-opm-market/723102/).
- The idea's own math needs 28,600 enrolled students (100+ programs) and says "this sits where the OPM industry has been losing". It also barely touches the pool: a cheap program removes the need for a private loan; it does not finance the gap.
- RN-to-MSN/NP is on the interim professional list, so the "cap makes $20,500 the price" argument weakens for its own wedge. [S]
- Median ~$5M, upside ~$40M.

### 7. Earnings Replica (kill)

- The premise "schools cannot see the data, public data is stale" is out of date: PPD:26 (above) and the March 2026 Scorecard update. The rule ("STATS") was finalized on 1 Jul 2026. One source gives first calculations 1 Jul 2027 and earliest loss of eligibility 1 Jul 2028, which conflicts with the idea's "early 2027 determinations"; ED's own chief-economist deck says the real data arrives in early 2027 [S, conflicting]. [collegehelpguide](https://www.collegehelpguide.com/blog/stats-earnings-rule-final-july-2026/).
- ED projects about 6% of programs failing (the idea says 4% of graduate programs); the failing ones are mostly low-budget counseling, social-work and certificate programs, which are poor payers.
- The method is statistically weak. The test is a cohort median at 6-digit CIP; a consent sample of 300 alumni across two schools cannot reproduce it for small programs.
- IVES is an approved-participant, lender-oriented channel with per-person consent, a $4 fee per transcript and IRC 6103(c) penalties for misuse. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/income-verification-express-service).
- The $105M assumes 2,700 institutions at $30-50k, which is saturation.
- Median ~$3M, upside ~$25M.

### 8. Verified-Pay Part-Timer (kill)

- Payroll-linked lending exists for personal loans: Kashable ($60M Series C, April 2026, Goldman), Highline ($13M Series A, sells to lenders), Pinwheel ($50M; infrastructure) [S]. I found no payroll-split in-school graduate loan. [Highline](https://www.cuinsight.com/press-release/highline-secures-13-million-in-series-a-funding-to-extend-credit-access-for-borrowers-and-reduce-risk-for-lenders/), [Pinwheel](https://www.pinwheelapi.com/solutions/paycheck-linked-lending).
- The supplier layer sells to every lender, including Sallie Mae, so there is no moat. Working professionals with in-field jobs are the best credits, so verified pay is not scarce.
- A revocable split can be turned off, and the FTC's Credit Practices Rule (16 CFR 444.2) bans irrevocable wage assignments in consumer credit [M, from memory; not searched].
- Volume is unsourced. Part-time students are about 43% of graduate enrollment (CGS, fall 2020 [S]) and federal limits are now prorated by enrollment intensity. "20% of 370,000" is the idea's own assumption. [UWA](https://www.uwa.edu/financial-aid/2026-federal-financial-aid-updates/).
- The $100M is gross interest on a $1B unsecured book. Net revenue after about 6.5% funding and 1.5-2% losses is about $15-25M.
- Median ~$4M net, upside ~$25M net (gross $25M and $100M).

## Picks

**None.** No idea cleared the bar (upside at least $100M a year on named, believable assumptions, median at least $20M, and a defensible edge) on a net-revenue basis.

### Closest miss and what would change the answer

Seasoning Factory is the only idea whose size is a capital question rather than a market question. On the lender convention of gross interest it shows about $30M median and $150M upside, so it clears the numeric bar only on that basis. I do not pick it because (a) the net figures are $12M and $55M, (b) the edge over Nelnet, KKR and Ascent's own ABS is unproven, and (c) the first-loss capital ($100M+) is a precondition, not an outcome. It would become a pick if the cheapest test below came back clean.

**Cheapest next test (about 8-10 weeks, low cash):** (1) written interest from three originators (for example a state authority, a CU Student Choice-type CUSO and a second-look lender) naming a combined annual flow of at least $100M; (2) one insurer or ABF desk's indicative term sheet for delayed-draw notes against a standard tape; (3) a counsel memo on program-level grid pricing under Reg B and the laws of Massachusetts, New York and California. If all three come back, a 10% first-loss slice is a credible raise. If any fails, the idea is dead.

## What this pool taught

- Loan volume is not revenue. For lender-model ideas, report net revenue; gross interest on a funded book inflates the size by about 2.5-3x.
- The school-side wedge ideas (ledger, desk, score, replica) all hit free public data or a free lender tool and all top out under $40M, because the buyers are budget-constrained aid, IR and compliance offices.
- Each "gap" the ideas claimed (no aggregator, no ABS, no data, no cap-fit price) was already partly filled by Nelnet, Ascent, ED or WGU. This repeats round 1's hidden-incumbent failure.
- Open items I could not verify: the post-July status of the professional-degree stay; the exact date of the first earnings-test calculations; Sallie Mae's second capital partner and the Adtalem agreement's closing terms; Clasp's lender plans.

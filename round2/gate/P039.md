# Gate report: pool P039, US utilities' outsourced T&D engineering and line-construction contracts

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with about 45 searches and a handful of page fetches (Locusview, FYLD, DWT/insidetowers on pole-attachment jurisdiction, Globes and Israel Desks on Locusview's size). Result: 3 kill, 5 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The pool is real ($31B+ annualized at Quanta alone), but every idea is a thin wedge into it, and the wedges run into the same three walls as round 1.

1. **Software category leaders in this pool are $20-50M businesses, not $100M ones.** Locusview (utility construction management, founded 2014) has about 120 employees, raised about $70-75M, serves 30+ utilities, 180+ contractors and 4,000+ crews daily, and sold to Itron for about $525M (Itron's 10-Q shows a $546.6M preliminary price). Itron guides its whole Resiliency segment (Locusview plus Urbint) to $65-70M of 2026 revenue, so Locusview is probably in the low-to-mid $30Ms (my inference, not disclosed). IKE's PoleForeman hit NZ$11M ARR in two years with about 200 customers, and IKE's whole group exit ARR is NZ$20.7M. SBS (distribution design) was bought by Enverus in April 2026. A new entrant needs 2-3x the best analog's price per seat, or heroic share, to reach $100M.
2. **Every services wedge books gross revenue at 10-18% net.** Ideas 5 and 7 reach "$100M" only by counting pass-through EPC, and their own text says net is $12-18M. That is a contractor, not a venture-scale business.
3. **Free or existing substitutes cover the "pooling" ideas.** The USDA RUS Technical List of Materials already does product acceptance for co-op borrowers; joint purchase programs have run in public power since 1994; co-op-owned contractors exist; RUS rules (7 CFR 1726) dictate contract forms and bidding.

Two premise errors were found: Idea 8's FCC shot-clock case does not govern its California wedge (California is a certified state, so the CPUC's GO 178 applies), and Idea 3's "no utility accepts another's test data" is false for roughly 830 co-ops.

## Idea-by-idea notes

### 1. Ready-to-Build: weak (median $12M, upside $70M)

- **Competitors found.** Locusview/Itron (digital construction management, close-out and invoicing; no scheduling or readiness module on its product list: https://locusview.com). FYLD (UK, $41M Series B led by Energy Impact Partners, customers include Quanta, Kiewit; expects 40%+ of revenue from the US by end of 2026; field execution and risk, not gating). ARCOS (utility crew scheduling, https://www.arcos-inc.com/?p=143), Assignar (https://tour.assignar.com/answers/construction-crew-equipment-scheduling-software), CGI OpenGrid Work (https://cgi.com/au/en-au/brochure/utilities/cgi-opengrid-work), Oracle Primavera and Hexagon work packaging with constraint gates, Utilen (seed closed Dec 2025, size undisclosed, https://www.utilitydive.com/press-release/20260106-as-us-doubles-down-on-power-infrastructure-minneapolis-tech-startup-rais-1/). The claim "no product built around the six gates for T&D crews" survives only because the gate concept lives in industrial work-packaging tools that have not been adapted to outage windows and 811 tickets.
- **Revenue math.** BLS line-installer employment is about 131k (aggregator of OEWS May 2025; https://www.bls.gov/OES/CURRENT/oes499051.htm). The industry table shows 57.1k in electric power G/T/D and 36.3k in utility system construction; at 4 per crew that is about 23k addressable crews, not 33k, so 3,300 crews is about 14% of the addressable base, not 10%. At the idea's $30k per crew-year it takes 14% share. At Locusview's implied $8-10k per crew-year, $100M needs about 11,000 crews, nearly half of all addressable crews.
- **Premise gaps.** Standby is billed on time-and-equipment work, so the pain only reaches the contractor on unit-price work; the idea admits the share is unverified. Bain's 40% wrench time is one anonymous utility's in-house crews (https://www.bain.com/client-results/boosting-utilitys-workforce-productivity/).
- **Upside needs:** (a) price at 2-3x the best analog, plausible only if the pilots prove hard-dollar standby savings (about 25% likely); (b) 15-20% of addressable crews, which Locusview showed is reachable but took 11 years (about 40%); (c) Itron, Maximo, SAP and FYLD do not bundle a readiness module (about 25%). Joint odds are low. Without (a), the ceiling is about $40-50M.

### 2. Zero-Redline: weak (median $10M, upside $60M)

- **Competitors found.** SBS/Enverus, Itron Distribution Staker, Powel MiniMax StakeOut (https://www.tdworld.com/smart-utility/article/20962707/northfork-electric-cooperative-chooses-powel-mini-max-software); outsourced design: SEH, McCall-Thomas, JSG, Actalent (https://actalentservices.com/services-and-solutions/engineering-services/distribution-engineering), Asplundh Engineering; offshore and records work: Cyient (https://www.esri.com/partners/cyient-a2T70000000TNVkEAO); funded AI-native entrants Unlimited Industries ($12M seed), Tiros (seed, https://f4.fund/startups/tiros), Piq ($5M seed, studies not design), Continuum Industries ($10M Series A, routing).
- **Revenue math.** 1.5-2.2% of outsourced design dollars is not heroic share. But the $9-14B design pool rests on water/wastewater benchmarks and an unsourced 2M work orders a year. At $900-1,000 a package, the price is about 8-10 hours at $100 an hour, the same as hourly firms charge; the only difference is outcome pricing. It is a PE-stamped services business, so scale means thousands of designers.
- **Why it stays weak, not dead.** Outcome pricing is a real wedge nobody sells, and nearshore pods give the first 60%-margin years. Upside needs a tool-enabled acceptance rate above 95% on 100k+ packages a year while hourly firms and AI-native entrants copy outcome pricing.

### 3. Spec Passport: kill (median $4M, upside $30M)

- **Free substitute.** USDA RUS Technical Standards Committee "A" evaluates and lists materials for all RUS borrowers; the List of Materials was last revised July 2, 2026 and 7 CFR 1728 makes it binding for RUS-financed purchases. Sponsors (manufacturers) apply with test reports; no fee schedule was found. This is exactly "test once, many utilities accept" for about 830 co-ops, the idea's own wedge.
- **Other prior art.** EPRI's Vetted Product List (EV chargers, https://msites.epri.com/vpl), IEEE ICAP conformity programs, CEATI utility consortia, accredited labs (KEMA/CESI: https://www.cesi.it/testing-certification-inspection/testing-labs/transformer-test-laboratories/). Cigre 2026 is publishing methodology for qualifying new supplier factories, so utilities are solving it individually.
- **Revenue.** The $600M qualification pool has no source; the real buyers are the dozens of new factories and importers, not 1,500 firms. The sourcing-desk leg competes with Wesco and Graybar. Fees that buy listing next to a "never ranking" promise is a neutrality problem. IOUs will not cede engineer-of-record liability.

### 4. Capacity Upgrade Desk: weak (median $8M, upside $60M)

- **Competitors found.** Quanta Technology and EPCs (Quanta, MYR, Pike), engineering firms with NERC facility-rating practices, conductor makers (CTC Global, TS Conductor, Southwire), dynamic-line-rating vendors (Heimdall $25M Series B: https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/heimdall-power-raises-25m-for-grid-optimization ; LineVision, $33M raised), Carbon Direct's PJM DLR analysis (https://carbon-direct.com/insights/dynamic-line-rating-the-fastest-gigawatt-is-the-one-you-already-have), DOE SPARK funding 31 projects (https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328398/20261002/spark-picks-31-grid-projects-reconductoring-doubles-capacity-without-new-land.htm). I found no firm selling neutral limiting-element audits to developers, which is the one real gap.
- **Revenue math, corrected.** The idea uses about $1M per mile; vendor cost tables show $0.30-0.55M at 132 kV and $0.60-1.15M at 230 kV. At 12% fees on $0.55M a mile, $100M needs about 1,500 miles a year, which is about 21% of the 7,000 miles a year (1% of 700,000) the idea itself derives. If it books turnkey EPC instead, $100M is only about 100-180 miles, but then it is a small contractor and not a neutral adviser.
- **Premise.** Shock facts verified. Developer-funded upgrades for capacity rights exist for generators (network upgrade credits) but not as a standard for load, so each state PUC and utility rider is bespoke. The GridLab/Berkeley study says most US lines are short enough to be conductor-limited, which contradicts the insider claim that a terminal or crossing is usually the limit.

### 5. Accredited Delivery Partner: kill (median $8M gross, upside $50M gross)

- **Legal premise.** Partly right: California Rules 15/16 offer applicant design and installation, and Ohio's rule allows utility-approved third-party installation. But it is permissive, utility by utility, and large campuses are pushed to exceptional-case agreements, where STACK built the 115 kV Ringwood switching station itself under an EPC and transferred it to PG&E.
- **Prior art.** Electrical contractors (Cupertino Electric, Rosendin, MYR's Sturgeon) and campus civil engineers already do customer-funded service work; in the UK, independent connection providers deliver about 75% of new connections by capacity (UKPN's figure, https://www.ukpowernetworks.co.uk/our-connections-business/competitive-market) and the result is a fragmented contractor market. Ofgem's self-build consultation closes 2026-11-13 (https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/ofgem-consults-on-enabling-greater-self-build-of-transmission-connections).
- **Revenue.** Gross $100M at 12-18% is $12-18M net, so the idea's own text concedes it is not venture-scale; the pool is limited to states that allow third parties.

### 6. Pooled Crews: kill (median $6M, upside $35M)

- **Prior art.** FMPA's Joint Purchase Project (poles, wire, transformers since 1994), Oklahoma's Power Partners, co-op-owned crews (3C Construction), Sourcewell generalist IDIQs (https://www.sourcewell-mn.gov/cooperative-purchasing/ca-r6-e-101723-ael), and a draft joint storm-restoration procurement among co-ops.
- **Rules.** RUS Part 1726 requires RUS standard contract forms (790/830 for line construction) and defined bidding methods, so an aggregated "pre-competed" vehicle has to be wrapped around each co-op's own contract.
- **Revenue.** An NRECA/CFC-commissioned study puts co-op capital at $75B over 2018-2022 (about $15B a year including generation; https://carolinacountry.com/your-energy/electric-co-ops-are-powerful-engines-of-economic-development), so the $20B pool with munis is plausible, but 2.5% of 33% of it is heroic by the idea's own flag. Contractors will not reserve crews for unpaid-for demand, and co-ops cannot pay standby without board and lender scrutiny. Co-ops lose lineworkers to contractor pay (https://www.electric.coop/georgia-co-ops-high-school-outreach-reaps-results-in-filling-linework-gaps), so the crew scarcity is real, but the fix is wages, not a vehicle.

### 7. Pre-Accepted Substations: weak (median $15M gross, upside $100M gross)

- **Competitors found.** GE Vernova (turnkey data-center substations, equipment package or EPC), Hitachi Energy and Siemens Energy (OEM turnkey), Burns & McDonnell, Sargent & Lundy, Quanta/MYR/Primoris (with PayneCrest), Michels (prefab, https://www.michels.us/news/data-center-projects-prosper-with-prefabrication-services), Unlimited Industries (a16z, $12M seed, vertically integrated AI design plus construction; no customers disclosed), powered-land developers such as Cloverleaf (Nvidia-backed) that plan their own substations.
- **Revenue math.** The idea's $30-70k per MW looks low: Dominion's PJM filings show about $20M for a utility-built 230 kV four-breaker ring substation serving more than 100 MW (https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/teac/2026/20260203/20260203-item-11---dom-supplemental-projects.pdf), which is about $200k per MW before customer transformers. That raises the pool. But the volume is lower: Goldman's realized US additions were 6.4 GW and 8.5 GW, with 13.6 GW expected in 2026 and 36.3 GW scheduled for 2027 at about 50-60% on-time delivery. Non-top-six share of 4-10 GW a year at $150k per MW is a $0.6-1.5B pool, so $100M gross is 7-17%.
- **Revenue quality.** At 8-10% fees plus 10-15% margin, $100M gross is $10-15M net. Fee-only needs about 5 GW a year. The "cornered resource" is thin because TO requirements are available on request under FAC-001-3, and utilities are unlikely to give pre-acceptance of a design before an application. The page-140 insight is real but fixable by an experienced EPC with a checklist.
- **Upside needs:** 4-6 sites a year at about $25M each from neoclouds with no track record to buy from (about 15% likely), reserved transformer slots (needs $10M+ of deposits), and Unlimited Industries not getting there first. Median stays under $20M, so no pick.

### 8. Make-Ready Rail: weak (median $10M, upside $70M)

- **Premise errors.** The FCC July 2025 order (bulk applications, shot clocks, self-help via utility-approved contractors) is real, but 23 states plus DC have certified they regulate poles themselves, California among them (the trade list names 24 including DC), so the FCC clocks do not apply to the California wedge or to co-op and municipal poles. The CPUC's GO 178 (D.26-05-007, adopted 2026-05-14) governs instead. The FCC opened comment in June 2026 on revoking certifications, which could change this, but not on a timeline a wedge can rely on.
- **Competitors found.** Alden ONE (joint-use hub; routes one application to every owner; FCC shot-clock dashboard; Minnesota Power), IKE PoleForeman (NZ$11M ARR, about 200 customers), Katapult (https://g2.com/sellers/katapult-engineering), Osmose, CHR with IKE (https://chrsolutions.com/?p=5767), Davey (https://davey.com/utility-services/communications-construction-services/make-ready-engineering), Wade Trim (nine states), LaBella, QUES, plus Dycom, MasTec and Quanta field crews. The claim "no funded startup doing bulk make-ready" is true but the incumbents are engineering firms and software vendors already selling to both sides.
- **Revenue math.** ACLP: 3.9M BEAD poles, baseline $1.25B pole costs (about $320 per pole), range $534M-$4.63B (https://broadbandbreakfast.com/utility-pole-costs-a-major-expense-for-bead-broadband-projects/). At $80 a pole, the entire BEAD book is about $312M of fees over a multi-year build, or roughly $80M a year at 100% share. BEAD fiber is also shrinking: California's final plan is 52.9% fiber, 27% LEO satellite and 20.1% fixed wireless (https://www.fierce-network.com/broadband/ntia-approves-californias-final-bead-proposal). So $100M of net fees needs both non-BEAD volume the idea says it cannot source and 10-20% share. The gross field-work path ($100M at 170-300k poles) is 15% net and competes with Dycom.
- **Data point.** Pole demand is growing: Xcel Minnesota's yearly applications rose from about 6,500 (2019) to about 18,000 (2024) (https://mn.gov/deed/assets/feb-2025-pole-owner_tcm1045-669353.pdf). The pain is real; the position is not.

## Picks

None. The three nearest misses and why they miss:

- **Idea 1** has the best margin structure (SaaS plus a desk) and a real field pain, but its $100M needs 3x the price of the only analog at scale, or about half of all addressable crews, and Itron/FYLD can bundle. Upside is $70M on named assumptions, not $100M.
- **Idea 7** is the only one with a pool that can carry $100M, but that is gross pass-through ($10-15M net), the library moat is public-on-request, and OEM turnkey plus a funded a16z entrant are already there. Median is under $20M.
- **Idea 2** is the least heroic by share (1.5-2.2% of outsourced design), but it is a PE-stamped services business against software owners (Enverus, Itron) and AI-native entrants, and its ceiling is $60M.

Cheapest tests if the owner wants to keep one alive anyway: for Idea 1, the $15k Standby Autopsy on 10 crews (does standby on unit-price work exceed 8% of paid hours); for Idea 7, ask three neocloud developers whether they would sign an LOI for a 100-150 MW substation at a fixed fee and date, before building any library.

## What this pool taught us

T&D services is a big pool with capacity-limited incumbents, but every software wedge has already been bought at about $0.5B for a $30-50M-revenue business, and every services wedge books gross revenue at 10-18% net. Free substitutes (RUS acceptance, public power joint purchasing) kill the pooling ideas. Next time, require a net-revenue path and name the existing leader's revenue, not just its existence.

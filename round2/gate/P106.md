# Gate report: pool P106, State Medicaid and SNAP eligibility and administration spend

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas, about 35 searches plus primary fetches (Equifax Q4 2025 8-K text, Warren/Wyden/Sanders press release, SAS and Maximus product releases, Nevada Independent on the Carahsoft contract, Justice in Aging on the retro cut, Argyle's Washington announcement). Result: 5 kill, 3 weak, 0 strong. **No picks.**

## Headline findings

1. **The shock is real and the dollars states actually spend are small.** Observed SNAP-accuracy contracts: Nevada $2.1M (Carahsoft, against about $50M a year of exposure), Virginia $1.7M for six months (KPMG), Florida a $4M appropriation (against roughly $1B of exposure), NYC $409k over 36 months, Maine $242k (PCG rapid assessment). Exposure is $50M-$2.5B a state (California $2.0-2.5B per the LAO and State Auditor). Value to the state is enormous; willingness to pay is 0.4-4% of exposure. Idea 1's $25-35 per case-year would be about 10x Florida's appropriation.
2. **Every idea that is a "feature of the eligibility system" meets a free or discounted incumbent.** Ten integrators (Accenture, Acentra, Conduent, GDIT, Deloitte, Gainwell, Maximus, Curam, Optum, RedMane) pledged about $600M of discounted or free services to CMS. Gainwell gives its 2,000-code frailty logic to every state at no charge. Deloitte's verification hub, Maximus Accuracy Assistant and SAS Payment Integrity are already in states.
3. **Funded startups are already on the plan side and the consent-data side.** Fortuna Health (a16z, $18M Series A, payer clients covering 25M+ Medicaid lives), Reema Health ($7.6M), Argyle (Washington State contract 10925, GSA schedule), SteadyIQ (Missouri), Truv.
4. **Several pool-level premises in the ideas did not hold.** The "OIG found consultants who eliminated errors" point is an old finding (FY2011-12 audit), and the contingent-fee consultant behind the Wisconsin DOJ case was Julie Osnes Consulting, not PCG. Equifax's "$800M government revenue" is not in any filing I could open (the Q4 2025 8-K gives growth rates only). The Senate delay bill is stuck (a 10-11 committee vote failed in September per one report; a conflicting report says it passed; New York's delegation wants two years).
5. **The best idea is a near miss.** Second Look (SNAP) is the only one with a hard-dollar, dated cliff, a named signer and observed contract sizes that are non-trivial. It fails the bar on median (about $12M vs $20M) and on upside (about $85M vs $100M), and the gap is the price-to-exposure behaviour above.

## Per-idea findings

### 1. Second Look: weak (best of the pool)

- **Competitors.** Maximus Accuracy Assistant (launched 2026-01-27: case-prep review before authorization, error-risk scoring, dashboards, training insights from error-prone cases). SAS Payment Integrity for Food Assistance (Nevada, June 2026: monthly risk scores for determinations, "every active SNAP case" in the product pitch; $130k a month of overpayments flagged in two months; contract value not stated). Deloitte (25+ states, "up to 35%" self-reported reductions, NextGen360 hub), PCG (accuracy consulting), KPMG (Virginia), Carahsoft (Nevada), Gainwell (sponsored content), Code for America with Anthropic (free SNAP Policy Navigator), US Digital Response. Sources: https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/Industries/government-public/about/snap-payment-accuracy.html ; https://publicconsultinggroup.com/human-services/public-assistance/snap-consulting-services ; https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-anthropic-ai-snap-caseworkers/ ; https://www.usdigitalresponse.org/resources/is-this-working-data-driven-strategies-to-reduce-snap-payment-error-rates
- **What is genuinely new.** Independent, fixed-fee shadow QC with confidence intervals and an evidence ledger. SAS's own release concedes states "rely on legacy quality control methods that estimate error rates from small samples", so the measurement gap is real. But SAS and Maximus already sell the pre-issuance recompute.
- **Revenue math.** Price vs observed spend is the break. $660M pool at $30 per household-year assumes about 22M households paying the full list price. Observed behaviour is 0.4% (Florida) to 4.2% (Nevada, $2.1M on about $50M) of one year's exposure. At 1-4% of roughly $9-10B of exposure the whole vendor pool is $90-400M, shared with SAS, Maximus, Deloitte, PCG, KPMG. $100M is 25-100% of it. The "CA alone supports $76-133M" line is not credible: no state has signed a single $70M+ error-reduction contract and the incumbents would take it.
- **Premise checks.** Tier math confirmed (0/5/10/15% at 6/8/10; FY2028 may use FY2025 or FY2026; FY2025 national 10.62%, $10.1B). Florida, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey and New York lost delay eligibility (Ballotpedia). FY2025 and FY2026 rates are already locked for FY2028, so the controllable window is FY2027 (sample year began 2026-10-01), which is real. Senate delay risk is live. The OIG and Wisconsin items are stale or misattributed (above). Contingent pricing is correctly avoided: DOJ settled with Wisconsin, Virginia, Texas, Alaska, Louisiana and the consultant over doctored QC data.
- **Upside assumptions.** (a) Cost share survives roughly intact to FY2029-30 (about 60%). (b) States spend 2-3% of exposure, 5-10x today (plausible only after the first bills arrive). (c) A new entrant takes 15-20 states against SAS, Maximus and Deloitte by being the trusted independent measurer (low, about 15%). (d) Medicaid eligibility assurance becomes a second line: H.R.1's 3% eligibility threshold bites from FY2030 and the rolling PERM eligibility rate is already 4.42% (CMS 2025 table), but PERM is measured on a three-year rotation by CMS, not by the state (plausible). Joint probability roughly 5-8%.

### 2. Medicaid-Pending Bridge: kill

- **Competitors.** PointClickCare launched outsourced revenue-cycle services with Medicaid eligibility specialists for skilled nursing in 2021. Optum sells outsourced Medicaid eligibility and enrollment (case study: 15,000 patients, 96% approval). Application-prep firms charge families about $2,500-5,000 and are referred by nursing homes; elder-law firms; operators' business offices. Sources above plus https://www.eldercarelawyer.com/blog/applying-for-medicaid-cost-and-services/ . A vendor page titled for the 2027 retro change (asprcmsolutions.com) returned 403, so unread.
- **Premise.** The cut is one month, not a cliff: applicants other than the expansion group go from three months of retroactive coverage to two, and coverage keys off the application month (Justice in Aging). A facility that files in the admission month loses nothing. The exposure is late filers. That is a thin why-now.
- **Revenue math.** The 600-800k applications a year is unverified (no national count found). My estimate from about 1.2M residents, roughly 60% on Medicaid and multi-year stays is 300-400k new Medicaid starts a year. $100M of fees at $1,000 would need 100k filings, 25-30% of that. The financing leg needs $0.6-0.8B of advances against applications; factoring needs an approved, billed receivable, Medicaid anti-assignment rules complicate it, and healthcare receivables lenders carry the NCFE scar. Revenue there is yield, not margin.
- **Upside.** Everything has to go right (fee-for-filing wins from PointClickCare and Optum, and a balance sheet appears): about $45M.

### 3. Continuity Manager: weak

- **Competitors.** Fortuna Health ($18M Series A led by a16z, YC; says its payer clients cover 25M+ Medicaid lives; claims a 15% churn reduction, company-reported), Reema Health ($7.6M), plans' in-house engagement teams, Conduent's work-requirements suite. https://endpts.com/exclusive-reema-health-raises-7-6m-to-reach-people-on-medicaid ; https://www.conduent.com/?p=453005
- **Legal premise.** Holds in outline: the statute bars states from delegating compliance determinations to plans that have a financial relationship with providers, and a Crowell read says it extends to financially affiliated contractors. Outreach and evidence support are allowed. A vendor paid by the plan is by definition not independent of the plan, so "firewalled" means "does not determine", which is what Fortuna already does.
- **Revenue math (price vs value).** At $30 per member-year the pool is $480M. But a retained expansion member contributes roughly $250-350 a year after medical costs (capitation about $7k, MLR 85-90%, variable admin). Retaining 3.75% (25% loss rate x 15% relative reduction, Fortuna's own claim) is worth about $11 per enrolled member-year; 8% (41% Manatt loss x 20%) is about $25. So the fee is at or above the value at the plan's break-even. Realistic plan budget is $8-15 per member-year: a $130-240M pool shared with Fortuna and in-house teams.
- **Upside.** Two or three of the Big Five sign outcome-priced PMPM, state-level free-rider fix funded pro rata: about $60M. Median is a handful of plan pilots: about $8M.

### 4. Hours Marketplace: kill

- **Competitors.** Better Impact CE Hours (coordinator-validated hours from 65,000+ organizations, pitched to state Medicaid), Helper Helper, Galaxy Digital, VolunteerMatch, Equifax and Maximus trackers. No public state contract found for Better Impact.
- **Demand is small.** KFF finds a majority of expansion adults already work 80+ hours; many of the rest are in school or disabled and exempt. Georgia Pathways, the only live precedent, enrolled about 6,500 in 18 months at $86M cost. The 12-15% needing non-job hours (2.4-3.0M people) is an unsourced assumption, probably 3-5x too high.
- **Capacity and legal.** The idea's own "binding constraint" is host supervision, and it says that must be in kind. Compelled-service FLSA and workers' comp exposure is unresolved for Medicaid; workfare participants were held to be FLSA employees in Stone v. McGowan (TANF, 2004) and Oregon and Ohio treat SNAP workfare as covered. https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/97-1038.html . A marketplace that is also the employer of record is a services business with a liability tail.
- **Upside.** $30M even with a plan or state funding 100k slots.

### 5. Open Verification Exchange: kill (already done)

- **Competitors.** Argyle (Washington contract 10925, GSA MAS schedule, claims up to 90% below The Work Number, vendor claim), SteadyIQ (Missouri; 18-minute average, optional use), Truv, Equifax's own TotalVerify and Complete Income, Deloitte's verification hub, the federal Hub (student and veteran checks; Emmy folded into it), and NDNH, which SNAP agencies already must query at certification. States and integrators already orchestrate sources; a router is a thin layer they can build or buy.
- **Premise checks.** Antitrust case is real (E.D. Pa., motion to dismiss denied February 2025; direct-purchaser mortgage lenders, not states). Senate letter confirms 126% / 120% / 95% / 69% per-query hikes and "about half of states", but names no exclusive partners and gives no revenue number. Equifax government is growing (Q4 2025 "low double digit", Q1 mid-teens) but fell about 4% in Q2 2026, with about $300M of annual contract value signed, mostly state. The $800M and 19% share arithmetic is therefore unverified.
- **Revenue math.** A savings-share router takes perhaps 30% of the savings on a book that is itself partly federal (SSA) and partly mortgage-adjacent. Even if half of a $500-800M book moves at a 40-50% discount, the router's share is $30-60M. The payroll exchange needs vendors Equifax already locked up.
- **Upside.** About $45M.

### 6. Renewal Autopilot: kill

- **Competitors.** Maximus, Conduent, GDIT, Deloitte, Gainwell, Accenture, Optum, Curam, RedMane, all inside the CMS $600M pledge, and all sitting on the eligibility systems a new vendor must integrate with. States use ex parte automation already (federal evaluation: ex parte renewals up 21.6 points in four states).
- **Premise.** Maximus does say growth returns with Medicaid and SNAP volume, which supports counter-positioning in principle. But I found no state outcome-based or per-renewal contract, no published pricing, and the 2027 start is January 1 (not July as the idea says). States will not contract around the incumbent system in a year.
- **Revenue math.** 8.3M cases at $12 is 15% of 55M national volume even at 100% auto-completion; the idea's own figure of 30% is heroic and the idea calls it the weakest path. Upside $45M.

### 7. Exemption Evidence Utility: kill

- **Competitors / free substitutes.** Gainwell ships frailty logic (2,000+ codes across medical, behavioral and pharmacy claims) free to every state "regardless of technology partner". Milliman Frailty Framework (Feb 2026), CHCS analytic guidance, Wipro MCAID360, Conduent suite, state analytics teams. https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/medically-frail-determinations-medicaid-insights ; https://www.chcs.org/media/Data-and-Analytic-Approaches-for-Medical-Frailty-Exemptions.pdf
- **Premise.** IFR narrows frailty to condition plus significant functional impairment, requires a state-maintained code list, allows self-attestation once from 2028 (KFF). So demand for audited code lists is real, but the product is free from the vendor the state already pays.
- **Revenue math.** The idea's own total is $77-111M at near-total adoption. The clinician-packet half ($45 each) is a thin-margin services network. Upside $40M.

### 8. Rules Twin: kill

- **Competitors.** PolicyEngine (open source microsimulation of US benefits), Nava, Alluma rules engine, Beeck Center Rules as Code community, Urban Institute TRIM3, Milliman and Manatt forecasting, integrators' proprietary engines.
- **Revenue math.** $107M needs about 70% of states plus 25 plans; the idea says to kill if four states will not share caseloads. Policy-simulation buyers are legislatures and think tanks, with project budgets. Upside $20M.

## Picks

None. Second Look is the only idea within reach of the bar. It fails on median (about $12M, not $20M) and its upside rests on states paying 5-10x what any state has paid so far.

**What would reopen Idea 1:** (1) A public-records pull from five of the lost-delay states and two large counties showing a funded accuracy line above $10M a year. (2) FNS or a state QC unit confirming it will accept a third-party shadow-QC sample as evidence. (3) The Senate delay resolving at one year or less. Cheapest test: public-records requests for the Florida DCF AI solicitation (due 2026-09-01) and its award, Nevada's SAS and Carahsoft scopes, and New York's and Massachusetts's SNAP accuracy budget lines; build the three-state wage error-element model from FNS public tables.

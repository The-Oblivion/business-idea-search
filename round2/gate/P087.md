# Gate report: pool P087, US homeowners insurance in wildfire-, hail- and hurricane-exposed markets

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with about 3-5 searches each (about 35 searches in total). Primary text was read only for search-returned excerpts of Reg X 1024.37, Cal. Ins. Code 678 and 791.13, and AB 493 analyses; no full PDFs were opened. Result: 3 kill, 5 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The pool is huge ($173B of premium), and the shocks are real: the DFPI order is verified, AB 493 is enacted, the Verisk/AccuLynx ruling is verified, and the FAIR Plan rate and commission changes are verified. But every idea is a service or exchange layered on top of a flow that is already owned, or that pays the wrong party.

- **Every adjacent layer has an incumbent or a funded entrant.**
  - Tracking: Assurant tracks 34M+ loans, and fee-based trackers (Lee & Mason, Coforge BPS, Proctor) already exist.
  - Loss drafts: iink, DIMONT, OSC, SWBC and Assurant all sell loss-draft processing.
  - E&S submission: Bridge Specialty, Pathpoint, Appulate, QuoteSweep and Semsee are all in market.
  - Adjusting: Pilot (about $1B), Sedgwick, Crawford and Reserv ($100M ARR) are all in market.
  - Wildfire mitigation: RockRose, Wildfire Defense Systems and thousands of local firms.
- **The best-funded analog calibrates the ceiling far below the pitch.** iink, the one funded loss-draft fintech, reports about $3B of cumulative claim checks processed against a pool of about $30B a year. The leading online home and auto distributors (The Zebra, Insurify, Policygenius) sit at roughly $60-100M of revenue after about 10 years and hundreds of millions raised. The ideas' year-5 to year-7 $100M+ paths assume 6-22% shares of pools in which the best funded entrant holds low single digits.
- **Wrong payer or price above value** recurs (round 1 lessons 3 and 5): the servicer does not pay for LPI and gains little from a cure (idea 1); a $1,000 a year subscription against a discount worth a few hundred dollars (idea 8); a "regulatory warranty" whose maximum penalty exposure is a few million dollars against $6,000 a file fees (idea 4).
- **The pool's strongest premise is the one nobody has built a payer for:** carrier-originated, consent-based placement of non-renewed policyholders (idea 5). It has real upside mechanics (recurring commission, a cornered distribution channel) but no carrier has been shown willing to sign, and free public substitutes sit in every non-renewal notice.

## Revenue-math corrections (where the $100M paths break)

- **Idea 1:** $6 times 18M loans is $108M only if servicers pay a per-loan fee they have never paid for a service that matters on about 2-3% of loans. Per-event pricing at an assumed $75 on roughly 1.4M mortgage-linked non-renewals a year (2.03M unverified, 70% mortgaged assumed) is about $107M at 100% share. The "Rocket/Mr. Cooper 10M loans" buying event was not verified; Rocket's own deal commentary treats Mr. Cooper's cost to service as about a third below average, which describes a cost-obsessed buyer that already gets tracking from LPI.
- **Idea 2:** $4.14B of E&S homeowners premium at a 6% wholesale take is about $250M today, so $100M is 40% of the whole layer today and about 26% of the idea's own 2029 projection. The 15% annual growth assumption conflicts with the idea's own why-now (urban homes returning to admitted carriers). The 144k a year FAIR exit rate extrapolates 24k in two months and is unverified; FAIR growth was reported under 2% in Q2 2026.
- **Idea 3:** $165.2B times 61.59% times 60% times 50% is $30.5B, which is arithmetically right but the 50% monitored share is assumed. The best-funded incumbent has processed about $3B cumulatively. Reaching $100M needs a blended take of about 2.2-2.6% on 15% share; servicer-side fees on payment rails are normally a fraction of 1%. The jump to $100M+ comes from the completion guaranty and bridge-loan layers, which are a lending business with a balance sheet, not the desk.
- **Idea 4:** $100M is about 6.6% of a $1.5B major-loss segment (using IBISWorld's $10.8B times 35% property times 40% major loss, both fractions assumed), or about 16,700 closed files a year at $6,000. The LA fires destroyed about 16,000 structures in total across all carriers.
- **Idea 5:** the arithmetic checks (183k placements a year, 678k in-force after five years, about $135M). $100M needs about 11% of the national re-placeable flow held for five straight years; the idea needs several top carriers to send the link, and none has been shown willing.
- **Idea 6:** the idea concedes it does not reach $100M alone.
- **Idea 7:** $70M in LA assumes 7,150 contracts signed from 2027 when roughly half of the 36 months of ALE have already run and many homes are rebuilt, sold or abandoned. The national 60k major rebuilds a year is an unsourced assumption.
- **Idea 8:** $100M needs 100,000 subscribers at $1,000 (5% of about 2M Zone 0 homes), against free inspections, grants, local landscapers and an insurer discount that is worth less than the fee.

## Picks

None. No idea clears both bars (median at least $20M, upside at least $100M on assumptions I believe). The closest are idea 4 (median $16M, upside $85M, but a plain TPA with a warranty that is worth little) and idea 5 (upside $75M; median $10M; only structural route to a recurring, owned distribution asset). Shrinking either to a California-only or single-carrier boutique is the failure mode round 1 documented.

What would have to be true to reopen the pool:

- **Idea 5:** a signed letter of intent from one top-10 homeowners carrier to put an opt-in placement link in its non-renewal notices, with a written Department of Insurance view that carrier-paid placement help is not steering or an unlicensed referral. With that in hand, a free 5,000-policy pilot (the idea's own wedge) would give a measured opt-in and bind rate; if it is 25% or better, the upside moves toward $100M.
- **Idea 3:** published servicer pricing for loss-draft work above about $400 a file, or a second state copying AB 493 with a servicer-custody mandate, which would remove the float objection and create a compliance purchase outside California.
- **Idea 4:** a state or federal rule that makes a delegated claims operator share liability for statutory clocks, which would give the warranty a real price.

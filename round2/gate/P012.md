# Gate report: pool P012, US homeowners-insurer-paid property repair and restoration

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with roughly 4-7 searches each plus primary-source reads (Fannie Mae Servicing Guide B-5-01, AB 1795 bill page, Texas Insurance Code 4102.104, iink and SquareDash pages). Result: 5 kill, 3 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The pool is real ($82B of incurred homeowners losses, per III) but this batch was written as if the contractor-payment layer were empty. It is not, and three of the load-bearing premises are contradicted by primary text.

- **The "no funded competitor" claim for contractor cash acceleration is false.** SquareDash (founded 2022, "over $20M" raised, a $15M credit facility expandable to $25M, targets roofers and restorers at $2-15M revenue, sells claim funding plus a billing/collections subscription) and iink (Advanced Funding, $6M debt facility with a $100M accordion, 1,500+ servicers, $2.5B+ loss drafts processed) already do ideas 1 and 2 together and are wired to each other (24-48 hour funding via digital endorsement, Oct 2024). Checkbook bought sureti in Jan 2025. The idea cited only sureti's $3.25M.
- **The "60-90 day" payment premise is the tail, not the norm.** The 2026 Restoration Benchmarking Survey says only about 4% are paid in 1-2 weeks, most wait 3-8 weeks, and just over 18% wait more than 8 weeks. A 45-day average turn, not 70, cuts a lender's revenue per dollar outstanding by about a third at the same fee, or doubles the implied APR.
- **The Fannie Mae text kills the float legs.** B-5-01 requires undisbursed proceeds in an interest-bearing T&I custodial account for the borrower's benefit, with the interest paid to the borrower. California AB 493 adds a 2% simple-interest floor. So the "$123M of net float" in idea 5 is not free money a neutral escrow can keep, and the guide also says a final inspection is not required for loans current or under 31 days delinquent, which shrinks idea 8's inspection wallet.
- **The California smoke laws are later and narrower than pitched.** AB 1795 (Ch. 240, signed 15 Sep 2026) depends on health-based standards that DTSC has not written (emergency regs due 1 Jul 2027 per one source, end of 2028 for lead and asbestos per another). One report says neither law applies retroactively, so the 13,000 January 2025 smoke files sit in litigation (suit deadline 7 Jan 2027), not in a new program.
- **Public-adjuster fee law is a patchwork, not "TX 10%, FL 10/20%" plus 48 states.** Louisiana R.S. 22:1703(A) voids percentage-contingency PA contracts; Virginia bars fees on pre-contract payments; California AB 597 would too; Texas caps at 10%.

## Revenue-math corrections (where the $100M paths break)

- Idea 1: $100M at 3% needs $3.3B of advances and $630M outstanding at 70 days. At a 45-day turn and a likely 2.5% price it needs about $4B of advances and about $490M outstanding, and the lender keeps only 20-35% of revenue pre-tax. It is a balance-sheet business competing with two funded incumbents that already own the endorsement rail.
- Idea 2: 3.5-4% of collections is about 2x the in-house cost for a mid-size firm. At 2.5% the required collected base is $4B, 7% of the contractor-bound pool, and the large platforms are Rebuild's named customers.
- Idea 3: $5B of member purchases is over 50% of insured-job roofing materials, not 25%, and the 1.7% fee duplicates existing 1-3% manufacturer rebates.
- Idea 4: $3.3B of managed volume exceeds Crawford's $2.5B. The ALE pool ($5-7B) is unsourced by the idea itself.
- Idea 5: float of $123M is not retainable under Fannie B-5-01 and AB 493. Remaining pool is the draw fee, $120M at 100% share.
- Idea 6: $100M needs 100k signed claims a year at $1,200 per claim, which needs a fee on the whole settlement. That is barred or restricted in several states and gives homeowners little net benefit on $1-4k uplifts.
- Idea 7: LA's $85M assumes the 13,000 existing files flow into the new regime. They do not if the laws are prospective.
- Idea 8: the $530M pool counts 1.2M servicer files, likely 3-5x too many, and double-counts clearance with idea 7.

## Picks

None. No idea clears both bars (median at least $20M, upside at least $100M on assumptions I believe).

Closest to the bar is idea 2 (median about $15M, upside about $90M), and it would only reach $100M if all three of these hold: outcome pricing at 3%+ survives against Rebuild-style software (about 40%), 10-15 platforms with $100M+ of carrier billings outsource rather than build shared services (about 15%), and a 5-point collected-rate lift is measured and repeatable (about 30%). Joint odds are roughly 5%. Shrinking it to independents-only or to Texas hail roofers would be the failure mode round 1 documented.

What would reopen the pool:

- A price quote from three PE or franchise restoration platforms showing they will pay at least 3% of collected carrier dollars for an outsourced revenue cycle, with a measured lift.
- Evidence that a servicer or investor will let a third-party trust hold loss-draft proceeds and share the economics, which Fannie B-5-01 does not currently contemplate.
- Published ALE-days-saved data from a managed-repair program that a carrier will price against.
- DTSC's final clearance standard naming an independent clearance role that carriers cannot self-select.

## Method note

About 45 searches and 10 page fetches. WebSearch worked throughout; the DuckDuckGo fallback was not needed. Items I could not verify and treated as unverified: the 64% TPA dependence figure, the "pending supplement" AR share, Crawford's contractor-fee revenue, the exact operative date of AB 1795, and AB 493's scope for loans made before 2026.

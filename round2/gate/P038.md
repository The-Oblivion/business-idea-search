# Gate report: pool P038, US nuclear fleet operating, fuel and capital spend (and the outsourced services ecosystem behind it)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 2-4 searches each, plus several page fetches (DOE licensing test, WNN on TNC Services, Utility Dive and ANS on the DOE loan, SEC release on NLI, ProPublica 990 for PRI). Result: 3 kill, 4 weak, 1 strong. One pick (idea 1), sharpened below.

## Headline findings

- The money is real but it already flows to an oligopoly that is consolidating fast. Day & Zimmermann says it is incumbent maintenance contractor at 52 of 100 US units and ran a TVA temp-labor contract valued at $950M over 7 years; Westinghouse bought BHI Energy (8,500 peak workers) in 2022 to build an "integrated outage" business; Enercon was bought by Arlington Capital in April 2026 (reported $1.25B equity, merged with Pond, 2,700 professionals, add-ons planned); Pelican Energy (Fund IV, nuclear-only) bought Riggins, Veridiam and Container Technologies in 2026; BWXT bought Precision Components Group (July 2026); Oklo bought machine shop ARMEC.
- The AI wedge is already occupied by funded software vendors that sell to the licensee engineer, who stays accountable: Nuclearn ($10.5M Series A, 65+ reactors, "Engineering AI" product), Everstar/Gordian ($4M pre-seed plus $5.5M later; EXCEL Services and Pelican are investors; DOE/INL/Argonne/Microsoft test turned a 4-6 week drafting task into one day), Atomic Canyon ($7M seed, Diablo Canyon), Westinghouse bertha/HiVE (with Google Cloud), and The Nuclear Company (about $70M raised, 288 open roles, AI plus services arm). The pool's premise that "a tool's output cannot be used until a qualified organization owns it" is overstated: no NRC rule bars human-verified AI-assisted analysis, and the vendors' own framing is "experts design, AI accelerates, experts validate."
- Several pitched pools were inflated by 2-10x once counted: the Nadcap analogy (PRI's entire revenue is $70.3M), the "$17.5B" DOE loan (a conditional loan for big long-lead equipment, not a sub-tier parts budget), the outage-traveler pool (local craft and plant-direct hires are in the headcount), and the licensing-clock pool.

## Notes by idea

**1. Signed-Off.** Competitors named: Sargent & Lundy, Enercon/Pond, Jensen Hughes, Framatome, Westinghouse, Bechtel, Aptim, EXCEL Services (services); Nuclearn, Atomic Canyon, Everstar, Westinghouse bertha, The Nuclear Company (AI). Substitutes: owner in-house engineers using those tools (this is the real substitute: tools that make plant engineers 30% faster shrink overflow demand). Legal check: Appendix B and NQA-1 (RG 1.28 Rev 4 endorses NQA-1) do not prohibit AI-assisted analysis; NRC has only a working group listing AI guidance options and no AI-specific rule; the real gates are customer acceptance of the vendor's NQA-1 Subpart 2.7 software QA and approved-supplier-list status (NUPIC-style audit). The NEI/EUCG $7.59B 2025 capital figure and the outsourced-engineering pool ($1.3-1.4B) were not independently verified (2023 NEI data: $7.06/MWh capital x 816 TWh is ~$5.8B). Upside needs: (a) buy or white-label an ASL-listed boutique in year 1 (plausible; PE is actively buying nuclear engineering); (b) AI removes >=40% of hours on bounded deliverables with human sign-off (uncertain; Nuclearn's CEO told the NRC that actual 50.59 evaluations and complex analysis are hard); (c) 5+ owner QA organizations accept the software QA program inside 24 months (moderate); (d) hiring ~250 qualified engineers into the same shortage (hard, which is why AI leverage must be real); (e) developer/restart licensing work adds $25-35M (moderate; Everstar shows demand); (f) Enercon/S&L do not match fixed-price AI-enabled pricing for 3+ years (low-moderate; Enercon has fresh PE capital and a mandate to expand).

**2. Dose Ledger.** Real shortage (senior contract RP technician pool down ~37% over three years, Health Physics 2022; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9028287 ). But Part 26 and 10 CFR 73.56 stay licensee-run, PADS already moves access status between sites, and a typical outage is 400-600 contract workers (POWER), with Cook reporting only 30-40% of its 900 added workers as specialty. A competent staffing startup could reach $20-30M; $100M of billings needs share no new entrant has.

**3. Certified Cell.** Best-structured manufacturing idea in the pool (one large factory is plausibly ~$100M revenue; BWXT's Precision Components deal is 450+ employees and 500,000 sq ft). Kept at weak because demand beyond the conditional AP1000 loans is unproven, N-stamp work needs human Authorized Nuclear Inspector sign-off so "automatic paperwork" saves less than pitched, and Pelican/BWXT/Oklo are buying capacity with deeper pockets. Median misses the $20M bar for a greenfield build.

**5/7.** Both reduce to revenue that is mostly equipment or labor pass-through with 5-15% fee income and incumbents' existing alliances (D&Z, Westinghouse/BHI, Framatome, GEV Hitachi). Liquidated damages for lost generation also risk being read as insurance (round 1 NY DFS lesson).

## Picks

### Pick 1: Signed-Off, sharpened (acquire-and-automate nuclear engineering house)

Sharpened pitch (full text also in the structured output): do not lead with "AI output is unusable until a qualified org owns it" (no rule says that and the AI vendors already sell to licensee engineers). Lead with capacity, speed and a price guarantee: a fixed-price, rework-guaranteed supplier of finished Appendix B engineering deliverables, built by buying or white-labeling an ASL-listed 30-100 person boutique (skipping the 12-24 month audit queue), then rebuilding its workflows AI-first under an NQA-1 Subpart 2.7 software QA program, with qualified engineers signing every output. Sell to single-site operators, restarts and the ~25 developers; merge idea 8's licensing factory (docketing completeness, RAI response) as the developer-facing line and idea 7's uprate engineering as a fleet line. Data asset: version-locked, customer-accepted calculation and evaluation templates per plant design. Path: $20-25M boutique in year 1-2, $50-60M by year 4 across 8-10 owners, $100M+ by year 6 with 25% from developer licensing and new-build. Cheapest test: a paid pilot of ~20 50.59 screenings and ~10 calculation revisions, plant engineer signing, measuring hours vs. baseline and rework, while interviewing 10 QA directors on what they would need to accept the software QA program.

## Pool note

Big, budgeted nuclear spend, but incumbents are consolidating and capital is already buying certified capacity and the AI software wedge. Several pools were 2-10x inflated on inspection. Only the accountable, AI-enabled services wedge stayed open, and it is thin.

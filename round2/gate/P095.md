# Gate report: P095 (cyber and liability premiums carved out for generative-AI risk)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Adversarial due diligence on 8 ideas. Default assumptions: someone already does it, and the market is smaller than claimed.
Method: 22 web searches plus 5 page fetches this session, on top of the 21-search ideation log (`backups/P095/P095.research-notes.md`). Running notes and versioned snapshots are in `gate/backups/P095/`.
Sizing: MEDIAN = annual revenue in year 5-7 with competent execution. UPSIDE = year 5-7 with top-decile execution. Both in USD millions.
Pick rule: at most 2, only if the upside is at least $100M on assumptions I can name and believe, and the median is at least $20M.

## Result in one paragraph

No pick. Eight ideas, two kills, six weak, zero strong. This pool has real pain (ISO CG 40 47 / 40 48 / 35 08 took effect January 2026) but the money that is actually movable is far smaller than the headline. Affirmative-AI premium is forecast at only about $4.7-4.8B globally by 2032 (Deloitte, carried from ideation notes), and that small pool is already contested by Corgi (reported $4B valuation, more than $370M raised in 2026), AIUC ($55M raised, $40M Series A on 2026-09-17), Armilla (Lloyd's coverholder, up to $25M limits), HSB/Munich Re, Mount (YC), Klaimee (YC), Testudo and Coverwatch. Every idea here either (a) sells to the same small set of buyers those players already chase, (b) needs a counterparty to give up something it values (rescission rights, quiet denials, uniform forms), or (c) rests on a market-size number that falls apart when checked against the actual ILS, claims or customer counts. The median case for every idea is below the $20M floor.

## Idea-by-idea evidence

### 1. Seam Map: weak (median $8M, upside $40M)

**Competitors found.**
- Risklytics (San Francisco): AI platform that analyzes commercial policies and insurer filings, finds AI-exclusion gaps, names carriers that do not apply the exclusion or offer affirmative AI cover, and helps clients assemble the governance documentation underwriters ask for; partners with wholesale brokers and MGAs. Funding, size and pricing undisclosed. [Interview](https://datainnovation.org/2026/07/5-qs-with-samuel-gold-founder-of-risklytics/). This is about 60% of the Seam Map product, already live.
- Coverwatch: flat-fee AI commercial broker, $4.5M pre-seed July 2026 (carried from ideation notes). Harper: AI-native commercial agency, ~$47M seed+A in Feb 2026, 160+ carriers, small and mid-sized businesses ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/26/harper-raises-47m-to-modernise-business-insurance-with-ai/)). Different segment, but proof that investors fund the buyer-side channel.
- Armilla's partner program with Trustible (AI governance platform) already bundles risk assessment with placement ([Armilla](https://www.armilla.ai/partnerships)).

**Substitutes.** Coverage counsel publish the checklist for free (Fenwick: review insuring agreements, exclusions and endorsements together, consider manuscript endorsements, captives or DIC layers; Haynes Boone: negotiate at renewal). Brokers fold analysis into commission. I found no Marsh, Aon or WTW packaged AI-exclusion tool, which means the service is delivered inside the commission and is therefore "free" to the buyer.

**Revenue math check.** 28,000 reachable firms x $40k a year = $1.1B at full take-up; 2,500 customers is 9%. Problems: (1) the seam reconciliation is a one-time event per program (after the first pass, the recurring work is maintenance, which buyers value at $10-15k, not $40k); (2) a $100M-revenue company's whole tower (GL/umbrella, cyber, E&O, D&O) costs roughly $300k-1M a year (my estimate, no source found), so a $40k recurring fee is 4-13% of program cost, which a free broker service undercuts; (3) the number of firms with an actual seam is smaller than 28,000: Gallagher says "a few carriers" are adopting the ISO forms and Zurich says amending wording is usually unnecessary. My funnel: 28k x 25% facing a live AI exclusion in 2026-28 = 7,000; 5-8% choose a fee-only third party = 350-560 diagnostics at $35-60k = $12-34M cumulative over the cycle, roughly $4-11M a year plus maintenance. Median $8M by year 6.

**Upside case ($40M).** What has to go right: (a) the exclusion wave is broad and sticky (plausible, ~50%); (b) peer-wording corpus becomes a real cornered resource, which requires brokers or counsel to share wordings, and that conflicts with the fee-only stance (low plausibility, ~15%); (c) it graduates into a fee-only placement shop for residual gaps, which is just a brokerage competing with Harper-style funded entrants and the big five (moderate, ~25%). 1,000 recurring customers x $25k + $15M of placement and benchmark revenue = $40M. $100M requires the full 2,500 x $40k, which I do not believe.

**Legal/premise check.** Fee-only advice is licensable but regulated: for example New York allows compensation for a program review only under a written agreement stating the amount ([NY DFS opinion](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2008/rg080711.htm)). Manageable, not fatal.

**Deciding reason.** Real pain, real budget (coverage counsel hourly), but a recurring $40k price is not supported and Risklytics plus free broker analysis cap the market.

### 2. Warranty File: weak (median $5M, upside $45M)

**Competitors found.**
- Verification of cyber application answers exists: IntellectAI's Risk Analyst cross-checks submissions against open ports, leaked credentials and SOC 2 reports; Coalition continuously scans insureds; Cyberwrite quantifies cyber risk; SecondSight/INSUREtrust Cyber Pre-Check for brokers ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/07/22/how-ai-can-rescue-cyber-submissions-from-the-black-hole/), [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230629837555/en/SecondSight-and-INSUREtrust-Partner-Utilize-AI-to-Transform-Mid-Market-Cyber-Insurance)).
- The AI-specific evidence layer is being filled by GRC incumbents with AIUC's help: Drata announced native AIUC-1 support on 2026-07-16 (continuous control monitoring, evidence collection, Audit Hub) and SafeBase published the same day. I did not find Vanta support or any named cyber insurer that accepts the evidence.
- AIUC-1 itself: ~5,000 test scenarios, quarterly re-tests, paired with AIUC's own insurance (reported up to $50M; $40M in another source).
- Klaimee (YC, $5.5M seed July 2026, insurance-backed warranties and A-F certification; from pool data).

**Substitutes.** The signer's co-signature and counsel review; the carrier's own follow-up call; SOC 2 reports.

**Legal premise.** Travelers v. International Control Services (C.D. Ill., filed 2022-07-06) sought rescission over an MFA statement on the application; per case summaries it ended when Travelers voluntarily dismissed on 2022-08-26 with ICS stipulating the policy was rescinded (dismissal detail from a search summary of [Barton](https://www.bartonesq.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Cyber-Insurance-Rescission-and-Damages-Exposure.pdf); I did not open the PDF). Columbia Casualty v. Cottage Health argued negligent misrepresentation suffices. So the pain is real. But I found no precedent for an incontestability-style endorsement in commercial cyber, and the reason may be that rescission is a claims-leakage tool carriers keep; a carrier will only waive it for answers it independently trusts, and a $250 file fee does not buy that trust. Legal premise (state rescission rules, form filing, anti-waiver) is unverified and could differ by state.

**Revenue math check.** Carrier side: 400k policies x 35% verified x $250 = $35M. The 35% verified share needs many carriers to sign the endorsement; a 3-5% share in five years gives $3-5M. Insured side: 25,000 x $3k = $75M. This fails on price versus value (lesson 5): the insured pays $3k to protect a policy with $10k+ premium, but any underwriting credit for verified answers is a few percent of premium ($500-1,000 on $10k; $2.5-5k on a $50k policy); the rescission insurance value is a low-probability tail. Insureds already buy the same evidence inside Vanta/Drata-class tools they pay for anyway. Realistic insured-side addressable base: policies with premium of $50k+ (est. 3-5% of 4.4M = 130-220k) x 5% adoption x $3k = $20-33M in the best case.

**Upside case ($45M).** Needs: (a) 2-3 large carriers or MGAs sign the endorsement and publish it as a quoting advantage (plausible, 25%); (b) the file becomes the accepted AI supplement across carriers, displacing bespoke forms (plausible only if AIUC-1 is not the standard, which it is trying to be, 20%); (c) GRC vendors do not simply add a carrier export (low, 25%). Joint probability is low; $100M requires all three plus the heroic 35% share.

**Deciding reason.** Sharpest insight in the pool (claims die by rescission), but the evidence layer is taken, the insured-side price exceeds its value, and the carrier concession is unproven.

### 3. The Mutual: weak (median $12M, upside $80M)

**Competitors found.** The idea is the AIUC founders' own published proposal: a frontier AI mutual owned by labs, member capital, shared safety commitments, incident reporting; the authors say it needs no new law and could issue policies "within three months" but name no members, capital or limits ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/bootstrapping-frontier-ai-governance-by-mutualizing-risk)). AIUC (CEO Rune Kvist, ex-Anthropic first product hire; Rajiv Dattani; Brandon Wang) raised a $40M Series A led by Ribbit on 2026-09-17, total $55M, to extend audits, standards and insurance to frontier models ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/17/aiuc-raises-40m-to-police-risk-in-frontier-ai-agents/)). Others in the tower: Aon-placed towers, Lloyd's, Munich Re aiSure, Armilla, Corgi (Technology RRG), Testudo. CrowdStrike's Project QuiltWorks (May 2026) has insurers (Coalition, Liberty Mutual, Lockton, Resilience, Marsh) financing frontier-AI cyber risk ([CrowdStrike](https://ir.crowdstrike.com/news-releases/news-release-details/crowdstrike-expands-project-quiltworks-cyber-insurance-industry)).

**Substitutes.** Captive per lab (OpenAI reportedly discussed one but says it has none now), self-insurance from investor funds, and balance-sheet settlement (Anthropic's $1.5B authors settlement was paid from its own resources). The OpenAI "$300M via Aon" figure is anonymous-sourced and disputed (another source says much lower) ([Outpost summary of Reuters/FT](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/open-ai-and-anthropic-face-insurance-challenges-amid-multibillion-dollar-lawsuits-20757/)). The CSA's note calls foundation-model concentration the clearest insurability frontier ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-foundation-model-concentration-insurance-r/)), which cuts both ways: real need, but reinsurers and ILS investors balk at correlated single-name risk.

**Revenue math check.** The pitch assumes 12 developers x $30M. The $30M anchor is one lab's tower (about $300M at 8-10% rate on line). Independent labs able to pay that scale number perhaps 3-5; the long tail pays low single-digit millions. Corrected lab premium: 4 x $25M + 8 x $3M = ~$125M, not $360M. Deployers: 250 x $1.2M = $300M assumes large banks, insurers and health systems buy AI-specific excess with no loss history, when many own captives and Corgi/Armilla/AIUC already sell up to $25-50M; call it $100M. Total ~$225M gross premium. A sponsor/manager earns 10-15% = $22-34M if the mutual forms and fills, and a mutual's surplus belongs to members, so the "if it owns the paper, gross premium is revenue" line is not revenue to a founder (it is balance sheet and claims). Median (probability-weighted for formation, with a signed anchor lab): ~$12M.

**Upside case ($80M).** Needs: (a) 3+ top labs choose a shared mutual over their own captive (plausible but not likely, ~25%); (b) reinsurers and ILS provide $1B+ of layers over correlated single-model risk, despite their stated reluctance (~30%); (c) lawsuits at the scale already seen push premium to $2B-limit towers, ~$150-200M a year per top lab at 8-10% rate on line (~35%); (d) this team, not AIUC, is the manager (low, ~15%, unless the plan is to partner). With 3 labs x $160M + deployers $300M = ~$780M gross premium x 10-12% = $78-94M. That is the only path in this pool where the pool itself is big, but my probability for the whole chain is under 5%, and the final dollar figure is still below $100M.

**Legal/premise check.** Bermuda Class 3A/3B and sponsored cells are real; US RRG route (Corgi's Technology RRG) is taken and bars loans to members under Vermont Act 77 (carried from P096 notes). Antitrust: a safety-standards mutual of competing labs needs counsel review (Lawfare claims compliance; unverified). No enacted federal backstop exists (Stetler 2025; arXiv 2605.18784).

**Deciding reason.** Biggest pool, wrong buyer count (3-5 whales who can self-insure), and the best-resourced would-be builder wrote the proposal.

### 4. Gate Meter: weak (median $8M, upside $70M)

**Competitors found.** No live telemetry-priced AI liability product found (low-confidence negative). But the idea is in the air: two 2026 arXiv papers propose trace-conditioned pricing for agents ([2606.16465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16465), [2607.11999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11999)); Mount (YC P26, founders Fabian Amherd and John Bachmann) sells a liability policy for deployed agents and says each red-team evaluation feeds its pricing engine ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/mount)); AIUC-1 already names execution-chain logs and tool-call records as the evidence set; ElevenLabs announced an AIUC-1-backed policy (terms undisclosed; [tracker](https://vorplabs.com/ai-agent-liability)). Corgi, Armilla, HSB, Testudo, Munich Re aiSure sell cover. No gateway vendor (Portkey, LiteLLM, Kong, Cloudflare) has an insurer tie-in that I could find.

**Substitutes.** Questionnaire-plus-test underwriting (Armilla, AIUC), cyber endorsements (Coalition since 2024, base policy 2025), and doing nothing: most enterprise agents carry no AI-specific cover.

**Revenue math check.** $100M of MGA revenue needs ~$350M of premium at 25-30% take. The pitch gets there with $60-100B of 2029 enterprise AI spend, a quarter metered through gateways ($15-25B), and 1.5-3% premium. Three weak points: (1) a growing share of agent traffic runs inside SaaS platforms and hyperscaler stacks (Copilot, Agentforce-style), not through a third-party gateway, so 25% is optimistic and LiteLLM is open source; (2) loss is driven by what an agent does, not token volume, and a gateway sees tool calls but not business context, so telemetry's pricing edge is unproven until years of claims exist; (3) 55% of cyber policies being endorsement-type says nothing about AI cover demand. Also $350M of premium is 7% of Deloitte's ~$4.8B 2032 global AI premium and has to be won against funded rivals. Median: 20-30 enterprise programs and one capacity partner, ~$30M premium, ~$8M revenue.

**Upside case ($70M).** Needs: the AI premium pool is 2-3x Deloitte (plausible, ~30%); a hyperscaler or top-two gateway makes this the default attach (~15%, and that same partner can swap MGAs, which is the author's own "race the bundle" risk); telemetry-based pricing demonstrably beats questionnaires in loss ratio (~35%, needs 3+ years of claims). Net: ~$70M in the best case, with $100M possible only if all three land.

**Legal/premise check.** Usage-based commercial rating needs filing or surplus-lines treatment; the gateway or cloud marketplace collecting premium on an invoice and sharing it implies producer licensing or a referral-only structure. Not a blocker but a delay. Telemetry that excludes content is feasible.

**Deciding reason.** Good pattern transfer (Snapshot/Root), but a pricing method rather than a moat, replicable by funded rivals, and the channel assumption is the weakest link.

### 5. Waive List: weak (median $5M, upside $45M)

**Competitors found.** No product that scores insureds' generative-AI usage for underwriting turned up. Adjacent players sit in the intake path and can extend: CyberCube (externally observable security signals, scoring built into Duck Creek intake, Exposure Manager launched Sept 2025; [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220228005481/en/Insurers-to-Benefit-From-Streamlined-Underwriting-and-Risk-Evaluation-as-CyberCube-Partners-with-Duck-Creek-Technologies)), Kovrr (clients Mapfre, Acrisure Re, Lockton Re, Deutsche Ruck), Verisk (GenAI Commercial Underwriting Assistant, Sept 2025, [Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/verisk-launches-generative-ai-commercial-underwriting-assistant-to-revolutionize-risk-assessment-and-underwriting-efficiency/)), and ingestion start-ups Cytora, Kalepa, Sixfold ($30M, Jan 2026, per the idea text; not re-verified).

**Substitutes.** The cheapest one is the application: Westfield's underwriters now ask which models insureds use, how the decision to use them was made, and what controls verify output accuracy ([Insurance Business](https://www.businessinsurance.com/insurers-brokers-adjust-as-ai-exclusions-emerge/)). Carriers can also sell coverage back onto the CGL once they understand AI use and guardrails (reported, unnamed carriers, no prices).

**Premise check.** The "blanket-attach equilibrium" and the hypothesis that treaties require the exclusion on all cedent policies are both unconfirmed: I found no reinsurer, Lloyd's or broker treaty bulletin requiring it (only an Evercore forecast that insurers will add exclusions). Gallagher says "a few carriers" are adopting the ISO forms, though other sources say 60+ groups filed or adopted; the true attach rate is unknown. An outside-in footprint (public chatbots, job postings, SDK strings) mostly misses internal use, vendor-embedded AI and SaaS features, which is where "arising out of" bites. Verisk is unlikely to be a clean villain: it also sells carriers analytics.

**Revenue math check.** 8M policy-terms is unsourced (NAIC counts 4.4M cyber policies alone). 8M x $6 = $48M requires 100% share. A realistic 15-20% share by year 6 is $7-10M. Reinsurer/portfolio scans at $0.5-1M x 60 = $30-60M assume every large writer buys; CyberCube-class portfolio products are a niche, so $0.2-0.4M x 40 = $8-16M. The author's own words: honest ceiling for the exclusion decision alone is $40-70M and "path is heroic". Median ~$5M.

**Upside case ($45M).** Needs: a filed rating variable that regulators accept (they are slow and AI footprint accuracy is unproven, ~20%); treaty pressure that makes reinsurers demand the score (~20%); incumbent data vendors do not bolt on a copy (~25%). All three plus 60% share of top-50 groups gets ~$45M. Not $100M. The why-now also decays: once affirmative AI cover is standard (2028-29), the exclusion decision stops being the scarce moment (lesson 6).

**Deciding reason.** Small even in the upside, premise on treaties unverified, and the obvious competitors are the carriers' existing data vendors.

### 6. Mirror Policy: weak (median $10M, upside $60M)

**Competitors found.** No CG 40 47 BI/PD buy-back form found. But the gap is not empty: Armilla (Lloyd's, up to $25M, Chaucer-backed Vanguard AI), Testudo (up to $10M), AIUC (up to $50M), Corgi (Technology RRG plus new admitted carrier aimed at small business; reported $4B valuation), HSB standalone AI liability launched March 2026 for small and mid-sized businesses (low-authority source: [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/10/ai-liability-insurance-coverage-gap-small-business-guide)), Munich Re aiSure. These mainly cover financial loss to AI vendors, so the BI/PD wrap for operating companies is genuinely less served.

**Substitutes.** Primary carriers: one trade source says some will sell coverage back onto the existing CGL for added premium once they see AI use and guardrails. A carrier that can do that undercuts an exclusion-triggered DIC on both price and convenience (no second claims handler). Also: manuscript endorsements negotiated by counsel; captives (Fenwick lists DIC layers and captives as options).

**Revenue math check.** The author's own numbers: AI-only MGA revenue tops out well under $100M "even at 100% share" (~$75M at 40% attach). To reach $130M by year 7-8 the pitch adds other ISO optional exclusions (PFAS, cyber-in-GL, abuse/molestation, silica, communicable disease) at a 3% load and 25% attach. That is a different business: those exclusion gaps already have dedicated E&S markets, and each needs its own underwriting. Also, 30% of firms with "material AI exposure by 2028" and an 8% load are assumptions with no loss data; frequency of AI-caused BI/PD today is low, so the reasons to buy are mostly contractual (customers requiring the cover). Pricing as 5-12% of the primary premium also invites adverse selection (only AI-heavy insureds buy) and the mirror cannot reprice without a loss model. Median: a surplus-lines MGA at ~$40M premium x 25% = $10M.

**Upside case ($60M).** Needs: ISO exclusions attach broadly to GL at 2027-28 renewals (~40%); customer contracts force a buy-back (~50%); reinsurers give casualty quota share with an AI cap despite no loss data (~35%); the MGA keeps 25%. 40% attach on $9B of AI-exposed GL+umbrella premium x 8% = $300M premium x 25% = $75M is the author's figure at full ambition; with a 20-25% attach I get $40-60M. To reach $100M the business has to become an all-exclusion wrapper, which I would call a new thesis, not this one.

**Legal/premise check.** Surplus lines or Lloyd's coverholder paper avoids admitted-form filing but needs a diligent-search and eligibility path per state; a DIC that triggers on "denial or probable denial" invites bad-faith and moral-hazard disputes with the primary carrier.

**Deciding reason.** Genuine unserved BI/PD gap, but the author's own math caps it, primaries can sell back, and the wrap-everything stretch is a different, more crowded business.

### 7. Causation Desk: kill (median $2M, upside $20M)

**Competitors found.** "Nobody as a neutral" is roughly right, but the capability is easy to extend: digital-forensics firms already serve the cyber claims ecosystem (Surefire Cyber, Kroll, Mandiant; Surefire formed an AI Advisory Board 2026-06-18, per ideation notes), AAA/JAMS can add technical panels, and any carrier can build an expert-determination clause with existing neutral experts.

**Premise check (false in the load-bearing way).** The pitch frames the clause "like the appraisal clause in property policies". Appraisal is limited to amount of loss and does not decide causation, coverage or liability: courts have confined appraisers to valuation, and a Minnesota appellate decision held a dispute turning on contract meaning, causation and liability improper for appraisal ([Minnesota](https://minnlawyer.com/wp-files/fulltext-061812/opa100714-0613.html)); general commentary says the same ([appraisal vs declaratory relief](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/knowing-when-to-invoke-the-appraisal-process-versus-filing-a-lawsuit-for-declaratory-relief/)); a California case to the same effect appeared in search results but I did not open it. A panel finding that is "binding on facts, not on how the wording applies" leaves the carrier free to litigate coverage, so the $250-600k declaratory-judgment saving the pitch counts on mostly does not materialize. Evidence-preservation is real (courts treat prompts and outputs as ESI; the OpenAI output-log preservation order was reportedly narrowed and later ended, sources differ on dates), but it is a legal-hold service, not a neutral panel.

**Payer.** Chief claims officer from loss adjustment expense: carriers that prefer quiet denial to a published ruling on a form filed ~2,369 times (the author's own point) are not natural buyers. The adopters named (AI-native carriers, Mirror, Mutual) are products that do not exist at scale yet.

**Revenue math check.** 50,000 cyber claims (NAIC 2024) x 8% AI-involved by 2030 = 4,000 and 1.5M third-party GL/E&O/D&O claims (unsourced) x 2% = 30,000 are assumptions on assumptions; 20% to determination at $18k yields ~$122M only if carriers adopt the clause. The author concedes that otherwise this is a $10-20M expert practice.

**Upside case ($20M).** An AAA-like neutral that wins a handful of form-level adoptions: maybe 1,000 determinations x $18k = $18M. Needs the same scale of carrier adoption that the pitch calls fragile.

**Deciding reason.** The central legal analogy fails and the buyer prefers silence; at best a services practice.

### 8. Trigger: kill (median $3M, upside $25M)

**Competitors found.** Parametrix already (a) structures the Cumulus Re cloud-outage cat bonds for Hannover Re and acts as calculation agent, with a proprietary dataset of thousands of outage events ([Artemis deal page](https://www.artemis.bm/deal-directory/cumulus-re-series-2026-1/), [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/parametrix-places-35m-cloud-outage-cat-bond-for-hannover-re/)) and (b) extended monitoring to AI services (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) in 2024 so insurers can build parametric BI products ([Parametrix](https://www.parametrixinsurance.com/in-the-news/parametrix-expands-monitoring-capabilities-to-ai-services-like-chatgpt)). Munich Re aiSure reportedly uses model-accuracy benchmark triggers (single secondary source). Monitoring substitutes: Downdetector, ThousandEyes, Datadog, public leaderboards.

**Revenue math check (fails by orders of magnitude).** The idea's $5B of referenced limit compares with: Cumulus Re $13.75M (2024), $20M (2025), $35M (2026), none triggered; the largest single cyber cat bond is Beazley's $300M PoleStar Re (Dec 2025); all cyber cat bonds transferred roughly $0.8B at end-2024 ([Geneva Association](https://www.genevaassociation.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/cyber_ils_summary_120826.pdf)); the entire cat-bond market is $65.5B outstanding ([Artemis Q3 2026](https://www.artemis.bm/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/catastrophe-bond-ils-market-report-q3-2026.pdf)). So $5B referenced to AI-provider incidents alone would exceed the whole cyber ILS market several times over; at 0.5-1% fee it is worth $25-50M only if that happens. The monitoring stream (1,500 enterprises x $30k) competes with observability and gateway vendors that already track provider latency and drift. The parametric-MGA margin has no source.

**Premise check.** The CSA note frames foundation-model concentration as the "uninsurable" risk; investors want uncorrelated returns but have no loss history to price against, and the May 2026 paper's call for a named provider-incident trigger is a recommendation, not demand. Independence is a real moat in principle (PCS-style), but Parametrix is already the independent calculation agent in the closest adjacent market.

**Upside case ($25M).** Parametrix-style economics extended to AI: a few $50-100M deals a year at 1-2% plus a $10M monitoring line.

**Deciding reason.** Occupied by Parametrix and sized against a cat-bond market 100x too small.

# Gate report: pool P013, US HOA, condo and co-op association assessments and capital repairs

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with roughly 3-6 searches each, plus two primary-source reads (OPPAGA Report 26-04 and Fannie Mae LL-2026-03, both read as PDFs). Result: 2 kill, 6 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The pool is big ($124.2B of assessments, $31.1B into reserves, 373k associations) but the shock-driven spend is far smaller than the ideas assumed, and every adjacent layer already has an owner.

- The Florida repair number is about 10x too high. OPPAGA Report 26-04 (read from the PDF) reports 903 repair permit applications from Phase 2 milestone inspections, averaging $337,229 in 2024 (671 permits) and $496,236 in 2025 (232 permits). That is about $341M reported over two years, from only 71% and 64% of jurisdictions, so roughly $0.25B a year once scaled up. About 1 in 10 permits is over $1M. Idea 1 assumed a $3M average and $2.4-3B a year, and idea 3 assumed about $1B a year of Florida structural borrowing. Source: https://oppaga.fl.gov/Documents/Reports/26-04.pdf
- The Fannie shock is real but narrower than pitched. Primary text of LL-2026-03: Limited Review retires for applications dated on or after Aug 3, 2026; the 15% minimum reserve allocation applies to Full Review applications dated on or after Jan 4, 2027; the reserve-study alternative now requires the budget to include the study's highest recommended allocation, and baseline funding is banned. Source: https://www.tenaco.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Fannie-Mae-Lender-Letter-LL-2026-03-03-18-26.pdf . No source found shows a delay (FHFA said it is implementing on time: https://www.mpamag.com/us/news/general/the-big-interview-what-brokers-need-to-know-before-januarys-condo-deadline/589997 ). The three-year age limit on a reserve study comes from law-firm summaries, not the letter text, and describes existing policy: https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-fannie-mae-announces-significant-changes-to-project-standards-and-property-insurance-requirements-for-community-associations
- The channels are owned. Managers run captive finance, banking and insurance arms (Associa Capital Advisors, FirstService Financial). Deposit-funded banks hold the cheap money (Western Alliance HOA deposits passed $10B in Q1 2025). Vantaca ($1.25B valuation, 550+ management companies, 6.5M doors) owns the ledgers and just launched payments. Honeycomb, SmartProperty, InspectHOA, CondoSafe and the GSEs' own free tools cover insurance, reserve studies and condo project review.

## Revenue-math corrections (where the $100M paths break)

- Idea 1: 4% of the real Florida base ($0.25B a year) is about $10M, not $100M. The pool's $2.4-3B Florida base used a $3M average that the state data contradicts by roughly 9x.
- Idea 2: $100M needs 40,000 subscribers at $2.5k, 10.7% of all associations, for a product the lender does not require annually. Even if all 373k associations bought a study every four years at $3k, the whole study market is about $280M a year.
- Idea 3: $98M gross interest needs a $1.3B book, about 120 originations a year at $2M. Florida has only about 40-60 repair permits a year over $1M. The $4B national borrowing base is unsourced, and no one publishes association-loan volume.
- Idea 4: the $103M sums four fees; the largest ($40M of float) depends on a bank partner sharing deposits that association banks already collect.
- Idea 5: needs 40,000 associations at $2.5k per fee, against incumbents that sell the same savings as a bundled program.
- Idea 6: 14,000 associations means about 500 licensed managers at 28 each, at services margins.
- Idea 7: a $900M book of 30,000 loans is equity-funded growth with accrued, not cash, interest for 4-5 years.
- Idea 8: the idea's own total addressable wallet is about $350M, so $100M is 25-30% share against four incumbents and two free GSE tools.

## Picks

None. No idea has a bottom-up path to $100M that survives the data, a gap free of incumbents, and an intact legal premise. The two closest, ideas 8 and 3, fail on wallet size and entrenched channels respectively, and shrinking either into a Florida-only or lender-desk boutique is the failure mode round 1 documented.

What would have to be true to reopen the pool:

- A market-wide count of association loan originations (call-report or CAI data) showing at least $4B a year of non-bank-addressable borrowing; today no such number is published.
- A per-review price for lender condo project review above about $400 and a lender willingness to buy a determination with liability, which would change idea 8.
- A bank or insurer willing to commit forward-flow funding below deposit-funded cost, which would change idea 3.

Closest-to-viable follow-up (not a pick): a conflict-free association insurance and reserve-cash program sold on commission rather than fee-only would reach scale on premium volume, but Honeycomb and the manager-captive agencies already hold that position and no national association premium total was found.

# Gate report: pool P126, federal services after the April 30, 2026 fixed-price executive order

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with roughly 3-6 searches each, plus primary-source reads of FAR 19.502-2 and FAR 8.405-5 and the FAR 32.x, GAO, TIGTA, DoD IG, SBA, Oracle and Booz Allen documents cited below. Result: 5 kill, 3 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline findings

1. **The whole pool is one bet on one executive order.** EO 14402 (April 30, 2026) makes fixed price the default and orders a review of each agency's top-10 non-fixed-price contracts ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/new-executive-order-directs-agencies-to-default-to-fixed-price-contracting/), [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/05/president-trump-signs-executive-order-mandating-fixed-price-contracting-in-federal-procurement)). Every idea needs the order to hold through 2029 and beyond, and the order's own purpose is to shrink the T&M and cost-type pool that ideas 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 sell against. The next presidential election falls inside the 5-7 year build window.
2. **Fee-on-flow models cannot reach $100M here.** The best benchmark in the pool is Oracle's purchase of Textura: about $87M of revenue on roughly $41B a year of payment volume, an implied take of about 0.21%, after about 12 years, in construction ([Oracle](https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2016/UPDATE---Oracle-Buys-Textura/default.aspx), [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/de/cloud/oracle-expands-cloud-services-with-acquisition-of-textura/)). Ideas 1, 2 and 3 price at 0.3-0.45% of a pool they must capture 8-13% of. That is Textura's result in a smaller market, in half the time.
3. **Several load-bearing statistics are stale or unconfirmed.**
   - The TIGTA "$394,430 of $1M" figure comes from an October 2012 audit of Recovery Act contracts, not a current sample ([TIGTA](https://www.tigta.gov/articles/press-releases/tigta-finds-deficiencies-irss-documentation-recovery-act-labor-charges)).
   - The CPARS "83 of 238 late, 200 of 238 non-compliant" figures come from a 2017 DoD IG report covering May 2013 to May 2016 ([DODIG-2017-081](https://media.defense.gov/2017/Dec/19/2001858450/-1/-1/1/DODIG-2017-081.PDF)).
   - The "5,100 grants staff at four agencies" figure is from GAO-13-591 (2013) ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-13-591)). I could not find the "$1.2T / 1,386 programs" figures or a "$50,000 two-appointee review" rule in any source.
   - The GSA "39% attrition" figure traces to an older GSA IG item I could not date to 2026 ([FedWeek](https://www.fedweek.com/federal-managers-daily-report/gsa-facing-personnel-challenges-ig-says/)). FedScoop separately reports a 30% cut to GSA's acquisition workforce last year ([FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/gsa-procurement-leader-says-community-readiness-poses-challenge-to-acquisition-overhaul/)).
   - The "43 and 76 day" shutdowns: 43 days is confirmed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_government_shutdown)). The second lapse was a partial DHS shutdown reported as 75 days by one source I could not corroborate.
4. **The "nobody does this" claims fail in at least five ideas** (see the table): FAR 32.10 performance-based payments and X-Caliber for idea 4; Unanet SubK, JAMIS, Contract Logix, GovForce and supplier.io for idea 2; ASC Group SeeSOR for idea 3; Deltek AutoTime and GovTime plus DOJ/OIG badge-data practice for idea 1; the Pentagon's own AI and Advana push for idea 5.
5. **Services share of the $148B pool is the only structure with a $100M ceiling.** Ideas 6 and 7 sell the labor itself, so $100M is 0.07% of the pool. But both need to win trust and set-aside eligibility that a new entrant does not have, and the medians stay at $6-12M.

## Idea notes

### 1. Badge-to-Bill (kill)

- **Competitors and substitutes.** There is no productized pre-payment hours control in my searches, but each piece exists. Timekeeping and labor-charging control sit inside Deltek AutoTime ([Deltek](https://www.deltek.com/partners/find-a-partner/autotime/)) and GovTime ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10010130/GovTime/)). After-the-fact badge-to-timesheet matching is already how DOJ, GSA IG and DIA build cases (the Booz Allen matter was one employee, 2018-2022, $422,557). A CO can request timesheets and badge data today at no cost. Inspectors general keep flagging inconsistent invoice review ([FTC OIG](https://oig.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/reports/2025-07/2023-12-22ftc-contractor-invoice-processes-audit-reportfinalpublic.pdf)), which is the problem, but also a sign that agencies treat it as a procedure to fix, not a product to buy.
- **Revenue math.** The T&M pool is real: $139B over FY17-21, about $28B a year, civilians 11% of obligations versus 1% at DoD ([GAO-22-104806](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-22-104806)). But the EO's point is to move that pool to fixed price, so the exposed base falls during the years the product would ramp. The agency side needs about $13.8B of covered spend at 0.4%. The TIGTA sample extrapolated to only $927,992 on a $5.3M procurement, so the fee on a covered order is small. Contractor side (300 accounts at $150k) is the credible half, about $25-45M, and it competes with ERP and timekeeping vendors that already hold the data.
- **Premise problems.** Badge logs only prove presence at an agency facility; much IT T&M work is performed at contractor sites or remotely, and agency security offices guard PIV logs. The recovery-audit statute (31 U.S.C. 3352(i)) mandates programs, not a vendor, and the 2020 amendment removed contractor-payment language ([DoD FMR](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/documents/fmr/current/10/10_22.pdf)). The "$6.8B FCA record in FY2025" claim I did not verify.
- **Upside assumptions.** OMB or a CFO council names this a standard control (low plausibility, no sign of it); primes adopt it voluntarily as FCA defense (moderate, but then it is compliance software at $150k per account).

### 2. The subcontract rail (weak)

- **Competitors.** Unanet Enterprise SubK (sub portals, shared invoice forms, FAR/DFARS tracking) ([Unanet](https://info.unanet.com/rethink-subcontractor-management)); JAMIS (sub timesheets, flow-downs, reporting) ([JAMIS](https://jamis.com/products/contract-management/)); Contract Logix (flow-down status per sub) ([Contract Logix](https://www.contractlogix.com/solutions/industries/government-contractors/)); GovForce, a seed-stage flow-down startup ([CityBiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/383924/govforce-raises-2-5-million-seed-round/)); supplier.io for eSRS filing ([supplier.io](https://supplier.io/solutions/supplier-compliance-software/esrs-filing)). Blackstar Capital reports a $1B facility for contractors and subs ([01net](https://www.01net.it/blackstar-capital-secures-1-billion-in-funding-to-support-government-contractors-and-subcontractors/)).
- **Revenue math.** The $86.4B small-business subcontract figure is real ([OFPP memo](https://whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/OFPP-Memorandum-Increasing-Small-Business-Subcontracting-Participation-in-the-Federal-Marketplace.pdf)). At the 33.34% share it implies a total near $259B (my arithmetic, not sourced). At the idea's 0.45%, $100M needs about $22B of flow, 8.5% of everything. At Textura's 0.21%, the whole $259B would yield about $54M. Only the early-pay margin gets above Textura, and that is financing, which brings idea 4's problems.
- **Premise problems.** FAR 52.232-40 already requires primes to pay small-business subs within 15 days of an accelerated government payment, without fees ([FAR](https://acquisition.gov/far/52.232-40)), which caps what "paid within days" is worth. FSRS was retired into SAM.gov in March 2025 ([SAM.gov](https://sam.gov/fsrs)), so the "FSRS report generation" wedge is a SAM.gov task. The 8(a) wave is real: 1,091 suspended, nearly 800 in termination proceedings ([SBA Jan](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/01/28/sba-suspends-over-1000-8a-firms-program-following-december-document-request), [SBA Mar](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/03/04/sba-moves-terminate-over-620-firms-8a-federal-contracting-program-refused-turn-over-financial-data)), but it is a one-time compliance spike, not recurring spend.
- **Upside assumptions.** Portable prime-signed performance records become something primes and subs demand (low-moderate; CPARS is the official channel); a sponsor bank enables early-pay at scale without transmitter status (unverified); the services market does not shrink (contradicted by Booz Allen's civil decline). Top-decile reaches about $60M, not $100M.

### 3. The Acceptance Rail (kill)

- **Competitors.** ASC Group sells SeeSOR inspection and QASP SaaS to DOE, DoD, State, NSF, USDA and GSA ([HigherGov](https://www.highergov.com/awardee/asc-gp-inc-10041538)); a published patent application describes software claiming to automate about 90% of COR monitoring ([Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20220245589)). IV&V is a real services market, but conflicted firms and state pre-qualified pools dominate ([Maryland DoIT](https://doit.maryland.gov/EPMO/Pages/MITDP/ivv.aspx)), and demand can shrink: Kentucky moved from mandatory to risk-based IV&V.
- **Revenue math.** The $200-300B "outcome-priced under measurement" pool is labeled an assumption in the idea, and no source supports it. The 0.3% fee on $27B verified value is again a toll that the CO's COR budget (1-2% of contract value, also an assumption) must fund while the product is free for 90 days.
- **Premise problems.** The CPARS timeliness data is from 2017. Acceptance is inherently governmental, so the product informs but cannot replace the CO, and no regulation makes a vendor's metric spec a standard. GSA and OMB would write that standard, not a startup. OneGov consumption pricing is real ([GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/gsa-expands-onegov-ai-offerings-with-discounted-openais-chatgpt-09102026)), but I found no confirmation of "RFO batch 2" milestone pricing.
- **Upside assumptions.** A GAO or COFC decision cites the format as the neutral standard (speculative, years away); the EO's performance-incentive language gets enforced rather than waived (unknown).

### 4. Verified-WIP lender (kill)

- **Competitors.** X-Caliber GovCon Capital already offers contract-based advances with a $250k minimum draw and no maximum, without factoring ([X-Caliber](https://x-calibercap.com/x-caliber-govcon-capital/)). Blackstar, United Capital Funding, Porter Capital, Bay Street and bank ABL desks cover invoices.
- **Premise problems.** The government itself offers milestone-style financing: performance-based payments are the preferred method on fixed-price contracts when the CO agrees, tied to objective events and recoverable on default ([FAR 32.10](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-32.10)); progress payments run at 80-85% of cost ([FAR 32.5](https://www.acquisition.gov/sites/default/files/current/far/compiled_html/subpart_32.5.html)) and give the government title to work in progress. The Assignment of Claims Act reaches only amounts due or to become due and requires a bank or "other financing institution" with notice ([FAR 32.8](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-32.8)); an ABA article in Fall 2026 says modern credit structures raise unresolved questions ([ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/measuring-lenders-recovery-assignment-claims-act)). The shutdown shock is real but the second lapse was a partial DHS shutdown.
- **Revenue math.** The idea concedes this is the weakest. $100M of revenue at 12% means about $830M outstanding and about $100M of equity, which makes it a specialty-finance company with credit risk, not a venture-scale product. Median $8M is about $65M outstanding; upside about $45M is about $375M outstanding.

### 5. The Evidence Desk (kill)

- **Competitors and substitutes.** Deloitte, Booz Allen, Accenture Federal, Guidehouse, Grant Thornton and Kearney already do this, and many hire ex-auditors; the independence rule bars the same audit firm from non-audit services on its own audit entity, not individuals who left ([GAGAS independence](https://gaoinnovations.gov/yellowbook/independence.html)). DoD is restructuring Advana to put an Advana for Financial Management team on audit remediation, and the Deputy CFO says the plan relies on AI and automation to hit FY2028 ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/05/05/pentagon-plans-to-use-ai-for-audits/), [DefenseScoop Jan](https://defensescoop.com/2026/01/23/advana-transition-dod-financial-report-stephen-feinberg/)).
- **Revenue math.** The $4.11B FY2018-22 remediation spend is government plus contractor and produced "minimal progress," with more than $75M charged to non-remediation work in FY2022 ([DoD IG](https://www.dodig.mil/In-the-Spotlight/Article/3808856/press-release-audit-of-financial-improvement-and-audit-remediation-contracts-fo/)). The idea's own $1.1-1.3B pool means $100M is 8-12% for a boutique, and the DoD OIG's finding is that the problem is poor contract management, not missing talent. The December 2025 result (eighth disclaimer, 26 material weaknesses) is confirmed ([DoD IG](https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/4365178/independent-auditors-reports-on-the-dod-fy-2025-financial-statements/)).
- **Deciding reason.** Demand is real and time-boxed to FY2027-28, but a services boutique that needs clearances, a firewall and 8-12% of a pool where the customer is building the capability in-house tops out near $40M.

### 6. The cost-guaranteed sub (weak, closest to a pick)

- **Competitors and substitutes.** The primes are doing this themselves. Booz Allen says it wants to accelerate outcome-based procurement "and bring agentic AI to enable these conversions" ([Q2 call](https://www.aol.com/articles/booz-allen-bah-q2-2026-154409501.html)). Leidos says customers are asking to convert work to fixed price and the fixed-price share is rising ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/leidos-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-05/)). Ask Sage was bought for $250M by BigBear.ai ([BigBear.ai](https://bigbear.ai/newsroom/bigbear-ai-finalizes-250m-acquisition-of-ask-sage/)). Palantir partners such as Foxtrot Services report 9.1x growth and 60+ staff ([profile](https://yespress.io/christine-williams.md)). Two entrants the idea cites are not competitors: Kaizen sells state and local software ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/kaizen-21-million-series-a-raised-for-modernizing-americas-public-services/amp/)) and Pryzm is a capture platform ([Tectonic](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/pryzm-raises-12-2m-led-by-a16z/)). So the absence of outcome-priced AI delivery competitors is thin evidence of a gap, not proof.
- **Revenue math.** The idea's own math holds: about $15B addressable sub revenue, $100M is 0.7% or 30 contracts at $3.3M. Pressure is real: Booz Allen Q4 civil revenue fell 23% and FY27 civil is guided down high single digits ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001443646/000162828026037519/bahexhibit991q4fy26_fina.htm)). The 2,500 layoff was May 2025 ([Washington Technology](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2025/05/booz-allen-plans-7-workforce-cut/405564/)). The median case is the issue: a first slice on one task order, then 6-8 slices by year five, is $10-15M.
- **Upside assumptions.** (a) Fixed-price conversions proceed and survive a 2029 administration change: moderate. (b) Primes will subcontract out 30-50% of their own revenue to a startup instead of building: low-moderate; their stated plan is in-house. (c) The startup can guarantee a 25-40% cost-down on regulated work: unproven. (d) 30 contracts at $3.3M with consent-to-subcontract cleared: low in the first five years. A top-decile case reaches $100M, but at a probability I would put near 5%.
- **What would make it a pick.** One signed prime that has published its restructuring plan and agrees to a guaranteed-price slice, plus a second prime inside 12 months. That is a test worth running as a sales experiment before more diligence money.

### 7. The Outcome Catalog (weak)

- **Premise problem found in the rules.** The simplified acquisition threshold did rise to $350,000 and micro-purchase to $15,000 on October 1, 2025 ([SmallGovCon](https://smallgovcon.com/statutes-and-regulations/finalized-increases-to-micro-purchase-simplified-acquisition-and-other-thresholds/)). But FAR 19.502-2(a) automatically reserves acquisitions above the micro-purchase threshold and up to the SAT for small business, with narrow exceptions ([FAR](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/19.502-2)). Orders under multiple-award schedules are only discretionary set-asides ([FAR 8.405-5](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/8.405-5)), so MAS soften this, but the segment the idea targets is built for small firms, and a $100M firm is large in every relevant NAICS code. The idea acknowledges this; it does not solve it.
- **Competitors.** Thousands of small MAS holders. Authorization tooling is deflating the lead SKU: Paramify and RegScale automate packages and OSCAL documentation under FedRAMP 20x ([Carahsoft](https://www.carahsoft.com/paramify), [Knox](https://knoxsystems.com/resources/paramify-alternatives)); Applied Information Sciences and Anitian already sell managed ATO-as-a-service ([Microsoft Marketplace](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/el-gr/product/applied_information_services.atoaas)). Kaizen and Pryzm are not delivery competitors.
- **Revenue math.** 400 orders at $250k is $100M, and $150k average makes the $100M path need about 670 orders, each a separate CO decision in a workforce down by about 30% at GSA. A moderate-impact package at $90-140k is also the item most likely to fall in price as tooling spreads.

### 8. Award Ops (kill)

- **Premise problem.** NHGRI's fall from 11 to 3 grants specialists is one former official's account, and the same account says awards signed by staff came back stopped by HHS, OMB or the White House ([NPR via STLPR](https://www.stlpr.org/npr/2026-09-24/these-6-charts-show-how-nih-research-funding-has-been-reshaped-under-trump)). EO 14332 requires senior-appointee review of funding opportunities and annual review of discretionary grants ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-09-04-new-executive-order-increases-oversight-of-award-and-performance-of-federal-grants)). So automating 20 mechanical checks does not unblock awards, and this administration is cutting grants, not buying capacity. The Uniform Guidance rewrite is still pending ([NACo](https://www.naco.org/resource/omb-proposed-rule-2-cfr-part-200-advocacy-hub)).
- **Competitors.** GrantSolutions is HHS's own shared service ([GrantSolutions](https://int-home.grantsolutions.gov/home)); Guidehouse sells managed grants services ([Guidehouse](https://guidehouse.com/-/media/new-library/industries/state-and-local-government/documents/2026/grants-management-service-sheet-(2025).pdf)); Booz Allen won a $419M NSF merit-review and grants-system contract and says it will use AI ([ExecutiveBiz](https://executivebiz.com/?p=324784)); GSA is already running market research for Fogarty grants support ([HigherGov](https://www.highergov.com/contract-opportunity/hhs-grants-management-specialist-rfi1811724-r-51-re/)). Maximus Federal Consulting obligations fell from about $140M (FY2020) to $55M (FY2025) ([Fed360](https://fed360.agency/contractors/maximus-federal-consulting-llc-RYMXZHTX4WP3)), one subsidiary, but a sign this contractor line is not growing.
- **Revenue math.** The 5,100-employee base is from 2013, covers four agencies and counts everyone "significantly involved," program specialists included. The idea's $3-6B federal labor guess looks high by about 2x, which puts the contractor-addressable slice near $0.3-0.6B and $100M above 15-30% share, which fails the pool's own "over 20% is heroic" rule.

## What this pool taught

The 2026 fixed-price order creates urgency but no new budget lines for tooling, so ideas that bill a toll on the flow cap near Textura's economics. The one structure that scales is selling the labor itself, and that is the part where primes, set-aside rules and trust block a new entrant. Zero picks is the honest result.

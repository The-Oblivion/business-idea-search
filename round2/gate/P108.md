# Gate report: pool P108, US prison and jail health care

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 2-4 searches each, plus primary reads of the Pew 2018 hospital-care report (PDF, text extracted), the Texas Tribune and Prison Legal News coverage of Ward v. Muth, the BH Business report on Wellpath Recovery Solutions, and the DHCS/CHCF reentry material. Result: 3 kill, 4 weak, 1 strong. One pick (Day 21).

## Headline findings

1. **The shock produced "replace the vendor", not "invent a new structure".** In every 2026 failure the buyer swapped one full-service vendor for another: Alabama cancelled YesCare (about $1.06B contract) and moved to NaphCare in days (https://alabamareflector.com/2026/04/24/alabama-department-of-corrections-abruptly-cancels-1-billion-health-care-contract/), Maryland gave Centurion $1.7B (https://marylandmatters.org/2024/06/05/board-awards-health-care-contract-over-objection-of-losing-bidder/), Polk FL moved to CFG. No state split off-site care, insurance or staffing out of the vendor contract. That is bad news for the three ideas that need a buyer to restructure (Outside Care Plan, Custody Mutual, Piggyback Prime).
2. **Gross pass-through revenue keeps hiding small net revenue.** Piggyback Prime ($100M gross is $8-12M net by its own math), Open-Book (6-10% fee), Cure Carve-Out (6-10% of drug spend) and Custody Mutual (an MGA keeps about 15-20% of premium) all need 5-10x the headline number to reach $100M of money the company keeps.
3. **Pew's primary text shrinks the off-site pool and shows the structure already exists.** 17 states deliver on-site care directly and 4 use a state-university model, so those states already pay off-site claims from their own line; 5 states carve out inpatient care; 8 hold vendors to an all-inclusive capitation; 6 share risk. In Medicaid-expansion states most inpatient stays now go to Medicaid, and Medicaid fee schedules are the lowest rates in a state. Mississippi pays 200% of Medicaid; Utah and North Carolina law pay lower rates to non-contract hospitals. Source: https://www.pew.org/-/media/assets/2018/07/prisons-and-hospital-care_report.pdf (text read from the PDF).
4. **The one idea with a court mandate, an existing budget line and physical capacity as its edge is Day 21.** Ward v. Muth (W.D. Tex., Judge Ezra, permanent injunction 2026-07-07) orders Texas HHSC to admit committed defendants within 21 days, with four years to comply. HHSC already buys about 747 private beds a day, and told lawmakers it still will not have enough beds by the end of 2027. The state has appealed to the Fifth Circuit, and the earlier 2012 21-day order was paused, so appeal risk is the main thing that can break it.

## Per-idea notes

### 1. Custody Mutual: weak
- **Competitors and substitutes.** Jail liability is a mature niche served by county risk pools (Oklahoma's ACCO pools were created because counties could not buy cover elsewhere), E&S wholesalers (https://completemarkets.com/Jail-Insurance/Storefronts/), Correctional Risk Services programs run through state county associations (https://www.granitecountymt.gov/DocumentCenter/View/498/2025-2026-CRS-Renewal-Agreement), Maine's statutory county jail-medical pool (https://legislature.maine.gov/ros/LOM/LOM120th/2Pub51-100/Pub51-100-51.htm), and self-insurance. No funded jail-liability insurtech found.
- **Revenue math.** The idea's loss pool uses a 30-40% claim rate and $2-3M all-in per claim. Observed settlements are lower and selection-biased: Cuyahoga paid $1.35M across four suits, San Bernardino $3.23M across four; the $9-20M cases are the news. BJS's last national count is 1,200 local jail deaths in 2019 (https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/mortality-local-jails-2000-2019-statistical-tables). At about $0.75M per claim the loss pool is nearer $0.5B, premium-equivalent about $0.7B, insurable share about $0.3-0.4B. Real county premiums are $0.35M (Rockingham NC, jail plus law enforcement) to $0.7M (Camden GA) for the whole package. $100M GWP would be 25-30% of the insurable pool, and as an MGA the company keeps roughly 15-20% of it. Booking $100M of revenue means becoming the risk-bearing carrier, which needs about $60-70M of surplus and long-tail reserving.
- **Legal premise.** PSQIA protects only patient safety work product reported to a PSO, never original records; in Crawford v. Corizon (a jail death) the court ordered most documents produced because the vendor could not show they were assembled for a PSO. Whether sharing with a liability insurer waives it was not found. Deliberate indifference turns on what officials knew, so a dashboard that shows notice creates the exhibit it claims to prevent. Punitive damages against a county are barred under Newport v. Fact Concerts (from background knowledge, not re-verified), so that gate is narrower than the idea says.
- **Upside assumptions.** (1) A vendor hands over EHR timestamps despite discovery risk: low plausibility. (2) Indicators separate losses in 50 closed claims: unknown, cheap to test. (3) A fronting carrier and reinsurer back a first-of-kind program: plausible. (4) 20%+ share of insurable jail liability in 6 years: low.

### 2. Outside Care Plan: weak (closest runner-up)
- **Competitors and substitutes.** Prime Health Services (savings-share repricing, no risk; Flagler County saved $1.05M in a year, North Carolina sheriffs' plan since 2011), Heritage Health Care Solutions (TPA sole-sourced by Polk County, 2026), CorrectCare Integrated Health, Centene's Centurion (Medicaid-plan parent with provider contracts in many states), NaphCare, state university models, and California's own network and prior-authorization procurement (CDCR/CCHCS SD25-00019, five-year term, https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16772111). The idea's own number, $88M cumulative documented savings for Prime, means the pure repricing fee pool is tiny.
- **Premise checks.** Off-site 20-27% of prison health budgets: confirmed (Pew: Virginia 27%, New York 23% in 2015; about 20% across ten states 2007-11). Georgia: more than 750 medical and EMS providers claim about $75.6M in the Wellpath case (AJC via JEMS). But the Pew text shows the vendor-gatekeeper conflict applies only in the 8 all-inclusive and 6 risk-share states, and the expansion shift of inpatient costs to Medicaid reduces the manageable pool. My haircut: $2.2-3.0B headline becomes about $1.4-2.0B reachable.
- **Revenue math.** As a risk-bearer, revenue is premium, so $100M is about 5-7% of the reduced pool: one large state or 2-3 mid-size. Margin is 5-8%, so that is $5-8M of gross profit. Needs insurer or health-plan licensure (not verified per state) and stop-loss.
- **Upside assumptions.** (1) 2-3 states restructure a vendor contract to carve out hospital payments to a third party: no 2026 precedent, low plausibility. (2) Hospitals accept Medicare-plus for 15-day pay: plausible. (3) Licensing resolved: unknown. (4) Stop-loss priced at a workable margin: plausible.

### 3. Clinical Corps: weak
- **Competitors.** AB Staffing Solutions (corrections-specific since 2002, serves ICE, USMS, federal, state and county), 21 competitively bid nurse vendors in North Carolina, California's statewide Mental Health Registry Network RFP SD25-00031 (one contractor, 3-year firm-fixed price from 2027-01-01, listed due 2026-09-09; aggregator source, unverified; incumbent probably Management Solutions, unconfirmed), the Coleman telepsychiatry policy built around CDCR-run hubs.
- **Revenue math.** The 2.5x contract-versus-state hourly gap ($64 vs $25.40) is from a 2023 DAC table; NC Health News (July 2026) says contract nurses cost about 30% more, so the pilot saving ($54 vs $64) is probably overstated. The idea's $0.9-1.3B agency-labor pool (8-12% of $11B) is not sourced. $100M is 8-11% of that.
- **Upside assumptions.** Multi-state clearances stay portable (low), states let pay exceed civil-service scales via contractors (plausible), and the pool keeps a margin once the idea's own "6-12 month copy" arrives (low).

### 4. Piggyback Prime: kill
- No cooperative contract for jail medical services found: NASPO has correctional supplies (Bob Barker, South Carolina lead) and now NaphCare's TechCare, which is an EHR only. Absence reads as buyer liability aversion and operators that are rivals, not as an open gap.
- A prime that holds the master agreement carries the same liability and thin balance sheet that sank Wellpath and YesCare. $8-12M net on $100M gross is the idea's own admission; $100M net would need about 20% of the pool.

### 5. Day 91: weak
- **Competitors.** In California the handoff is already done by FQHCs such as Peach Tree Health with Partnership HealthPlan, community health workers, county systems, CBOs funded by PATH ($1.85B over five years), plus custody vendors' reentry offerings. Only startup found: Total Reentry Solutions (pre-seed, about $75k, aggregator). Cityblock is the risk-bearing analogue but no reentry contract was found.
- **Premise checks.** CMS approved Louisiana, Minnesota and Nevada on 2026-09-23 (22 states plus DC, terms through 2031) and I found no rescission. Michigan withdrew for lack of appropriation, so the payer can walk. California year one: more than 159,000 billable services and more than 24,000 people enrolled in Medi-Cal pre-release (DHCS via CHCF). The idea's "42,000 eligible" and "$16M reimbursed" did not verify. Pre-release bundles pay per claim (about $256 health risk assessment, $199 care plan, $167 warm handoff), so the pre-release pool is small and the business lives in post-release care management, whose PMPM I could not find.
- **Revenue math.** The idea's 500,000 high-need releases overstates the reachable cohort: jail stays under 90 days cannot use a 90-day benefit. Reachable looks like 100-200k a year, so 33,000 members is 17-33% share (my estimate).
- **Upside assumptions.** Plans sub-capitate 12-month total cost of care to a specialist (not yet seen), 4-5 states stay funded through 2031 (plausible), and outcomes show ED and overdose reduction large enough to pay (unproven).

### 6. Cure Carve-Out: kill
- A 2019-2023 survey puts 15.2% of state prisoners antibody-positive and 8.7% viremic, about 91,090 people (not the idea's 100-170k). Louisiana's pre-deal prison price was about $10k a patient; its subscription model capped spend and drove marginal cost to near zero. H.R. 9682 (introduced 2026-07-14, committee stage) would create a federal subscription with correctional opt-in. So the pool is about $0.9B once, mostly drug pass-through, with a 6-10% operator fee, and a mandate pre-empts the operator.

### 7. Day 21: strong (see picks)

### 8. Open-Book Health Authority: kill
- Wellpath already runs a management-fee MSO (Oswego County NY: $500k fee on about $3.07M budget). Allegheny and Barnstable show in-house is a county choice, and Alameda is floating its own public health system. 6-10% of $10-19M a county is $0.6-1.9M; $100M would need 50+ counties.

## Picks

### Pick 1: Day 21 (strong, with thin moat)

**Sharpened pitch.** A multi-state operator of full-service secure competency-restoration beds, sold on 5-year availability-based contracts to state forensic agencies, delivered in leased and modular licensed units next to existing campuses, with an owned staffing pool and an outpatient restoration track for low-risk defendants. Do not sell treatment-only jail-hosted slots as the core: Liberty's San Bernardino contract works out to about $170 per bed-day ($17.98M over three years for 96 beds, county supplies the bed) and Harris County's program about $261, versus $412-$800 for full-service beds (Wellpath RS revenue per patient is about $388 a day). $100M needs about 600 full-service beds at $450.

**Path.** Texas: the wait list was 1,626 in June 2026, HHSC adds 175 net new beds by 2027, and says it will still be short; I estimate the gap at 1,000+ beds, worth $160M+ a year at $450. Texas spent more than $2.5B for 680 state-owned beds (about $3.7M per bed, my arithmetic) against a contracted bed at about $164k a year, which is the state's reason to contract. Add Washington (350-bed hospital not due until 2028, about $100M fine tranche in 2026), Oklahoma ($928,400 fined in the first month), Colorado (decree through 2027-12-01).

**Biggest risk.** The Fifth Circuit. Texas has asked it to pause benchmark-setting and argues the Constitution never required the remedy; a 2012 21-day order was paused. Second: incumbents with beds (Wellpath RS, Liberty, GEO, UHS, Acadia).

**Cheapest next test.** Public Information Act request to HHSC for every private inpatient competency-restoration contract (vendor, per diem, beds, term, occupancy) plus the SB 528 cost-comparison report; read the benchmark order and any Fifth Circuit stay ruling; get three modular-unit quotes for 60 beds.

### No second pick
Outside Care Plan clears the numeric bar (median about $25M, upside about $150M) but its upside needs states to split a vendor contract, which no state did in the 2026 shock. Day 91 fails the median bar. Reopen Outside Care Plan only if a public-records pull from three states shows off-site spend well above Medicaid-rate equivalents and a DOC finance director will take a shadow bid.

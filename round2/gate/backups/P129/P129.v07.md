# Gate report: pool P129 (offshore IT-services vendor economics, AI-era repricing)

Gated 2026-10-18. About 50 searches and fetches; evidence log with every URL is in `P129.notes.working.md` (versioned copies in `backups/P129/`). Stance: assume someone already does it, assume the pool is smaller than claimed.

## Bottom line

- 1 strong, 4 weak, 3 kill. One pick: **Run-Base Shrinker** (ESCO-for-the-application-estate), sharpened. Everything else is either already sold by advisers/vendors, rests on a premise that checks out only partly, or tops out under $100M even if it goes right.
- Cross-cutting findings that hit several ideas:
  1. "Advisers will not sell continuous governance" is false. ISG (GovernX), Avasant (AvaSense) and ServiceNow already sell invoice validation, service credits and earn-back tracking for IT-services contracts.
  2. "25% of contracts renegotiated each year" is a Cognizant CFO forecast (Nomura call, Sept 2025), not a measured rate. The past rate was 15-20%.
  3. Incumbents are buying the AI tools directly and getting embedded vendor engineers: Cognizant-Cognition (Jan 28 2026), Wipro-Factory (Jan 28), Infosys-Cursor (Jan 27), Infosys-Cognition (Jan 7), Hexaware-Factory (Jul 2026). The Foundry's "no evidence" claim is wrong.
  4. Sponsor-channel AI services now has two giant-capital players: Ode (Anthropic/Blackstone/H&F, $1.5B) and OpenAI's Deployment Company (TPG/Advent/Bain/Brookfield, about $4B).
  5. Observed price of governance: ISG's record $17M deal covers $300M of spend across 200 vendors for up to 8 years, about $2M a year or roughly 0.7% of spend.

## Per-idea evidence

### 1. The Meter (weak)
- Competitors that already do the core jobs: Avasant "validate and reconcile supplier invoices... service credits, charge backs, and earnbacks" with a client case study of up to 25% savings; ISG GovernX (invoice review for duplicate charges and missed credits); ServiceNow Vendor Management Workspace (SLA KPIs, automated service credits, per [SoftwareOne listing](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/vendor-management-workspace/PCP-0739-5645)); BlueOptima (coding effort by outsourcing supplier); CAST (two decades of vendor-output measurement; [Forrester 2009](https://www.forrester.com/report/function-points-and-application-outsourcing/RES54263) says function points add little for maintenance work).
- Numbers: ISG's $17M deal is "up to", multiyear, up to 8 years, $300M spend, 200 vendors. The idea's blended take (20% of 4% savings plus $200k on $47M) is about 1.2% of spend, above that benchmark. The "25% a year renegotiated" figure is a [forecast](https://nearshoreamericas.com/?p=106554); [HFS](https://www.hfsresearch.com/news/ai-drives-it-contract-renegotiations-within-24-months-as-pricing-models-shift/) confirms renegotiation inside 24 months, with $50M+ ACV contracts under the most pressure.
- Premise checks: [Section 174A](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/new-section-174a-restores-domestic-r-and-e-deductibility-but-other-changes-bring-mixed-results) still amortizes foreign R&E over 15 years (verified), but application maintenance is often not R&E, so the VP-Tax pull is thin. The H-1B $100K fee was [vacated June 8 2026](https://www.globalimmigrationblog.com/2026/06/federal-court-vacates-100000-h-1b-fee/) yet a Sept 18 proclamation extended it to Sept 2027 and litigation continues, so "vacated" is contested.
- Why not kill: the specific join (client-owned identity/ticket/commit/gateway logs against vendor invoices) has no direct startup found, and ISG says neither side can see how [AI savings are achieved](https://isg-one.com/articles/index-insider---1h26-isg-index-recap). Best used as the measurement layer under idea 5 rather than as a standalone company.

### 2. Penny-Exact (weak)
- Shock verified: IBM -13.1% to $223.39 on Feb 23 2026, worst day since Oct 2000.
- The "translator grades its own homework" premise is half right. AWS Transform for mainframe (Dec 2025) ships test-plan generation, data-collection scripts, automated execution and functional-equivalence checks; AWS says testing is over 50% of project duration. Mechanical Orchard's Summer 2026 release integrates AWS Transform for verification on production data flows and adds a Leidos federal partnership. Cognition claims automated validation. Hypercubic ([$5.3M seed](https://pulse2.com/hypercubic-raises-5-3-million-seed-to-turn-mainframe-modernization-into-a-software-driven-process/)) and Kodesage ([$6.6M seed](https://tech.eu/2026/06/04/kodesage-raises-66m-for-ai-powered-legacy-software-modernisation/)) address understanding and translation.
- Substitute: IV&V is an established services category ([Maximus for Ohio UI](https://maximus.com/news/ohio-ui-system-modernization-oversight), BerryDunn for Vermont). Parallel run is standard practice.
- Legal: no current OCC/FFIEC rule found requiring independent equivalence certification (the idea says the same). Demand is for defensible evidence only.
- Math: market-size estimates for mainframe modernization run $8.4B (2025, MarketsandMarkets) to $20.9B (2026, TBRC); no source counts programs a year. The $60M "other migrations" leg (150 programs at $400k) is unsourced. Median needs 25-35 programs a year at $0.4-0.6M; upside needs a recognized-standard outcome.

### 3. The Middle (weak)
- New competitors the idea missed: Ode ($1.5B committed, acquired Fractional AI May 21 2026, 100 engineers by July) and OpenAI's Deployment Company ($4B, TPG-led, uses 2,000+ investor portfolio companies as channel, buying Tomoro). Both sell AI deployment, not offshore maintenance, but they own the sponsor relationship with 100x the capital.
- Prior art: [AlixPartners](https://www.alixpartners.com/insights/102hq3j/pes-savings-opportunity-in-shared-services/) (GPs warming to low-touch shared services), [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/consulting/global-capability-centers/how-shared-service-platforms-add-value-to-private-equity-firms) (vendor-claimed 50-60% labor-arbitrage savings), PE-focused MSPs (NexusTek, Resultant, Connext, Aeries GCC for PE).
- Count: the 82,927 figure could not be verified; the 8,000-company band is an unsourced estimate. $100M needs about 83 companies, e.g. six sponsors mandating a vendor across 14 companies each. Sponsors usually recommend rather than mandate.

### 4. Clawback (kill)
- Vaudit: AI-native, SOC 2 Type II, claims $2B+ audited and $100M+ recovered, 3-10% recoverable. At a 25% fee that is about $25M of lifetime fees. Clawback needs $400M of recoveries every year for $100M.
- PRGX contract compliance exists; the claimed IT-contract-labor case study was not found (an [APEX paper](https://go.apexanalytix.com/hubfs/apex%20Legacy%20Website%20Files/APEX_White_Paper_Contract_Comp_Gig_2020.pdf) reports a $2M+ contract-labor recovery). The idea's math fails at 1% leakage (needs 40% share) and the 3% leakage rate has no independent study. The claims-financing leg earns a spread, not a platform.

### 5. Run-Base Shrinker (strong, picked)
- What checks out: the ESCO figure ([LBNL: $10.7B in 2024](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/energy-services-companies-are-becoming-a-15b-industry/831664/)); AI-native maintenance startups are tiny (AppFactor [$4M seed](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260203620489/en/AppFactor-raises-%244M-seed-to-deliver-an-agentic-orchestration-platform-for-enterprise-software-maintenance), LogicStar $3M pre-seed, DataAgent $10M pre-seed); a roughly 30% savings share is the reported norm for IT savings products ([ProsperOps charges a percent of savings](https://prosperops.com/pricing), with third-party guides estimating 30-35%; a [vendor article](https://techbullion.com/the-cfos-guide-to-outcome-based-it-consulting-why-shared-savings-is-replacing-retainer-models/) cites about 30% for IT asset/licensing work). These are unverified third-party figures, not audited benchmarks.
- What does not: the ESCO analogy is weaker than stated. K-12 and public agencies are the largest segment ($5B) and about 70% of commercial/industrial ESCO work is design-build, not performance contracting. "Nobody removes repeat incidents" is false: Rimini Street ($421.5M 2025 revenue, 3,102 clients, flat) sells root-cause "Managed Services Optimization" with an unlimited-ticket model. Self-funded transformation is a live vendor pitch ([HFS/UST](https://www.hfsresearch.com/?p=197112), Deloitte, SOVRA). SAP ECC mainstream maintenance ends Dec 31 2027 but paid [extended maintenance runs to 2030](https://www.crowe.com/id/insights/sap-ecc-support-ends-in-2027-what-leaders-should-do), so the deadline is softer than "cliff".
- Math: $30M run base x 12% x 35% = $1.26M per client-year, 79 clients; pool view $40B x 12% x 35% = $1.68B, so $100M is 6%. Nasscom puts [India IT services at ~$149B](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/india-fy26-tech-revenues-hit-usd-315-billion-enn26022404879), so a $40B US run-related offshore pool is the right order of magnitude (still an estimate).

### 6. Evergreen Release Utility (kill)
- Guidewire FY26: revenue $1,475.4M, ARR $1,242M, so about $2.2M average ARR across 570+ insurers (Q1 FY26 count). Release-driven remediation of $1.5-3M a year per carrier before discount is not credible against that; $200-600k is more defensible, putting the Guidewire leg near $24M, not $60M. Cadence is three releases a year since [April 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230403005365/en), which settles the idea's "sources conflict" note.
- Crowded: Cognizant factory-model CoE, Hexaware Tensai (claims 55% effort savings), TestingXperts, Aspire iAFTA, SBI AITest on the Guidewire Marketplace, Appvance, Tricentis Tosca; Tessera claims SAP, Salesforce, Workday and Oracle. Guidewire's Qusar (Aug 2026) adds an agentic framework and developer assistants. The only real moat (platform co-sell) is controlled by the platform vendor.

### 7. The Foundry (weak)
- Direct evidence against the premise (see cross-cutting item 3). Cognition also embeds its own "Partner Deployed Engineers" at integrators to help them roll Devin and Windsurf out. Persistent, Coforge, Mphasis and Zensar tool deals were not found, but they can sign one at any time. Persistent's CEO reports clients want 25-30% less and says rivals guarantee 70-80% productivity gains at locked prices ([column](https://www.outsourceaccelerator.com/articles/25-percent-off/)).
- Math: one $1.5B anchor routing $75-105M of delivery reaches the target but is one point of failure, and the vendor keeps the spread while the Foundry carries the warranty. Subcontracting is already 4-12.6% of revenue at the big four (idea's figures, not re-verified).

### 8. Hot Spare (kill)
- DORA Art. 28(8) verified: exit plans must be "comprehensive, documented and sufficiently tested and reviewed periodically" with alternative providers or in-house reincorporation. Nothing requires a shadow-run; practitioner templates accept tabletop and partial extraction tests. So the compliance floor costs tens of thousands, not $2M a year.
- Substitutes: dual sourcing is common (12 of 21 Swiss cantonal banks), parallel-track negotiations (Scotiabank), and AI-assisted insourcing (Citi ties a 30% year-on-year drop in tech vendor spend to it). Revenue "as principal on gross billings" is mostly pass-through to the shadow vendor.

## Picks

### Pick 1: Run-Base Shrinker, sharpened (verdict strong; median $20M, upside $110M)

**Sharpened pitch.** An independent, vendor-neutral "pay from audited savings" company for the application run estate. We remove the run cost your offshore vendor is paid to maintain, in this order of who controls the savings:
1. **Client-controlled lines first**: zombie-application retirement (licences, infrastructure, third-party support, vendor seats), dead custom code removal before conversion, and cloud/licence right-sizing. These cut real invoices without waiting for the AMS vendor to agree.
2. **Vendor-labor lines second**: repeat-incident elimination (AI root cause, patch, regression, deploy), sold only on unit-priced or soon-to-renew contracts, or with a co-negotiated baseline-reset clause.

We fund the engineering and take 30-35% of audited run-cost reduction for 36 months (inside the 30-35% range seen for IT savings share; pushing above that is a negotiated premium for engineering risk).

What the gate changes, without shrinking the pool:
- **Baseline = contract-guaranteed AI pass-through, not today's bill.** We earn only on reductions beyond what the vendor contract already owes. This answers the "you are taking credit for AI deflation" objection and what Internal Audit will ask first.
- **Publish an IT run-cost measurement-and-verification protocol** (the IPMVP analogue the ESCO industry runs on, where M&V is done on about 80% of projects), co-signed by a Big 4 audit partner. Telemetry from the Meter idea (identity, ITSM, commit logs) is the evidence layer, so it becomes part of this company rather than a separate one.
- **Sell through sponsors and boards under cost-take-out mandates** (PE operating partners, CFOs with no transformation budget). This borrows idea 3's channel without its low-moat delivery business.
- **Finance the 36-month receivable tail** with a specialty lender (ESCO-style project finance). That is the balance-sheet layer the lessons ask for.

Path: 79 clients at $1.26M (or about 110 at $0.9M), reachable in 6-7 years at roughly 12-15 new clients a year after year 2.

**Payer and signer.** CIO (run budget) and CFO sign; Internal Audit signs the savings method; board/PE operating partner is the forcing function.

**Competitors and edge.** Rimini Street (consulting-led, not paid from savings), AMS vendors' self-funded transformation offers (conflicted: they lose volume), ISG/Avasant (invoice governance, no engineering), app-retirement software (OpenText InfoArchive, Solix), and AI-native maintenance startups (sell cheaper replacement, not smaller volume). Edge is independence plus payment only from audited net reduction in the client's ledger.

**Why now.** ISG says providers are pricing deals on [assumed AI savings](https://isg-one.com/articles/index-insider---1h26-isg-index-recap) that nobody can verify; clients want 25-30% cuts; the Cognizant CFO expects 25% of contracts renegotiated a year; engineering for fixes and deletion just got cheap enough to fund on spec; SAP ECC mainstream maintenance ends Dec 2027.

**Moat.** Counter-positioning (the vendor cannot copy it without cannibalizing its revenue), a cross-client root-cause and retirement library, an auditor-accepted protocol (process power), a 36-month contract tail, and receivable financing.

**Biggest open risk.** Private-sector CFOs may not sign third-party shared savings (the ESCO analogue is mostly public-sector) and baseline disputes or vendor obstruction under FTE/volume-band contracts can erase measured savings.

**Cheapest next test (30-60 days, near-zero cost).** Take 3 historical ticket/CMDB exports from unit-priced or soon-to-renew contracts under NDA and produce a repeat-ticket heatmap plus zombie-app list with dollar values. Kill criteria: under 6% addressable reduction in the first three accounts, or Internal Audit will not accept the method memo, or CFOs refuse any share above 20%.

- **The Meter**: folded into the Shrinker as its evidence layer.

# Gate report: pool P136, private-school-choice money (state ESAs, vouchers, tax-credit scholarships, federal credit from 2027)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas, about 25 searches and fetches, plus a full read of the Treasury/IRS proposed regulation (REG-117199-25, Federal Register 2026-20277, extracted to text and grepped) and the Arizona Department of Revenue FY2023 STO credit report (PDF read). Result: 4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. 0 picks.

## Headline findings

- **The pool is real but the vendor revenue in it is thin.** Texas law caps the TEFA administrator's pay at 5% of the initial $1B (about $50M). Odyssey won at up to about $52M over four years, roughly $13M a year, or about 1.3% of flows; the losing bids were ClassWallet $79M and Student First Technologies $93M (https://news4sanantonio.com/news/local/texans-could-begin-applying-for-school-vouchers-in-february ; figures come from search-result summaries of Texas news coverage, pages not fetched, contract itself not seen). ClassWallet has moved $7.5B+ cumulatively across 38 states and is "profitable", but third-party revenue estimates are $10-50M (https://classwallet.com/fstc ; https://dealroom.co/companies/classwallet/). So a platform taking 1-3% of flows needs 20-33% of the whole sector's vendor revenue ($300-500M, the pool's own figure) to reach $100M. That is heroic share on the lessons.md rule.
- **Every idea's central case of "$5B a year of federal-credit giving" is a mature-state ceiling, not a median.** Arizona's individual STO giving (original plus switcher credit) was $123M in FY2021, $130M in FY2022 and $116M in FY2023 (https://www.azdor.gov/sites/default/files/document/REPORTS_CREDITS_2024_fy2023-private-school-tuition-org-credit-report.pdf). Arizona is about 2.2% of the US population, so Arizona-level engagement scales to about $5.3-5.9B nationally, after 28 years of habit. The FY22 to FY23 fall was 10.8%, not the 23% the ideas cite (I could not reproduce 23%). Treasury's own $26B a year by 2030 rests on a 10% take-up assumption, about three times Arizona's rate (https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20277.pdf , footnote 5). Realistic first years are $1-2B.
- **The market's incumbents are bigger and closer than three of the ideas assumed.** ACE Scholarships (25+ years, 4-star Charity Navigator) already sells "EmpowerEd", a co-branded white-label SGO platform to partner schools, tax and financial advisors, faith organisations, associations and other SGOs, with ACE doing donations, awards, compliance and a partner dashboard (https://www.acescholarships.org/eftc/services/). FACTS (Nelnet, nearly 12,000 K-12 schools; Nelnet's education technology and payments segment ran about $147M a quarter in early 2025) launched FACTS Compass on 15 Oct 2026 with 120+ SGOs and five state programs, and ACSI's Children's Tuition Fund picked it in July (https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/nelnet-facts-introduces-facts-compass-a-scholarship-administration-platform-for-the-federal-scholarship-tax-credit-20261015). ClassWallet has a dedicated federal-credit page, a multi-program wallet and a Missouri private-scholarship wallet. Odyssey raised a $74M Series B (a16z and Tusk named) and runs 13 programs in 10 states.
- **The regulatory premise behind the wallet and clearinghouse ideas is true, and it cuts both ways.** The proposed rule defines a "qualified digital wallet" (proposed 1.25F-1(a)(13)), says an SGO that does not retain ownership of funds transferred to one is treated as having spent them at transfer for the 90% test, and says an SGO using a wallet is deemed to meet the expense-verification requirement (proposed 1.25F-3(c)(5)(v)). That is a real shock. But the same safe harbor bundles the duplicate-award control inside the wallet, and earnings credited to an SGO's segregated account are part of income that must be spent. Comments close 1 Dec; hearing 15 Dec 2026.
- **Treasury's own numbers:** 30 states opted in (Aug 2026); 600-700 SGOs by 2030; 11M+ donors and $26B a year by 2030 at 10% take-up; 11 SGOs operated in several states in 2026; $100 per household for income verification at 52% take-up; funds 2M full ($12,000) or 5M part-time ($4,500) scholarships. No aggregate national credit cap appears in the proposed rule text (one search snippet claimed $5B; I could not confirm it and the ideas' $5B is their own assumption).

## Per-idea detail

### 1. National ESA provider network (kill)

- Competitors found: ClassWallet marketplace and vendor pay (AZ, AL, AR, NC, NH, SC, IN); Odyssey TEFA Marketplace (TX, UT, IA, LA, WY, GA, MO), nearly 2,400 vendors and 54,000 products at launch; Student First Technologies (WV, TN); Step Up For Students MyScholarShop (FL). Outschool and Varsity Tutors sit inside platform marketplaces. No national credential or directory found (https://edubracket.com/articles/how-to-become-an-esa-approved-vendor-2026).
- Substitutes: the platforms' own onboarding, which states mandate by statute. A neutral credential cannot replace a statutory vendor list, and states can accept or ignore it.
- Revenue check: "$100M" is 40,000 providers paying $2,500 each, which is a 5% take on $50k of billings per provider while the state platforms already pay in 2-10 days and Texas charges vendors nothing. Honest model: 40,000 x $60 a month ($29M) + fast-pay premium on a quarter of $2B billed ($7.5M) + credential checks ($2M) = about $40M ceiling.
- Upside needs: states accept the credential (low-to-moderate plausibility, no precedent found; the closest is the FSMB Uniform Application for physicians, which works because boards agreed), and a large share of the 40,000 to subscribe (moderate).
- Premise: the 7-30 day vendor payment claim from EdChoice is older than the 2-5 and 2-10 day figures on current state pages.

### 2. School payments and capital OS (weak)

- Competitors: FACTS/Nelnet (tuition payment plans about $36M a quarter, payment processing about $37.5M a quarter, from Nelnet's Q2 2025 supplement; nearly 12,000 schools), Building Hope Lending Lab (ESA bridge loans, lease guarantees, schools up to 100 students, terms undisclosed), Charter School Capital (receivable sales for charters). I found no startup advancing cash against ESA payments.
- Premise errors in the idea: (a) Texas private-school awards pay 25% on 1 Jul, 25% on 1 Oct and 50% on 1 Feb, so half the money is in before October and the real gap is Oct-Feb; (b) ESA money sits in a family-controlled account, so the school has no assignable state receivable. A lender underwrites enrollment and payer behaviour, as the idea says, but the "scheduled payments" collateral is weaker than described.
- Revenue math: $70M from advances assumes a 14% yield on $500M average outstanding. State-backed, front-loaded payments would more plausibly price at 7-9%, which makes it $35-45M on $500M of capital that a startup would have to raise. The $114M total is then about $80M, and needs 6,000 schools and 30% of choice-funded tuition volume.
- Upside needs: payment-direction rights from one wallet partner (the platforms are the gatekeepers and sell to states), a $300M+ warehouse line, and FACTS not copying (it has the schools and balance-sheet parent).

### 3. K-12 education wallet and custody (weak)

- Premise confirmed in the primary source: proposed 1.25F-1(a)(13) defines a qualified digital wallet; the preamble treats funds transferred to a wallet as spent at transfer if the SGO retains no ownership; proposed 1.25F-3(c)(5)(v) deems wallet use to satisfy the verification requirement. Comments are invited on the definition and on further safe harbors.
- Competitors: ClassWallet (fstc page, multi-program wallet, $7.5B+ distributed, 38 states, SOC 1 and 2 Type II, says it is not an SGO), FACTS Compass, ACE EmpowerEd (wallets, donor management), Odyssey (ACH after proof of delivery, marketplace). None publishes a yield or custody policy for SGOs, so the treasury-first angle is open.
- HealthEquity analog checks out: custodial revenue $636.8M on $18.0B HSA cash (3.53% average yield), 17.8M accounts, about $36 custodial revenue per account. But HealthEquity holds its members' cash under a custodial agreement; SGO earnings must be spent on scholarships, and Idaho made Odyssey return $478,656 of interest earned on a $30M state account (https://www.idahoednews.org/top-news/analysis-how-idahos-troubled-empowering-parents-vendor-expanded-its-national-brand/). Yield retention of 60% (the idea's number) is the weakest input. At 20% retention each yield dollar shrinks threefold.
- My sizing: median flow $3B at 2031, 20% share, float 58% of flow, 3.5% yield x 50% kept, 0.75% servicing, 1.7% interchange on 25% = about $13M. Upside: Treasury-scale $26B, 30% share ($7.8B), float $4.5B at 3.5% x 20-50% kept ($32-79M), servicing 0.5% ($39M), interchange ($33M) = $104-151M. The "believable" upside, at $11B flow and 30% share, is about $55-85M.
- Upside needs: flows three times Arizona's per-capita rate (low-to-moderate), 30% of a market with four funded or incumbent rivals (low-to-moderate), a final rule that keeps the transfer-equals-spent treatment (moderate; comments close 1 Dec), and yield pass-through terms SGO boards accept (moderate to low).
- Why not a pick: median fails $20M, and the $100M case needs two heroic conditions at once.

### 4. SGO host (kill)

- Competitor found, and it is the idea: ACE Scholarships' EmpowerEd is "co-branded" and "white-label", for partner schools, tax and financial advisors, faith organisations, professional associations and other SGOs, "zero out-of-pocket" for partners, with onshore support, SOC 2, a dashboard and a stated 3% partner share (ACE marketing; I could not verify the share from a second source). The idea's "I found no white-label host" is false.
- Also: ACSI Children's Tuition Fund (a national Christian-school association SGO) chose FACTS Compass in July 2026; Kaleidoscope launched a new public-charity scholarship fund in Jan 2026 aiming at "up to $1 billion".
- Legal premise: the proposed rule requires a separate segregated account per covered state for multistate SGOs, 85% of activities as scholarship granting, donor designation by state, and no earmarking for a particular student. Branded funds are possible, but a host's service company at 8% of gifts leaves little after paying affiliates, audits, wallet and verification inside a 10% overhead.
- Revenue math: $100M at 8% needs $1.25B hosted, 25% of the $5B central case against a 25-year incumbent that gives the same thing away.

### 5. Eligibility and anti-duplication clearinghouse (kill)

- The rule does require systems to prevent and detect duplicate awards to the same student for the same expense, and states must have policies to detect duplication. But proposed 1.25F-3(c)(5)(v) says wallet use meets the requirement, so duplicate control is bundled into the wallets SGOs will already use. Odyssey and ClassWallet detect duplicates inside their own programs; the cross-platform gap is real but small, because most SGO awards are paid directly to a school that knows the family's other aid.
- Recipient universe: Treasury's $26B scale funds 2M full scholarships or 5M part-time; at the $5B central case it is about 0.4-0.6M. At $12 each plus 2.5M credentials at $10 plus 30 states at $1M, the idea's own total is $92M and needs nearly every recipient. States have no budget line: Arizona's Treasurer says fraud monitoring is not her office's job and is buying analytics inside the platform contract (RFI 27 May 2026, upgrade with the incumbent 22 June).
- Analog: National Student Clearinghouse revenue $102.5M in FY2024 over 30 years, with near-universal college reporting. K-12 has no equivalent mandate. FERPA/consent and platform rivalry are the real barriers; a nonprofit standard would need funding from the platforms it audits.

### 6. Family funding stack and net-price front door (kill)

- The idea itself says to treat it as a funnel that feeds 3 and 5. FACTS Grant & Aid already lets a family apply for aid at several FACTS schools with one application and one fee, and FACTS Compass is an SGO application and eligibility platform. ACE collects applications for the SGO it runs. Universal-application tooling exists for community foundations (Foundant, others).
- Payer check: an SGO pays about $100 per awarded student only if applicant supply binds and the SGO cannot get applicants from its schools and parishes; it can. Schools paying $300 per enrolled student compare with the FACTS and Niche products they already use.
- Revenue: $60M from 0.6M students at $100 is 55% of the idea's own newly funded students, heroic. At the median ($3B flow, 0.4M students, 20% share) it is about $4M plus the school side.

### 7. Permitted-seat platform (weak)

- Competitor found: Primer Microschools (Khosla and Speedinvest on its jobs pages; 23 schools in FL, AL, AZ; real-estate engine for approvals; listing of a new San Antonio building at $3.19M, 6% cap, 10-year lease to Primer). Alpha School converts existing buildings under TDLR filings. Building Hope offers partial lease guarantees.
- Capacity is binding in the Catholic sector (San Antonio Archdiocese: all 35 campuses participating, about 9,000 students) and Texas had 274,000 applications, 102,000+ awards and about 120,000-145,000 waiting. But the waitlist is a political appropriation question (new comptroller seeks about $2B; $980M to clear the waitlist), so a landlord is betting on Texas budgets.
- Revenue math: 34,000 seats x $2,900 = $99M is rent. At the 6% cap rate a 100-seat site renting $290k a year is worth about $4.8M, so 340 sites is about $1.6B of real estate, not $15-30M. That is a REIT, and landlord margin after master-lease rent to churches is perhaps 25-30%.
- Not verified: the Utah 100-student cap and Group E occupancy claims (not checked); the median microschool size of 20.

### 8. Credit concierge (weak)

- Premise holds: nonrefundable credit up to $1,700 per individual and $3,400 for joint filers, five-year carryforward, donors may give to a participating state's SGO even if their own state has not opted in, and Treasury expects 46.5M taxpayers could use both state and federal credits. No CPA planning calculator found.
- But channel and economics are weaker than described. ACE's partner program already lists tax and financial advisors as partners at a stated 3% share. The giving decision follows affinity (parish, school, denomination), not an accountant's selection of an SGO. A 6% fee takes 60% of an SGO's 10% overhead, which must also cover audit, verification, wallet fees and staff.
- Revenue check: $90M assumes 30% of a $5B mature ceiling flows through the tool. Arizona's scaled-up ceiling is $5.3-5.9B; realistic 2027-28 is $1-2B. At 15% of $5B and 5% = $37M; the 30% case is heroic. The payroll plus W-4 Step 3 cash-neutral pattern is unendorsed by Treasury (the idea says so) and not something I could source.

## Picks

None. No idea clears both bars (median at least $20M, a believable upside of at least $100M).

The closest is idea 3, the wallet and custody layer. It has the only confirmed regulatory shock and the highest ceiling. It fails because (a) my median is $13M, (b) the $100M case needs Treasury-scale giving of about three times Arizona's rate and about 30% share against ClassWallet, FACTS, ACE and Odyssey together, and (c) yield retention, the highest-margin line, is contested by the "earnings must be spent" rule and the Idaho precedent. Shrinking it to a sub-$20M treasury niche would produce the boutique the lessons warn against, so I do not pick it.

What would change the verdict: a final rule that explicitly lets a wallet operator keep a defined share of float; evidence by mid-2027 that federal-credit giving is running at more than $6B annualised; or a signed multi-state ESA wallet contract paying more than 2% of flows.

## Not verified in this gate

- ACE's 3% partner share (ACE's own marketing, one source).
- The 23% Arizona fall and the 83% tuition share for older EdChoice cohorts (not reproduced; the AZ DOR report shows -10.8% for individual STO giving FY22 to FY23, and I did not read FY2024-25 reports).
- Texas administrator contract price (cap of 5% and the $52M ceiling come from news summaries, not the contract) and the $79M and $93M competing bids.
- Utah 100-student cap and Group E occupancy rules; AICPA commission rules; money-transmitter or FBO treatment of a wallet that holds SGO funds without the SGO retaining ownership.
- Odyssey's Series B date and lead (a16z and Tusk named as backers); ClassWallet revenue (third-party estimates only).
- Final regulation text: all rule citations are to the proposed rule and may change after the 1 Dec comment deadline and 15 Dec hearing.

# Gate report: pool P022, US auto-loan servicing, collections and repossession layer

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with about 5 searches each (about 45 searches total), plus primary-source reads of the Fed FEDS Note on buy-here-pay-here lending and the CFPB repossession report (read as PDF text). Result: 4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. One pick (Idea 1), flagged as borderline.

## Headline findings

- Every layer of the pool already has a funded or entrenched owner. Locate-first, forwarding and remarketing are sold today by Primeritus, Servicing Solutions and Resolvion. Lender-direct plate-sighting subscriptions are sold by Motorola's DRN. Third-party servicing is sold by Westlake Portfolio Management, Vervent, Santander and others. Cross-lender double-pledge checks are sold by Setpoint. A captive-as-a-service for auto dealer groups launched in January 2026 from Octane.
- The "29% recovery ratio" that anchors Idea 1 is not a dollar recovery rate. In the CFPB data it is the share of repossession assignments completed (27% in Sept 2022 vs 38% in Sept 2019). It counts repeat assignments: only 54% of accounts were assigned once, so roughly half were assigned again. It also counts voluntary surrenders (10-19% of completions) and borrower redemptions (22-34% of completions). Mixing it with the 38.2% ABS dollar recovery (KBRA) inflates the "invisible failure" story.
- Buyers already outsource. Forwarders handled 66% of completed repossessions by Dec 2022, up from 31% in Jan 2018 (CFPB). That helps a new forwarder (low switching friction, easy champion/challenger tests) and hurts anyone claiming a gap.
- Several "$100M" paths only work if you count 100% of a small universe (Idea 4) or a third of an assumed flow (Idea 8).

## Revenue-math corrections (where the $100M paths break)

- Idea 1: unit counts hold (RDN: 2.42M assignments and 706k repossessions in Q2 2025, about 2.9M a year; Cox counted 1.73M auction-delivered units in 2024), but a quarter to a third of completed repossessions are redeemed by the borrower (CFPB), so remarketable units are about 1.8-2.1M. $100M of net at about $650 a unit is 154k units, 7-9% of remarketable units and likely over 30% of the 20-25 reachable non-captive lenders. $100M of gross billing is a different and easier number: if the lender is invoiced once for agent fee, transport, storage, auction coordination and title (about $1,450-1,700 a unit all-in by the idea's own cost stack), it is about 60-70k units, or 3-4 anchor lenders at 15-25k repossessions each. The lender value test also mixes units and dollars: "+3 points of recovery on a $17k balance" applies to dollar recovery (KBRA 38.2%), not to the 29% unit-completion ratio.
- Idea 2: 100k cases is 3.4-5.6% of repossessions, but the case economics need the dealer to overpay wholesale by roughly 15%; at wholesale the lender gain falls from $1.9k to a few hundred dollars and the fee is hard to defend. Dealer absorption would be about 100k purchases and 100k replacement BHPH loans, about 4-5% of the idea's assumed $18-22B BHPH origination flow.
- Idea 3: 277k policies is about what Root-Carvana (a fully embedded checkout channel at the largest online used-car retailer) took years to pass. $100M of commission needs about 2% of non-prime loans converted into policies the agency keeps, with 6-month renewals and non-standard retention risk.
- Idea 4: $100M needs about $10B managed, about two Driveway Finance Corps, from a customer universe where only about 30-35 groups exceed 20k units and several run their own or are courted by Octane.
- Idea 5: needs about 15% of all scheduled US auto payments at a price 2-3x the going ACH cost.
- Idea 6: $7 per loan per month is unsourced; even at a higher full-service price (not sourced), $100M means billions of dollars of third-party-serviced balances, against Westlake Portfolio Management's disclosed onboardings of $100M-$741M a deal over eight years.
- Idea 7: $3.50 per loan per year on 20M loans needs coverage of half of all pledged paper; the certificate leg (about $36M) has no price discovery.
- Idea 8: $300M is the figure at 100% of the assumed flow; $100M is a third.

## Pick

### Idea 1, Last Mile (borderline: median $22M, upside $100M)

Upside assumptions, with plausibility:
1. It signs 3-5 anchor non-captive lenders with 15-25k repossessions each and consolidates their recovery spend into one invoice. Plausible only if a 90-day randomized pilot beats the incumbent forwarder. Lender switching is easy here (champion/challenger tests are routine; forwarders went from 31% to 66% of completions in five years), but incumbents can copy the DRN-fed locate step quickly. About 10% chance.
2. Net-proceeds pricing is a durable edge. Today nobody found sells it; it is copyable once proven, so the moat has to be the per-unit outcome dataset and the managed-resolution workflow, not the pricing.
3. Compliance holds: pay-on-net-proceeds must not reward aggressive repossession (CFPB has already linked forwarder use to higher fees; Senate Banking is active on repossessions).

Why it still beats the rest: it sits on an existing budget line ($2.6-4.3B of annual recovery cost at $1,450 a unit), it has a named signer (SVP Collections/Recovery with CFO co-sign), a cheap test exists, and its $100M upside needs share of a measurable, bake-off-able service rather than a new category.

## What I could not verify

- Whether DRN sells locate-only to lenders without a recovery attachment; pricing.

- The 35-40% subprime uninsured rate, 10-15% non-standard commission and $3,000 premium in Idea 3.

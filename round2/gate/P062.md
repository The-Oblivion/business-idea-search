# Gate report: pool P062, US clinical laboratory testing

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 2-4 searches each, plus a full read of XiFin's 2026 Payor Denial Impact Report and of the CMS/CDC proposed CLIA rule (FR doc 2026-20613). Result: 4 kill (Lab Order Rail, Telelab, Gain-Share Desk, Lab OS), 3 weak (Know-Before-You-Bill, Lab Switch, Third Lab), 1 strong-as-restructured (Outreach Without Selling). 1 pick, sharpened.

Scale used (same as other round-2 gates): kill = already done, false premise, or upside under about $40M. Weak = real but upside $40-100M, or an unresolved structural problem. Strong = survives the checks, median at least $20M, upside at least $100M on named assumptions.

## Headline findings

- **The vendor slices of this pool are small; only plays that own test revenue reach $100M by mass.** XiFin (30M+ claims, 73% of the largest labs) shows successful appeals are 0.35% of clinical-lab insurance receipts at $171 each, and 0.03% at $48 in hospital outreach. Even at 100% share of a $36B pool, a 25% contingency on that appeal pool is about $30M. Fees of 0.4-3.5% of lab collections or per order need 8-28% of the pool to reach $100M. Source: https://media.xifin.com/prod/uploads/EWC-2026-XiFin-2026-Payor-Denial-Impact-Report.pdf
- **The CLIA rule is real but narrower than two ideas need.** CMS-3478-P was published 2026-10-08 (comments due 2026-12-07). "Virtual access" is review done under the primary testing lab's own certificate by qualified personnel who must be listed on that lab's Form CMS-209, with the lab director responsible for all of it; the text is framed around a lab's own staff working remotely, not a third-party nighthawk market. The same document still assumes one director can oversee up to five labs. Sources: https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20613.pdf and https://www.aabb.org/news-resources/news/article/2026/10/14/cms-proposes-changes-to-clia-personnel-and-virtual-access-rules
- **The remote-review value is tiny next to the price.** The only published remote smear-review study (Tel Aviv Sourasky, 10,704 smears, 5 months) found a 15.8% overall turnaround cut but only about 12.76 net work hours saved per month (about 36 gross, less 23.24 added). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9683999
- **Hospital outreach is the one huge, in-motion pool.** Quest's 2025 investor day sizes hospital outreach at about $20B of a $58B physician channel across 600+ health systems, and says 8 acquisitions in 2024 added about $1B of annualized revenue (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1022079/000094787125000321/ss4596431_ex9901.htm). Quest paid $230M (Allina), $200M (OhioHealth) and $183M (University Hospitals) in 2024 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1022079/000102207925000044/dgx-20241231.htm); Labcorp did 13 hospital or regional lab deals in 2025 (https://www.360dx.com/clinical-lab-management/labcorp-reaffirms-outreach-acquisitions-jpm-key-growth-cites-strong-deal). Outreach program size is very skewed: Chi's 2016 survey had median $12.8M vs average $24.9M (https://clpmag.com/resource-center/research/survey-laboratory-outreach-enjoys-favorable-profitability/).
- **An independent outreach operator has existed and was absorbed.** PAML ran sales, couriers, IT, customer service and billing for about nine hospital JVs, did about $300M revenue, and was sold to Labcorp in 2017 (https://www.darkdaily.com/?p=21318 ; https://www.thelundreport.org/content/paml-announces-sale-labcorp). Sonic's US labs show 0% organic growth with an operating review ongoing (https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260820/pdf/072zhg5rhzbt79.pdf).

## Per-idea detail

### 1. Lab Order Rail (kill)

- Competitors: Health Gorilla (Lab Network requisition API, 120+ vendors, $75-80M raised); ELLKAY (58k facilities, 43k lab interfaces, Quest was its first client; real-time eligibility at order; revenue only a third-party estimate near $75M, unverified); Concert Genetics with Availity for genetic-test coverage and prior auth (https://www.availity.com/news/availity-concert-partner-on-prior-auths-for-genetic-testing/); Epic and athenahealth native modules; Quest and Labcorp portals.
- Substitutes: the EHR's own eligibility check at registration; lab client-services phone calls.
- Revenue math: the idea's 600-820M non-Q/L requisitions is untested arithmetic. At $0.60 it needs 20-28% of them. At a Surescripts-like 2-17 cents per transaction, 167M orders is $3-28M. A neutral rail has no mandate (e-prescribing had Medicare and state e-Rx mandates behind it) and Quest/Labcorp will not join one.
- Legal: labs were removed from the EHR-donation safe harbor in 2013 (interfaces untouched): https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/final-rule-excludes-laboratories-ehr-donation-exception-stark-extends-exception-expiration-date/ . A lab-funded free tool that influences order routing is remuneration to the practice; Health Gorilla shows it can be run, but it needs counsel and possibly an OIG opinion. Unverified: whether cross-lab duplicate checks are a real denial driver (XiFin's 15.2% "duplicate" share is not defined as cross-lab).
- Upside case needs: Epic and two big EHRs list it, one national lab joins, price holds at $0.30+, and the AKS opinion is clean. Plausibility: low.

### 2. Know-Before-You-Bill (weak)

- Confirmed from XiFin: 11.2% denial rate in 2025 (up from 8.0%), "procedure not paid separately" 59.4% of clinical-lab denials (61.2% in hospital outreach), duplicates 15.2%; successful appeal pays $171 (clinical), $48 (outreach); molecular is the exception at $1,684 per successful appeal and 9.30% of receipts, which the idea's $5-170 focus skips.
- Revenue math: 1.5-2.5% of collections needs $4-6.7B of collections (11-18% of the $36B pool); the 3.5% full-service version needs $2.9B (8%) and 400 labs at $7M. Real mid-size independents and billing shops are a small number of accounts; $100M also needs outreach and molecular.
- Substitutes: XiFin Empower, LigoLab, Sunquest/Clinisys, offshore billing shops, in-house billers, hospital RCM (R1, Waystar).
- Upside needs: remit data from 100+ labs, edit engine that beats XiFin's own, a billing-shop roll-up for distribution, and extension to molecular and hospital outreach. Plausible but XiFin can copy; the idea says so itself.
- Unverified: that PNPS denials are mostly preventable timing problems (the idea's own inference; XiFin does not define the category).

### 3. Telelab (kill)

- Premise check passed on the rule (real, dated, comments due Dec 7) and failed on fit: virtual access is the lab's own personnel under its certificate (FR doc 2026-20613, proposed sections 493.43 and 493.55); a vendor workforce has to be listed on the hospital's CMS-209 with the hospital's director liable. About a dozen states license lab personnel (unverified count), which fragments a national bench.
- Price vs value: net 12.76 hours/month saved at a tertiary center with about 2,100 smears a month. A 200-bed hospital has far fewer. At $52 an hour loaded that is under $10k a year against a $100-150k ask. The "1.5-2.5 FTE of shift cost" is not removable because the on-site person does specimen handling, QC and blood bank.
- Upside: 1,500 clients at $20-30k is $30-45M. vRad's economics came from radiologist reads at $50-100 each; a tech smear review is a few dollars.

### 4. Lab Switch (weak)

- Competitors: Avalon (see table), Anthem and Blue plan site-of-service programs, Quest's QuestSelect (steers to itself), Carelon/EviCore lab management. Found no neutral employer-direct vendor with patient routing, but Avalon's TPA/broker pitch covers the same buyer.
- Math check: HCCI sample $6.2B is about one third of claims; $18.6B employer spend and $11.2B hospital outpatient are the idea's arithmetic and hold up. Fee of $4.90 a life needs about 20M lives (13% of employer-insured). Avalon, with 90M members through carriers, shows the scale needed took more than a decade and carrier distribution.
- Upside needs: TPA and stop-loss carrier distribution deals covering 10M+ lives at $0.40-0.70 PEPM, and carriers not extending their own programs. Plausibility: low to moderate.
- Legal: plan design is fine; the unverified piece is carrier all-products or anti-steering contract terms that can block TPA-delivered steering.

### 5. Outreach Without Selling (strong as restructured)

- Why it survives: budget line exists (outreach is a contribution-margin business; Chi's older surveys show 23-33% contribution margin, Mayo's manager says 30-40% for moderately developed programs); mandate and shock exist (2027 CLFS cut, H.R.1 Medicaid cuts, Labcorp's CEO saying hospitals under Medicaid and ACA subsidy pressure may sell); pool is about $20B across 600+ systems; the signer is the system CFO.
- What the idea gets wrong: (a) the 8% fee cannot fund couriers, patient service centers, billing and sales (they cost well over 8% of revenue), so it is either cost-plus plus a percent or an equity JV; (b) the "875 x $24.9M" arithmetic uses a mean; the median program is $12.8M (2016), so a typical contract is about $1M a year at 8%; (c) "found no independent operator" is true today only because PAML was bought by Labcorp in 2017; (d) hospitals that want out take Quest and Labcorp cash; the target is the ones that want to stay.
- Legal: EKRA covers lab marketing payments even when aimed at physicians; percent-of-revenue pay is not per se unlawful (Schena) but is outside the safe harbor and needs counsel: https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/ninth-circuit-clarifies-permissible-marketing-compensation-under-ekra-laboratories-beware/ . Anthem New Hampshire shows payers can force hospital outreach to independent-lab rates, so payer enrollment risk is real.
- Competitors: Quest Co-Lab and JVs, Labcorp, Sonic USA, Mayo Clinic Laboratories and ARUP (advisory, connectivity partnerships; ARUP also publishes arguments against selling), PAML precedent, consultants (Chi Solutions).

### 6. Third Lab (weak)

- Revenue by acquisition is easy arithmetic (8-10 labs of $10-12M) but revenue shrinks when 775 tests lose 15% a year, the 0.4-0.8x price is contradicted by BioReference's 2.4x, and Q/L bid on the same assets with synergies.
- The payer premise is unproven and partly contradicted: UHC and Aetna already prefer Quest and Labcorp (Eurofins sits on UHC's full-menu list); no payer commitment found. Sonic (US labs) and BioReference are the cautionary precedents for a "third national".
- Better asset supply than distressed independents: hospital outreach programs (see 5). Hub overflow is folded into the pick as stage 2.

### 7. Hospital Lab Gain-Share Desk (kill)

- Savings math: send-outs about 6% of budget (one negotiator, unverified), reagent rentals and mix; realistic total 5-8%, not 12%. A vendor-reported 60% reference-lab saving in a three-hospital alliance does not transfer to a single site. Gain-share lines are crowded (GPOs, consultants) and Quest and Labcorp bundle it with management deals.

### 8. Lab OS for Small Labs (kill)

- Premise holds (director visit dropped in proposal; qualifications loosened) but the market is thin: director services at $18-30k a year per lab already exist, the five-lab cap forces headcount, multi-site operators are few, and the Medicare cut shrinks the number of labs owners want to run.

## Picks

### Pick 1: Outreach Without Selling, sharpened to "Outreach Partner Labs"

One-liner: an independent operator that co-owns a new outreach lab company with a health system, runs every commercial function, and leaves the hospital's core lab, brand and a real equity stake intact.

Sharpened version (does not shrink the $100M path; it changes how revenue is earned):

1. Structure: a hospital-and-operator co-owned outreach LLC with its own CLIA, NPI and TIN (the Frisbie, PAML and Corewell templates), hospital core lab keeps 80-85% of testing, operator takes equity (50-51%) bought with growth capital, plus a cost-plus management agreement covering sales, couriers, patient service centers, billing, payer enrollment and client services. The fee is no longer 6-10% asked to cover costs it cannot cover.
2. Revenue: where the operator controls the LLC, revenue is the LLC's outreach revenue (one $50M system is half a $100M business); where it only manages, revenue is fee plus services margin.
3. Hub: once three or more systems share a metro or state, route routine overflow and send-outs to a regional hub (this is Third Lab, but fed by hospital volume instead of distressed independents).
4. Sell the CFO on retained contribution margin and on what a Quest or Labcorp takeout strips (fixed-cost absorption in the core lab), and give them the one thing the nationals refuse: control.

Path to $100M: about $20B pool, 600+ systems. Two controlled JVs of about $45M each is $90M; plus 8 managed systems averaging $35M at 9% adds about $25M; total about $115M. Capital is about 1.0-1.3x revenue for the equity stake, so $50-90M of growth equity at 50-51% ownership (inference from Quest and Labcorp deal sizes; Quest's three 2024 outreach buys cost $613M in cash).

Biggest open risk: hospitals that want cash take Quest and Labcorp; hospitals that want to stay can use ARUP, Mayo or Chi to run it themselves; PAML's end state was acquisition by Labcorp. Second: raising $50-90M of equity without a track record.

Cheapest next test: build the outreach contribution model for 25 systems from CMS and PAMA data; get 5 CFO or outreach-VP conversations asking what would make them pick this over Quest's 51/49 JV; get one healthcare PE or infrastructure sponsor to say in writing whether it would fund $75M of equity; pay for a short EKRA and AKS opinion on the fee and equity structure.

### Not picked

- Third Lab: folded into the pick as stage 2.
- Know-Before-You-Bill: best of the software ideas but median $12M and upside $65M; the pool's appeal base is too small and XiFin is the incumbent.
- Lab Switch: upside $50M and Avalon is already in the channel.

## Unverified or open

- ELLKAY and Avalon revenue figures are third-party estimates.
- The $36B non-Quest/Labcorp outpatient pool and the 600-820M requisition count are the ideas' own arithmetic.
- Quest's outreach acquisition revenues were not disclosed; the 1.0-1.3x revenue multiple is inferred from older benchmarks (Health Alliance sold at 1.3x) and BioReference (2.4x).
- Whether the proposed CLIA rule would let a contractor's staff count as the hospital's "qualified laboratory personnel" is not settled in the text I read.

# Gate report: pool P021, US personal-auto physical-damage claims (repair, parts, calibration, total loss, salvage)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 3-6 searches each, plus primary or near-primary reads (RDN's NAIC 2024 auto market-share table, PartsTrader Spring 2026 via RDN, Copart FY2025 10-K excerpt, NY DFS OGC opinion 10-11-03 on dent-repair memberships, the Copart/ACV deal coverage, CCC Estimate-STP adoption numbers, Matthews/Focus/Romans MSO data). Result: 4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. **No picks.** Every idea fails at least one of: median at least $20M, credible upside at least $100M.

## Headline finding

The pool is real ($92B of incurred physical-damage losses; about $50B of US collision-shop revenue) and the pain is real. But every idea here was written against a pool where the incumbents are the data owners (CCC, Mitchell, Audatex, LexisNexis, Copart, IAA, LKQ), and four of the eight lean on a number that does not hold up on checking.

- **The "tier 2/3 carrier" market is about 24% of personal auto, not 35-40%.** RDN's NAIC 2024 table puts the top 10 private-passenger-auto groups at 76.2% (State Farm 18.9, Progressive 16.7, Berkshire/GEICO 11.6, Allstate 10.2, USAA 6.2, Farmers 3.8, Liberty 3.3, Travelers 2.0, AAA 1.8, American Family 1.7). Severity Pool and Cycle Close both sized their base at 35-40%. Severity Pool then cannot reach $100M even at 100% share of the corrected base ($79M).
- **Insurer automation is a smaller shock than claimed.** The pool says Estimate-STP covers "about half of US claims volume". The primary figure is 40 insurer clients (7 of the top 10) but about 5% of total claims actually flowing through it (one large carrier at 20%); CCC's CEO said in Aug 2025 that aggregate volume "doesn't yet move the needle". Counterweight's why-now is overstated.
- **The legal tailwind for calibration is narrower than claimed.** New York's Dec 19, 2025 law (GBL 392-K), like Utah's and Arizona's, covers glass repair facilities, not collision shops. Maryland and Illinois are only pending.
- **Two "gaps" are already being filled by small startups.** Salvato Auctions (Texas, first auction 7 Aug 2025, fees claimed 20-40% below Copart/IAA, "decentralized vehicle storage", signed carriers, expanding to the Midwest and Southeast in 2026) is a version of Open Yard. Opus IVS's ADAS MAP (Allstate's Good Hands network reimburses its $3 report fee) already sits in Proof Plate's "plan" layer.
- **The one salvage partner that mattered just got bought.** Copart agreed on 10 Sep 2026 to buy ACV Auctions for about $1.9B (close expected by end of 2026), putting the strongest dealer-wholesale buyer base inside the incumbent.
- **Two numbers in the parts idea are wrong.** The "9 days for OEM" is PartsTrader's January/February figure (winter storms; described as median plus two standard deviations; 8.7 days in February, March not reported), and the "18% insurer price-matched OEM" is actually 18% of all parts being price-matched, "predominantly aftermarket".

## Idea notes

### 1. Counterweight: weak (median $9M, upside $55M)

**Who already does it.** The pool says nobody funded does shop-side revenue cycle, and that is nearly true, but only because the market has been tried and stayed small. Collision Billing Services launched in July 2011 to rebuild shop estimates in the insurer's platform, add missed operations and run a desk review; it never became a scaled company. Today: Claimory (AI flags missed lines, drafts adjuster rebuttals, $49.99 per location per month, listed June 2026), Audatex Shop Recoverables (since 2016), Mitchell Estimate Review plus a central-desk review mode in its MSO suite, ALLDATA Collision, VaraFix (25 Aug 2026), GuideCoat, and small supplement desks such as Impact Illumination (Wisconsin, free first-file review). Crash Champions advertises a "Centralized Estimatics Reviewer" role specialised by carrier, including supplement audits, so the large MSOs already run this in-house.

**The $100M path does not survive.**
- The base: Romans puts the US market at about $50B; Focus's "Big Five" hold 3,836 shops and over $15.6B (about 30%); Focus's 2023 count of 803 smaller MSOs owns only 2,400 facilities and $7.5B. The MSO tier is therefore about $23B, and the mid-sized tier a customer can win without beating an in-house team is about $7.5B across mostly two- and three-shop operators.
- A vendor's take is capped by what it recovers. At the pool's own 2.5% incremental recovery, a desk that keeps 25% (generous against 12%) plus $8 a repair order is about 0.8% of insurer-paid revenue. $100M needs about $12.5B of insurer-paid revenue, over half of all MSO revenue. The author already calls this "heroic".
- The 2.5% itself is an assumption. Supportive: 55% of shops in a 2026 Collision Repair magazine reader survey (Canadian) add supplements on nearly every job, and older surveys found 26-43% of shops never ask for second-colour set-up. Against: CCC says over 60% of estimates already need supplements, so some of the leakage is already being claimed.
- Legal: a fee of 12% of "verified incremental approved dollars" on insurer payments looks like a contingency fee on a claim. Oregon's regulator and an attorney quoted by RDN say a shop negotiating with the insurer for the owner may need a public-adjuster licence, and contingency fees are void for adjusters in Louisiana. A vendor acting for the shop is a different role, but this needs a state-by-state opinion before gain-share terms are written.
- Why-now is weaker than stated: only about 5% of claims flow through Estimate-STP.

**What would change the verdict:** evidence that a Big-5 MSO outsources revenue-cycle work today and would pay at least 1% of revenue. Nothing found says one does.

### 2. Severity Pool: kill (median $5M, upside $40M)

- **Math.** With tier 2/3 at 24% instead of 35-40%: 15M claims x 24% x 70% repairable x 50% DRP-eligible = 1.26M files x $63 = $79M at 100% of the base. Adding $40-60 of rental, salvage and glass legs gives $130-155M at 100%, so $100M needs about 70% of all tier 2/3 volume. Even a top-decile 25% share is about $35-40M.
- **Substitutes exist.** Innovation Group sells managed repair networks to tier 2/3 carriers (American Farmers & Ranchers); Sedgwick runs managed networks; CrashBay put Branch's network live in under three weeks (28 Jul 2026), which the pool itself cites as proof that network setup is no longer the barrier. RDN already floated the idea of small carriers pooling in 2019; nobody built it, and the closest analogue is France's Assercar (a shared 1,400-shop network for several insurers).
- **Legal and structural.** Competing insurers jointly purchasing from shops is a Sherman Act question (an attorney quoted by FenderBender says dealings with shops are not "the business of insurance", so McCarran-Ferguson may not protect it). Carrying a per-claim severity guarantee makes the pool a risk-bearer, which regulators will treat as insurance-adjacent. The top-10 carriers (76%) will not dilute their own tiers.

### 3. Cycle Close: weak (median $8M, upside $65M)

- **Already built, in software.** LossExpress (Dallas, 2019): real-time loan payoff and lien-release records, LexisNexis partnership delivering data into the claim system for up to 80% of a carrier's total losses, IAA and Guidewire integrations, and a Champ Titles tie-up. Champ Titles has raised over $30M. CCC Total Loss Care covers FNOL to lien and title, with PDP Group's 1,100 lenders, and CCC serves 350 insurers including 19 of the top 20. Verisk Lien Check, One Inc ClaimsPay and the Dealertrack/IAA pilot sit on the same leg.
- **Premise not verified.** LexisNexis does say the average total loss runs 19-22 days. I could not find its "lienholder confirmed on day 13" milestone, so the claim that 12 days vanish between owner acceptance and lien payment is unproven.
- **Math.** 3.5M TLs x 24% tier 2/3 = 0.84M files x $175 = $147M at 100% of the base; $100M is 68%. A top-decile case that also wins half of one top-10 carrier's files adds about $30M, giving roughly $65M. The $175 price has no source.
- **Substitute.** Offshore claims BPO and carrier in-house TL units, with licensed adjuster and E&O burden in every state.

**What would change the verdict:** a carrier telling you it pays by the closed file for the post-valuation leg today. None found.

### 4. Same-Day: kill (median $6M, upside $50M)

- **Premise.** Verified from PartsTrader Spring 2026: OEM 52% of parts ordered, aftermarket 20%, price-matched 18%, recycled 6%; OEM +4.3% since the tariffs, aftermarket under 1%. But the 9-day OEM delivery is a January/February figure and described as median plus two standard deviations; aftermarket and recycled are about 3 days. Toyota says most metros get same-day OEM parts from dealers, and MSOs such as Body by Cochran cut parts cycle time from 11.5 to 1.3 days by running their own warehouse (serving 500 shops).
- **Competitors.** LKQ/Keystone (alternative-parts utilisation over 40% in Q2 2026; North America EBITDA margin 14.1%), PartsTrader, OEConnection CollisionLink, Apfusion ($6.5M seed for shop parts ordering), uParts.
- **Math.** A 3% delivery fee on a roughly $300 order is $9, below the cost of a courier run; the pool's $100M needs about $1.8B of GMV, 11.6% of all collision parts.
- **Unproven leg.** Net-45 financing against the insurer-approved estimate has no data behind it (the author says so).

### 5. Open Yard: weak (median $8M, upside $70M)

- **Real money.** Copart's FY2025 US service revenue was $3.45B (insurers supply 81% of units); RB Global's automotive GTV was $2.45B in Q2 2026 with units up 11% on "net market share gains", and a major insurer scaled across 30 states in about 90 days. So insurers do move volume quickly, and the incumbents are cutting take rate (RB Global's service take rate fell 110bp to 20.0%).
- **Already being done.** Salvato Auctions: Texas launch Aug 2025, carriers signed (a first customer plus two more in Aug 2025), "decentralized vehicle storage", digital lien and title, fees claimed 20-40% below Copart and IAA, expansion announced for the Midwest and the Mid-Atlantic/Southeast in Q2 2026. Volume and funding are undisclosed.
- **The moat is buyers.** 69.8% of Copart's US units go to buyers registered outside the state where the car sits, and international buyers are about 40% of volume. Copart is also acquiring ACV, adding dealer-wholesale buyers.
- **Upside logic.** $100M at $300 a unit is 333,000 units, roughly 8% of the market; against a duopoly that is now bigger and better funded I do not believe it. At 5% share and $250 it is about $50M. $70M is the top-decile number.
- Caveat: the "about 80% duopoly" share figure is one investor write-up; other sources differ.

### 6. Dent Plan: kill (median $4M gross, upside $60M gross)

- **Legal premise fails as designed.** NY DFS OGC opinion 10-11-03: a prepaid membership fee plus a per-service fee that responds to a fortuitous event is not insurance only if the per-service fee covers the provider's actual cost plus reasonable overhead, with "not insurance" disclosure. A $290-a-year fee for a $1,500 credit at marginal-cost menu prices is risk transfer. The compliant version (membership plus cost-plus per-service fee) is a discount club, which removes the product. British Columbia reclassified dent and windshield products as insurance in 2024; Massachusetts treats third-party-obligor contracts as insurance.
- **Substitutes.** Dent Wizard's Ding Shield and OEM appearance programs (Buick, Volvo) are service contracts sold at dealer F&I; Deductify (launched 2 Sep 2025, $10.99-$19.99 a month) and ZeroedOut (14 Oct 2025) pay deductibles after a claim. No member counts or funding are disclosed for either.
- **Revenue is gross.** The author's own contribution estimate is 25-30%, so $100M of memberships is about $25-30M of net revenue. I could not find a figure for how many vehicles lack collision cover (the one quarter in the pitch is an assumption).
- Distribution (MSO past-customer lists, dealer F&I, credit unions) is slow, and dealer F&I reaches buyers of new cars, not owners of old ones.

### 7. Proof Plate: weak (median $9M, upside $55M)

- **Competitors found beyond the pool's list.** Opus IVS ADAS MAP is a VIN-specific calibration identification platform integrated with CCC estimating and with Mitchell; Allstate's Good Hands Repair Network (27 Oct 2025 bulletin) reimburses its $3 report fee and offers five-tier flat-rate calibration pricing; Allstate said in Aug 2025 that Opus and asTech are "approved, not preferred" vendors. asTech's Connected Calibrations (Nov 2025) flags calibrations from estimate to completion in a dashboard; in 2021 Repairify reported 185,000 ADAS reports and 28,000 customers.
- **Price anchor.** The market price of the plan layer is $3 a report. The pool's $9 record plus $4 insurer fee is three to four times that.
- **Legal premise is narrower.** NY (signed 19 Dec 2025), Utah and Arizona cover glass facilities. Litigation went from 3 suits (2018) to 61 (2024) per an analyst via trade press; the $200k-$1M settlement range and "nearly half of shops miss calibrations" are analyst commentary.
- **Math.** KPMG's $3B US calibration-services forecast for 2030 is conditional on widespread adoption. If half is sublet and Proof Plate dispatches 25% of it at 8%, that is about $30M, plus $15-25M of record and approval fees. About $55M is the top-decile number; $100M needs both dispatch and records at roughly half the market.

### 8. Ground Truth: kill (median $6M, upside $45M)

- **Premises hold.** The NAIC 12-state AI pilot is real and adoption is targeted for the Fall National Meeting (a target, not a scheduled vote). Progressive's $15.24M Colorado settlement has an opt-out deadline of 5 Nov 2026 and a fairness hearing on 6 Jan 2027; Progressive's 10-Q lists certified or conditionally certified classes in Colorado, North Carolina and Ohio; State Farm's Arkansas case was $15.58M.
- **Size.** The author's own build is about $55M with a $40-60M ceiling; exposure and budget are lumpy and tied to litigation.
- **Data.** The raw sold-price feeds are licensed from the same vendors being audited (J.D. Power, classifieds), so the claimed cornered resource is not yours to corner.
- **Legal.** The author flags the round-1 killer: an audit report is likely discoverable unless it is built under counsel, which then limits what a regulator can be handed.
- No dedicated total-loss audit product found; Level ($3.09M seed, Sep 2025, lender-side) is the nearest funded name.

## Picks

**None.** No idea reaches a median of $20M with a credible $100M upside.

Closest misses, for the record:
- **Open Yard** has the biggest fee pool and a buyer (insurers) who demonstrably moves volume. It fails on a surviving incumbent moat plus a live challenger. What would flip it: Salvato disclosing multi-state insurer volume with net proceeds at or above Copart's on matched cars.
- **Counterweight** has the clearest budget line and the fastest sales cycle (a few signers). It fails on a ceiling set by what it can recover. What would flip it: a Big-5 MSO agreeing to pay at least 1% of revenue for outsourced revenue-cycle work, which would also show the in-house risk is lower than it looks.

## 1. US farmers' fertilizer purchases

- **Money flow:** farmers pay ag retailers and co-ops (Nutrien Ag Solutions, CHS, independents), who buy from producers and importers (Nutrien, Mosaic, CF Industries, Koch, Yara, OCP).
- **Size:** about $35-40B a year. USDA ERS farm income forecast (page updated Sept 3, 2026) shows fertilizer, lime and soil conditioners of roughly $34.6B in 2025 and $39.9B forecast for 2026. Those two values were backed out from stated changes, so they are approximate. Earlier ERS vintage: $29.2B for 2025 (Farm Policy News, Feb 2025). Source: https://ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/farm-sector-income-forecast
- **Shock (2026-03):**
  - Early 2026 war with Iran pushed fertilizer up. One Iowa farmer's reported nitrogen price went from $530 to $950 a ton within days.
  - Bloomberg reported in early March 2026 that DOJ is investigating possible price coordination (Nutrien, Mosaic, CF, Koch, Yara named). FTC chair confirmed an FTC probe in May.
  - 17 farm groups asked the Senate Judiciary Committee in July 2026 to press DOJ.
  - Farmer multidistrict litigation alleges coordination since 2021.
  - Countervailing duties on Moroccan and Russian phosphate (16.6% to over 47%) are in a five-year sunset review. A second source says the Moroccan duties were suspended, with a USDA estimate of $1.82B a year in farmer savings. The two reports conflict and I did not resolve it.
  - Sources: https://farmpolicynews.illinois.edu/?p=34281 and https://www.kcrg.com/2026/07/17/corn-growers-urge-doj-investigation-into-collusive-fertilizer-industry-practices/
- **Incumbents:** few producers supply most US nutrients. Nutrien alone makes about 20% of US phosphate. Retailers add a margin on top (my recollection: gross margins in the high teens to 20s percent; not verified).
- **Fragility:** political and legal pressure is the weakness, not technology. Farmers are angry, the DOJ and FTC are investigating, and USDA's deputy secretary publicly criticized Mosaic for idling two Brazil plants. Incumbents are otherwise strong.
- **Funded startups (2023-2026):**
  - Pivot Bio: $430M Series D in 2021; laid off 62 and closed Berkeley in mid-2025; a third-party aggregator marks it far below its $1.7B peak.
  - Nitricity: $50M Series B, Sept 2025.
  - Atlas Agro: Macquarie committed up to $325M; the final investment decision has slipped to 2027.
  - Farmers Business Network (FBN Direct input marketplace): restructuring reported, timing unverified.
  - Verdict: contested. Production technology is capital heavy and funded. Distribution and procurement is the thinner area.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Buying groups or forward-purchase pools for mid-size farms.
  - Price-transparency and procurement tools for co-ops and retailers.
  - Nitrogen-rate reduction services paid per ton avoided.
  - Manure and biosolids to fertilizer marketplace.
  - Input prepay finance.
- **Insider roles:** co-op general managers, ag retail agronomists, fertilizer barge and terminal traders, Farm Credit input lenders, certified crop advisers.

## 2. US farm employers' hired labor spend (H-2A in particular) [HG]

- **Money flow:** farm employers pay workers, plus recruiters, labor contractors, H-2A agents, housing and transport providers.
- **Size:**
  - ERS: cash labor of $44.3B in 2026 forecast (Sept 2026 page above). Broader labor expense was $54.3B in the Sept 2025 forecast (Texas Farm Bureau, https://texasfarmbureau.org/?p=47299). farmdoc daily puts hired labor near $49B in 2023.
  - H-2A slice, my arithmetic: employers requested about 391,600 positions in FY2024 (Federal Register). At roughly $18.12 an hour national average AEWR, about 1,200 hours each (my assumption) and about $10,000 of non-wage cost per worker (USDA-funded study cited in the FR), that is about $12B a year.
- **Shock (2025-10):**
  - DOL interim final rule, Oct 2, 2025, changed how the H-2A Adverse Effect Wage Rate is set. It uses BLS OEWS data with two skill levels and a housing adjustment, in place of the USDA Farm Labor Survey, which USDA discontinued Aug 31, 2025. The 2023 AEWR rule was vacated Aug 25, 2025.
  - UFW sued Nov 21, 2025.
  - The FR notice cites a study of possible loss of about 225,000 farm workers from enforcement.
  - A third-party blog says DOL projects $2.4B a year of savings; I could not confirm that in the FR text.
  - Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-10-02/html/2025-19365.htm
- **Incumbents:** farm labor contractors, state grower H-2A associations, immigration attorneys, payroll providers. The process is paper heavy, and the 2M farms are fragmented. Wage setting is now regulatory-driven.
- **Funded startups:**
  - Seso: $25M Series A (Index, 2022) and $26M Series B (BOND, Apr 2024).
  - Croft: $2.67M seed, Feb 2024.
  - I found no 2025-26 rounds.
  - Verdict: contested but thinly funded.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Employer-provided H-2A housing, where the AEWR housing adjustment changes the economics.
  - H-2A compliance and recruitment for mid-size growers.
  - Payroll, banking and remittances for farmworkers.
  - Ag workers' comp.
  - Harvest and packhouse automation sold as a service.
  - Wage benchmarking against the new OEWS-based rates.
- **Insider roles:** farm labor contractor owners, H-2A agent-association staff, farm bureau labor directors, state workforce agency job-order staff, HR managers at 1,000+ worker growers.

## 3. Federal crop insurance premiums [HG]

- **Money flow:** farmers pay about 38% of premium. Treasury pays the premium subsidy and an administrative and operating (A&O) reimbursement to Approved Insurance Providers (AIPs), who sell through agents. Treasury also reinsures losses and shares gains under the Standard Reinsurance Agreement.
- **Size:** 2025 crop year, RMA Summary of Business snapshot as of Apr 21, 2025 (interim): total premium $18.04B, subsidy $11.26B (farmer-paid about $6.78B, my subtraction), liability $177.0B, indemnity $17.66B. Source: https://public-rma.fpac.usda.gov/ReportShare//Extranet/SOB/WeeklyReports/20250421/sobrpt2022-2025.pdf
  - A&O is not sourced for 2025. CRS reported $1.6B in 2009 and it scales with premium; a Watts analysis said nearly all of it goes to agent commissions (2018 source). My estimate is about $2B or more now.
- **Shock (2025-07):**
  - One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised premium subsidies 3-5 points for buy-up coverage (38-64% to 41-69%).
  - The Supplemental Coverage Option subsidy went from 65% to 80%.
  - Enhanced Coverage Option reaches 90% coverage in 2026.
  - Extra support for beginning farmers.
  - The A&O reimbursement gets an extra 6% of premium for policies in high-loss states (farmdoc daily, unverified dollar value).
  - RMA bulletin Aug 20, 2025, effective for sales closing dates on or after July 1, 2025.
  - CBO: +$4.4B over 10 years. farmdoc argues earlier RMA ECO actions added $13.2B.
  - Source: https://www.rma.usda.gov/policy-procedure/bulletins-memos/managers-bulletin/mgr-25-006-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-amendment
- **Incumbents:** about 16 AIPs (an older, 2018-era count), selling through private agents. RMA sets rates and subsidies, so AIPs compete on service, not price. Product is identical across AIPs, which makes service quality and distribution the only differentiation.
- **Fragility:** moderate. Agent books and paper-heavy claims and adjuster workflows are the soft spots. Barriers are real: AIP approval, capital, reinsurance.
- **Funded startups:** I found none building a federally reinsured AIP or agency. Arbol (parametric, $60M Series B, Apr 2024, about $250M gross written premium in 2023) is supplemental. Verdict: open for agency, claims and AIP-adjacent services.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Remote-sensing and drone claims adjusting sold to AIPs.
  - Digital agency or agent-book roll-up.
  - SCO/ECO decision tools.
  - Supplemental private coverage layered on federal policies.
  - Specialty-crop MGA.
- **Insider roles:** crop insurance agents with large books, AIP claims managers, loss adjusters, RMA compliance reviewers, farm lenders who require coverage.
- **Path to $100M:** needs about 5% of a roughly $2B A&O pool. That is a roll-up or digital AIP outcome, not a software outcome.

## 4. US cattle and beef supply chain (cattle receipts)

- **Money flow:** packers pay feedlots, feedlots pay cow-calf ranchers and auction barns; Mexican feeder cattle historically filled part of the gap.
- **Size:** USDA cattle and calves cash receipts of $134B in 2025, up 19% from $112B (final figures, April 2026 summary as relayed in search). Source: https://esmis.nal.usda.gov/sites/default/release-files/795883/meatan26.pdf (I did not open the document itself).
- **Shock (2026-07):**
  - Beef cow herd 27.6M head at the Jan 2026 inventory, lowest since 1951, seventh straight year of contraction.
  - New World screwworm: Mexican cattle imports suspended (the trade was about $1.2B a year); 42 US cases as of about August 2026, nearly all in Texas. USDA announced a phased reopening on July 24, 2026: Douglas, AZ from Aug 24 at 700 head a day, then 900, with RFID tags required.
  - Tyson announced closure of Lexington, NE (about 5,000 head a day, 7.5-9% of industry slaughter capacity) in Nov 2025. Tyson beef lost $1.135B in FY2025 and guided to a $400-600M loss for FY2026.
  - USDA's Ranchers First Initiative (Aug 31, 2026) includes a heifer-retention Livestock Risk Protection endorsement ("BRAND"), with no rollout date.
  - Sources: https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/us-begin-lifting-suspension-mexican-cattle-imports-starting-135076018 and https://www.drovers.com/news/tyson-foods-close-lexington-nebraska-beef-plant
- **Incumbents:** a handful of packers (JBS, Tyson, Cargill, National Beef), large feedlots, and tens of thousands of undercapitalized cow-calf operations. Packers lose money and have excess capacity, while ranchers need capital to rebuild.
- **Funded startups:** none found with venture funding for herd-rebuilding finance, EID/traceability or screwworm compliance in the US. The nearest is France's EDS (Bovicash, EUR100M, herd leasing, launching autumn 2026). Public money dominates: USDA screwworm grand challenge had about $105M available against about $664M requested. Verdict: open.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Heifer leasing and herd-rebuild finance, possibly tied to the BRAND insurance product.
  - Screwworm and EID compliance logistics at ranch and border scale.
  - Feeder-calf sourcing for feedlots.
  - Custom-grazing marketplace.
  - Small regional packing capacity brokerage.
- **Insider roles:** auction market owners, order buyers, feedlot risk managers, ag bank and Farm Credit lenders, accredited large-animal vets.
- **Caution:** the nearest comparable idea (cattle leasing) has regulatory and mortality risk, per the same coverage.

## 5. US utilities' vegetation management [HG]

- **Money flow:** utility operating budgets, recovered in rates, pay line-clearance contractors for trimming, tree removal, herbicide and work planning.
- **Size:** about $7-8B a year. Harris Williams (2022) put it at $8B and Power Magazine (2026) at $7B. Nobody has a government total. Cross-check: Davey Tree's 2025 revenue was $1.95B, with Utility Services up 5.4% (about $54M); Asplundh is private, with a secondhand revenue estimate of $5.7B that includes non-vegetation work. Source: https://www.powermag.com/after-the-l-a-wildfires-why-vegetation-management-cant-afford-to-stay-on-a-fixed-cycle/
- **Shock (2025-01):**
  - The January 2025 Los Angeles fires; investigators pointed to Southern California Edison equipment for the Eaton Fire, and LA County and DOJ sued SCE.
  - California inverse condemnation (strict liability) and insurers retreating.
  - Move from fixed trim cycles to risk-based work.
  - NERC FAC-003-5 in force since Apr 1, 2024.
  - Davey reported a 37.9% decline ($10.2M) in its Utility Resource Group, which it attributed to utility vegetation management market challenges in 2025, so demand is not uniformly strong.
- **Incumbents:** Asplundh, Davey Tree, Wright Tree and others. Contractors are paid by volume (miles, trees), so risk-based programs that reduce billable work conflict with their model.
- **Funded startups:**
  - Overstory: sources conflict; reported EUR14M round in 2023 and a $5.2M round led by Convective Capital.
  - Pano AI: $44M Series B reported June 2025 (wildfire detection).
  - Vibrant Planet: about $45M raised in total.
  - Verdict: contested in the analytics layer, open in execution and verification.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Independent post-trim LiDAR audit of contractor work for utilities and regulators.
  - Risk-based work planning sold with outcome data.
  - Hazard-tree prioritization.
  - Biomass disposal and wood utilization marketplace.
  - Contractor labor staffing.
- **Insider roles:** utility vegetation program managers, utility arborists, contractor general foremen, work planners, wildfire-mitigation-plan regulators.

## 6. US packaging EPR producer fees [HG, size borderline]

- **Money flow:** consumer packaged goods brands and retailers pay fees to a producer responsibility organization (Circular Action Alliance), which funds municipal recycling and MRF upgrades.
- **Size:** California's first CAA program plan (filed June 15, 2026) projects a 5-year budget of $9.3-17.2B, about $1.9-3.4B a year (my division). Oregon is reported at about $100M a year (one weak guide). Colorado, Maine, Minnesota, Maryland and Washington have laws. No sourced national total exists; my estimate at maturity is $4-7B a year, so this pool is borderline against the $5B bar and depends on plan approval and litigation (SB 54 is challenged in federal court). Source: https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/06/californias-epr-program-plan
- **Shock (2026-06):** California plan filing; Oregon first invoices Jan 2026 (another guide says the program went live July 1, 2025; the dates conflict); Colorado program starting 2026; next fee cycle 2027, from detailed 2026 supply reports.
- **Incumbents:** CAA (new, building systems from scratch), haulers and MRF operators (collection and landfill revenue conflicts with recycling goals), and CPG brands with weak SKU-level packaging data.
- **Funded startups:** ForSURE (Utrecht, EPR compliance software, $3.6M total, seed Sept 2024); AMP Robotics ($91M Series D, Dec 2024) and Sortera on the sorting side. I found no sizable US producer-side EPR compliance round. Verdict: contested.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - SKU-level packaging data and fee reporting for CPG companies.
  - Eco-modulation optimization (redesign to lower fees).
  - Audit and dispute of PRO invoices.
  - Reuse and refill systems paid with fee credits.
  - Financing for MRF upgrades.
  - Recycled-content resin sourcing.
- **Insider roles:** CPG packaging engineers and sustainability directors, MRF operators, Oregon DEQ and Colorado CDPHE program staff, municipal recycling coordinators.

## 7. US farm equipment repair, maintenance and parts [HG]

- **Money flow:** farmers and custom operators pay dealers (and a minority independents) for parts, labor and diagnostics.
- **Size:** NASS Farm Production Expenditures: "farm supplies and repairs" of $24.0B in 2024. Source: https://data.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Highlights/2025/2025_FarmExpenditures_Highlights.pdf. Repair and maintenance alone was $18B in 2023 (Senate Ag deck citing ERS; not verified against the ERS table). PIRG estimates restrictions cost farmers $4.2B a year (about $1.2B excess repair cost plus about $3B downtime); that is an advocacy estimate.
- **Shock (2026-07):**
  - FTC and five states (IL, AZ, MI, MN, WI) settled with Deere on July 8, 2026: 10 years, same repair tools and software as dealers for farmers and independents, including parts pairing; no retaliation.
  - Separate $99M class settlement (April 2026) for repair payers since Jan 2018.
  - State right-to-repair bills (Iowa House passed HF 2763, 70-18, April 2026).
  - Sources: https://www.equipmentworld.com/business/article/15829573/john-deere-settles-ftc-states-lawsuit-over-right-to-repair and https://pirg.org/articles/deere-announces-99-million-settlement-in-right-to-repair-lawsuit/
- **Incumbents:** Deere, CNH, AGCO and Kubota dealer networks. One dealer anecdote: parts are 20% of revenue at 35% margin, service 10% at 70% margin. Dealers' profit center is service, which the settlement attacks directly.
- **Funded startups:** none found for independent farm repair or parts marketplaces in 2025-26. Verdict: open, but a long-standing used-equipment and parts ecosystem exists.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Dealer-equivalent diagnostics and subscription tools for mixed fleets.
  - Mobile technician network.
  - Independent shop parts procurement.
  - Remanufacturing.
  - Technician training.
  - Downtime insurance.
- **Insider roles:** independent ag repair shop owners, dealer service managers, custom harvesters, large-farm shop managers, former OEM service engineers.
- **Caution:** compliance and actual access are unproven until the order is entered and tested.

## 8. US water systems' mandated lead-line and PFAS capex [HG]

- **Money flow:** about 50,000 community water systems (fragmented) pay engineering firms, contractors, treatment media and equipment suppliers; funding comes through ratepayers, state revolving funds and federal infrastructure money.
- **Size:**
  - AWWA estimates PFAS compliance at $3.8-5.5B a year (relayed by secondary coverage); EPA's own estimate is about $1.5B a year.
  - Lead: EPA's final LCRI estimate is $1.47-1.95B a year in total (service line replacement $1.17-1.64B). National replacement estimates run $46B to over $90B; spread over 10 years that is about $4.6-9B a year.
  - On the AWWA basis the pool is about $8-15B a year; on EPA's basis it is about $3B a year, which is under the bar. Treat the size as disputed. Source: https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-10/final_lcri_fact-sheet_cost_benefit.pdf
- **Shock (2026-05):**
  - EPA announced May 14, 2025 that it would keep the 4 ppt PFOA/PFOS limits, rescind four others and extend the deadline.
  - Formal proposal May 20, 2026: extend PFOA/PFOS compliance from April 26, 2029 to April 26, 2031 for systems that ask.
  - LCRI compliance date stays Nov 1, 2027; oral argument in the AWWA v. EPA challenge was Sept 30, 2026, with a decision likely in 6-12 months.
  - Source: https://advocacy.sba.gov/2026/05/20/epa-proposes-to-extend-deadlines-and-revise-definitions-for-pfas-drinking-water-rule/
- **Incumbents:** engineering and design firms, utility contractors, media and equipment makers. Municipal procurement is slow and fragmented; many utilities lack complete service line inventories.
- **Funded startups:** BlueConduit (predictive lead-line identification; $2.25M across 5 rounds per an aggregator, last seed July 2023). I found no 2025-26 rounds for lead or PFAS water startups in search. Verdict: contested by large incumbents but thinly funded by venture money.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Turnkey lead-line replacement programs for small and mid utilities, solving homeowner consent and access.
  - Inventory verification data.
  - SRF grant and loan navigation for small systems.
  - PFAS treatment sold as a service (media change-out and regeneration).
- **Insider roles:** utility water-quality managers, state SRF program managers, GAC and ion exchange media suppliers, public works directors.
- **Risk:** timing. The PFAS deadline slipped and LCRI is in litigation.

## 9. US biofuel producers' fats, oils and grease feedstock [HG]

- **Money flow:** renewable diesel and biodiesel producers buy soybean oil, tallow, used cooking oil, corn oil and canola oil from crushers, renderers, collectors and importers.
- **Size:** EIA, July 2026 monthly feedstock use: 1,687 soybean oil, 453 corn oil, 330 canola, 771 tallow, 34 poultry fat, 610 yellow grease/UCO, 101 other, about 3,990 million lb in one month, or about 48B lb a year at that run rate. At an assumed $0.50-0.60 a lb (my assumption; I did not verify current prices), that is about $24-29B. Source: https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/pet_pnp_feedbiofuel_dcu_nus_m.htm
- **Shock (2026-03):**
  - EPA finalized 2026-27 RFS volumes in late March 2026 (published Apr 1): 9.07B gallons of biomass-based diesel for 2026, about 60% above 2025. One source gives 8.95B for 2027, so figures conflict.
  - From 2026, the 45Z credit applies only to North American feedstocks; from 2028 imported feedstock and fuel get half the RFS value.
  - Used cooking oil imports fell 38% year on year (China-origin down 67%). Soybean oil's biofuel share is about 54%, up from 40% two years earlier.
  - Source: https://dieselnet.com/news/2026/04epa.php
- **Incumbents:** large renderers, crushers (ADM, Bunge, Cargill), fragmented UCO collectors, and refiners. Feedstock supply is inelastic in the short run, so the scarcity rent goes to whoever controls domestic collection and documentation.
- **Funded startups:** none found in feedstock sourcing or traceability. Verdict: open, but capital heavy at scale and with strong incumbent renderers.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - Grease route-density consolidation with software.
  - Origin and carbon-intensity documentation for 45Z and ISCC audits.
  - Waste-oil brokerage with North American origin certification.
  - Carbon-intensity measurement for soybean growers.
  - New oilseed offtakes (camelina, carinata, pennycress).
- **Insider roles:** renderer procurement managers, UCO route collectors, refinery feedstock buyers, RIN traders, carbon-intensity auditors.

## 10. US metal scrap (ferrous and copper) purchases [HG]

- **Money flow:** generators (auto shredders, demolition, fabricators) sell to dealers and processors, who sell to mills, brass and rod mills, foundries and exporters.
- **Size (USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026):**
  - Iron and steel scrap: domestic purchases $19.7B in 2025 ($20.4B in 2024); consumption about 57M tons; exports fell to 13M tons from 15M; price about $319 a ton.
  - Copper: 160kt from old scrap plus 760kt from new scrap, about 920kt of contained copper. At 490 cents a lb (about $10,800 a ton), that is about $9.9B (my arithmetic). Copper from scrap is about 30% of US supply.
  - Total at least $29B, before aluminum and other metals.
  - Sources: https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-iron-steel-scrap.pdf and https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-copper.pdf
- **Shock (2025-07):**
  - Section 232 copper proclamation (July 30, 2025): 50% tariff on semi-finished and derivative copper from Aug 1, 2025; scrap, cathode and concentrate exempt. It directed Commerce to pursue domestic sales requirements, including 25% for high-quality copper scrap. I found no implementing rule. The White House did not impose scrap export controls.
  - A phased refined copper tariff (15% from Jan 1, 2027, 30% in 2028) was tied to a Commerce report due June 30, 2026.
  - US refined copper imports jumped to about 1.7Mt in 2025 from 0.9Mt, with year-end stocks of 450kt and net import reliance of 57% (USGS).
  - Source: https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/president-trump-orders-50-percent-section-232-tariff-copper-imports
- **Incumbents:** a few large processors (USGS: five leading processors handled 26M tons through 354 facilities in 2023) and a long tail of independent yards. Exporters and domestic buyers have opposed interests in any sales mandate.
- **Funded startups:**
  - Sortera: $30.5M Series C (alloy sorting).
  - AMP Robotics: $91M Series D (Dec 2024).
  - Redwood Materials: $350M (Nov 2025, battery materials).
  - Metaloop: EUR16M (2023).
  - ScrapAd (Spain): EUR6M Series A (Dec 2025).
  - Verdict: crowded in sorting, contested in marketplaces, with no dominant US B2B scrap marketplace found.
- **Wedge hints:**
  - B2B marketplace and price discovery for fabricators selling new scrap.
  - Chain-of-custody and grading certification if a domestic sales mandate is implemented.
  - Yard software (weighing, ID checks, payments).
  - Alloy-sorting as a service.
  - Fabricator scrap financing and hedging.
- **Insider roles:** scrap yard general managers, mill scrap buyers, brass mill metal managers, metals brokers, shredder operators, customs brokers.

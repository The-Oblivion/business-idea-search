# Round 2 pool map: aging rich-world economies (Japan, Korea, Germany, Italy, UK, US Northeast)

Written 2026-10-14/15. Ten spend pools, each with a size derivation, a dated 2025-2026 shock, incumbents, a crowdedness check, wedges and insider roles. FX used for rough conversions: 1 USD = 150 JPY, 1 EUR = 1.15 USD, 1 GBP = 1.30 USD.

Coverage note: Korea and Italy are not covered. Searches there did not surface primary sources in the time available, and I would not put a pool in the map without a source. Seven of the ten pools are hidden giants (marked HG).

Verification legend: [V] = opened or read in this session's search results with the figure shown; [D] = derived by me from cited inputs; [M] = from memory or a single secondary source, not re-verified. Anything marked [M] should be re-checked before it carries weight.

Honest summary of which pools look like real $100M-revenue paths: Japan home-visit care consolidation (9), Northeast heating-oil consolidation-plus-conversion (9), Germany Mittelstand succession financing (4), Japan trucking compliance/intermediary (7) and UK repairs/Awaab's Law (3). The US Main Street pool (10) and the England care-workforce pool (2) are real money but crowded. The NY CDPAP pool (1) is a statutory monopoly and mostly a political, not a startup, opportunity. Germany live-in care (5) is borderline on size.

---

## 1. New York CDPAP: Medicaid consumer-directed home care (US Northeast)

- **Money flow:** NY Medicaid (state + federal) pays a fiscal intermediary (FI), which pays personal assistants (often family or friends) chosen by about 280,000 disabled and elderly consumers.
- **Size:** about $9B a year. [V] Spectrum News (Jan 2025) calls it "a $9 billion Medicaid home care program" with about 280,000 users. Advocates (CIDNY) call the PPL contract "$11 billion"; that is their characterization. https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nys/central-ny/politics/2025/01/24/will-medicaid-home-care-changes-be-part-of-n-y--budget-talks-
- **Shock (2025-04):** On 1 April 2025 Public Partnerships LLC (PPL) became the single statewide FI, replacing roughly 600-700 FIs. A federal TRO on 31 March 2025 forced continuity of pay. Hochul claimed more than $1B saved by Jan 2026; the Fiscal Policy Institute says only about $500M is clearly admin savings. Advocates claim 150,000 workers and 90,000 consumers left the program (contested, unverified). [V] https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/press/releases/2025/03-20-2025_cdpap_update.htm ; https://www.wxxinews.org/text/new-york-public-news-network/2026-01-22/hochul-touts-cdpap-changes-but-advocates-arent-convinced
- **Incumbents:** PPL now holds the FI function by statute and contract. Advocates allege private-equity control and service failures (claim, unverified). About 700 displaced small FIs lost their business model.
- **Why fragile:** a single state contract with political exposure, a court record, and a payer (the state) that wants savings and a functioning program at the same time. The state cannot easily replace PPL quickly, so friction accumulates at the edges instead.
- **Funded startups:** none found at the FI layer, because it is closed by law (no dedicated startup search was run for this pool). Adjacent funded home-care platforms (Papa, Honor, Cera in the UK) exist [M].
- **Crowdedness:** contested (one entrenched statutory vendor; adjacent platforms funded).
- **Wedges:** (a) the 700 displaced FIs converting into licensed home care agencies (LHCSA) and needing back-office tooling; (b) caregiver/consumer enrollment, timesheet and benefits-navigation help layered on PPL; (c) the same "financial management services" function in the other ~45 states with self-directed Medicaid programs, which is a national pool that this shock exposes as bid-able.
- **Insider roles:** former FI owners and compliance leads, independent-living-center staff, Medicaid managed long-term care plan care managers, home-care payroll specialists, elder-law attorneys.
- **Hidden giant:** no.
- **Path to $100M:** only through the national FMS function (multi-state contracts), not NY alone. Heroic.

## 2. England adult social care: council purchases from independent providers

- **Money flow:** councils (council tax, central grants, NHS transfers) pay independent home-care and care-home providers, who pay care workers.
- **Size:** GBP 29.4B gross current expenditure on adult social care in 2024-25 (about $38B); GBP 23.6B is long-term support. Averages: GBP 23.56 an hour external home care; GBP 1,185.55 a week residential or nursing. [V] https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/adult-social-care-finance-report-england-2024-to-2025
- **Shock (2025-07):** On 22 July 2025 the Care Worker visa route closed to new overseas applicants (SOC 6135/6136); in-country switching allowed until 22 July 2028. Skills for Care 2025/26: international arrivals into direct-care roles fell from 105,000 (2023/24) to 50,000 (2024/25) to 30,000; posts held by British nationals fell by 40,000 in a year and 130,000 since 2020/21; vacancy rate 6.2% (about 96,000), a decade low; projected need of 410,000 more posts by 2040. Home Office licence revocations rose sharply in 2024-25 (a blog puts it above 470 licences and about 39,000 workers needing new sponsors; unverified). Employer NIC rise in April 2025 added cost [M]. [V] https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-care-worker-visa-route-closes ; https://www.localgov.co.uk/Social-care-vacancy-rate-falls-to-decade-low-but-challenges-ahead/64563
- **Incumbents:** thousands of small providers plus groups (HC-One, Bupa, Care UK) [M]; councils as a monopsony buyer that sets fee rates below cost; staffing agencies.
- **Why fragile:** providers cannot raise prices (council-set), cannot recruit abroad, and domestic supply is shrinking. The transition window to 2028 sets a clock.
- **Funded startups:** Cera (about $150M combined debt and equity, Jan 2025, reported valuation above $1B from one aggregator); Birdie (GBP 24.7M Series B, older); Florence (shift-matching, GBP 28.5M Series B, older). [V]
- **Crowdedness:** crowded on the software and platform side; contested on workforce supply.
- **Wedges:** domestic pipeline (recruit and train UK-born and returners), sponsorship-transfer matching for in-country switchers before July 2028, pay-on-demand and retention tooling for care workers, commissioning-side tools that let councils price true cost of care.
- **Insider roles:** registered managers, care-home recruitment leads, council commissioners, immigration solicitors, payroll bureau staff.
- **Hidden giant:** no.
- **Path to $100M:** exists in principle (a share of a GBP 23.6B purchase pool) but the buyer fragmentation and incumbent funding make it a crowded race.

## 3. England social landlords' repairs and maintenance (HG)

- **Money flow:** tenants' rents, grants and borrowing pay housing associations, which pay contractors (Mears, Wates, Kier, Morgan Sindall and similar [M]) and in-house repair teams, who pay trades.
- **Size:** GBP 10B in the year to 31 March 2025 for private registered providers (about $13B), of which GBP 3.9B capitalised; up 13% from GBP 8.8B; providers forecast an average GBP 10.9B a year over the next five years. This excludes council housing (HRA) repairs, so the true pool is larger. [V] https://www.gov.uk/government/news/regulator-of-social-housing-2025-global-accounts-published
- **Shock (2025-10):** Awaab's Law phase 1 in force 27 October 2025: emergency hazards within 24 hours; damp and mould investigation and make-safe deadlines of days; more hazards added in 2026 and 2027. The government's impact assessment shows a net cost to business of only GBP 10.8M a year; the sector (ALMOs, LGA) says that vastly understates cost, and a government official acknowledged the consultation-stage figure was too low. [V] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukia/2025/125/pdfs/ukia_20250125_en.pdf ; https://management.insidehousing.co.uk/home/ihm-editors-picks/the-first-days-of-awaabs-law-how-are-social-landlords-coping-94697
- **Incumbents:** large repair contractors and housing-management software vendors (NEC, MRI, Civica [M]). Nineteen providers with 40,000 or more homes hold 42% of units and 47% of turnover.
- **Why fragile:** schedule-of-rates contracts pay per job, not per outcome; landlords face hard legal clocks that their legacy systems cannot track; margins are already stretched by rent caps.
- **Funded startups:** Switchee (Mould iQ launched May 2025 with three housing associations covering 92,000 homes), Mobysoft (RepairSense, damp and mould module), Aico (HomeLINK sensors), Made Tech. Funding amounts not found. [V]
- **Crowdedness:** contested.
- **Wedges:** compliance clock and evidence trail for Awaab's Law cases (Ombudsman-ready); dispatch for sub-GBP 500 hazard jobs across small subcontractors; ventilation and mould-remediation retrofit crews as a service; landlords' contractor performance audit.
- **Insider roles:** repairs managers and damp-and-mould leads at housing associations, contractor operations directors, Housing Ombudsman case officers, surveyors.
- **Hidden giant:** yes.

## 4. Germany Mittelstand ownership succession (HG)

- **Money flow:** successors pay retiring owners; advisers, banks and KfW take fees and interest.
- **Size:** KfW Nachfolge-Monitoring 2025 (Jan 2026): about 109,000 successions a year planned through 2029 (545,000 of 3.87M firms), average intended sale price about EUR 499,000 (+34% since 2019). [D] 109,000 x EUR 499,000 = about EUR 54B (about $62B) a year, an upper bound because not every succession is a sale and the price is the owners' hope. IfM Bonn expects about 37,200 actual handovers a year in 2026-2030 (reported by Deutsche Handwerks Zeitung, unverified), about EUR 18B at the same price. The capturable layer (advice, M&A fees, financing spread) is perhaps 3-8%, so a $100M revenue path needs $1.5-3B of transacted value. [V] https://www.kfw.de/PDF/Download-Center/Konzernthemen/Research/PDF-Dokumente-Fokus-Volkswirtschaft/Fokus-2026/Fokus-Nr.-526-Januar-2026-Nachfolge-Monitoring.pdf
- **Shock (2026-01):** for the first time planned closures without a successor (about 114,000 a year) exceed succession wishes (109,000). More than 2M owners are 55 or older; 42% of firms planning closure cite bureaucracy (+12 points). Employee buy-out (28%) and management buy-out (24%) are being considered more than discussed. [V]
- **Incumbents:** Steuerberater (tax advisers), Sparkassen and Volksbanken, IHK and HWK succession advisers, M&A boutiques, nexxt-change (free state-backed listing board), KfW loans.
- **Why fragile:** free public substitutes set a zero price on matching; advisers skip sub-EUR 1M deals; information is hidden ("im Verborgenen"), valuations are opaque.
- **Funded startups:** none found with a 2023-2026 round. Historical: Carl Finance (EUR 3M, 2019), Valuedesk (EUR 5M, 2024, valuation SaaS, not succession), UnternehmerTUM ETA programme (Sept 2025). US analogs now funded: Baton, Rowan, Iconic. [V]
- **Crowdedness:** open on the German platform side, with the caveat that the free substitutes and thin small-deal economics are why.
- **Wedges:** EBO and MBO financing with KfW and Bürgschaftsbank guarantees; closure-prevention for the roughly 114,000 planned closures (buy-and-hold ETA vehicles); Handwerk (about 1M mittelständische Handwerksbetriebe) in installation and electrical trades; valuation benchmarks (KfW Table 1 shows multiples such as 1.2x revenue in some sectors).
- **Insider roles:** Steuerberater, IHK and HWK Nachfolgemoderatoren, Sparkasse Firmenkundenberater, Notare, Unternehmensmakler.
- **Hidden giant:** yes.

## 5. Germany live-in elder care ("24-Stunden-Betreuung") (HG)

- **Money flow:** households (topped up by Pflegegeld and the EUR 125 a month Entlastungsbetrag) pay a German placement agency and an Eastern European posting company, which pay live-in carers.
- **Size:** the Federal Health Ministry working group (Nov 2024) estimates 100,000-210,000 live-in carers present on a given day and 200,000-420,000 a year with rotation, with a "high dark figure" and no registry. Prices run above EUR 2,000 a month plus board, lodging and agency fees (older study, via search). [D] 100,000-210,000 x EUR 2,000-3,000 x 12 = about EUR 2.4-7.6B, midpoint about EUR 5B (about $5.8B). Borderline on the $5B floor; low confidence. [V for headcounts] https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/5_Publikationen/Pflege/Berichte/241127_Abschlussbericht_Live_Ins_bf.pdf
- **Shock (2026-06):** the BMG report (Nov 2024) states that working-time, rest-time and minimum-wage rules are often not met, with false self-employment common, and that even a German placement agency does not guarantee a legally safe model. The Federal Labour Court counts on-call time toward minimum wage (3-year back-claims). The BMG published a draft Pflegeneuordnungsgesetz (PNOG) on 5 June 2026; a Bundestag lobby-register entry dated 22 Sept 2026 ties live-in care reform to it. What the draft actually says about live-in care is unverified. Statutory minimum wage was EUR 12.41 in 2024; later increases not verified here. [V/partly unverified] https://www.vbw-bayern.de/Redaktion/Frei-zugaengliche-Medien/Abteilungen-GS/Sozialpolitik/2026/Downloads/RefE-Pflegeneuordnungsgesetz.pdf
- **Incumbents:** placement agencies (about 60 in 2009, 266 by 2017, no minimum standards) and posting companies, mostly in Poland and Romania; the legal models (Entsendung with A1, employer model, self-employed) all carry risk.
- **Why fragile:** a compliant model roughly forces employment, on-call pay and rest time, which raises cost sharply; agencies cannot pass that on without a subsidy.
- **Funded startups:** marta (Berlin, EUR 6.6M seed Aug 2022, over 13,000 families), Pflegehelden (sold to Rigeto 2021, over 3,000 customers), ennie 24h. [V]
- **Crowdedness:** contested.
- **Wedges:** compliant-employment infrastructure (A1 admin, time recording, payroll across borders); family-side financing that bridges Pflegekasse benefits; a certification standard (DIN SPEC exists but does not protect households).
- **Insider roles:** agency founders and dispatchers, Polish posting-company owners, Pflegekasse caseworkers, care counselors (Pflegeberater), labour-law attorneys.
- **Hidden giant:** yes.

## 6. Japan municipal water-supply pipe and facility renewal (HG)

- **Money flow:** water bills and general-account transfers fund municipal waterworks bureaus, which pay contractors, consultants and pipe makers.
- **Size:** Health Ministry forecast: replacing at the current rate needs an average of JPY 1.8T a year (about $12B) over FY2021-2050, about JPY 500B more than the prior decade; pipes alone JPY 33.5T over 30 years (JPY 28.3T if downsized). Sewers are additional (about 500,000 km, about 7% past 50 years, about 20% in 10 years) and not sized here. Replacement rate cited at 0.64% a year. [V via search summaries; the decn.co.jp article returned 404 on direct fetch] https://isvd.or.jp/en/columns/water-infrastructure-aging-crisis
- **Shock (2025-01):** the Yashio sinkhole (Saitama, 28 Jan 2025). The Land Ministry's inspection of large sewers older than 30 years found 297 of 621 km inspected showed exposed rebar or cracks needing repair within five years, and 72 km needed work within one year. [V] https://asianews.network/?p=231259
- **Incumbents:** municipal waterworks bureaus, consulting engineers, pipe contractors, pipe makers (Kubota, Kurimoto [M]); AI diagnostics from Fracta Japan (with NTT Business Solutions, Kakegawa, 1,062 km, Nov 2025), Hitachi Systems, Marubeni.
- **Why fragile:** shrinking customer bases, falling water volumes, aging engineer workforces, and a decision rule (a fixed 40-year legal life) that does not match real pipe condition.
- **Funded startups:** no 2025 venture round found for a Japanese water-pipe AI company (funding amounts not found). Robotics: Maeda/Sumitomo Mitsui/Univ. of Tokyo; Bwell Technology (Shenzhen) filed for Hong Kong listing. [V]
- **Crowdedness:** contested (corporate and consulting players; thin venture funding).
- **Wedges:** condition-based prioritization to stretch asset lives (a Tokyo paper argues 80 years for sewers); void and cavity detection for sewer back-side; wide-area utility consolidation back-office; PFI and operator bundles.
- **Insider roles:** municipal waterworks engineers, sewer inspection contractors, pipe manufacturers' sales engineers, MLIT officials, consulting engineers.
- **Hidden giant:** yes. Caveat: the buyer is Japanese municipal procurement; hard to enter without a local partner.

## 7. Japan trucking: shippers' spend on contracted road freight (HG)

- **Money flow:** shippers pay prime carriers and forwarders, who sub-contract down multi-tier chains of small carriers.
- **Size:** [D] about 63,000 operators (MLIT, undated) x JPY 264M average 2023 revenue (All Japan Trucking Association) = about JPY 16.6T (about $110B). Whole logistics market about JPY 24.2T FY2023 per a secondary summary of MLIT data. Of operators, 99.5% have capital of JPY 300M or less. [V for inputs, D for product] https://book.st-hakky.com/en/industry/logistics-market-size-25-trillion-yen-2026-kpi-trends
- **Shock (2026-01):** the Subcontract Act became the Act on Proper Transactions for SME Entrusted Businesses on 1 January 2026, widening coverage to companies with more than 300 staff; the Trucking Business Act amendment requires written contracts specifying charges (including fuel surcharges) and an actual-carrier register; designated shippers must appoint a Chief Logistics Officer and file a first medium- to long-term plan by the end of October 2026. [V] https://en.worksap.co.jp/insights/toriteki-act-fy2026/ ; https://www.nri.com/jp/media/column/mcs_blog/20260615.html
- **Incumbents:** Yamato, Sagawa, NX, Fukuyama [M]; 60,000-plus small carriers; forwarders earning layered margins.
- **Why fragile:** the new laws attack intermediary margin and unpaid waiting time; small carriers lack compliance capacity; the driver overtime cap (2024 problem) shrinks capacity.
- **Funded startups:** Hacobu (MOVO; JPY 700M in May 2025, cumulative above JPY 5.6B), T2 (autonomous trucking; JPY 5B pre-Series B Aug 2025, JPY 16.5B-plus cumulative per one July 2026 report). [V]
- **Crowdedness:** contested.
- **Wedges:** compliance back-office for 60,000 small carriers (contracts, itemized charges, registers); waiting-time and berth data for shippers' CLO plans due October 2026; accessorial-charge billing and collection.
- **Insider roles:** transport managers (unko kanrisha), dispatchers at mid-size carriers, forwarder pricing staff, warehouse berth managers, JFTC and MLIT enforcement officers.
- **Hidden giant:** yes.

## 8. Japan home-visit long-term care (HG)

- **Money flow:** municipal LTC insurers (half tax, half premiums) plus 10-30% copays pay home-visit agencies, which pay helpers.
- **Size:** JPY 1,214,189M in FY2024 (JPY 1.21T, about $8.1B), up 5.1%; all LTC costs JPY 11.94T. [V] https://www.mhlw.go.jp/toukei/saikin/hw/kaigo/kyufu/24/dl/03.pdf
- **Shock (2025-06):** the April 2024 cut to home-visit base fees (about 2-3%) was followed by record bankruptcies: 176 care-provider bankruptcies in 2025 (second straight record), 91 of them home-visit (third straight); 87% of the bankrupt had fewer than 10 staff (Tokyo Shoko Research via secondary summary). Villages with zero home-visit offices: 107 at end-2024 (Kyodo), 116 at end-2025, with 285 municipalities left with a single office by June 2026. [V via search] https://www.sakigake.jp/news/article/20250625CO0170/ ; https://www.tsr-net.co.jp/data/detail/1202048_1527.html
- **Incumbents:** large chains (Sompo Care, Nichii [M]), municipal social-welfare councils, thousands of micro-agencies.
- **Why fragile:** per-visit reimbursement leaves rural travel time unpaid; helpers are aging; a fee cut hit while wages needed to rise.
- **Funded startups:** CareMaker (about JPY 100M, Feb 2025, visiting-nurse SaaS), CareFran (care-plan platform, Dec 2025), EMC Healthcare (Jan 2026). No large round found for home-visit billing or records. [V]
- **Crowdedness:** open to contested.
- **Wedges:** roll-up of distressed micro-agencies with shared back office; municipality-level shared helper pools and route optimization; claim optimization for treatment-improvement add-ons; foreign specified-skilled-worker caregivers (home-visit allowed from April 2025 [M]).
- **Insider roles:** care managers (kea mane), agency service managers, municipal LTC section staff, Kokuho-ren billing clerks, social-insurance labour consultants (sharoushi).
- **Hidden giant:** yes. Caveat: operating a Japanese care business is hard for a foreign team.
- **Path to $100M:** consolidating about 1% of the pool (JPY 12B) is plausible; this is the best path in the Japan trio, but requires Japanese operator talent.

## 9. Northeast US home heating oil retail (HG)

- **Money flow:** homeowners pay local oil dealers (delivery plus service contracts plus installs); dealers pay terminals and wholesalers.
- **Size:** [D] EIA via Fuel Oil News (Nov 2025): about 5.0M US households (4.1M in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic) use oil as main heating fuel; expected winter spend about $1,390 each at about $3.50 a gallon and about 400 gallons, giving about $6.9B for primary-heating homes, plus service and installs (19% of Star Group's revenue) and commercial and multifamily oil, so an estimated $8-10B. Household counts date to EIA's 2020 survey (cited in Star's filing). [V for inputs] https://fueloilnews.com/2025/11/18/eia-winter-fuels-outlook-2/
- **Shock (2025-11):** Star Group FY2025 10-K: net account loss 4.7% (3.6% in FY2023); gross losses 13.5%; conversions explain only about 1.3% of lost accounts. NY and CT raised the bioheat minimum from 5% to 10% on 1 July 2025, RI from 10% to 20%. NY's all-electric building law start (1 Jan 2026) was delayed on 12 Nov 2025 pending an appeal. A $450M New England Heat Pump Accelerator (midstream incentives, first products Feb 2026). [V] https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002590/000119312525311490/sgu-20250930.htm
- **Incumbents:** Star Group (largest, 5.5%-plus share, 406,400 full-service and 63,200 delivery-only customers, bought one oil and three propane businesses for $80.5M in FY2025), plus a long tail of family dealers (trade group NEFI represents about 1,100 oil and propane providers).
- **Why fragile:** fixed route and truck costs against 3.6-4.7% net annual attrition produce a density death spiral; owners are aging; trade associations lobby against heat pumps instead of converting.
- **Funded startups:** Dandelion Energy (geothermal; $40M Series C reported around late 2024; targets NY, CT, MA oil and gas homes). No venture-funded dealer roll-up or dealer-software startup found. [V]
- **Crowdedness:** contested on electrification; open on dealer consolidation and conversion.
- **Wedges:** buy dealers' customer books and convert them to heat-pump, hybrid and service contracts (using the $450M incentives and rebates); delivery route and degree-day SaaS; dealer succession brokerage; financing for oil-to-heat-pump swaps at the moment of burner failure.
- **Insider roles:** dealer owners and office managers, oilheat burner technicians, terminal operators, trade-group (NEFI/NORA) staff, state energy-office program managers.
- **Hidden giant:** yes.
- **Path to $100M:** about 60,000-70,000 customers at about $1,500 a year; roughly 15-25 small-dealer acquisitions. Thin margins and a shrinking base; it only works as conversion, not as an oil business.

## 10. US Main Street business sales: SBA 7(a) change-of-ownership financing

- **Money flow:** buyers (mostly with SBA-guaranteed bank loans) pay retiring owners; brokers, lenders and closing professionals take fees.
- **Size:** SBA FY2025: $37B across 77,600 7(a) loans. Change-of-ownership loans: more than $9B (23.6% of 7(a) dollars, per the Coleman Report) or $8.29B and about 7,003 deals (CT Acquisitions); BizBuySell Q3 2025: 2,599 closed deals worth $2.13B in the quarter (about $8.5B annualized on its marketplace). So about $8-9B a year; the fee layer (broker and lender) is under $1B. Borderline on the $5B floor and weak on the $100M-revenue test. [V] https://www.sba.gov/article/2025/09/30/trump-sba-delivers-record-capital-small-businesses-fy25
- **Shock (2026-08):** SOP 50 10 8 (1 June 2025): at least 10% equity injection; seller note counts only on full standby and for at most 50% of the injection. A citizenship rule effective 1 March 2026 (100% of owners must be US citizens or nationals; permanent residents ineligible; not confirmed against the SBA notice). SOP 50 10 8.1 issued 14 Aug 2026, effective 1 Oct 2026: debt-service coverage on historical earnings only, a quality-of-earnings report for deals of $3M or more, and 7(a) Small no longer usable for ownership changes. [V via lender blogs] https://colemanreport.com/this-just-in-sba-releases-sop-50-10-8-1-effective-october-1-2026/
- **Incumbents:** Live Oak Bank (top acquisition lender), Huntington, First Internet Bank; brokers and the BizBuySell marketplace.
- **Funded startups:** Baton ($10M Series A, Jan 2025), Iconic ($6M seed, May 2026), Rowan ($3.3M seed, Mar 2026), DealFlowAgent (about $646K-750K, 2026), Boom ($3.3M seed), Acquire.com (older rounds). [V]
- **Crowdedness:** crowded.
- **Wedges:** lightweight earnings normalization for sub-$3M deals (below the QoE threshold); seller-note standby servicing; non-citizen buyer alternatives.
- **Insider roles:** SBA lender underwriters, business brokers, CPAs who do quality-of-earnings work, SBA closing attorneys.
- **Hidden giant:** no. Honest verdict: real shock, small and crowded fee pool; likely dies on the size test.

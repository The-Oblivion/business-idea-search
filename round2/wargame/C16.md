# C16 war-game: Regional Partner Group (rollover-first anchor-and-fill consolidator of independent uniform and linen laundries)

Written 2026-10-24 as if founding on Monday 2026-10-26. About 65 searches and fetches. "Mine" marks my own arithmetic or judgment. "Unverified" marks vendor, broker or search-snippet claims I could not open at the primary source. Where a page returned 403 (TRSA, WFIN) I used the search snippet and say so.

## 0. Bottom line

The idea is a conventional buy-and-build with a thin edge, and the war-game found the edge is already on offer from several buyers with more money and a track record. Wildman (family operator, keeps names and teams, owner stays on) and Alsco (fifth-generation family firm, four tuck-ins in about 18 months) are the closest. Unitex just took the first outside equity in its 100-plus-year history from CVC with the family staying in charge, and acquisitions are named as a use. My anchor model gives about 8% IRR in the median case and about 1% if entry is 7.5x. The only reason to start is that the first test costs almost nothing and can kill it in three weeks. Do the test. Do not raise money, quit anything, or incorporate a vehicle before it passes.

The peer-trust channel the pitch relies on (owner-to-owner sourcing through TRSA) is already occupied by the most acquisitive peers. The TRSA board chair runs a buyer.

### How Alsco responds once the platform is visible (mine)

1. Within weeks of any visible activity (a TRSA presence, a lender call, an adviser mention), it approaches the same owners with a strategic price. Route density lets it pay 7-9x for route-dense books versus 5-6x for route-thin ones ([CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/how-to-sell-a-uniform-rental-business/), a buy-side broker, unverified). On a $3.75M-EBITDA anchor, each turn is $3.75M, so the gap to a 6.0x bid is $3.75M-11M.
2. It leads with trust and track record ("family, four recent deals, no disruption"), which a new vehicle cannot answer.
3. Wildman adds equity or earn-out language; Unitex/CVC can offer stock in a bigger family-controlled group.
4. Where it has a plant within about 60 miles, it wins on synergy. It will not fight where it has no density.

### Counter-move

- **Screen out Alsco/Wildman/Cintas/UniFirst plant overlap before the first call** using their branch locators. Bid only where a strategic cannot fold the book into existing routes, and accept that this thins the target pool to a handful a year (mine).
- **Do not enter any process with two or more strategics.** The refined pitch already says this; hold to it.
- **Win on what an all-cash family buyer cannot give:** 20-35% rollover pari passu, a board seat, a formula put after year 4, the real estate kept on a market lease, and a 120-day close with proof of funds attached to the LOI. A $5.6M rollover on a $22.5M price becomes about $8-11M at the 1.5-2.0x MOIC in my model, so rollover only closes the price gap if the seller believes the thesis. That belief needs references, which is why deal 1 is the whole game.
- **Get references from deal 1's owner family** doing the owner calls for deal 2; peer trust beats pitch.
- **Treat Alsco and Wildman as exit buyers and partners as well as rivals.** Do not make an enemy of the buyer you most plausibly sell to in year 6.
- **A fact that cuts both ways:** WARN notices were filed for Advanced Uniform Dust Control & Linen, LLC (Patterson CA, 89 workers; Salinas CA, 4 workers; filed 2026-01-05, effective 2026-03-06), the same week Alsco announced Advanced Linen ([WARN tracker](https://www.warntracker.com/layoff/advanced-uniform-dust-control-linen-2026-03-06)). I could not confirm that entity is the one Alsco bought, that it closed plants, or that workers were not rehired. If confirmed, "no plant closure, no route change in year one" becomes a real counter in front of owners who care about their people. Do not use it before it is confirmed.

## 4. Quit conditions

Stop and set alive=false (do not narrow to a boutique) if any of these occurs:

1. **By Fri 2026-11-20:** fewer than 2 of the first 25 owners engage, or fewer than 5 agree to a second meeting or share financials (2-4 allows one two-week extension, no more).
2. **By 2026-11-20:** every price owners name or have been offered is 8x EBITDA or 1.5x revenue and up, or advisers say two-strategic processes are the norm at $15-25M revenue.
3. **By 2026-11-20:** no lender signals 3.5x or more on a $3M-plus EBITDA anchor with rollover and holdback.
4. **By 2026-11-20:** fewer than 2 advisers can cite a closed 2025-26 sub-$15M deal at or below 1.2x revenue.
5. **By 2027-01-24:** fewer than 3 signed IOIs, or none from an owner who has been approached by a strategic or PE buyer; or no capital-partner soft-circle of at least $4M; or the operator co-founder is not committed in writing.
6. **By 2027-07-31:** no LOI with proof of funds on an anchor at or below 6.5x. **By 2027-10-31 (month 12):** anchor not closed.
7. **Cash:** more than $250K spent out of pocket without a signed LOI.
8. **Price:** the anchor needs more than 7.0x normalized EBITDA. At 7.5x my model returns about 1% IRR.

## 5. Unit economics

### Price (what each side pays)

- **Sellers:** the platform buys at 5.5-6.5x normalized three-year EBITDA (about 0.85-1.0x revenue at a 15% margin), walks above 7x, with 65-80% cash, 20-35% rollover and a holdback of up to 1x EBITDA. Strategic comparables run higher: 7-9x for route-dense books per CT Acquisitions (unverified), 2.9-3.3x revenue for UniFirst/Clean Uniform, 2.26x revenue for Cintas/UniFirst (dossier).
- **Capital partners:** the sponsor earns a closing fee of about 2% of EV (the most common level; 56% of respondents in the [Citrin Cooperman 2025 survey](https://www.citrincooperman.com/In-Focus-Resource-Center/Uncharted-No-More-Fees-and-Carried-Interest-in-the-Independent-Sponsor-Sector); about 60% of deals roll closing economics per [McGuireWoods 2022](https://media.mcguirewoods.com/publications/2022/Independent-Sponsor-Survey-May2022.pdf)), a management fee of 5% of trailing EBITDA (most common in that 2022 survey), and carry (rate not found; 15-25% over a preferred return is the usual range, unverified).

The real payback is equity (rollover and carry) at an exit in year 5-7, not fees.

### Capital to first revenue

- **About $350-550K**: $120-250K at risk if a deal breaks (QoE, counsel, environmental) plus $150-300K of founder runway for 9-12 months. First revenue is the acquired business's revenue at close, months 9-12, plus fees at that date. Equity for the anchor is $3.9M cash plus $5.6M rollover; the capital partner funds it at LOI, not the founders.

## 6. Biggest risk, mitigation, founder fit

**Biggest risk.** Sellers have better, trusted alternatives, so the platform either cannot buy at or below about 6.5x or buys only what the strategics decline (adverse selection: deferred capex, key-person risk, no fold-in value). Nobody found a closed 2025-26 multiple for a sub-$15M independent, and at 7.5x the business returns about 1%.

**Mitigation.**
- Run the 25-call test and the 60-day IOI gate before spending anything; the kill rules are in Section 4.
- Sort the target pool by conditions where price is not set by a strategic: no strategic plant within about 60 miles, owners with a capital or transition reason (the Tier A and B names), and rollover as the thing they value.
- Underwrite at 6.0x with a hard 7.0x walk-away and a 0.5-1.0x holdback tied to retention. Treat Tier A firms as minority-recap conversations now and control conversations after commissioning (Arway mid-2026, Gunderson May 2027, Loop about Oct 2027).
- Keep the operator co-founder's role as the credibility bridge; an acquired owner family becomes the regional bench with pari passu equity and a 5-8% rep pool, as the refined pitch says.

**Founder fit.** A two- or three-person team: (1) an operator who has run a plant or regional P&L at a TRSA-type laundry, is known to family owners, and has a clean non-compete; (2) a deal-and-finance partner with lender and capital-partner relationships and prior independent-sponsor or lower-middle-market closing experience; (3) optionally an environmental/industrial-labor-literate counsel on call. Each founder needs $150-300K of personal runway or a salary elsewhere, because fee cash arrives only after the first close. A solo founder, or a team without an operator the owners already trust, loses on the credibility gap alone.

## 7. Verdict

Not attractive to start on Monday unless the test passes. The offer is already sold by buyers with more cash and a record, the median return is about 8%, and a seller's price decides everything. The three-week owner test is cheap and informative, so run that and nothing more.

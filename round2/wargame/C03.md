# C03 war-game: HCBS Operations Utility for managed-LTSS plans

War-gamed 2026-10-24 as if founding on Mon 2026-10-26. Inputs: h2h brief C03, refined pitch, dossier. About 55 searches and fetches this session.

Evidence tags: (S) read in a primary source this session; (S-sum) seen only in a search summary or secondary site; (S-c) carried from the brief or dossier, not re-fetched; (A) my arithmetic or assumption; (U) unverified.

## 0. Bottom line

**Narrow yes, big no.** Real, dated triggers exist right now, and a priced pilot can be tested for under $1M. But the three things that decide it are unfavorable: the strongest rival (Carelon/CareBridge) already sells 24/7 HCBS operations to rival plans, the EVV data layer in three of the five target states (Minnesota, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin) is owned by HHAeXchange (directly or via Sandata), and the labor rung (R3) is roughly break-even on my math. Treat it as a 95-day falsifiable test of "will a non-Elevance, non-UHC plan pay a fixed fee for an exceptions desk plus HCBS back office," not as a $100M company.

Three findings that change the pitch:

1. **Timing is better than the pitch assumed.** Arizona's ALTCS E/PD re-bid (RFP YH27-0001) closed 9/24/2026 with five offerors; oral presentations run 10/26-11/10/2026, award is anticipated 1/5/2027, and services start 10/1/2027 (S). That is a nine-month readiness window for the winners, inside my 90-day plan. Pennsylvania's CHC award was voided 4/2/2026 (S). UCare's Medicaid business moves to Medica on 10/1/2026 (S-sum). CareSource is integrating two acquired LTSS plans (S).
2. **R3 cannot be priced per member.** Arizona caps a case manager at a weighted 96, which is 43 own-home HCBS members per case manager (S, RFP text). My in-house cost per such member is about $3,500, not the pitch's $1,300-2,100. R3 has to be sold per coordinator seat, and at 95% of in-house cost my cost to serve leaves a 0-8% margin, not 10-15% (A, section 5).
3. **The sale is a deadline sale, not a neutrality sale.** The 4/2026 Pennsylvania ruling, the Arizona award and the UCare handover all force plans to prove operational readiness on a date. That is where a fixed-price, variable-capacity desk has a hook that "neutral" never did.

## 1. Strongest incumbent response, and the counter

### Who is strongest

**Carelon/CareBridge (Elevance Health).** It is the only rival that already sells the same thing (24/7 HCBS operations plus data) to the same buyers, with scale and a balance sheet.

What they already sell and own:
- "Nearly 200,000 patients across 16 states and DC," 24/7 D-SNP model added 2025 (S-c, Elevance page via dossier). CareBridge's own site says 18 states plus DC (S-c).
- Goodwill of $1,827M plus $305M finite-lived intangibles from the CareBridge purchase (S-c, FY2025 10-K via dossier).
- **It is the EVV layer for all three Iowa managed care plans, including the two rivals.** Iowa HHS says the MCOs "have chosen CareBridge as the EVV solution" and that all use the same vendor; a May 18, 2026 Iowa EVV presentation still lists CareBridge as the provider-facing vendor ([Iowa HHS](https://hhs.iowa.gov/media/18729/download), S-sum). Participants: Iowa Total Care (Centene), Molina, Wellpoint Iowa (Elevance).
- Q3 2026 (reported 10/21/2026): Carelon Services segment revenue $7,708M (+5.2%), operating gain $292M (+33.3%), described as driven by "continued expansion of Carelon Services risk-based solutions" ([Elevance 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001156039/000115603926000068/a3q2026elvearningsrelease.htm), S). Elevance Medicaid membership 8.22M, down 4.9% (S).
- Q2 call (7/15/2026): management said CareBridge delivers mid-teens medical savings and is being expanded into new markets (S-sum; the brief quotes "continue to accelerate the deployment of CareBridge," S-c).
- Elevance is exiting Medicaid markets: left DC, says more exits over 12-18 months, 2026 is the Medicaid margin trough ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/elevance-medicaid-momentum-q3-2026-elv/833559/), S). That shrinks captive CareBridge volume, so Carelon needs third-party plans more, not less.

Second threat, **HHAeXchange** (Hg and Cressey backed; 180+ MCOs per dossier):
- EVV dashboards give payers missed-visit, short/late-start and unconfirmed-visit widgets, and a Dec 2025 newsletter added configurable alerts for members with no confirmed visits and recurring missed visits ([HHAX payer dashboard docs](https://knowledge.hhaexchange.com/payer/Content/Documentation/Payer/Payer-Dashboard-EVV-P.htm), S-sum). The software half of R1 is already sold.
- It is the state-sponsored EVV system in Minnesota (S-sum). It bought Sandata in 2024 ([Healthcare IT Today](https://www.healthcareittoday.com/2024/11/12/hhaexchange-acquires-sandata-technologies-enhancing-ability-to-serve-homecare-providers-payers-and-caregivers-nationwide/), S-sum), and Sandata is the listed EVV vendor in Pennsylvania and Wisconsin (S-sum, older sources). Arizona is the exception: AHCCCS ended Sandata and brought its EVV aggregator in-house on 10/1/2025 (S-sum).
- It launched managed revenue-cycle services for agencies in April 2026 (S-sum), so it now sells human-in-the-loop services. An "exceptions desk" is a short step.

Others: CareCentrix (independent LTSS, carved out of Walgreens Aug 2025; I found no 2026 Medicaid contract news, S-sum); plan in-house teams (Centene 338K LTSS lives, S-c); Cityblock/Homeward (adjacent).

### How Carelon responds once we show traction (ordered by likelihood)

1. **Bundle and underprice.** CareBridge EVV is free to Iowa providers; Carelon can quote EVV, data feed and a 24/7 desk as one line at or below our R1 $300 (A). A $300 fixed fee loses to a bundle priced near zero to a plan that also takes risk.
2. **Copy the neutrality clause.** A firewall, audit rights and a governance seat are contract language and Elevance can add them in one quarter (brief critic 1, S-c). Boudreaux's public line is that Carelon will "serve all its customers."
3. **Offer risk instead of a fee.** "We take the risk, you pay nothing up front" beats a CFO-visible fixed fee in a 2026 cost-cutting environment (Medicaid MLR pressure, S-c).
4. **Poach the reference plan or the ops lead**, then make a purchase offer. The 2022 pattern was investor then acquirer (Anthem into CareBridge, then Elevance bought it, S-c).
5. **Use the EVV position** in Iowa-type states to make our data ingestion harder.

### Counter-move

- **Do not fight on risk or EVV.** Sell only fixed-price operations that sit in administrative budget. Never carry downstream risk (also avoids licensure).
- **Be feed-agnostic and make it a contract term:** plan-held data rights, daily export, termination without penalty on change of control (customer-held, per the refined pitch). Work through the plan's own data rights; in Arizona the aggregator is the state's, so no vendor gatekeeps it.
- **Pick states where CareBridge is not the EVV layer** (Arizona, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Virginia in this list), and pool two or three competing plans on one desk. That is the only place neutrality is worth money, because the staffing floor is shared (section 5).
- **Be the second source that disciplines Carelon's price**, and treat a Carelon or HHAX acquisition inquiry as the exit, not an attack. Quit trigger: if Carelon or HHAX publicly launches a managed exceptions desk at or under about $200 per member-year before the first pilot signs, stop (section 4).
- Partner with HHAX where it is the data layer (it is also the likeliest acquirer and channel).

## 3. 90-day plan (Mon 10/26/2026 to Fri 1/22/2027)

Key external dates: AHCCCS orals 10/26-11/10; award 1/5/2027; the plan day 90 is 1/24/2027.

**Weeks 1-2 (10/26-11/6): priced-offer sprint, cost $0-3k.**
- One-page offer: R1 $300, R2 $600 per member-year; R3 sold per coordinator seat (see section 5); 90-day paid pilot at cost plus 10%.
- 40 contacts at 10 or more non-Elevance, non-UHC plan entities (the ten above plus regional plans); 3-4 expert-network calls with former plan LTSS directors.
- Output: 12 or more calls, 8 or more with director-level or higher; 5 or more loaded-cost-per-member numbers; documented answers on who signs and which budget line pays.

**Weeks 3-6 (11/9-12/4): data proof.**
- Two free retrospectives on 2,000 or more members each: three-way match of EVV visits, authorizations and paid claims, plus measured exceptions per member-month and time-to-resolve.
- Bind E&O and cyber ($2M), start a SOC 2 Type I readiness program, draft MSA, BAA and a delegation-oversight pack that maps to 42 CFR 438.230 (revocable, annual review, CAP).
- Output: two written reports; measured exceptions rate and resolution-time distribution; leakage dollars per 1,000 members.

**Weeks 7-10 (12/7-1/8): convert.**
- Send pilot SOWs to 4 or more plans. On 1/5/2027, within 48 hours of the AHCCCS award, send a "readiness and transition capacity" offer to every Arizona awardee.
- Output: 1 signed LOI or pilot SOW of at least $150k (90 days, cost plus 10%) with a named budget owner; at least 2 of the 4 SOW recipients in legal or compliance review.

**Weeks 11-13 (1/11-1/22): go-live prep.**
- Contract a staffing partner for 6-10 desk agents on 24/7 shifts, run a one-week shadow mode on the plan's data, write the plan's delegation notice package.
- Output: pilot live or SOW signed by 1/22; measured baseline on time-to-activate backup care and percent of exceptions resolved within 4 hours.

**Day-90 scorecard:** 12 or more calls; 5 or more loaded-cost numbers; 2 retrospectives delivered; 1 signed pilot or LOI of $150k or more; 4 plans at proposal stage; cash spent under $400k.

## 4. Quit conditions (measurable, dated)

1. **11/6/2026 (day 12):** after 12 calls, 6 of the first 8 director-or-higher respondents insist on savings-only, in-house or CareBridge, or fewer than 2 will name a budget owner. Stop. (Matches the brief's kill line.)
2. **12/4/2026 (day 40):** none of 10 asked plans will share a de-identified sample of 2,000 or more members. No data, no product. Stop.
3. **12/31/2026 (day 66):** both retrospectives show fewer than 0.5 exceptions per member-month and median resolution under 4 hours. R1 has no pain. Stop or pivot to pure claims-leakage.
4. **1/22/2027 (day 88):** no signed pilot or LOI of $150k or more at cost plus 10%. Stop, or at most spend 60 more days on a plan that has put the fee in writing.
5. **Any time before the first pilot signs:** Carelon or HHAeXchange publicly launches a managed exceptions desk priced at or below about $200 per member-year, or Carelon announces a firewalled third-party HCBS operations unit with a plan customer. Stop.
6. **7/31/2027 (month 9):** no paid contract annualized at $1M or more, or R1 gross margin under 20% at 6,000 or more pooled members.
7. **10/29/2027 (month 12):** under $1M ARR (the brief uses "no paid pilot of $1M annualized by month 12").
8. **10/2028 (month 24):** under $5M ARR or a single parent over 60% of revenue. R3: gross margin under 10% at 5,000 lives means drop R3 and the $100M claim.

### R1 exceptions desk: cost to serve

- Variable: 1.5 x 8 min x 12 = 2.4 hours per member-year. At $70k / 1,500 = $46.7 per productive hour, that is $112 per member-year. Add 8% supervision, $15 RN triage, $15 tooling, 10% overhead: about **$165 per member-year**.
- Staffing floor for 24/7: 2 seats day and evening plus 1 overnight is 40 seat-hours per day, 14,600 hours per year, about 9.7 FTE, plus a supervisor and on-call RN triage and tooling: about **$1.0M per year**.
- The floor binds until about 6,200 members ($1.0M / $165). At 3,000 members cost is about $340 per member against a $300 price, a loss. At 6,200 or more pooled members, cost is $165 against $300, **gross margin about 45%**.
- This matches the pitch's illustration ($280 at 3,000 members) and says its $100 at 30,000 members is optimistic: my number stays near $165 because the work is human handling time (A).

### R2 operations stack

R2 adds reauthorization and reassessment scheduling, provider reliability scoring, program-integrity workflows. Add about $170 per member-year (analysts at roughly 1 per 1,500 members, data and engineering amortization, other). R2 cost about $335 against a $600 price, **gross margin about 44%** at 6,000 or more members.

### R3 coordination capacity: why the pitch's margin does not hold

In-house cost per case-manager seat (A): base $90k, loaded x1.28 = $115k; supervision 1 per 10 at $14.7k; overhead 15%. About **$149k per seat**.

| Setting | Members per case manager | In-house cost per member-year |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona own-home HCBS | 43 | about $3,470 |
| Arizona alternative HCBS | 53 | about $2,810 |
| Virginia/Pennsylvania-style waiver | 75 | about $1,990 |
| Institutional / low-need | 96 | about $1,550 |

- The pitch's $1,300-2,100 coordination cost corresponds to the 75-96 range. In Arizona the same service costs about $3,500, so a flat $1,500 R3 price is far below in-house cost there and a flat price does not work anywhere. **Price R3 per seat.**
- At 95% of in-house cost, a seat bills about $141k. My cost to deliver that seat with the same wages is about $143k (wages, supervision, 10% overhead). That is a **margin of roughly -2% to +5%**. To reach the pitch's 10-15% I need about 15% lower cost than the plan, and the ratio caps headcount while in-person visits block geographic arbitrage. Skill mix helps only where the contract lets navigators carry non-clinical tasks.
- The Truven/AdvancingStates deck calls full delegation of LTSS coordination rare (S-c), and AHCCCS bars delegated agreements that "inhibit integrated service delivery" for Medicaid or D-SNP lines and does not allow delegating quality-of-care investigations (S, RFP text). My read: R3 is a staffing pass-through at 0-8% margin. **Sell R3-lite (after-hours RN triage and surge capacity) only, and only after R1-R2 are live.**

### Price, blended margin, and the first contract (A)

- **Price:** R1 $300, R2 $600 per member-year. R3 per seat, about $141k (95% of $149k), 0-8% margin, not in the first 12 months.
- **Pilot:** 3,500 members, R1 only. Cost max($1.0M floor, 3,500 x $165) = about $1.0M per year; at cost plus 10% revenue is $1.13M per year ($323 per member), so a 90-day pilot bills about $283k with about $26k gross profit.
- **Year 1 after conversion:** 6,500 pooled members (for example 3,500 plus 3,000 across two competing plans), R2 at $600: revenue $3.9M; cost R1 $1.07M + R2 add $1.10M = $2.18M; **gross profit about $1.7M (44%)**.
- **Blended gross margin with R3:** the brief's 34% needs R3 near 10%+ margin. With R1-R2 at 44% and R3 at about 4%, the blend is about 28% if R3 is 40% of revenue and about 20% in the upside case, where R3 is 59% of revenue ($60M of $101M) (A).
- **Revenue ceiling:** without R3 about $55-60M (brief), so the $100M case needs a labor rung that my math says is thin.

## 6. Biggest risk and mitigation

**Biggest risk: no plan treats exceptions handling as a budgeted, owned line, so the offer is a nice-to-have that a cost-cutting plan cuts first or buys from Carelon or HHAX at near-zero marginal price.** Nothing measured yet shows exception rates (the only hard public number is New York's 56% claim-to-EVV match, S-c), no plan has said it will pay, and the nearest paid precedents are risk-based (CareBridge) or software seats (HHAX).

**Mitigation:**
1. Make the first ask a free, measured retrospective on 2,000 or more members, so the plan's CFO sees leakage in dollars and exceptions in hours before any fee.
2. Sell into dated readiness events (Arizona orals now and award 1/5/2027; Pennsylvania readiness review when it restarts; the UCare-to-Medica handover; the CareSource integrations) where the plan must prove 24/7 coverage, backup plans and EVV monitoring to a regulator.
3. Price the pilot at cost plus 10%, and keep R3 out until R1-R2 margins are proven.
4. Pre-commit to the quit gates in section 4, so a no-demand outcome costs under $400k and 95 days.

## 7. Founder fit

The team that can win needs, on day 1, (a) a former plan LTSS or care-management operations leader (VP level) whom directors will take a call from, because buyers do not buy cold from an unknown vendor and need delegation-oversight credibility; (b) a healthcare contact-center or BPO operator who has run a 24/7 desk with QA and compliance (Maximus, Conduent, Gainwell or ModivCare background); (c) an integration engineer who has loaded EVV (Sandata, HHAX, state aggregator), 837/835, 834 and authorization files; and (d) fractional healthcare regulatory counsel for 42 CFR 438.230 delegation, HIPAA and state subcontract notices. A solo founder without plan-operations credibility cannot cold-sell this; the test must be run with, or through, an ex-plan operator as co-founder or paid advisor.

## 8. Verdict

Narrow yes, big no. Real deadlines exist now (Arizona ALTCS orals 10/26, award 1/5/2027; Pennsylvania CHC; UCare-to-Medica) and a pilot costs under $1M. But Carelon already sells 24/7 HCBS operations to rival plans, HHAeXchange holds the EVV data in three of five target states, and R3 is roughly break-even. Run it only as a 95-day test; raise nothing before a signed pilot.

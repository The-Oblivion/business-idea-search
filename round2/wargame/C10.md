## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

Alive but not attractive as a Monday start. After war-gaming it is a consensus private-equity roll-up with an unproven AI hours edge, entering a market where Arlington (reported $1.25B equity in Enercon) and Pelican (GSE + EXCEL + Everstar) are already buying the same boutiques, where the best accounts buy through alliances, and where a standard architect/engineer E&O form excludes the exact nuclear-facility loss the product implies (new finding, section 5). ### 1.2 How ENERCON/Arlington responds once this shows traction

What I could verify about their current position, and what I could not:
- Arlington's own release names AI only as a driver of power demand, not as a capability [V]. ENERCON's public site shows no AI product and no fixed-price offer [V]. So the incumbent has a gap today; it is not a gap that survives a visible customer win.
- Four likely moves, in order of how fast they land:
  1. **Buy your target first.** Arlington has add-on mandate and capital; the tell is your orphan target going into a sell-side process or getting an unsolicited letter after you sign an NDA. This kills the sourcing rule (no auctions) faster than anything else. Pelican does small deals too (IEPSON, Iddeal), so small size is not protection [S].
  2. **Offer the same bounded scope as a fixed-price option inside the existing engineer-of-choice contract.** Costs them nothing: they already hold the hours history per deliverable class, and the owner already has the contract vehicle. The pilot owner's engineering manager can say yes in one email, with no new supplier evaluation. This is the most damaging response.
  3. **Bid up the same verifiers.** Independent verifiers and signatories are the scarce input; S&L alone grew headcount 14.5% [S]. Expect retention offers to anyone you try to hire from them.
  4. **Use the co-op.** USA's stated lever, in the 2020 S&L award, is "measurable duplications and similarities" across members [S, via the refined pitch and the S&L release] and USA reported $72M+ documented value and 37:1 return in 2025, target $100M by 2030 [V]. If your pilot produces a measured hours-saved number, USA can write it into the next engineer-of-choice renewal for S&L or ENERCON. You supply the benchmark, they get the contract.

### 1.3 Counter-moves (honest version)

- **Speed and structure beat price in sourcing.** Go for a founder-to-founder close with a 3-5 year earn-out and staff protections; do not run to a higher bid. If an auction forms, walk (the 6.5x / 0.9x cap is the rule). There is no structural defence here; I would not claim one.
- **Become the incumbents' overflow, not their replacement.** Sell AI-verified deliverables to primes as sub-tier (door 2). Hourly firms billing owners by the hour do not lose revenue on overflow they cannot staff. This is the lowest-conflict route and probably the most likely to produce revenue first.
- **Own the metric.** Get the first owner to sign off on a measured hours baseline per deliverable class. A published owner-measured number is what lets the owner (and USA) hold every supplier, including you, to the same bar. It only helps you if your measured result is better than the incumbents' by a margin they cannot close for 12-24 months.
- **Tie the bench.** Phantom equity or earn-in for the two or three independent verifiers who sign. The key person risk is theirs, not the AI's.
- **Kill rule.** If ENERCON or Pelican announces an owner-signed fixed-price, AI-assisted deliverable program before 2027-03-31, re-run the differentiation test; stop unless your measured hours cut is at least 10 points better (section 4, Q9).

## 5. Unit economics

### 5.1 Price
- Billing rate: $160-200 per hour blended. One S&L invoice from 2022 in a Texas filing shows $102-270 per hour from Associate I to Principal [S, interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/56211_218_1394038.PDF].
- Deliverables: calc revision about 100-150 hours = $16-30k; change-package technical section about 150-250 hours = $24-50k; 50.59 evaluation $30-50k [dossier I]. Blended $30k.
- Pilot: 10 deliverables x average $21k x (1 - 9% discount) = about $191k.
- Lane: 45 deliverables per site-year x $30k = $1.35M, less 10% discount = $1.215M (discount value $135k per site-year). The co-op's own claim is $72M of value for nine members, about $8M per member per year [V, usainc.org]; so the $135k discount is under 2% of what a member already books. The discount is not the lever; capacity and acceptance are. The contradiction in the pitch is real.

### 5.2 Gross margin and hours-cut rule
Baseline from the refined pitch [I]: labor 65% of revenue, other cost 20%, EBITDA 15%. With hours cut h and price cut p, margin on new revenue = ((1 - p) - 0.65(1 - h) - 0.20) / (1 - p). Gross margin (revenue less loaded engineer labor) = 1 - 0.65(1 - h) / (1 - p).

| h | p | Gross margin | EBITDA margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0% | 35.0% | 15.0% |
| 15% | 8% | 39.9% | 18.2% |
| 20% | 10% | 42.2% | 20.0% |
| 30% | 15% | 46.5% | 22.9% |

Caveat I add: this only holds if the freed hours are sold again. Engineers are salaried; a 20% hours cut with flat volume does not drop 20% of labor cost, so the first-order gain is capacity, not cost. Bake-off hours should be read as a capacity number.

### 5.3 Customer acquisition cost
- Acquisition path (the thesis): customers come with the book, so CAC is the purchase price. Platform deal 1: revenue $10M, EBITDA 15% = $1.5M; cap = min(6.5 x $1.5M, 0.9 x $10M) = min $9.75M, $9.0M = **$9.0M**. Precedents: DP Engineering sold in 2019 for $13.5M cash plus up to $5M earn-out on $20M+ revenue, about 0.7x upfront [V, SEC exh99-2]; GSE/Pelican letter of intent about $19.0M enterprise value on roughly $46M annualized revenue (about 0.4x), but GSE was distressed and half software [dossier]. Critics' range for quality assets is 9-12x EBITDA (1.2-2.5x revenue) [S]; at those multiples the cap rule means we do not buy.
- Greenfield lane (what the critics hammered): 12 targets, program spend about $600k per year (BD lead $200k, co-founder selling time $120k, travel and events $80k, pilot subsidy 30% overrun on a $191k pilot = $57k per pilot, compliance and legal $140k) = about $50k per pursued account. If 10% of pursued accounts sign a pilot and 50% of pilots convert to a lane, 5% of pursued accounts become a lane: **CAC per lane about $1.0M** [I]. Sales cycle 6-18 months (Nuclearn's comparable per the critics). Greenfield revenue also needs supplier qualification: NUPIC's standard cycle is 33 months with a five-licensee minimum [V, nupic.com per dossier], so revenue before an acquisition comes only through sub-tier arrangements or an owner's own supplier audit (a licensee can also qualify a supplier through its own audit under its QA program, and Criterion VII makes it assess suppliers [dossier V]; I found no timing or precedent for a first-time supplier via that route [I]).
- Payback: a converted lane yields $1.215M x 42% gross margin (median row of 5.2) = about $513k gross profit per year, so CAC of $1.0M pays back in about 2.0 years from first lane revenue, plus a 6-18 month cycle, so roughly 30-42 months from first spend to cash-back [I].

### 5.5 Insurance, the new problem
- A standard architects-and-engineers form excludes "nuclear reaction, radiation or contamination, under any circumstances and regardless of cause, within or originating from a nuclear facility" (exclusion L in the Victor A/E policy form) [V, victorinsurance.com policy PDF]. A nuclear engineering house needs a specialty or endorsed policy. Coverage fights are live: the Fifth Circuit held Hartford had no duty to defend DP Engineering in a nuclear-plant stator accident, then remanded indemnity [S, ca5 opinion 15-10443; Business Insurance].
- Price-Anderson covers off-site public liability for a nuclear incident, not rework, outage or replacement-power losses [S].
- AI exclusions are layered on top: ISO generative-AI general-liability forms CG 40 47 / CG 40 48 / CG 35 08 effective January 2026, and Berkley's absolute AI exclusion for D&O/E&O reported (form PC 51380, second-hand) [S]. Armilla/Chaucer (Lloyd's) writes AI-specific cover [S]; I saw no engineering wording.
- The refined pitch assumes cover at about 3% of revenue. That is unverified [I]. At $10M revenue, 3% is $300k, which is 20% of $1.5M baseline EBITDA. Treat any broker indication above 5% as a Q3 trigger.

## 6. Biggest risk, mitigation, founder fit

**Biggest risk.** The edge does not exist, or exists and the buyer takes it. The only thing separating this from the Pelican/Arlington roll-up is that AI plus independent verification cuts total accepted hours by 30% or more on calculation-grade work with 90%+ first-pass acceptance. Evidence is drafting-only: an INL case study estimated about 50% savings on a narrative safety-analysis chapter but found hallucinations and a leak-rate limit of 10,500 cc/s against a 5,500 source value [V via critic, dossier]; Westinghouse claims 25% (sponsored) [S]. Nobody has measured calculations or change packages. If it is real, a 30% cut at a 15% discount puts 23% EBITDA in your pocket, but the owner's own review hours do not fall and an incumbent can match the narrow scope inside an existing contract. Second-order risk: no insurance for nuclear-facility loss (section 5.5).

**Mitigation.**
1. Run the $10k bake-off before spending a dollar of capital (Gate 1).
2. Hard discount rule: price cut at most half the measured hours cut (break-even is 0.65 x hours cut).
3. Sell capacity and acceptance, not discount; use gain-share against an owner-measured baseline.
4. Fee-capped liability: re-performance plus fee credit, no consequential or lost-generation exposure; no EPC scope.
5. No auctions, stop buying at month 12 (Q6).
6. Be willing to sell the overflow to the incumbents; do not require displacing them.

**Founder fit.** A team of three, none optional:
- A nuclear QA / licensing operator: 10+ years as a design engineering manager, QA director or NUPIC-qualified supplier auditor at a licensee or approved supplier; ideally PE-licensed; known to at least five of the section 2 accounts. Without this person no owner opens the door (dossier), and the SBA lender will not underwrite the deal.
- A deal CEO: has closed at least one lower-middle-market services acquisition (search-fund, independent sponsor or PE operating partner), can personally guarantee an SBA loan, and can hold integration discipline.
- An applied-AI engineer who has built verified, auditable engineering-calculation workflows (not a chatbot builder) and who can design the gold-set regression and the controlled procedure change.

A solo, first-time or non-industry founder cannot win this. Someone younger or outside the industry can only enter as the capital-and-AI-operations partner behind a credentialed nuclear operator, with equity that reflects that.

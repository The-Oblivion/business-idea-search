# Gate report: pool P003, England social landlords' repairs and maintenance (Awaab's Law era)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 2-4 searches each, plus full reads of the Sureserve Property MOT page, the Clyde & Co Phase 2 note, the Housemark Voids Club article, the Housemark Pulse article, Weightmans' Awaab tool page and Millbrook Healthcare's FY25 results page. Result: 4 kill, 3 weak, 1 strong. 1 pick (Hot-Spot Contract, sharpened).

Working notes and every intermediate version are saved in round2/backups/P003/ (P003.notes.working.v01 to v07) and round2/gate/P003.notes.working.md. Weekly usage was 78-79% against the 92% cap during this run, so nothing was cut short.

## Headline findings

- The pool is real but almost none of it is open. The 10B GBP of repairs and maintenance (3.9B capitalised) is already bought through total-asset contracts, price-per-property deals, frameworks and in-house teams. Examples: Mears-Thurrock (GBP240M, 5+5 years, all repairs, compliance and planned works, May 2026), Dacorum-Osborne (total asset management, 10,500 homes), the Procurement for Housing GBP480M repair, void and disrepair framework (12 named winners, July 2026), Riverside's GBP657M disrepair framework (12 regional lots). A newcomer wins by taking a slice from these, not by finding an empty budget line.
- Several "nobody does this" claims were wrong. Sureserve (Morgan Sindall) already sells a one-visit gas-plus-everything check ("Property MOT" and "Healthy Homes Check"). Mears already runs void works as a single "Day One" basket-rate order at Havering. Councils already use their own voids for decants under temporary licences. Law firms (Weightmans, Forbes) already sell Awaab tooling and services.
- Phase 2 of Awaab's Law is confirmed for 30 November 2026, but it covers seven hazard categories (electrical, falls and trips, fire and explosion, excess cold, excess heat, structural collapse, domestic hygiene), not the 29 HHSRS hazards. The full HHSRS comes in phase 3 (2027). The right to sue is real: a missed clock is a breach of s.10A of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, with damages and specific performance in the County Court. Source: https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/08/awaab-s-law-phase-2-the-next-compliance-challenge (read in full).
- Case-volume claims behind the Awaab ideas are inflated. Portsmouth received 1,941 damp and mould cases in 2025/26 but only 92 were significant hazards (about 5%): https://www.portsmouth.co.uk/news/politics/awaabs-law-prompts-thousands-of-damp-and-mould-cases-in-portsmouth-9068199 . Housemark's hazard counts vary from 0 to 160+ per 1,000 homes because landlords record differently, not because conditions differ. The clocked "significant" case is a low single-digit percent of homes, not the 8% assumed.
- The void-time headline depends on the measure. Housemark's Voids Club says 74 days for all empty homes in 2023-24 and about 50 for standard voids, against under 30 days five years ago (https://www.insidehousing.co.uk/news/empty-properties-cost-social-housing-sector-16bn-in-2023-24-research-shows-90440 , read). Housemark's own monthly Pulse says 41 days in April 2025, down 17% on the year, with void-and-available homes at the lowest level since April 2021 (https://www.scottishhousingnews.com/articles/housemark-arrears-and-voids-performance-hit-multi-year-highs , read). "74 days against a 35-day target" overstates the gap.
- Millbrook Healthcare does not prove a $100M adaptations business. FY25 group turnover of GBP114M is GBP90M community equipment plus GBP24M of everything else, on GBP3.8M EBITDA: https://millbrookhealthcare.co.uk/fy25-financial-statements/ (read).
- The one-off electrical catch-up wave is nearly over. Existing social tenancies must have their first five-yearly EICR by 1 November 2026, two weeks from now. After that the steady state is one fifth of homes a year.

## Per-idea detail

### 1. One Knock (weak)

- Competitors: Sureserve (Property MOT, Healthy Homes Check), Mears (says it intends to lead compliance and build full asset management: https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/mears-targets-compliance-services ), Equans, Aspect, Gas Call (from the idea; not independently checked). Multi-skilled gas-and-electrical operatives are an ordinary job at councils and housing associations (Broxtowe, Kingdom).
- Substitutes: in-house multi-skilled teams and combined lots.
- Revenue math: the GBP790M pool is almost entirely under existing 5-10 year contracts. GBP180 a home and 430,000 homes is about 10% of stock, which is not heroic by share, but it means winning against Sureserve and Mears on price. The GBP158M "on-the-spot fixes" line is an unsourced assumption.
- Premise check: the idea says 29 HHSRS hazards are screened and become clocked. In fact only seven categories are clocked from 30 November 2026. Access cost is real but small: Almond HA's forced-entry visits cost over GBP26,000 for 119 visits a year (about GBP220 each): https://almondha.org.uk/home-safety-checks . The five-yearly EICR duty for existing tenancies lands on 1 November 2026: https://insights.devonshires.com/post/102lq4m/electrical-safety-standards-extended-to-social-housing-landlords .
- Upside case ($70M) needs: 15-20 mid-size landlords' combined lots won on price, dual-ticket staff retained, and a 25-30% lower visit count proven. Plausible only for the first; the last is the same claim Sureserve already makes.

### 2. Hot-Spot Contract (strong, provisional)

- Competitors: analytics and sensors only (Mobysoft RepairSense, Switchee about $6.5M Series A in 2024, Aico, Plentific). Primes sell whole-stock total-asset deals. No one found carving out the worst-cost cohort, taking capitated risk and guaranteeing repeat visits.
- Substitutes: price-per-property contracts (Tamworth moved from schedule of rates), landlords' own damp teams, damp-proofing insurance-backed guarantees (from memory), and the disrepair panel law firms.
- Revenue math: 35,000 homes at GBP2,200 is GBP77M, or $100M. That is about 16% of the 5% cohort (220,000 homes) and 0.8% of all stock, and equals roughly three Mears-Thurrock-sized contracts of annual spend (GBP24M a year each; Thurrock home count not verified). If skew is anything like the 80/20 record, the cohort's true cost per home may exceed the idea's GBP2,800 and the pool would be larger. Not verified.
- Legal checks. (a) FSMA: a supplier's own warranty is usually not insurance where it mirrors ordinary quality obligations, but it tips into insurance when it runs well beyond market practice or a third party carries the risk for a premium (https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg6/perg6s7 ; https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/warranty-service-contract-or-insurance-navigating-the-regulatory-landscape-for-product-protection-arrangements/ ). A five-year uncapped guarantee is the risky wording. A capitated service contract is not: the contractor already owes every repair for the fee. (b) Procurement Act 2023: registered providers are generally contracting authorities (https://www.procurementforhousing.co.uk/article/is-your-housing-association-subject-to-the-procurement-act-2023 ), so a multi-year capitation needs a competitive route or a framework call-off. (c) Disrepair costs: fixed recoverable costs stay off housing disrepair until at least October 2028: https://blog.anthonycollins.com/post/102kq77/disrepair-claims-fixed-recoverable-costs-exemption-extended-to-2028 . (d) Most housing-condition claims are not covered by insurance (Weightmans: https://www.weightmans.com/media-centre/news/housing-claims-a-practical-summary/ ), and no damp-and-mould cover product turned up.
- Upside case ($100M) needs: the top 5% of homes carry at least 25% of repair spend (likely, unproven); 15-20 landlords hand over 1,500-2,500 homes each within six years (low plausibility without three reference pilots); a reinsurer prices the tail on a shared dataset (plausible once 12-24 months of data exist); a framework call-off route avoids 12-18 month tenders (plausible).

### 3. Clock Desk (kill)

- Competitors: in-house surveyors; Riverside's Total Disrepair Solutions Framework (GBP657M, 12 lots, built with Awaab demand in mind); Weightmans' compliance tool (hazard tracking, deadlines, audit trail); Forbes Solicitors (injunction-for-access service); Insite and other damp specialists; Alphatec ClaimControl.
- Revenue math: the idea assumes 8% of 4.4M homes, 350,000 cases a year. Portsmouth: 1,941 cases, 92 significant. Housemark's median emergency-hazard counts moved from 6.3 to 35.8 per 1,000 homes between January and February 2026, which is recording change. Even at 3% of homes, 130,000 cases at GBP450 is GBP59M gross for the whole country.
- Legal: the fund that pays on missed deadlines is insurance. Litigation needs SRA-regulated counsel. No competent-person scheme exists, so the credential has no force.
- Upside ($35M) assumes phase 2 and 3 widen the pool and private renting is added. No date for private renting; a consultation opened 9 October 2026 (per search summary, not verified).

### 4. Home Safety Technician Guild (kill)

- Competitors: employer academies (NCHA/Access Training, primes), colleges, recruiters (Hays), Gas Safe training providers. Gas Safe lists about 155,653 engineers on about 80,014 businesses (2024 government tender notice, https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/003347-2024 ); the "27,000 extra gas engineers by 2028" claim comes from training providers.
- Revenue math: 1,250 billed technicians at GBP62k with 60% completion needs about 420 starters a year for three years. Take-or-pay offtake from landlords is not a thing this sector does. It is a labour-supply business at 12-18% gross margin with no data, network or balance-sheet asset, by the idea's own admission.

### 5. Void Clock (weak)

- Competitors: primes' bundled voids (Mears at Havering), void-only frameworks (Bernicia, Merthyr), the PfH framework with 12 named contractors (Axis, Bell, Cardo, Chigwell, CLC, CFM, Equans Regeneration, Ian Williams, Jones Building Group, Synergize, TW Group, United Living), Made Tech's voids prioritisation tool (https://www.madetech.com/saas-products/ ), Kirona, Helpthemove.
- Revenue math: GBP77M is about 19,000 voids at GBP4,000. The landlord's incremental gain is small: if the right comparison is 41-50 days for standard voids, not 74, then 35 days saves 6-15 days, or GBP90-225 of rent per void, plus a 5-10% works saving. The rest of the price is works already in the budget.
- Strengths: a clear budget line (over GBP1.1B of void repairs and GBP220M of lettings costs a year, about 300,000 voids), a named signer (Director of Assets), and a measurable metric. Weakness: no moat beyond process, and Awaab does not change voids.

### 6. Decant Reserve (kill)

- Competitors: PfH managed decant framework suppliers (ICAB and others), Hyde's earlier decant framework, councils' own leasing, nightly-paid providers.
- Revenue math: 3,100 units at GBP80 a night (assumption; no decant rate found). The in-pool decant market is about GBP6M a year (GBP25M over four years). The rest is temporary accommodation, which the idea itself says is a different pool.
- Premise: of the 61,000 vacant homes at any moment (the idea's arithmetic), most are mid-works or awaiting allocation, and nominations agreements and licence terms block short subletting. Not tested by the idea.

### 7. Falls on a Clock (kill)

- Competitors: Millbrook (turnover GBP114M, GBP90M of it equipment), Foundations, council home improvement agencies, stairlift and bathroom suppliers, housing association in-house teams.
- Revenue math: DFG is GBP711M for 2025-26 per Housing LIN (https://www.housinglin.org.uk/News/Disabled-Facilities-Grant-Allocations-confirmed/ ); the idea's GBP761M is not confirmed. The statutory maximum from complete application to approval is 18 months and some areas run to two to three years from first contact (https://thiis.co.uk/?p=21839 , summary only).
- Premise: the Awaab clock is triggered by a hazard in the dwelling, so a missing stair rail can start it, but the OT queue and DFG decisions stay with the council.

### 8. Whole-Home Once (weak)

- Competitors: Mears (about 5,000 retrofits), Wates, Kier, Morgan Sindall, Willmott Dixon, Energiesprong UK/Melius, bathroom pod makers (Walker Modular 2,000 pods, https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2026/09/14/walker-modular-lands-2000-bathroom-pods-deal/ ).
- Revenue math: 4,500 homes a year at GBP17k is about 5.5% of the assumed GBP1.4B of interior component spend. Working capital and bonding for a factory plus 12-year performance guarantees are heavy. Offsite housebuilders Ilke and L&G Modular failed (from memory).
- The "fifth visit" insight is plausible but unproven (no data on visits per home). Capitalising a bundled renewal is a good finance argument. Could fit as a later module of the pick, not a first business.

## Pick

### Hot-Spot Contract, sharpened

Sharpened version, answering what the gate found without shrinking:

1. Sell it as a capitated service contract, not a guarantee. The contractor takes every repair, disrepair-defence cost share and root-cause fix on the costliest 5-8% of homes for a fixed fee set at about 80% of trailing three-year cost. The "five-year guarantee" is just the capitation running five years. The contractor buys its own stop-loss reinsurance. That keeps it outside the FSMA insurance perimeter and moves the tail risk to a regulated carrier.
2. Keep the statutory clock with the landlord and price it that way: the contractor runs the work under delegation, the landlord remains the duty holder, and claims-defence savings are shared, not guaranteed.
3. Enter through a framework call-off or a below-threshold pilot, not a standalone tender. The PfH GBP480M framework and similar vehicles let a landlord call off without a 12-18 month process.
4. Make the loss dataset the scaling asset. Every cohort adds job-level cost, cause and recurrence data across landlords. After 12-24 months that prices building risk better than any single landlord can, which supports reinsurance and, later, a regulated carrier-fronted product.
5. Use the disrepair legal budget as a second payer line. A Finance Director signs for the fee out of repairs, disrepair and compensation spend, with root-cause works capitalised.

Cheapest next test (6-8 weeks, no spend beyond analyst time): get job-level repair and disrepair cost data for the last three years from three landlords (ask via a housing-data intermediary or an FOI to councils with in-house records), rank homes by cost and measure what share of spend the top 5% carry. Stop if the top 5% carry under 15% of repair spend in two of three, or if no landlord will release job-level data. Next, ask two landlords whether their current repairs contract allows a cohort carve-out.

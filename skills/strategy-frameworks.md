# Strategy frameworks for evaluating and sharpening a startup idea

Use the frameworks that fit. Do not fill in all of them mechanically; apply each one only where it reveals something.

## 1. Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen)
- What "job" is the customer hiring this product to do? Functional, emotional and social dimensions.
- What do they "fire" to hire this (current workaround, spreadsheet, consultant, doing nothing)? Non-consumption is often the biggest competitor.
- Struggling moment: the specific situation that triggers a purchase. If you cannot name it, demand is speculative.

## 2. Blue Ocean (Kim & Mauborgne)
- Strategy canvas: list the 6-10 factors the industry competes on and plot incumbents vs. this idea.
- ERRC grid: which factors to Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create. A blue-ocean idea creates at least one factor no incumbent offers and eliminates something customers pay for but do not value.
- Six Paths: look across alternative industries, strategic groups, buyer chain (payer vs. user vs. influencer), complementary offerings, functional vs. emotional appeal, and time (trends that are irreversible and decisive).

## 3. Wardley mapping (evolution)
- Place the key component on genesis -> custom -> product -> commodity. Businesses are best built on components that just became commodities (cheap LLM inference, launch, sensors, payments), selling something still in custom/product.
- Ask what just moved from custom to commodity in 2024-2026, and which new genesis activities it unlocks.

## 4. First principles / cost stack
- Decompose the price the customer pays today into its underlying costs. Which cost component collapsed recently? The gap between old price and new cost is the opportunity.

## 5. Disruptive innovation
- Low-end disruption: overserved customers who would take "good enough" at far lower price.
- New-market disruption: people who could not consume at all before.
- Incumbent asymmetry: why will incumbents rationally NOT respond (margin cannibalization, channel conflict, regulation, brand risk)?

## 6. Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers (defensibility)
Scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power. Which one could this business realistically reach, and by when? Counter-positioning and cornered resources (proprietary data, licenses, relationships) are the most accessible to startups.

## 7. Lean Startup / riskiest assumption
- List assumptions (customer has problem, will pay X, can be reached at CAC Y, can be delivered at cost Z, regulation allows it). Rank by risk x importance.
- Cheapest test of the riskiest assumption in under 2 weeks (concierge MVP, smoke-test landing page, 20 customer calls, pre-sale, manual "Wizard of Oz" delivery).

## 8. Pre-mortem
- "It is 2028 and this company is dead. Why?" List the three most likely causes and whether each is fixable now.

## 9. Unit economics sanity check
- Price, gross margin, CAC by channel, payback period, LTV. For services-heavy ideas: revenue per employee. For marketplaces: take rate and liquidity path.
- Bottom-up TAM: (# of reachable customers) x (realistic annual spend). Prefer bottom-up to "the $X billion industry".

## 10. Why-now test
- Name the specific enabling change (technology cost curve, regulation, behavior shift, distribution channel, macro shock) and when it happened. If the idea would have worked equally well in 2019, the why-now is weak.

# I0573-M1 Cure Desk: validation dossier (as of 2026-10-13)

Labels: [F] sourced fact, [I] my inference, [A] my assumption (no source; stress-test it).

## Bottom line

The policy shock is real and dated, but the evidence supports a narrow service business, not the pitch's $1.15M year-one story. Five findings drive that:

1. The "documentation wall" is later and softer than pitched (section 1).
2. Nebraska's first coverage losses are mostly non-response, not missing proof.
3. The "plus one refugee language" add-on is dead: refugees and asylees lost federal full-scope Medicaid on Oct 1, 2026.
4. Bottom-up serviceable revenue is about $10-60M a year nationally and about $30-125K in Nebraska [A-heavy].
5. The attester rail carries HIPAA, TCPA and attester-exposure risks the pitch does not price in.

Verdict: CONDITIONAL, not yet fundable. Run the 30-day concierge test only with the kill criteria in section 8, and do not build the platform first.

## 1. Why-now check: real, but later and softer

- [F] The interim final rule (IFR) was issued June 1, 2026 and takes effect Jan 1, 2027. States must verify at application and renewal. If a state cannot verify, it must send a noncompliance notice with 30 calendar days to show compliance or an exemption ([CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicaid-community-engagement-requirement-certain-individuals-interim-final-rule-comment-period-cms)). The $580 monthly income path (minimum wage x 80 hours) and combining activities are both allowed (same source).
- [F] The look-back is 1-3 months at application, plus at least one month since the last renewal, and the state chooses ([SHVS](https://shvs.org/resources/cms-releases-interim-final-rule-on-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements/)).
- [F] In CY2027 states may require documentation or accept auditable self-declaration. From 2028 they must require documentation when reasonably available. If it does not exist, states must accept "other sufficient information" and cannot deny solely for missing documents. States define what counts as sufficient (SHVS, same page). AAMC asked CMS to extend self-attestation ([AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/aamc-raises-concern-over-medicaid-community-engagement-interim-final-rule)).
- [F] **Correction to the finalist file:** the "once per enrollment period" self-attestation from 2028 applies to medical frailty only (SHVS), not to hours or income.
- [F] Six-month renewals for expansion adults start with renewals scheduled on or after Jan 1, 2027, which doubles the touchpoints ([Georgetown CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/03/06/cms-releases-guidance-on-6-month-medicaid-renewals-for-expansion-adults/)).
- [F] Nebraska has been live since May 1. It mails a notice plus a declaration form due in 30 days. Through Aug 2026, 14% of 4,089 new applicants subject to the rule were denied (557), and 7% of 7,280 renewals lost coverage (533). Of those, 84% and 92% respectively were non-response, and only 16% and 8% were affirmatively found noncompliant ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/early-medicaid-work-requirement-insights-from-nebraska/)). That is about 130 people found noncompliant against about 960 non-responders.
- [F] 24 attorneys general sued June 29 over the IFR's "medically frail" reading ([CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-sues-trump-administration-over-unlawful-implementation)). It does not target documentation or the Jan 1 date.
- [I] 2027 is a soft-start year. Paid demand for cure packets is more likely 2028+, so year-one revenue assumptions are aggressive. Nebraska's problem today is outreach and engagement, which is what Pointcare, Findhelp, CareMessage and state notices target, not proof-building.
- [F] **Segment shrinkage:** refugees, asylees and parolees lose regular Medicaid from Oct 1, 2026. Only citizens, LPRs (subject to the five-year bar), Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA migrants remain ([SHVS](https://shvs.org/how-h-r-1-impacts-coverage-for-non-citizens/)). The refugee-language wedge and the undocumented day-labor core are largely ineligible. [Gap: no source on the immigration mix of cash workers.]

## 2. Market size (bottom-up)

**Anchors**
- [F] 1,359 health center awardees served about 32.4M patients in 2024. 48.56% of patients with known insurance were Medicaid/CHIP, and 39.38% were Hispanic/Latino ([HRSA](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/healthcenters/uds/overview/national), [snapshot](https://data.hrsa.gov/tools/data-reporting/data-snapshot)).
- [F] A GWU estimate counts 9M+ at-risk adult health center patients, 5.3M of them expansion adults, worth $6.7B in 2023 revenue ([GWU](https://geigergibson.publichealth.gwu.edu/node/1981)). The Commonwealth Fund's "up to 5.6M patients / $32B" is an upper bound on a different basis ([CF](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2025/community-health-center-patients-medicaid-coverage-work-requirements)). I saw both only as search summaries.
- [F] Nebraska has about 70,000 people subject to the rule, and 72% need no action ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/medicaid/nebraska-medicaid-work-requirement-fears-losing-coverage/)). Georgetown says "as many as 28,000" must act twice a year ([CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/05/11/the-new-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements-are-here-dont-let-the-nebraska-soft-start-fool-you/)); the two figures differ by a third. Omaha's One World has about 4,000 expansion enrollees among 52,000 patients (7.7%), and losing 10% would cost about $500K (KFF Health News). That implies about $1,250 per enrollee-year [I].

**National funnel**

5.3M expansion patients x 30-45% needing to act [A; Nebraska is 28%, weaker-data states higher] x 1.75 events a year [A; six-month renewals] x 15-30% cash or no digital trail [A; no source found] = 0.42-1.25M events. At $120 that is a $50-150M ceiling. If 20-40% buy the tool, serviceable revenue is **$10-60M a year**. That is a real niche and a thin venture market.

**Nebraska funnel**

70K x 28% = 19.6K x 10-15% at health centers [A] x 1.75 x 25-40% informal [A] = 860-2,060 packets. At 30-50% adoption and $120, revenue is **about $30-125K a year**.

**Per-site check**
- The average awardee has about 23.8K patients. Applying One World's 7.7% expansion ratio gives about 1,830 expansion adults and about 270 packets a year (22 a month), or about $32K a year per site [I].
- The pitch's 40 packets a month needs a One World-sized site at near-full usage. Twenty average sites yield about $0.65M, not $1.15M.
- The $120 blended fee implies a 73% acceptance rate ($40 + 0.727 x $110), above the 60% pilot target.

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

**Problem evidence (strong)**
- Nebraska's non-response rates (section 1). Nebraska's Medicaid agency is adding no staff, and One World plans to add form-help staff (KFF Health News). Health centers are already hiring certified application counselors ([ECDHD posting](https://ecdhd.ne.gov/employment/title/-urgently-hiring-outreach-and-enrollment-specialist-certified-application-counselor)).
- Arkansas 2018: 18,164 people lost coverage, and early studies found those hit were often already working but unable to report ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/state-data-for-medicaid-work-requirements-in-arkansas/)).
- Georgia Pathways: more than half of $80M went to contractors, and enrollment fell far below projections ([Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/2025/09/georgias-version-of-medicaid-had-high-overhead-federal-report-finds/)).
- CBPP documents documentation burdens for irregular workers ([CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/harm-to-low-wage-workers-from-taking-away-medicaid-for-not-meeting-work-requirements)).

**Payer evidence (weak)**
- No health center or MCO was found paying per case for this. The only spend signals are state-side ($2M per implementation, free tools), plus a one-time $200M federal implementation fund, half of it split equally (under $2M per state) ([CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/02/11/implementing-costly-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements-who-will-foot-the-bill/)).
- [F] **Substitute:** states publish their own free fallback. Georgia accepts a self-employment hours worksheet by client or a work-calendar snapshot ([Georgia FAQ](https://pathways.georgia.gov/document/document/ga-pathwaysgeneralfaqs2025/download)), and Nebraska uses a self-declaration form. If states accept worksheets, third-party attestation is optional, not required.
- [I] Demand is real for CHW time, not necessarily for a paid tool.

## 5. Unit economics

- **Variable cost** [I]: WhatsApp utility messages run roughly $0.006-0.015 each, plus a $0.003-0.010 provider markup ([Courier](https://www.courier.com/guides/whatsapp-pricing) vs [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/whatsapp-template-pricing-explained-2026), which conflict; Meta's [page](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing) says utility messages inside a 24-hour window are free). At 15-25 messages a case that is under $1 before human QA. Messaging cost is not the constraint.
- **Labor displaced:** the CHW median is $24.93 an hour ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/community-health-workers.htm)). Cutting 2 hours to 30 minutes saves about $37 unloaded, or about $50 loaded [I]. That is well below the $120-150 fee, so the fee must be justified by retained revenue.
- **Break-even for the buyer** [I]: fee $120 minus about $40 labor saved leaves about $80 net cost. With about $1,250 per retained enrollee-year, the tool must lift acceptance by about 6-7 points over the CHW's baseline. That is plausible, but the baseline is unmeasured. With six-month renewals, value may repeat.
- **Pricing mismatch:** the pitch charges $40 on open, which the buyer pays even if the packet fails. FQHCs on thin margins may balk. A pure success fee fits better but halves cash flow.
- **Vendor economics:** with sites worth about $32K a year (section 2), customer acquisition has to stay under about $10K a site. CAC and sales-cycle data for FQHC software were not found. [Gap]

## 6. Regulatory and liability

- **Per-enrollment pay:** in federal Marketplace assistance, per-application or per-enrollment compensation of assisters is barred ([Georgetown CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/new-rules-protect-navigators-and-certified-application-counselors/)). Medicaid assisters are governed by state certification and conflict-of-interest rules ([42 CFR 435.908](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/435.908)). Some states carve Medicaid out. A $110-on-acceptance fee needs state-by-state review. Counsel must also address the anti-kickback and billing-agent questions the pitch flags. [Gap: no primary source retrieved]
- **HIPAA:** the health center is a covered entity, so Cure Desk would be a business associate. Meta will not sign a BAA for WhatsApp, and most sources say not to put PHI on it ([LuxSci](https://luxsci.com/is-whatsapp-hipaa-compliant); mostly vendor sources). WhatsApp is the pitch's core channel, so intake needs a BAA-covered SMS or web flow instead.
- **TCPA:** texting attesters who never opted in carries $500 to $1,500 statutory damages per text with no aggregate cap ([Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/blog/tcpa-compliance-sms), [Holland & Hart](https://www.hollandhart.com/pdf/TCPA-Health-Law-Webinar-Slides.pdf)). The FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated ([MoFo](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule)); a revocation rule is delayed to Jan 31, 2027. The patient must supply the attester's consent, which is awkward. [I]
- **Attester exposure** [I, no source]: a crew leader or household employer who attests to cash wages creates a written record that could expose them to tax or wage-and-hour liability, and perhaps immigration exposure. Many will refuse or fudge. False attestations create fraud risk for the health center and a vendor paid on acceptance.
- **Privacy** [I]: a text telling an employer that a worker is applying for Medicaid discloses sensitive information and needs patient authorization.

## 7. Moat and synthesis

| Moat | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Network effects | Weak-moderate | Attester reuse is plausible but unproven. Volume per attester is small, and rules are state-specific. |
| Switching costs | Weak | Low integration. A packet PDF is easy to replace. |
| Scale economies | Moderate | Rule-pack cost spreads across health centers, but state count limits it. |
| Intangibles | Moderate | Outcome data on what caseworkers accept is real, but states publish rules and CBV tools shrink the residue. |

- **Durable:** accepted/rejected data per state caseworker office; trust with Spanish-speaking panels.
- **Vulnerabilities:** thin market (section 2); free state worksheets and self-declaration; HIPAA/TCPA re-architecture; 2027 soft-start year; policy-reversal risk [I].
- **Scenarios** (my judgment): Bull 15% (states tighten to documentation in 2028, accept third-party statements, a partner like Pointcare embeds the module; $1-3M ARR by end-2028). Base 50% (service-style business in 1-2 states, 5-10 sites, $100-400K ARR, feature gets absorbed). Bear 35% (states accept worksheets or CBV, attesters refuse, below $100K).

## 8. What to do next: the 30-day test, with kill criteria

Run Nebraska concierge on SMS or web, not WhatsApp. Stop if any of these hold:
- Fewer than 40% of named attesters answer within 72 hours.
- Nebraska caseworkers do not treat third-party statements as better than self-declaration, or the state accepts a free worksheet for the same patients.
- The CHW's baseline acceptance is already above about 70%.
- Pilot sites will not commit to $40 on open plus $110 on acceptance after the free month.

Pivot options: sell the rule pack and caseworker-outcome data as a subscription to navigators and legal aid; or license the packet logic into Pointcare or Findhelp rather than selling direct.

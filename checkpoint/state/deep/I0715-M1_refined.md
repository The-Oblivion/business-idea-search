# I0715-M1 Refined: Smoke Report Coverage Matrix

Date: 2026-10-13. Status: alive, narrowly. Dead as a venture, alive as a small, time-boxed, test-gated niche.

## 1. Verdict

The original pitch ($1.5M-$2.5M in year one, an "AB 1795 deficiency letter" generator, a $395 PA scorecard, $40k-$150k plaintiff analytics, CIH co-signed sampling plans) does not survive. All three critics are right on the load-bearing points:

- The statutory hook is unproven for the January 2025 fires, and inspection is elective.
- The buyer already has a better substitute (their own CIH) and free or cheap alternatives exist.
- The analytics tier is one buyer.
- The CIH co-sign economics and the unauthorized-practice exposure are real.
- The window is closing.

What survives is a much smaller thing that sidesteps most of those problems: a **descriptive triage service** for policyholder-side law firms and public adjusters that tells them which of their open smoke files have a carrier hygiene report worth paying a CIH to attack. Honest size: **about $7k-$60k in year one, a 12-18 month window, no durable moat.** It is only worth running because the decisive test costs about $600 and 14 days, and it has a hard kill rule (section 8).

## 2. What I verified this session

- Chaptered AB 1795 digest (Chapter 240, Stats. 2026), read from CalMatters Digital Democracy. Leginfo returned 403, so the full chaptered text is still unread.
  - It confirms an insurer "that chooses to exercise its right to inspect" must do so within 30 days, so inspection is elective.
  - It makes the insurer responsible for the cost of sampling and testing that is "necessary" to restore the property and clear it for occupancy. It does not define "necessary".
  - It creates a rebuttable smoke-source presumption in the wildfire impact zone.
  - A PA hired on a smoke claim must have the requisite license, training and certification.
  - The digest names no CIH or qualified-sampler standard and says nothing about the operative date or retroactivity.
- So two critic claims are corroborated (elective inspection, no sampling yardstick). Retroactivity is still unresolved from primary text. **The refined business does not depend on it either way.**
- Everything else below is taken from the three critiques and the dossier as given. I did not re-verify it.

## 4. The refined business

**Job to be done.** A new client file arrives with a carrier report that says "clean" or "minor" and a suit clock running. The firm needs to decide in minutes whether to spend $2k-$4k on a CIH second opinion. Today the choices are reading 40 pages by hand, paying a CIH for every file, or doing nothing. Many files get nothing, because the screening cost is too high.

**Wedge customer.**
- Primary: policyholder-side coverage and litigation firms with 20+ live Eaton or Palisades standing-home smoke clients (estimated 8-15 firms).
- Secondary: PA firms with 10+ disputed smoke files (estimated 10-20 of the 15-25 firms).
- Validators: 2-3 independent policyholder-side CIHs, who receive the tool free and are paid by the firm at their own rate. We take no referral fee.

**First product (concierge v0, no software UI).**
- Input: report and lab PDFs, address redacted.
- Process: LLM extraction into a fixed ~25-field schema, then a 10-20 minute human spot check.
- Output in 48 hours: a one-page matrix and a spreadsheet row. It covers sample counts by media, zones sampled and skipped, comparator, units, detection limits, lab details, and internal inconsistencies. Each flag cites its source and says what is undocumented.
- Explicitly absent: any "adequate/inadequate" label, letters, sampling plans or opinions.

**Pricing.** The first 3 files are free. After that, $95 per file in batches of 50+, $149 for a single PA file, paid up front. No subscription.

**Unit economics (assumptions).**
- Variable cost is about $2 of LLM/OCR plus 10-20 minutes of QA at $40-$60 an hour, so $8-$22. Gross margin on $95 is roughly 75-90%.
- Fixed year-one costs are about $5k: tech E&O around $2-3k (unverified estimate), a CIH advisor around $1.5k, and tools.
- Break-even is about 65 files.

**Guardrails.**
- Vendor-to-the-firm contract and NDA.
- No pooling of client reports across clients. Only template-level parser learnings are kept, with contract permission.
- Zero-retention LLM API settings.
- Liability disclaimer: triage aid, not an expert opinion.

**Why now (time-limited, not structural).**
- Structured extraction from messy PDFs is reliable and costs about $2 a report. That was not true in 2023, so the 2019 test fails, which is good.
- The Eaton and Palisades disputes are concentrated in Q4 2026 and 2027.
- AB 1795 gives future declared wildfires a statutory testing duty. That is an option, not the thesis.

## 5. Moat (7 Powers), honestly

- **Counter-positioning:** holds only against carrier-side vendors, who were never going to build this. It is not a defense against Purdue, Finch or legal-AI vendors. Withdrawn as a moat claim.
- **Cornered resource:** the "largest dataset" claim is withdrawn, because Purdue holds 500+ reports. What is reachable is trust with 2-3 scarce policyholder-side CIHs and 2-3 firms, plus CIH-validated parsers for specific vendor templates. This is weak.
- **Switching costs:** modest. A firm's 100-200 triaged files, with its CIH's annotations, sit in our structure and nobody will redo them.
- **Net:** no durable power within 12 months. The strategy is speed, trust and a harvest, plus a free option on the next fire.

## 6. Bottom-up market

- 13,000+ standing-home smoke claims (CDI task force). I assume 30-50% are PA-represented and 30-50% of those remain disputed, giving about 1,200-3,250 disputed homes. These are the dossier's assumptions, not counted figures.
- Year-one capture of 5-15% gives about 60-490 files.
  - Law and coverage firms: 1-3 paying firms x 50-100 files x $85-$95, which is $4k-$29k.
  - PA firms: 2-5 paying firms x 10-38 files x $149, which is $3k-$28k.
- **Base: $7k-$60k, central about $25k.** This is 5-20x below the dossier's $0.1M-$0.5M and about 40-100x below the original pitch. The gap is the analytics and subscription lines that nobody has shown they will buy.
- **Unpriced upside:** $0-$100k from a discovery-extraction vendor role, and the next-fire option.
- **Sanity check:** even a free, promoted Purdue effort drew only 145 homes of about 13,000 (518 files, 245 usable). Homeowner-direct demand is weak, which is why that tier is gone.

## 7. Pre-mortem (it is 2028 and this is dead)

1. **Nobody pays.** Experts say "I'll use ChatGPT or Purdue" or read the report themselves. This is the most likely cause, and it is what the test targets.
2. **The window closed.** Claims settled, suits were filed, and there was no new fire. The 12-18 month window is therefore the plan, and it was not fixable.
3. **A free tool took the job.** Purdue ships a structured export, or Finch or a legal-AI vendor adds an IH module. This is not fixable, only watchable.

## 8. Riskiest assumption and the 14-day test

**Assumption.** A policyholder-side law or coverage firm, or a PA, with live files will hand over client reports and pay about $95-$149 per file for a descriptive coverage matrix, instead of using a general LLM, Purdue's free review, or their own CIH.

**Test (concierge pre-sale, nothing built).** Budget is about $600 and about 40 founder hours.

- **Days 1-2.** Write the one-page schema and reference checklist from public sources (Purdue Oct 2025 deck, CDI task force report, AIHA guide). Hire one CIH advisor for 3-4 hours (about $450-$600) to review the schema.
- **Days 1-3.** Build a target list of 40: about 12 law and coverage firms, 20 PA firms and 8 policyholder-side CIHs. Use public firm sites, the CDI licensee lookup, and warm introductions through EFRU and Eaton Fire Survivors Network volunteers.
- **Days 3-4.** Send the offer: "Send one carrier smoke report from a live file, redacted. You get a one-page matrix in 48 hours, free. If it is useful, the next files are $95."
- **Days 4-10.** Deliver up to 10 free matrices. The CIH advisor checks the first 6, and I log QA minutes per file.
- **Days 8-14.** Invoice 5 files up front ($475) or a 25-file batch ($2,125). An LOI does not count.
- **Zero-cost parallel checks:**
  - Ask Purdue about launch date, scope and white-label terms.
  - Ask every attorney whether section 2060.2 reaches open 2025 claims.
  - Ask every buyer how many open standing-home smoke files they hold.

**Pass.**
- At least 2 distinct buyers, including at least 1 law or coverage firm, have paid at least $475 each by day 14.
- The CIH advisor confirms at least 90% accuracy on structured fields across at least 6 reports.
- QA is 20 minutes or less per file by file 6.

**One extension (2 weeks).** Exactly 1 payer, plus at least 3 buyers who commit in writing to send 20+ files by a named date.

**Kill.**
- Zero payers, or fewer than the extension bar.
- 4 or more of the first 6 recipients say they would use ChatGPT or Purdue for the same output.
- No price-cutting rescue. If it fails, the idea is dead.

## 9. If it passes

Hire more QA capacity. Sign 2 CIH validators with written case studies. Quote the discovery-extraction vendor role to the largest firm. Keep the reference checklist versioned so it re-maps to the 2028-29 DTSC standards. Park the carrier and TPA compliance product until a new event and a neutral position exist. Unresearched expansion ideas, such as mold or water-damage report triage, are not part of this plan.

## 10. Withdrawn from the original

The "AB 1795 deficiency letter", the supplemental sampling plan and CIH co-sign, the partner-lab referral fee, the $40k-$150k analytics tier, the direct-to-homeowner $295 tier, the $2,500 a month subscription, the "adequacy score" label, the "largest labeled dataset" moat claim, and the carrier pre-flight audit phase.

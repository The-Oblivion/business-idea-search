# I1175 Finish Crew: Stranded Solar Job Completion. Validation Dossier (2026-10-13)

## Verdict
The pain and the lender-paid demand are real, but the "GAP" label from the earlier check does not hold. GreenLancer already sells lender-paid completion plus permit/interconnection remediation. Lenders and servicers are building in-house capacity. The completion market is small and lumpy (my estimate: roughly $3-10M a year serviceable, see section 5). Anyone who takes a permit over must hold a contractor license and accepts liability for the dead installer's work. Recommendation: WOUNDED, not dead. Only the sharper angle (portfolio triage for servicers and credit unions, then success-fee cure) is worth testing, and only after 6-8 buyer calls.

## 1. Is the why-now real? Yes, with a timing catch
- Freedom Forever filed Chapter 11 on Apr 15, 2026 and converted to Chapter 7 on Aug 7. Claims are due Oct 16, 2026. About 1,600 workers were reportedly laid off. The court let Credit Human, EnFin, EverBright, GoodLeap, Participate, Project Solar and Sunrun take over unfinished projects, hire contractors and push systems to PTO without separate motions. GoodLeap got explicit authority to take physical possession of hardware. [pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/Freedom-Forever-Bankruptcy-Converted-To-Chapter-7-Liquidation-Key-Dates-Set/)
- Freedom Forever was reportedly the second-largest installer in 2025, with a 6.1% share per Wood Mackenzie. Sources disagree on its rank. [pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/04/15/residential-solar-company-freedom-forever-files-chapter-11-bankruptcy/)
- Causes: the 25D credit expired at the end of 2025, Wood Mackenzie sees about a 20% volume drop in 2026, and Mosaic (its largest creditor, over $110M) filed in June 2025. [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/what-freedom-forevers-bankruptcy-says-about-residential-solar-today/)
- Q2 2026 residential installs were 995 MW, down 12% year over year and the lowest quarter in five years. Wood Mackenzie now forecasts a 23% full-year decline. [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/us-solar-installations-jump-45-yoy-to-11-4-gw-in-q2-2026) Customer acquisition cost is forecast up 40% to $0.84/W. [Wood Mackenzie](https://woodmac.com/news/opinion/us-residential-solar-customer-acquisition-costs-set-to-spike-40-in-2026-before-gradual-decline/)
- Solar Insure counts 100+ installer closures since 2023. [Solar Insure](https://www.solarinsure.com/solar-bankruptcies-hub) Solar-loan ABS net losses were 3.67% annualized in July 2026, up 84 bps year over year. [KBRA](https://www.kbra.com/publications/ccYPfsKf/u.s.-solar-loan-abs-index-july-2026)
- Catch: the biggest failure's first wave is already staffed. A financier deal was approved Apr 24 [Law360](https://www.law360.co.uk/articles/2469813), and the stay-relief order is roughly two months old. Most Freedom Forever systems were third-party-owned leases and PPAs, so the financiers own those assets and have the most reason to finish them in-house. [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/06/what-homeowners-should-do-after-freedom-forever-filed-for-bankruptcy/)

## 2. Evidence of demand and willingness to pay
Strong signals:
- GreenLancer says seven financiers pay it to finish incomplete installs, and its press release says demand is "surging" from financiers acquiring orphan portfolios with incomplete systems. It reports 200+ field partners and uses SolarGrade software for documentation. This is promotional and unaudited. [PR Newswire](https://www.streetinsider.com/PRNewswire/GreenLancer+and+SolarGrade+Partner+to+Deliver+Premier+Solar+Field+Services/26097204.html), [Solar Builder](https://solarbuildermag.com/operations-maintenance/greenlancer-finds-solar-orphan-om-partner-in-solargrade/)
- Precedent: in 2021 the Empire Solar trustee arranged for GoodLeap to fund local contractors to finish 45 homes at no cost to homeowners. [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/solar/minnesota-solar-installer-bankruptcies-leave-unfinished-projects-calls-for-better-oversight/)
- Solar Servicing (Forbright Bank) hired a "pipeline team" for partially funded Mosaic loans. [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2025/09/solar-servicing-acquires-mosaic-assets-resumes-pv-lending/)
- A lender told a consumer attorney in a deposition that 40% of its installers had gone out of business. GoodLeap says it now has a team for customers whose contractor is gone, and one Texas borrower is still billed on a $67,000 loan. [CBS Texas](https://www.cbsnews.com/texas/news/bankruptcies-leave-some-solar-customers-with-broken-panels/)
- Exposure can exceed cure cost. Tucson Old Pueblo Credit Union fully funded 39 member loans of $30-35k on more than $1M of unbuilt or unfinished work, paused payments, and ended up-front full funding. [KVOA](https://www.kvoa.com/news/n4t-investigators-dozens-left-paying-for-unfinished-solar-jobs-that-lender-fully-funded/article_7bfef5c6-137b-11ed-a968-a785ad6288a3.html), [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-az/pr/solar-company-owner-indicted-fraud)
- Regulatory pressure on lenders: ten state AGs asked five solar lenders in Nov 2022 to suspend payments and interest for Pink Energy customers. [Virginia OAG](https://oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/2496-november-22-2022-attorney-general-miyares-joins-nine-states-in-urging-five-solar-lending-companies-to-suspend-loan-payments-interest-for-pink-energy-customers)

- I found no RFPs, published lender budgets, or earnings-call cost-to-cure figures.
- Dividend Finance's stated policy is to defer payments and, "where feasible," help find replacement installers. That is a low-spend default. [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/solar-panel-bankruptcies-leave-frustrated-homeowners-in-limbo)
- Lenders also cancel loans or defer instead of curing (one Mosaic cancel-and-refund case, via search excerpt only). [KENS 5](https://www.kens5.com/article/news/investigations/call-kens/southside-man-calls-kens-5-gets-solar-panel-loan-refund-in-days-investigative-san-antonio-texas-telephone-help/273-a7ad59da-43ac-4be4-9861-746ede725172)

## 4. Pressure on the "non-obvious insight"
- GreenLancer's own release says permit and warranty documentation is "often unavailable or erroneous." The records-recovery thesis is already its stated pain point.
- Records are not purely locked. Tucson Electric Power's orphan process lets the new installer submit the customer's records release, keeps the original interconnection clock, and refuses reassignment by the dead installer. The customer, not the lender, has the authority. [TEP](https://docs.tep.com/wp-content/uploads/Orphan-FAQs-for-Installers.pdf) This is one utility, so I cannot generalize.
- "Lenders can't see which loans are dark" is weak for loans funded in milestones. M2 is paid at installation and M3 (often about 10%) at utility approval, so the originator can see which jobs lack PTO. [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/news/mosaic-files-for-bankruptcy/) Visibility is probably worse on acquired legacy books and bank or credit-union holdings. That is untested.
- ABS trustees are a weak second wave. In Mosaic Solar 2021-2, pre-PTO loans were capped at 10% of the pool with a repurchase obligation. [ABS Report](https://asreport.americanbanker.com/news/mosaic-solar-trust-issues-191-million-in-abs) Stranded pre-PTO loans mostly sit with originators, warehouse lenders, banks and credit unions. That is my inference.

## 5. Bottom-up market size (my estimates, assumptions flagged)
- Base: 2026 residential is about 4.8 GWdc after an 18% drop, so 2025 was about 5.85 GWdc. [Surge PV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/us-residential-solar-market-trends-2026) A 6.1% share is about 357 MW, or about 45,000 systems at an assumed 8 kW.
- Assume a 3-4 month sale-to-PTO cycle, so 8-15k projects were in flight. If 15-30% were unenergized at collapse, that is roughly 1,200-4,500 stranded jobs.
- Assume 25-50% reaches independent vendors, since the seven financiers keep much in-house or with GreenLancer. At $2.5-3k all-in, that is roughly $1-7M, one time.
- Ongoing: 100+ closures since 2023 at 20-50 unfinished jobs each (Tucson Solar Pros had 39 loans, Empire 45 homes) gives roughly 600-1,400 jobs a year, or $2-4M a year at about $3k.
- Dashboard: 10-25 realistic buyers at an assumed $25-60k a year is $0.25-1.5M a year, with no pricing evidence.
- Total: about $3-10M a year in a good year. That is a services business, not venture-scale.
- Do not use the "820,000", "500,000+" or Titan "150,000 households" figures. I could not verify them. Titan said it had helped over 100,000 households, which is not a stranded count. [TIME](https://time.com/6989810/titan-solar-closing/) Most legacy orphans are energized systems needing O&M, a $700-inspection-quote business. [CBS Texas](https://www.cbsnews.com/texas/news/bankruptcies-leave-some-solar-customers-with-broken-panels/)

## 6. Unit-economics analogs
- Price ceiling: Atma quotes up to $1,200 for a Texas permit takeover and close-out, below the $1.5-4k paperwork tier. GreenLancer says permit work "can cost extra" but publishes no prices. [GreenLancer](https://www.greenlancer.com/post/orphaned-solar-projects)
- Budget anchor (inference): M3 at about 10% of a $30-50k loan is $3-5k, which fits the fee band if lenders redirect the unreleased holdback.
- Closest analog is mortgage property preservation, where lender-paid field work is priced to caps. Fannie Mae's June 2025 allowables raised initial securing from $250 to $325 (secondary source). [summary](https://lakemeadmobilenotary.com/insights/fnma-svc-2025-03-property-preservation-expense-limit-increases-nevada) Vendors report about $19.85 per work order in administrative and tech cost before service costs. [NAMFS](https://irp.cdn-website.com/836ce7e7/files/uploaded/NAMFS_Leadership_Summit_2026_NAMFS+Pricing+Initiative+Session+Brief.pdf) Volume is countercyclical and fixed costs hurt in quiet years, per Altisource analyses. [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2020/01/15/this-countercyclical-stock-could-be-a-good-hedge.aspx) Lesson: expect per-order caps and event-driven volume. A standby retainer is unproven in that analog (inference).
- Illustrative margin (unsourced assumption): if each job needs 8-12 coordinator hours plus an electrician sub at $800-1,500, a $2.5k job nets roughly 30-45%. Missing-hardware and inspection-fix tiers carry the margin.

## 7. Regulatory and liability landscape
- Licensing is the hardest barrier. In California an unlicensed contractor cannot sue to collect compensation for work needing a license. [B&P 7031](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_7031) The C-46 solar class needs about four years of journey-level experience and a $25k bond per a secondary source. [CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/About_Us/Library/Licensing_Classifications/Licensing_Classifications_Detail.aspx?Class=C46), [guide](https://contractorlicenserequirements.com/california/solar-license-requirements/) Other states vary: Arizona CR-17 and Nevada C-2g per the same guide. Subcontracting labor does not remove the need for a licensed contractor of record. Oregon tightened solar licensing on June 5, 2026. [Energy Trust](https://blog.energytrust.org/?p=13916)
- Taking over a permit carries liability. Florida's Sunrise form has the new contractor accept responsibility for correcting prior work. [Sunrise](https://sunrisefl.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/8456/638464612289300000) Dana Point may cancel and reissue the permit, require a "special investigation" of unpermitted work, and charge fees. [Dana Point](https://danapoint.org/home/showpublisheddocument/30946/637267090685100000) New Castle County requires owner consent and a termination notice. [New Castle](https://www.newcastlede.gov/DocumentCenter/View/476/Change-of-Contractor-Requirements-PDF)
- Lender exposure creates demand. The FTC Holder Rule lets borrowers assert installer claims against loan holders, generally capped at amounts paid. The CFPB flagged hidden dealer fees in 2024, and dealer-fee TILA suits are surviving motions to dismiss. [Ballard Spahr](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/03/26/todays-podcast-episode-residential-solar-finance-under-intensifying-scrutiny-key-regulatory-and-litigation-trends/) Connecticut has about 65 complaints against SunStrong [CT AG](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-announces-new-developments-to-hold-solar-industry-accountable), and Texas AG demands went to Freedom Forever and Sunrun. [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/04/texas-attorney-general-launches-investigation-into-residential-solar-sales-practices/)
- State recovery funds are weak: Minnesota's requires a civil judgment and a licensed contractor, so the lender is the realistic payer.
- Not researched: insurance cost for completion work, data-privacy limits on cross-matching loan tapes against utility records, and utility-by-utility interconnection rules beyond TEP.

## 8. Risks ranked
1. Lender default is cancel, defer, or use a captive team, not a third-party fixed-fee vendor.
2. GreenLancer (and Sunrun/GoodLeap crews) already hold the first-wave relationships.
3. Licensing in every state and AHJ, plus inherited liability.
4. Small, lumpy revenue with price caps.
5. Triage data may be unobtainable. Utility and AHJ records are fragmented and may need customer consent.

# I0428-M1 Dossier: Line-Keyed Cross-Examination Kits for LOP Defense

Research date: 2026-10-13. About 45 searches and fetches. Claims marked [unverified] rest on one secondary source or on my inference.

## Verdict

Re-scope; do not build as written. The idea has real forced-spend triggers and a plausible product. But the "gap" finding does not survive checking in Texas, because a Texas counteraffidavit industry already sells the signed-coder layer. The testimony profit center faces documented exclusion risk in Georgia's federal courts. No source confirms the $395 price point, the $3-5k expert-cost premise or the carrier pre-approval threshold. The surviving core is the line-keyed cross-examination package plus provider-bias work-up, which I found no one selling. Best entry: Georgia or Texas, sold as a counteraffidavit-plus-cross bundle, after mystery-shopping incumbents for price.

## 1. Market context (bottom-up)

**Claims backdrop.** Auto bodily injury payouts passed physical damage for the first time: BI was 52.3% of combined BI and PD dollars, up from 44.4% in 2022. Average third-party medical expenses submitted rose to $32,300 in Q1 2026 from $24,300 in Q1 2023 (CCC data, [Claims Journal, Aug 2026](https://amp.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/06/339296.htm)). This supports "medical specials are rising and contested." It is not LOP-specific.

**Volume.** Texas courts saw 61,782 motor-vehicle injury/damage civil filings in FY2024, up 16% ([OCA FY2024 highlights](https://txcourts.gov/media/1459785/2024-ar-highlights.pdf)). The extracted text does not state the court levels covered. I found no Georgia equivalent and no LOP-prevalence statistic for any wedge state. The only prevalence datapoint is an unverified Publix lawyer's claim that about two-thirds of its Florida slip-and-fall claims involve LOPs ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/republish/courts/letters-of-protection-personal-injury-cases-surprise-bills/)).

**Reachable buyers.** No count of 3-20 lawyer defense boutiques exists in public sources. Proxies from press releases surfaced in search (2010-2019, stale, pages not individually fetched): the Texas Association of Defense Counsel cited 1,500+ members in 2019 and up to 2,000 earlier; the Georgia Defense Lawyers Association cited about 920. My estimate: 80-150 wedge firms across Houston and Atlanta, once large panel firms and carrier staff counsel are excluded [inference].

**Revenue ceiling.** The finalist's own mix is 12 firms x 5 kits x $395, about $23.7k/month. With 10-15% upsell to $1,900 declarations, that adds $11-17k/month. At 100 firms and the same mix, the metro-wedge SAM is roughly $3-5M/year [inference]. This is a good lifestyle business or a seed-stage wedge, not a venture-scale market by itself.

## 3. Incumbents who could add this

- **Enlyte/CCC:** distribution to carriers is strong, but both sell to adjusters. Testimony and counsel-facing work product conflict with that channel. Likelier move: partner or refer.
- **Texas counteraffidavit shops:** most likely to copy. They already hold coder networks and defense relationships; adding an LLM-drafted cross outline is a template exercise [inference].
- **General legal-AI tools:** a defense associate can ask a generalist LLM to turn an itemized bill into cross questions. The unique value is the credentialed signature and defensible benchmark data, not the outline [inference].

## 4. Evidence of demand and willingness to pay

**Forced spend (strongest evidence):**
- *Texas § 18.001.* A counteraffidavit must be served by the earliest of 120 days after the defendant's answer or the expert-designation deadline; it must be sworn and give reasonable notice of its basis ([statute text](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._civ._practice_and_remedies_code_section_18.001), last amended 2019). The Texas Supreme Court upheld a nurse-CPC counteraffidavit and declined full Daubert gatekeeping at that stage ([*In re Allstate*, 2021](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1452189/200071.pdf); [summary](https://www.mdjwlaw.com/newsroom/news/TIN-20210510-Item2)). *Ortiz v. Nelapatla* (reported May 2026) held affidavits are assessed item by item, so partial challenges leave unchallenged charges admissible ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2026/advocacy-team-wins-texas-supreme-court-decision)). Line-by-line audit output is therefore well matched to Texas practice.
- *Georgia SB 68 (signed April 21, 2025).* Juries may see billed and paid amounts; LOP-related information is discoverable per secondary summaries, including referral source and sale of receivables ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/04/georgia-enacts-major-tort-reform-legislation); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/georgia-enacts-sweeping-tort-reform)). The medical-expense provisions apply only to causes of action arising on or after April 21, 2025 (Jones Day). With a two-year limitations period, the volume of qualifying suits builds through 2027 [inference].
- *Florida § 768.0427* already mandates disclosure of the LOP, coded itemized bills and factoring purchase price ([statute](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._768.0427)); appellate guidance on it is thin ([Sept/Oct 2025 article](https://mydigitalpublication.com/article/Evidence+/5039476/852249/article.html)).

**Carrier spend on adjacent services:** Enlyte markets "$5-7K improvement in average settlement" and up to $30,000 LAE savings per 100 demands (vendor claims, same Enlyte page). That implies carrier willingness to pay in the low hundreds of dollars per package [inference].

**Not found:** forum complaints, RFPs, published counteraffidavit prices, or any quote validating $395 or "$3-5k". General expert benchmarks are $356/hr for review, $448/hr for deposition and $478/hr for trial ([Expert Institute](https://www.theexpertinstitute.com/expert-witness-fees)). At those rates $3,500/day testimony is market, and $3-5k is plausible for a report plus testimony but is not documented.

**Efficacy caveat:** a plaintiff-side Georgia firm argues jurors rarely debate fair price once a procedure is deemed necessary ([Kastorf](https://kastorflaw.com/analysis/medical-billing-experts/)). A Louisiana note finds billing experts cannot reduce awards there at all ([LSU Law Review](https://lawreview.law.lsu.edu/?p=2981)). Willingness to pay depends on outcomes that I could not measure.

## 5. Unit economics

- Kit: $395 price, $40-90 marginal cost (founder estimate, unverified), implying 77-90% gross margin. Coder wage was not researched.
- Real costs are outside the estimate: retained testifying coders, Daubert defense, E&O insurance, data-licensing (Context4 versus TiC engineering) and sales to a fragmented buyer.
- Upsell math: the finalist's 10-15% attach rate fits Texas, where a counteraffidavit is a near-mandatory early step. In Georgia I verified no equivalent affidavit regime, so attach rate there is likely lower [inference].
- Panel-counsel hourly rates (the ROI comparison) could not be found; carrier guidelines are private.

## 6. Regulatory, liability and admissibility

- **Texas:** coders qualify for counteraffidavits (*Allstate*), so the signature is a commodity there; trial testimony still faces ordinary Rule 702 scrutiny [inference from the stage-limited holding].
- **Georgia/11th Circuit:** *Young v. Panera* (M.D. Fla. Aug 2024) excluded a defense coding expert who relied on national averages, finding no expertise on reasonableness and unfair prejudice that outweighed value; it cites *Frushtick v. FeroExpress* (N.D. Ga. 2022) and others ([case page](https://www.flexlaw.co/case/1419574/2024-young-v-panera-llc), AI-generated summary; verify the opinion). Other courts admitted a Context4-based coder (*Cordero v. Target*, [Marshall Dennehey](https://www.marshalldennehey.com/thought-leadership/overcoming-the-daubert-challenge-with-your-billing-and-coding-expert)). Exclusion risk is real, and it directly undermines "testimony is the profit center."
- **New benchmark risk:** no court decision found accepting Transparency-in-Coverage rates as reasonable-value proof. The Allstate-approved method is a UCR database.
- **Bias attack on the vendor:** "kits as funnel, testimony as margin" invites cross on the coder's financial interest in repeat defense work, the same attack the kit teaches [inference]. Separating the kit author from the testifying expert, and keeping consulting work product privileged, needs counsel review (no source found).
- **UPL, E&O and AI-error liability:** not researched; a gap.
- **Statutes not enacted:** Texas SB 30 (LOP disclosure, paid-amount limit) died in conference May 2025 ([LegiScan summary](https://legiscan.com/TX/bill/SB30)). HB 2446 (affidavit alone sufficient up to $50,000) passed the House engrossed; final status unverified ([text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB02446E.htm)). If enacted, it would weaken the Texas counteraffidavit market's role in small files.

## 7. Is the why-now real?

- **Real:** Georgia SB 68 (post-April 2025 claims), record BI severity, and scaled plaintiff AI. Plaintiff AI raises demand volume but no source ties it to defense expert spend.
- **Misframed:** Texas had no new LOP statute (SB 30 failed). Its counteraffidavit workflow dates from 2019-2021.
- **Premature/thin:** The 2026 TiC final rule (published Oct 6, 2026) removes implausible provider-service rates, adds taxonomy and utilization files, and lowers the allowed-amount claim threshold from 20 to 11. Rate-file changes apply 5 months after publication; the new contextual files, 11 months ([CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/transparency-coverage-final-rules-cms-9882-f); [Turquoise analysis](https://turquoise.health/resources/blog/transparency-in-coverage-final-rule-2026-what-changes-for-payer-machine-readable-files)). The "same billing TIN's own negotiated rates" anchor only exists where the LOP provider is in a payer network. LOP care often bypasses health insurance ([TMLT](https://www.tmlt.org/risk-alerts/what-physicians-should-know-about-letters-of-protection); [KFF](https://kffhealthnews.org/republish/courts/letters-of-protection-personal-injury-cases-surprise-bills/)). Expect many blank anchors [inference].
- The factor-price anchor is statutory only in Florida; in Texas and Georgia it depends on discovery success.

## 8. Moat assessment

- Track-record asset (Daubert/cross survival): potentially strong, but slow to build and fragile if one ruling goes wrong.
- Switching costs: weak; kits are per-file.
- Data: weak; Medicare, TiC and Context4 are all available to rivals.
- Process power (LLM pre-code plus coder sign-off): moderate, quickly copied.

## 9. Validation steps (cheap, in order)

1. Mystery-shop MedCost Contain, IMS Legal, No World Borders and Health Law Network for counteraffidavit and testimony quotes. If a Texas counteraffidavit costs under about $500, the kit's price edge disappears.
2. Run the 15 defense-lawyer calls to confirm the pre-approval threshold; a desk search found none (old ethics opinions say pre-approval interferes with counsel's judgment, [RI 1999](https://ezel.ai/ethics-opinions/rieap/99-18-insurer-litigation-guidelines-and-independent-judgment)).
3. Pull 20 real LOP bills and test how many providers appear in any payer's TiC files. Under about 40% coverage, redesign the benchmark around Context4/UCR plus Medicare.
4. Ask for one Georgia state-court ruling admitting a coder on reasonable value. If none exists, drop the testimony thesis.

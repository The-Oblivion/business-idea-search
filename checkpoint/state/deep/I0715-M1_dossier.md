# I0715-M1 Dossier: Smoke Report Autopsy for Policyholder Advocates

Research date: 2026-10-13. Basis: roughly 45 searches and fetches, including the CDI task force report and the April 2026 Assembly committee analysis, both text-searched in full.

## Verdict

**Conditional and weaker than the pitch.** The pain is real and documented, but the stated why-now is partly wrong. AB 1795 appears not to reach the Eaton and Palisades claims the wedge targets. The insurer's 30-day inspection is elective, not a duty. A free, NSF-funded AI report-reader is already in development, and a funded PA-software startup (Finch) sits in the buyer's inbox. Realistic year-one revenue is about $0.1M-$0.5M, not $1.5M-$2.5M (my estimate, derived in section 4).

If it proceeds, it should be reframed as workflow software for policyholder-side CIHs, litigators and PAs, built on existing claims-handling law. It should not be sold as an "AB 1795 letter generator" for the January 2025 fires.

## 1. Why-now audit: what holds and what does not

**Holds**
- AB 1795 was signed 9/15/2026 and takes effect 1/1/2027. It creates a rebuttable smoke-source presumption and makes insurers pay for "necessary" sampling and testing ([CDI release](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2026/upload/091526-Consumer-Alert-California-protects-families-from-hidden-smoke-damage-after-wildfires.pdf)).
- The dispute is real. CDI's task force says more than 40,000 claims were filed, more than 13,000 of them standing homes with smoke damage. It cites conflicting views on how and where to sample, no uniform standards, and allegations of biased experts ([CDI task force report](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/140-catastrophes/upload/CDI-Smoke-Claims-and-Remediation-Task-Force-Report-030926.pdf)).
- The litigation wave is real:
  - Ghazarian v. Fire Insurance Exchange and HygieneTech, a proposed class action ([Singleton Schreiber](https://www.singletonschreiber.com/newsroom/pressreleases/Lawsuit-Filed-Against-Farmers-Insurance-for-Bad-Faith-Smoke-Damage-Practices)), plus a second class action against hygiene vendors ([Recorder via firm](https://www.singletonschreiber.com/newsroom/news/hygiene-companies-face-class-actions-over-alleged-failure-to-properly-test-homes-for-toxins-after-eaton-wildfire)).
  - A federal suit against State Farm and its vendor EFI Global ([firm release](https://singleton.sitepilot11.firmseek.com/newsroom/pressreleases/lawsuit-filed-against-state-farm-and-environmental-consultant-for-bad-faith-smoke-damage-practices)).
  - LA County v. State Farm, 8/31/2026 ([Spectrum](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/ca/california/wildfires/2026/08/31/la-county-state-farm-lawsuit-wildfire-claims)).
  - A county probe of Farmers ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/la-county-opens-investigation-into-farmers-over-wildfire-claims-handling-589622.aspx)).
  - Beutner v. Chubb, filed October 2026 ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/10/ex-lausd-chief-austin-beutner-sues-chubb-over-toxic-palisades-home/)).

**Does not hold, or is overstated**
- **No retroactivity.** Hoodline states that neither AB 1642 nor AB 1795 "applies retroactively" ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/10/ex-lausd-chief-austin-beutner-sues-chubb-over-toxic-palisades-home/)). Merlin Law Group analyses say the principal testing, payment and restoration provisions apply prospectively to residential claims on or after the operative date ([Merlin 9/30/2026](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/california-wildfire-smoke-laws/); [Merlin follow-up](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/?p=46909)). The April 2026 committee analysis applied provisions to policies issued, amended or renewed on or after the operative date ([Assembly analysis](https://ains.assembly.ca.gov/media/364)). The "AB 1795 deficiency letter" therefore cannot be a statutory demand on January 2025 claims. At best it is a persuasive benchmark.
- **Inspection is elective.** Merlin says insurer inspection is optional, with the 30-day clock applying only if the insurer elects to inspect. The CalMatters digest says insurers that choose to inspect must do so within 30 days ([digest](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab1795)). The pitch's "30-day inspection duty" is wrong.
- **Testing is not automatic.** Kennedys' Brandon Sipple notes the law does not require professional testing of every impact-zone home and does not "dispense with" proving smoke damage is present ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/10/08/340579.htm)).
- **No statutory adequacy yardstick.** Merlin's analysis does not say who is qualified to test or what a sampling plan must contain, and I found no source saying the statute defines either. DTSC lead and asbestos standards are due end-2028, other contaminants end-2029 ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/2026/09/new-law-smoke-damage-in-homes/)). The Grid would be self-authored, not statutory.
- **The "inadequate sampling" premise is contested science.**
  - AIHA guide authors told the task force that smoke, ash and soot are accepted surrogates, that targeted sampling against background is correct, and that "exhaustive sampling" is unnecessary ([CDI report, summary and Section III.B](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/140-catastrophes/upload/CDI-Smoke-Claims-and-Remediation-Task-Force-Report-030926.pdf)).
  - A scorecard citing AIHA to penalize low sample counts invites a rebuttal that quotes AIHA.
  - Comparing results to background, which the pitch treats as a flaw, is the AIHA-endorsed method.
- **A closing litigation window.** Suits on the 2025 fires are generally due by 1/7/2027 under Ins. Code 2071's 24-month period, though tolling is argued ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/10/ex-lausd-chief-austin-beutner-sues-chubb-over-toxic-palisades-home/); [A&A Law](https://aa.law/blog/eaton-fire-claim-deadline-2026/)). Demand for report triage may spike before that date. Building and validating by then is a stretch.

## 2. Demand and willingness-to-pay evidence

- A United Policyholders survey (n=453 households, about 154 standing homes, self-selected) found 52% of standing-home respondents said the insurer "brought in experts they didn't trust," and only 3% reported no claim problems. It also found 83% of standing-home respondents had hired professional help (type undefined) and 86% had not moved back ([UP report](https://uphelp.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/REPORT_-2025-LA-Wildfires-6-Month-Survey-Results.pdf)).
- Current spend on workarounds:
  - Independent hygienist testing runs $2,000-$20,000+ ([Kerley Schaffer](https://www.kerleyschaffer.com/blog/fair-plan-hygenist-testing-guide)).
  - One family paid about $17,000 and another about $10,000. A consultant offered seven samples for $3,500, and an insurer paid one vendor $25,000 for a single inspection ([Consumer Watchdog transcript](https://consumerwatchdog.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Smoke-Mirrors-Episode-2-The-Underworld-of-Fire-Insurance-Subcontractors.txt)).
- A policyholder-side attorney reports that insurer hygienists took no more than ten samples in a 3,000 sq ft home and skipped wall cavities, attic insulation and HVAC unless the insured paid ([Merlin, 2025](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/california-wildfire-smoke-claims/)). This is an advocate's account. The Consumer Watchdog transcript independently describes six tape lifts and a 15-sample job.
- PA fees run roughly 5%-15% of settlement ([UP](https://uphelp.org/what-southern-california-wildfire-victims-can-expect-from-their-insurance-adjuster/)). A $395 audit is small against that if it moves outcomes.
- **Not found:** any evidence that a deficiency letter changes carrier behavior. I also found no PA, attorney or carrier purchase, RFP or pilot for report-adequacy scoring. The $40k-$150k plaintiff analytics price is unvalidated.

## 4. Bottom-up market size

My estimates, with assumptions stated:

- **Reachable homes.** 13,000 standing-home smoke claims (CDI). If PAs represent 30%-50% of them (assumed, because I found no PA count), that is about 3,900-6,500 homes. If 30%-50% of those still have a disputed carrier IH report, that is about 1,200-3,250 homes.
- **PA audits.** At 10%-20% paid conversion in 12 months, that is 120-650 audits. At $395 that is **$47k-$257k**. Subscriptions add little, because PA tools price at $99-$250 a month ([ClaimWizard](https://claimwizard.com/pricing/)).
- **Plaintiff analytics.** At most 0-2 pilots in year one at $25k-$75k, or **$0-$150k**. Plaintiff-side activity is concentrated: Singleton Schreiber brought both class actions and the State Farm/EFI suit. Buyer concentration is therefore high, and none has been shown to buy.
- **Year-one total, about $0.1M-$0.5M.** The pitch's math puts 15% of 13,000 at about $770k, so its $1.5M-$2.5M depends on $0.7M-$1.7M of unvalidated analytics contracts. 100% penetration of 13,000 homes at $395 would be only $5.1M.
- **Lumpy and event-driven.** Post-2027 TAM depends on future declared-emergency wildfires reaching urban standing homes. Other states have no comparable statute; I found none.

## 5. Unit economics

- **Gross margin.** If human QA takes 1-1.5 hours at $60-$150 an hour, plus LLM and OCR costs, gross margin on a $395 audit is plausibly 55%-80%. This is an assumption.
- **The $900 CIH-signed plan** splits 50/50, leaving about $450 for CIH time at the quoted $150 an hour. That works only if review takes about three hours or less, and it carries liability (section 6).
- **Customer acquisition.** The channel is small and relational, with PAs reached through associations and attorneys through referrals. CAC should be low at the start, but the market is shallow (assumption, no CAC data found).
- **Data moat.** The "largest labeled dataset" claim is weak. Purdue already holds 500+ reports ([Purdue deck](https://engineering.purdue.edu/PlumbingSafety/resources/Palisades-Community-Council-May-2026.pdf)), and EFRU had mapped 81 homes by April 2025 ([report](https://www.click2houston.com/news/national/2025/04/11/in-fight-over-insurance-neighbors-crowdsource-la-fire-contamination-data/)). Pooling client reports also raises consent and work-product issues.

## 6. Regulatory and liability landscape

- **CIH sign-off.** California does not license industrial hygienists generally. The CIH is a title-protected credential, and Cal/OSHA only requires "competent" supervision of exposure monitoring. A petition to define CIH as competent is pending ([Cal/OSHA staff evaluation](https://www.dir.ca.gov/oshsb/documents/petition-612-staffeval.pdf)). A non-CIH can therefore draft a plan, but credibility needs a CIH.
- **Certification risk.** The April bill version had CalEPA certify IHs, restorers and labs by 1/1/2028 and CDI certify adjusters and PAs ([Assembly analysis](https://ains.assembly.ca.gov/media/364)). The chaptered digest mentions only adjuster and PA training and Cal/OSHA worker certification ([digest](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab1795)). Which version survived is unverified.
- **PA licensing and direct-to-consumer exposure.** Ins. Code 15007 defines a public adjuster as anyone who, for compensation, "acts on behalf of or aids in any manner" an insured in negotiating a claim, and 15006 bars unlicensed activity ([CDI](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0200-industry/0050-renew-license/0200-requirements/public-adjuster/Authorizing-Act.cfm)). Selling a $295 statute-cited demand letter to homeowners could be argued to be unlicensed adjusting or unauthorized practice of law. I found no authority either way. Selling to licensed PAs and attorneys is safer.
- **Expert and liability exposure.** The scorecard is not an expert opinion. Expert-admissibility fights over qualifications and methodology are a general risk (not sourced in this research). An error that sinks a claim creates E&O exposure for the vendor and the CIH of record.
- **Referral fees.** A testing-firm referral fee on a tool sold to the policyholder side invites bias attacks, so zero fees is cleaner.

## 7. Risks, tests and sharper angle

- **Kill tests** (before building much):
  - Do 10 PAs or attorneys with live files pay $395 after a free audit?
  - Does any carrier reconsider after receiving a scorecard-based letter?
  - Will two policyholder-side CIHs say the Grid matches how they would attack a report?
  - Does the Purdue team confirm a launch date and whether its platform stays explanatory?
- **Sharper angle.**
  - Sell to the scarce policyholder-side CIHs and litigation experts as extraction-plus-gap-list workflow software. They testify, so the buyer-wants-a-testifying-expert objection disappears.
  - Use existing law as the hook: the carrier's duty to investigate and the CDI market-conduct findings. Use AB 1795 and AB 1642 as persuasive benchmarks.
  - Keep a version-controlled Grid so it re-maps to the 2028-29 DTSC standards and to future fires.
  - Pursue data-sharing with Purdue and EFRU rather than racing them.

## 8. Gaps and unverified items

- The chaptered AB 1795 text could not be read. leginfo.legislature.ca.gov returned a Cloudflare 403. Non-retroactivity rests on two secondary sources and the April analysis. The "qualified person" and "certification" points are unconfirmed.
- No count of licensed California PAs or of PA-represented smoke claims was found.
- The Purdue "free review" is documented only as one-on-one household meetings and the research platform. No formal SLA or capacity limit was confirmed.
- The sampling-density and skipped-zone claims come from advocates and are not independently verified.
- I found no data on how many Eaton and Palisades smoke claims are still open and disputed.

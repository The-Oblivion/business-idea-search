# I1175 Finish Crew, refined: Stranded-Loan Cure Desk (2026-10-13)

Sources: the finalist file, the validation dossier, and the three critiques. I did no new research. Every fact below comes from those documents and is only as good as they are. Numbers marked (assumption) are mine.

## Verdict

Alive, but only as a cheap, conditional option, and much smaller than the original pitch.

- Dead: "Finish Crew" as a crew business; Freedom Forever first-wave work; the seven named financiers as buyers; the dashboard subscription; the standby retainer; the workmanship warranty; "records recovery is the moat"; the $3-10M a year market; any venture-scale framing.
- Alive: a lender-side cure desk (file triage plus cure management, no crew, no license held by us) for credit unions and community banks that hold stranded solar loans and have no ops team.
- My odds that it survives the two-week test: about 25 percent. If it survives, it is a one-to-two person services business with a fee pool in the tens to low hundreds of thousands of dollars a year. I would spend no more than the two-week test (about 20 hours, $0) before payer evidence exists.
- Pre-committed kill rule (below). If it fails, the idea is dead. Do not revive it by relabeling the buyer.

## What I concede

1. The "GAP" label is wrong. GreenLancer sells lender-paid completion and permit/interconnection remediation today.
2. The seven Freedom Forever financiers, GoodLeap, Sunrun and Solar Servicing have captive capacity, court authority or contractual system access. They are not buyers.
3. Records are not a moat. EnergyAid buys dead installers' data, Scout sells a permit ledger, and permit transfers are public procedures.
4. The paperwork tier cannot be priced above the substitutes (Atma up to $1,200; in-house coordinators).
5. The dossier's $3-10M a year counted pass-through labor and assumed near-total capture. The fee pool is far smaller (see market section).
6. No payer evidence exists. No RFP, budget line, price or named paying lender was found by the dossier or any critic. This is the whole bet.
7. The Freedom Forever date is not a trigger. Only seven financiers got stay relief, and the Oct 16 date is a claims deadline that releases no spend.

## The refined business

**One-liner.** A cure desk for credit unions and community banks that hold stranded pre-PTO solar loans: triage every loan on a failed installer's book into cure or unwind, then manage the cure through a licensed contractor-of-record partner for a flat per-file fee. Panel enrollment is free and done before the next installer fails.

**Wedge customer.** Head of loss mitigation, special assets or consumer lending at a credit union, community bank or CUSO that holds indirect solar loans written through an installer that has just closed, where the loans were fully or mostly funded at install and payments are paused or at risk, and no staff or vendor handles cure.
- Archetype: Old Pueblo Credit Union in Tucson, 39 loans of $30-35k, more than $1M of exposure, payments paused, up-front full funding ended (dossier; critic 3 cites the same event).
- Found within days of a closure through the bankruptcy docket and creditor matrix, state contractor-board status, platform capital-partner lists (Digital FCU and Connexus are named Mosaic partner institutions), and borrower complaints. This lead-sourcing method is itself an assumption the test checks.
- Excluded: the Freedom Forever seven, GoodLeap, Sunrun, Solar Servicing and other holders with captive teams; milestone-funded loans where the unreleased holdback lets a local contractor be paid directly; ABS trustees (pre-PTO loans were capped at 10% of the Mosaic 2021-2 pool); legacy energized systems that need O&M, not completion.

**Why the lender pays at all.** Only when the borrower stops paying or a regulator is watching. Where borrowers keep paying, lenders defer or ignore. Exposure per loan ($30-50k) versus cure cost ($1.2-4k) is the economic logic, but only for paperwork and inspection-fix tiers. A never-started job is a bad loan, and the right answer is unwind. That is why triage ships first.

**First product: Triage Sprint.** 10 business days, one failed installer, one lender. Input: loan ID, service address, install date (no more personal data than that; GLBA service-provider terms to be confirmed with counsel). Output per loan:
- the public permit and utility interconnection status;
- a lien and estate-held-hardware screen;
- a tier: paperwork-only, inspection fix, missing hardware, unwind;
- clock risk (permit expiry, tariff or interconnection deadlines);
- a cost-to-cure range and a cure-or-unwind recommendation.

It needs no license, no homeowner contract and no crew. Liability is advisory, covered by E&O insurance. It is also the sales exhibit for the cure work.

**Cure management (upsell, only where a licensed partner exists).**
- A licensed electrical contractor of record holds the license, insurance and the contract. We are the file manager. The state-by-state legal structure of that arrangement (including referral and coordination fees) goes to a construction attorney before the first dollar.
- Lender-issued purchase order per job with a not-to-exceed amount; 40 percent deposit at file acceptance; hardware and permit fees paid by the lender directly or passed through at cost. We never front labor, permits or hardware.
- Fee milestones inside our control: 60 percent at inspection final plus complete PTO application accepted; 40 percent at PTO.
- Optional Structure B to test on calls: the homeowner contracts with the licensed partner and the lender funds by direction-to-pay or principal credit, which avoids vendor onboarding. GoodLeap's one Texas case ($2,000 credit plus a licensed contractor) is a precedent.

**Pricing (hypotheses to test, anchored on the substitutes).**

| Item | Price to lender | Our share |
|---|---|---|
| Triage Sprint | $150 per loan, $5,000 minimum, 100% credited against cure POs signed within 90 days | all of it |
| Paperwork-only cure | $1,200 all-in cap (matches Atma's ceiling) | about $450 file fee; a loss-leader for speed and references |
| Inspection-fix cure | $3,000-4,500 all-in | about $900-1,200 file fee |
| Missing hardware | hardware at cost, paid by lender direct, plus file fee; declined if the lender will not pay the distributor directly | file fee only |
| Panel enrollment | free; no retainer | none |

No free pilots. No pay-at-PTO-only terms.

## Why now (rewritten)

The dated catalyst is spent. What remains is a recurring base rate.
- 25D expired at the end of 2025. Q2 2026 residential installs were 995 MW, down 12% year over year and the lowest quarter in five years. Wood Mackenzie now forecasts a 23% full-year decline and customer acquisition cost up 40% to $0.84/W.
- Solar-loan ABS net losses were 3.67% annualized in July 2026, up 84 bps year over year (KBRA).
- Solar Insure counts 100+ installer closures since 2023, about one every two weeks. Each leaves 20-50 unfinished jobs (Tucson Solar Pros 39 loans; Empire 45 homes).
- Lender regulatory exposure to dark systems is rising: a Michigan AG suit naming financiers that released funds before PTO (July 2026, per critic 3's source), Texas AG demands to Freedom Forever and Sunrun, a Connecticut AG investigation of SunStrong.

Weak spots: this would have worked less well in 2019 because closures were rarer, but exposure under the FTC Holder Rule existed then, so the why-now is moderate, not strong. No dated trigger exists. Each new failure is the trigger, and I cannot predict timing.

## Moat (7 Powers)

None is reachable at launch, and I do not claim one. Two to three are reachable if volume arrives:
1. Switching costs through approved-vendor status. A credit union that has pushed a vendor through third-party-risk review (insurance, questionnaire, SOW, data terms) is unlikely to repeat that for a second vendor before the next failure, and panels are one or two vendors deep. We buy this position by enrolling at zero cost in calm periods, so the review is done before the event.
2. Process power after roughly 100 completed files: per-AHJ and per-utility transfer times, costs and failure modes. GreenLancer accumulates the same data and playbooks are copyable, so this buys speed, not exclusivity.
3. Weak counter-positioning through scope neutrality. Rescue contractors earn on labor and upsell. A flat-fee file manager has no incentive to inflate scope. A lender worried about scope creep may value that. Untested.

Ruled out: scale economies (pool too small), network effects, branding, cornered resource (the license sits with the partner; records sit with homeowners, AHJs, utilities and trustees). Consequence: with no durable moat the business must pay back from job one and stay at one to two people plus contractors.

## Bottom-up market (fee revenue, not billings)

All steps after the first two are my assumptions.
- Failures: 100+ closures since 2023 gives about 25-35 a year. Unfinished jobs: 20-50 each, so about 600-1,400 a year (dossier).
- Held by non-captive holders (credit unions, community banks, small servicers): 30-50%, so about 200-700 jobs.
- Worth curing after triage (not unwind or lien-encumbered): about 60%, so about 120-420 cure jobs.
- Win rate by year 2-3 against GreenLancer, in-house teams and local rescue firms: 25-40%, so about 30-170 files a year at about $800 blended fee, or $25-135k.
- Triage Sprints: 4-10 a year at $5-7.5k (20-50 loans at $150, $5k minimum), or $20-75k.
- Typical-year fee revenue is therefore about $45-210k, on about $75-425k of lender-paid cure billings including pass-through at about $2.5k per job. At 45 percent gross margin on fees, gross profit is about $20-95k.
- Mega-event year (a Freedom Forever-sized collapse, 1,200-4,500 stranded jobs per the dossier, 25-50% reaching independents, 10-15% capture): about 30-340 extra files, so about $25-270k more.
- The dossier's $3-10M figure included labor pass-through and near-total capture. I retract it as a serviceable market.
- Upside not counted: the same buyers hold other contractor-financed home-improvement loans with the same Holder Rule exposure. Untested and not in the plan.

## Competitors and edge

- GreenLancer (direct): design, permit and engineering platform, 200+ field partners, says it partners with seven financiers on completion and PTO remediation. Payer, prices and financier names undisclosed. Edge: none proven. The test checks whether its seven are TPO sponsors and originators, leaving credit unions and community banks uncovered. It is also the natural supplier of engineering stamps.
- Lender in-house: GoodLeap (settlement access to portals and repositories, a "Project Specialist" role, aged-project takeover rights), Sunrun crews, Solar Servicing's pipeline team, Dividend's defer-and-replace. Not buyers.
- Atma, ECG, Revival, Optimus: homeowner-facing takeovers at about $1,200. They are the labor bench and the price ceiling.
- EnergyAid and Otovo: buy dead installers' IP and customer data and sell post-PTO homeowner O&M subscriptions. Adjacent. They commoditize the records layer.
- Scout: sells a 98,000+ Freedom Forever permit ledger, so raw triage data is purchasable.
- Omnidian, SunStrong: legacy portfolio holders, mostly energized-system O&M. Possible buyers or rivals.
- Edge claim: event-triggered speed, cure-or-unwind advice, scope neutrality and a pre-approved panel position for holders who cannot staff a surge.

## Riskiest assumption

A non-captive holder of stranded pre-PTO loans will pay a third party by purchase order, about $1,500 or more all-in per job (or a $5,000-plus triage fee), rather than defer payments, cancel and refund, refer the borrower to a local rescue installer, use staff, or use GreenLancer. All three critics converge here.

## Two-week test (no crew, no license, $0)

Days 1-2: build the target list of 10-12 loss-mitigation or special-assets leads from dockets, platform capital-partner lists and LinkedIn: 3-4 credit unions or community banks with indirect solar books (Digital FCU, Connexus, Old Pueblo-type holders), Solar Servicing and Concord Servicing, Credit Human, EnFin and Participate, plus 2 former lender ops people who know the cure-vendor stack. Record how many leads the docket and capital-partner method yields. Optionally hand-build one aggregate exhibit from public permit records for a dead installer in one county: permits pulled versus permits finaled, no individual names.

Days 3-12: hold 8 or more calls. Five questions:
1. How many loans do you hold on systems not at PTO or dark, and can you see them?
2. Who works them today: staff, GreenLancer, borrower referral, nobody?
3. What did the last cure cost per job, and who paid?
4. Would you issue a PO for a flat file fee with pass-through labor? Who signs, and how long does onboarding take for a vendor under $50k?
5. Would you sign a one-page LOI today for a paid Triage Sprint at $150 per loan?

Also call 2-3 licensed electrical contractors for the contractor-of-record partner fee, license state, and E&O terms, and call GreenLancer as a prospective supplier, asking which buyer types it does not cover.

Days 13-14: ask for the LOI or PO and tally.

**Pass:** at least 3 of 8 say, unprompted, that they have no cure vendor and are not covered by GreenLancer, and state a per-job budget of $1,500 or more, and at least 1 signs an LOI or agrees to a paid Triage Sprint or a funded 10-job PO. And a licensed partner agrees to be contractor of record on terms that fit the price table.

**Kill:** 2 of the first 5 say in-house or "GreenLancer covers it"; or no one states a per-job budget of $1,500 or more; or no one will sign an LOI. If killed, stop.

## Pre-mortem (2028)

1. The payer does not exist; lenders defer, cancel or go in-house. The test targets this.
2. A liability or licensing incident on a cured job. Mitigated by structure (partner is contractor of record; E&O; lien screen; cure-or-unwind triage), not eliminated.
3. No event big enough in 12 months, so cash starves. Mitigated by zero fixed cost beyond E&O and by free panel enrollment, which keeps the option open without a retainer.

## Critique ledger

**Critique 1 (GreenLancer, in-house buyers, records, unit economics, wedge)**
- GAP wrong: conceded. GreenLancer becomes supplier or rival; the test checks its coverage; kill if two of five say it covers them.
- Buyers insource: conceded for the seven, GoodLeap, Sunrun and Solar Servicing; dropped. Wedge is non-captive holders found per event, not a fixed list of 10-25 subscribers.
- Records no moat: conceded. Records are a delivery input and sales exhibit. Moat claims are limited to switching costs and later process power.
- Capped economics and inherited liability: conceded. Paperwork priced at or below $1,200; revenue counted as fee; license, contract and defect liability sit with the partner.
- Wedge less empty: conceded for servicers, ABS and legacy books; dashboard and retainer dropped.

**Critique 2 (payer solved, lender-paid asserted, exposure, sales cycle, vendor risk)**
- Payer solved in-house: true for GoodLeap and the FF seven; excluded.
- Lender-paid unevidenced: conceded and made the riskiest assumption. No fallback payer exists; homeowner-paid money is post-PTO O&M (EnergyAid, Otovo), not completion.
- Exposure unclear: milestone-funded loans excluded; wedge is fully or up-front funded loans and paused-payment books.
- Fee versus 1,000 jobs: recomputed on fee revenue; the business is small and I say so.
- Sales cycle and signer risk: answered with free panel enrollment in calm periods, event-triggered outreach via dockets, and a vendor tier that needs no borrower data until activation. The unpaid pilot is replaced by a paid Triage Sprint.
- Vendor carries cash, regulatory and liability risk: answered with POs, a 40 percent deposit, pass-through hardware and fees, a licensed contractor of record, E&O and a lien screen.

**Critique 3 (timing, incumbents and price, licensing, lender behavior, defensibility)**
- Timing consumed: conceded; FF dropped as a trigger; recurring base rate used, with its weakness stated.
- Incumbent owns the space and price is too high: repriced; the $21/hour in-house comparison is acknowledged (at 8-12 hours a file, in-house paperwork costs roughly $170-500, so the pitch is surge capacity, not lower unit cost).
- Licensing and liability: partner structure; counsel review before the first dollar; pilots only in states with a partner.
- Lender behavior is cancel, defer or ad hoc, and the sales cycle outruns shelf life: unwind is a first-class output; clock risk is a triage field; the panel is built before events.
- No defensibility: conceded; see the moat section.

# Energy Transition & the Grid: Briefing (as of 2026-09-28)

Note: Many figures come from search-result summaries. Several are from SEO or aggregator sites and are flagged (low-confidence). Web search budget ran out mid-research, so a few gaps remain (heat pump shipment data, phantom-load queues).

## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)

- **Power, not chips, is the AI bottleneck.** US data center demand is about 42 GW in 2026 and is projected at 66 GW in 2027 ([pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/06/25/sunrun-tesla-renew-home-announce-plans-for-16-8-gw-virtual-power-plant-program/)). About 36.6 GW is under construction and 201 GW is in planning ([electricchoice.com](https://www.electricchoice.com/datacenters/interconnection-queue/), low-confidence). PJM waits for large campuses reportedly approach 7 years.
- **The gas turbine supply chain is sold out.** GE Vernova's turbine backlog plus slot reservations hit 100 GW in Q1 2026, and about 10 GW is left across 2029-30 ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-investor/807662/), [Manufacturing Mag](https://www.manufacturingmag.com/article/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-2030-data-centers-outbid-utilities)). Data centers are outbidding utilities for slots. BNEF says new gas plants cost about $2,157/kW, up from under $1,500 in 2023 ([EnergyNow](https://energynow.com/2026/04/cost-to-build-a-natural-gas-fired-power-plant-surges-66-bnef-says/)).
- **Batteries crossed a threshold.** Stationary packs fell 45% in a year to about $70/kWh, cheaper than EV packs at $99 ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/battery-storage-costs-hit-record-lows-as-costs-of-other-clean-power-technologies-increased-bloombergnef/)). Utility-scale storage is around $65/MWh outside the US and China ([Ember via Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/12/12/battery-storage-hits-65-mwh-tipping-point-solar/)). US installs hit a record 18.9 GWh in Q2 2026, up 17% YoY, and average duration rose from 2.8 to 3.5 hours ([Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/u.s.-energy-storage-market-posts-record-18.9-gwh-in-q2-as-longer-duration-systems-drive-growth)).
- **Policy whiplash.**
  - The 25C and 25D credits (heat pumps, panels, home solar) ended 12/31/2025. Residential solar is forecast down 21% in 2026 ([WoodMac](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/u.s.-energy-storage-market-posts-record-18.9-gwh-in-q2-as-longer-duration-systems-drive-growth), [Rewiring America](https://homes.rewiringamerica.org/federal-incentives/25c-electrical-panel-tax-credits)). The storage attach rate still rose to 46%.
  - FEOC rules require 55% non-FEOC content for storage that begins construction in 2026, rising to 75% by 2030. About 80% of US cells are Chinese, and Section 301 tariffs on non-EV Li-ion rose to 25% ([Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2026/july/the-prohibited-foreign-entity-(or-feoc)-rules-and-battery-storage/)).
  - The $8.8B state-run HOMES and HEAR home rebates survive, but state rollouts are uneven.
- **Grid operators are turning data centers into a flexible resource.**
  - PJM's July auction cleared at the $325/MW-day cap and came up 6.8 GW short. PJM proposes a backstop auction (Sept 30 to Oct 21, $555/MW-day cap, 15-year terms) and curtailment for large loads that do not bring their own capacity ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-board-backstop-capacity-auction-data-center-curtailment/826347/)).
  - The Duke study found 76 GW of new load could connect if it curtailed just 0.25% of the time ([Power Mag](https://www.powermag.com/duke-researchers-grid-flexibility-key-to-accommodate-load-growth/)).
- **Electricity affordability is now a midterm issue.** Residential rates rose 7.3% from April 2025 to April 2026. Utilities requested a record $31B in rate hikes in 2025 and $18.6B in H1 2026. About 1 in 6 households is behind on bills, and 13.4M households were disconnected in 2024 ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/data-centers/affordability-crisis-utility-bills-rate-hikes-profits-a6567329925/)). A Gallup poll found 7 in 10 oppose data centers in their community, and New York imposed a statewide hyperscale moratorium ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/20/electricity-bills-surging-not-just-data-centers/)).

## 2. Acute pain points

- **Transformers and switchgear.** Power transformer lead times average 128 weeks and generator step-up units 144 weeks. Distribution transformer prices are up as much as 95%. WoodMac models a 30% shortfall in power transformers and 10% in distribution units. Over 40M US distribution transformers are past service life ([Power Mag](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/)). One contrarian says standardized units can ship in 12-14 months, so procurement is the bottleneck.
- **Labor.** BLS projects about 81k electrician openings a year. Data centers pay 25-30% premiums ([Qmerit](https://qmerit.com/blog/electrician-shortage-what-it-means-for-electrical-contractors/), [iRecruit](https://www.irecruit.co/insights/electrician-shortage-2026-impact-data-center-pay)). A delayed commissioning on a 60 MW site costs about $14M a month. Residential electrification is competing for the same people.
- **Installer bankruptcies leave orphaned systems.** Sunnova, Titan, ADT Solar and Freedom Forever all failed. Equipment warranties survive, but nobody does the service labor ([EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/solar-company-warranty-bankruptcy/), [The Cool Down](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/freedom-forever-bankruptcy-solar-customers-repairs/)).
- **Utility-side delays for homeowners.** Xcel in Colorado is months behind on meter swaps and interconnection ([CBS Colorado](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/refunds-horizon-solar-customers-facing-utility-delays-wait-connect-power-grid-colorado)). Heat pump quotes of around $9,000 are common, and panel upgrades cost $1,500-5,000 ([Sustainable Construction Now](https://www.sustainableconstruction-now.com/article/548980/homeowner-says-heat-pump-push-ignores-9000-quotes-rewiring-and-insulation)).
- **Utility regulation rewards capex over efficiency.** Dynamic line rating gives 30-50% more capacity at about one-tenth the cost of reconductoring. Adoption is stuck because operating savings don't earn a return in the rate base ([Relae](https://carbon-direct.com/insights/dynamic-line-rating-the-fastest-gigawatt-is-the-one-you-already-have)).
- **FEOC compliance is opaque.** Non-compliant storage projects are about 43% more expensive, and IRS cost tables are not due until 2027 ([EticaAG](https://eticaag.com/feoc-and-the-itc-keep-your-bess-project-eligible/)).

## 3. Where the money is flowing

- **Home batteries and VPPs.**
  - Base Power raised $1B in Aug 2026. It owns the batteries, charges a $695 install fee plus a $19/month membership, and now sells retail power. Its fleet is over 500 MWh with 200+ MW of utility programs ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/08/03/base-power-raises-1b-to-roll-out-its-giant-new-home-battery/), [Canary](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/base-power-to-launch-100-mw-home-battery-network-for-texas-utility)).
  - Lunar Energy raised $232M ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/04/lunar-energy-raises-232m-to-deploy-home-batteries-that-prop-up-the-grid/)). Span is raising $176M ([Canary](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/climatetech-finance/lunar-energy-lands-232m-home-batteries)).
  - Sunrun, Tesla and Renew Home announced 16.8 GW of "capacity-as-a-service" for data centers, and Sunrun's stock jumped over 30% ([Canary](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/virtual-power-plants/tesla-sunrun-renewhome-vpp)). Dispatch is real: 580 MW from 140k batteries in California on Sept 9, 2026 ([mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/09/21/sunrun-and-tesla-push-580-mw-from-140000-home-batteries-into-californias-evening-peak/)).
- **Storage VC** rose 30% to $4.8B. Form Energy raised $900M+ ([Startup Research](https://blog.mean.ceo/energy-startup-funding-statistics/)).
- **Data center flexibility.** Emerald AI raised $25M from Nvidia, Eaton, GE Vernova and Siemens ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/energy/articles/emerald-ai-raises-25-million-110000588.html)). Voltus sold 100 MW of "bring-your-own-capacity" to Google.
- **Interconnection software.** Tapestry (Google) processed 811 PJM applications, about 220 GW, in under an hour. Pearl Street is deployed at MISO. DOE has a $30M AI4IX program ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/google-backed-tapestry-completes-first-deployment-of-ai-platform-for-pjm-interconnection-application-process/)).
- **Crowded:** home battery hardware and VPP aggregation, utility-side interconnection AI, behind-the-meter gas for hyperscalers, and long-duration storage.

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps

- **Small and mid C&I storage (100-500 kWh)** is stuck between residential kits and utility containers. Minimum order sizes of around 10 MW exclude $0.5-2M projects ([Turskov](https://www.gesungpowergen.com/news/top-10-commercial-battery-storage-companies-in-2026-bess-procurement-guide-for-c-i-projects/), low-confidence). WoodMac forecasts C&I growing 27% a year, but it is only 48 MW a quarter.
- **Orphaned-asset services.** Nobody owns post-bankruptcy O&M for millions of solar and battery systems. Neither installers nor utilities can be the aggregator of last resort.
- **Verification and settlement for VPP capacity sold to hyperscalers.** The Sunrun/Tesla deal has no disclosed pricing, metering or verification standard ([Latitude](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/sunrun-renew-home-tesla-unite-to-sell-16-gw-of-capacity-to-data-centers/)). A neutral firm that certifies and insures "firm" distributed capacity is a possibility.
- **Renters and multifamily.** There is no obvious product, and plug-in solar is the first crack (see below).
- **Things utilities can't do:** deploy operational fixes (DLR) that earn no return, or sell flexibility that shrinks their own capex base.
- **Things the hardware giants can't do:** service ten-year-old systems from a dozen dead installers, or serve non-Tesla and non-Enphase fleets neutrally.

## 5. Weird signals

1. **Data centers hold about 55 GW of idle diesel gensets.** Virginia alone has about 27 GW nameplate permitted, and units run only 10-30 minutes a month ([Latitude](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/the-data-center-boom-is-a-diesel-generator-boom/)). Almost no one treats this as dispatchable grid capacity because of air-permit rules. PJM's new IRAS ("switch to on-site backup") may change that.
2. **Ten states passed plug-in ("balcony") solar bills in 2026 alone.** A tracker lists nine signed into law by Sept 2026 (Utah, Maine, Virginia, Colorado, Maryland, Connecticut, Vermont, New Hampshire, New Jersey), with caps around 1.2 kW. UL 3700 certification now exists ([Third Act](https://thirdact.org/washington/2026/07/10/balcony-solar-update/)). Battery-plus-plug-in bundles are allowed in many of these states.
3. **Australia's home battery subsidy hit 500k installs in about 14 months.** The government raised funding from A$2.3B to A$7.2B and now targets 2M batteries by 2030 ([Energy Storage News](https://www.energy-storage.news/australias-cheaper-home-batteries-program-officially-passes-500000-installations/)). It is a demand-shock template for other countries and US states.
4. **xAI buys batteries on a scale nobody can pin down.** 720 Megapacks in Memphis are reported as 2.8 GWh counted versus 3.3 GWh claimed, and about $1B was spent over 2024-26 ([mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/09/26/nobody-will-say-how-big-xais-memphis-battery-is-2-8-gwh-counted-3-3-gwh-claimed-2000-mw-described/)). Data centers are becoming the largest storage buyers, for load smoothing rather than energy.
5. **Electrician wage arbitrage.** One report says an electrician went from $20/hr residential to $35/hr at a data center and never came back ([AI Home Building](https://aihomebuilding.com/articles/data-center-labor-drain-residential), low-confidence). Home electrification is losing its labor to the AI buildout.
6. **Second-life EV batteries.** Redwood is pairing about 100 used GM packs to give a Michigan plant 1.5 MW. They cost 30-40% less than new LFP, but falling new-cell prices threaten the economics ([Canary](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/long-duration-energy-storage/second-life-ev-grid-batteries-succeed)).

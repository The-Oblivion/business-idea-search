# Briefing: AI Agents and the Agent Economy (as of 2026-09-28)

Method note: 19 searches and about 14 page fetches. The session's WebSearch budget ran out before the last four planned queries (agent inboxes and KYA, Magentic Marketplace, agent sprawl, eval/observability valuations). Many sources are SEO blogs or aggregators, so I flag low-confidence numbers.

## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)
- **Bots passed humans.** Cloudflare says bots are 57.4% of requests vs 42.6% human ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/bot-web-traffic-overtaken-human-web-traffic-data-shows-rcna348522)). In March the CEO forecast the crossover for 2027 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/19/online-bot-traffic-will-exceed-human-traffic-by-2027-cloudflare-ceo-says)). It came about 18 months early.
- An agent doing a shopping task touches about 5,000 sites; a human touches about 5. "Bots don't click ads," so ad-funded web economics break.
- HUMAN Security reports agentic/agentic-browser traffic up roughly 8,000% in 2025, from a tiny base ([summary](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-agent-bot-traffic/)).
- **Unit price down, bills up.** Blended token price fell about 67% YoY ($18.40 to $6.07 per M tokens, Q1'25 to Q1'26). Agentic workflows use 5-30x more tokens per task. 73% of enterprises overran AI budgets ([source](https://pdpspectra.com/blog/ai-token-pricing-economics-2026/)). It also claims Uber's CTO burned the 2026 AI coding budget in four months, which I could not verify.
- **Payment rails for machines shipped.** Stripe/Tempo Machine Payments Protocol launched 2026-03-18 and had 50+ integrated services in week one. Coinbase's x402 went to the Linux Foundation in April 2026 ([overview](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/03/18/machine-payments-tempo-live/)). Also live: OpenAI/Stripe ACP, Google/Shopify/Walmart UCP, Google AP2, Visa TAP, Mastercard Verifiable Intent ([guide](https://opascope.com/insights/ai-shopping-assistant-guide-2026-agentic-commerce-protocols/)). MCP went to the Linux Foundation in December 2025.
- **Law tilted toward agents acting for users.** On 2026-08-04 the Ninth Circuit lifted Amazon's injunction against Perplexity's Comet. It held the *user*, not Perplexity, accessed Amazon, so the CFAA likely does not apply ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2230471/perplexity-has-successfully-overturned-amazon-injunction-on-its-ai-shopping-bot/)). Amazon can still seek rehearing or Supreme Court review.
- **Regulatory deadlines slipped.** The EU Digital Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744) moved Annex III high-risk duties from 2026-08-02 to 2027-12-02. Article 50 transparency stayed on schedule ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). Colorado's AI Act moved to 2027-01-01 and was scaled back ([Carpe Datum](https://www.carpedatumlaw.com/2026/05/colorados-ai-reset-two-weeks-a-white-house-callout-and-a-pivot-away-from-the-eu-model/)). A December 2025 executive order targets state AI laws. Net effect: little binding US or EU agent-specific liability regime for now.

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points
- **Security and identity.** Palo Alto's 2026 report puts machine identities at 109:1 vs humans, up from 82:1, with agents about 73% of them ([summary](https://www.miniorange.com/blog/iam-trends-ai-agents-2026/), secondary source).
- OpenClaw showed the failure mode. Censys found 21,639 exposed instances. 341 malicious skills were found in ClawHub (about 12% of the registry), later reported at 800+ (about 20%). Moltbook exposed 1.5M agent API tokens ([Reco](https://www.reco.ai/blog/openclaw-the-ai-agent-security-crisis-unfolding-right-now)).
- A backdoored LiteLLM package sat on PyPI for 3 hours with about 47,000 downloads ([Beam](https://beam.ai/agentic-insights/ai-agent-security-breaches-2026-lessons)). Google GTIG reports an autonomous multi-agent attack framework harvesting thousands of credentials in under 6 hours ([Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/autonomous-ai-agents-compromise.html)).
- OWASP maps prompt injection to 6 of 10 categories in its Agentic Top 10. Only 37% of organizations can detect shadow AI ([Help Net](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/11/owasp-prompt-injection-ai-security-failures/)).
- **Reliability and cost runaway.** Practitioners describe retry loops turning a $0.40 task into $40. Cases of five-figure overnight API bills are reported ([Reddit roundup](https://dev.to/lura_cardena_7de06f82aacd/ai-agents-on-reddit-late-april-to-early-may-2026-ten-threads-about-cost-reliability-and-real-4f20), [failure modes](https://www.aibuilderclub.com/blog/ai-agent-reliability-cost-control)). The consensus is that agents work in "structured, repetitive, exception-managed workflows" with review queues and rollback. Named pain: no central observability across models and gateways.
- **Project failure.** Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic projects will be canceled by end-2027 and says only about 130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). Anthropic's Project Vend turned profitable only after adding checklists, price-lookup procedures and a supervising "manager" agent ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)).
- **Disputes with no rulebook.** Neither Visa nor Mastercard has published a binding chargeback rule for agent-initiated purchases. Visa's merchant dispute threshold tightened from 2.2% to 1.5% on 2026-04-01 ([Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/ai-agent-chargeback-liability)). The transaction record shows only merchant, timestamp and amount, with no record of the decision ([Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/agentic-commerce/)).
- **Insurance retreat.** ISO's generative-AI exclusion (CG 40 47 01 26) took effect in January 2026, and Verisk is weighing agentic-specific exclusions ([The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/verisk-weighs-new-exclusions-for-agentic-ai-risks-2026-07-10/), paywalled). Carriers are adding sublimits, exclusions and questionnaires ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/end-silent-ai-emerging-ai-exclusions-coverage-fragmentation-and-practical-implications)).

## 3. Where the money is flowing
- **Security and identity consolidation.** Palo Alto closed CyberArk ($25B) on 2026-02-11, then bought Koi (agentic endpoint, April) and Portkey (AI gateway, May) ([PANW](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/company/press/2026/palo-alto-networks-completes-acquisition-of-cyberark-to-secure-the-ai-era)). Okta bought Permiso for about $200M on 2026-07-30 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/okta-buys-ai-security-startup-permiso-source-says-for-about-200m/)). Permiso had raised $29M and was valued around $80M in 2024. Its new SandyClaw product sandboxes and analyzes agent *skills*.
- **Infrastructure.** Temporal raised $550M at $12.55B (2026-09-14). Composio raised $25M Series A led by Lightspeed. Baseten acquired Blaxel. HelmGuard, a London agentic-GRC startup, raised a $7.3M seed ([roundup](https://aiagentsdirectory.com/news/ai-agents-see-major-funding-platform-launches-and-infrastructure-expansion)). Tempo (Stripe/Paradigm) raised $500M ([source](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/coinbase-stripe-back-competing-ai-180319450.html)).
- **Concentration.** Trackers say about 57% of agentic capital went to 10 companies. Cognition raised over $2B, Sierra $950M, Poolside about $1B ([tracker](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/ai-startup-funding-investment/)). This is an aggregator whose totals are internally inconsistent.
- **Crowded.** Generic agent frameworks, orchestration and observability, horizontal security/governance, and sandboxes. E2B, Daytona and Blaxel converged on identical pricing of $0.0828/hr per standard unit ([Bex](https://bex.co/blog/2026/09/09/e2b-daytona-modal-sandbox-pricing-self-hosted)), which signals commoditization. Consumer agentic checkout is crowded by giants (OpenAI, Google, Amazon, Perplexity, Shopify).

## 4. Underserved gaps and incumbent structural blind spots
- **Neutral evidence and dispute layer.** Networks have authorization frameworks (TAP, AP2 mandates, Verifiable Intent) but no fault rules and no post-hoc "flight recorder" that a merchant, bank or insurer would accept.
- **Underwriting needs telemetry.** Insurers are excluding what they cannot model. Only whoever holds runtime traces can price agent risk ([arXiv trace-economic underwriting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16465)). Model labs cannot be the neutral party.
- **Cross-vendor spend governance.** No lab will build the circuit breaker, budget cap or router that works across competitors' models. Enterprise pain is documented in Section 2.
- **Multi-protocol merchant plumbing.** Merchants must support ACP, UCP, AP2, MPP and x402. Agent usage is about 62% at comparison but about 23% at checkout ([report](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/17/3345937/28124/en/ai-shopping-agents-and-agentic-commerce-2026-adoption-trends-and-execution-limits.html)), so trust and execution are the bottleneck.
- **Platforms are conflicted.** Amazon fought Comet because agents skip its ads and upsell, and it alleged Comet disguised itself as Chrome. Consumer-side agents need someone neutral to represent the *buyer*.
- **Pay-per-bot monetization.** Publishers cannot micro-charge 5,000-site crawlers, and Cloudflare is the only major toll-booth builder so far.
- **Thin verticals.** Aggregator data suggests agriculture, energy and real estate agent startups raise only $5-8M rounds ([tracker](https://aifunding.me/ai-agent-funding)), which is low-confidence.

## 5. Weird signals
- **On-chain "agent commerce" is mostly fake.** Bloomberg (March 2026, via [Emerging Fintech](https://www.emergingfintech.co/p/the-agentic-web-inside-the-protocol)) put agent on-chain volume at about $24M, but only about $1.6M once wallets trading with themselves were removed. Base logged about 97M protocol transactions, and reporters say it is unclear how much is live vs testing. Real demand is far below the protocol hype.
- **Agents hiring humans: 50:1 oversupply.** RentAHuman (launched 2026-02-01) had about 590,000 signed-up "meatworkers" but only about 11,300 active bounties and 5,500 completed jobs by March, plus scams and payment errors ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/what-is-rentahuman)). Labor supply is abundant, and *trusted agent demand* is scarce.
- **A hobby project became infrastructure in 4 months.** OpenClaw hit about 247k GitHub stars by 2026-03-02. Its creator joined OpenAI on 2026-02-14, and China restricted its use in state agencies and banks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw)). "Skills" are the new npm supply chain: about 20% of the registry was reportedly malicious.
- **Supply glut.** One Reddit thread (low-confidence, self-reported) tracks 67,000 open-source agent projects, a 45x supply increase, and a 99% creator failure rate. Shipping an agent is easy, and getting anyone to care is hard.
- **Legal doctrine is converging on "the agent is the user."** The Ninth Circuit ruling means platform blocking rests on contract and technical measures, not hacking law. That could create demand for agent authentication and reputation services that sites *want* to accept.

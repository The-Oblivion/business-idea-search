# Briefing: Weird, niche and overlooked markets (hobbies, collectibles, faith, subcultures)
Date: 2026-09-28

**Research limits, read first.** The session's WebSearch budget (200 of 200) was exhausted before this scout started, so I ran zero searches. I fetched about 15 primary or reference pages directly. Some returned 403 or 404, and several Wikipedia pages held no data on the topic asked. Items marked **[F]** come from a fetched page. Items marked **[U]** are unverified background knowledge (training cutoff June 2026) and should be treated as leads, not facts. Wikipedia-derived numbers passed through a summarizer and are medium confidence at best.

## 1. Biggest shifts of the last 12-18 months

- **Trading cards have gone industrial.** Pokémon TCG passed **85 billion cards printed worldwide by March 2026**, up from 52.9 billion in March 2023, about 32 billion cards in three years [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Trading_Card_Game
- **Card prices are at extremes.** A PSA 10 Pikachu Illustrator sold for **$16.5M in Feb 2026** (it was $5.275M in 2021). A PSA 9 copy sold for $1.4M in Mar 2026. The PSA 10 to PSA 9 gap is about 12x [F, same source]. The gap between grades is the pricing engine of the whole grading industry.
- **Live-stream commerce is the new collectibles channel.** Whatnot went from $3B of goods sold in 2024 to **over $8B in 2025**, and claims about 60% of North America and Europe live commerce [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whatnot
  - Funding: Series E $265M at $4.97B (Jan 2025), then Series F $225M at **$11.5B** (Oct 2025, DST and CapitalG).
  - In Mar 2026 it added a Shopify integration and, in the same month, 15 arbitration claims were filed [F]. Top categories are sports cards, TCG and collectibles.
- **Faith affiliation stopped falling but stayed low.** Pew's 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study shows Christians at **62%** (78% in 2007, 71% in 2014) and "nones" at **29%** (16% in 2007). Christian share has plateaued at 60-64% since 2019 [F] https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/02/26/decline-of-christianity-in-the-us-has-slowed-may-have-leveled-off/
- **Pickleball is still compounding.** US participants were 13.6M (2023), 19.8M (2024) and **24.3M (2025)**, of whom 7.48M are core players (8+ sessions a year) [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickleball
- **Magic: The Gathering is saturating on licensed IP.** Wizards plans **7 standard-legal sets in 2026, its highest ever**, returning to 6 in 2027. Universes Beyond crossovers (Marvel, Final Fantasy, Fallout, Assassin's Creed, Spider-Man) will be legal in all formats [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering
- **Comics are having a strong 2026.** ICv2 (27 Sep 2026) describes Skybound's Energon Universe sales as "impressive, even among the high-flying comic sales of 2026" [F] https://icv2.com/
- **Macro effect [U].** The US end of the de minimis exemption for Chinese parcels (Aug 2025) and tariffs raised landed costs for sealed product, accessories and imported blind-box toys. I did not verify magnitudes.

## 2. Acute pain points

- **Scalping and sellouts.** Pokémon Center products sell out, bots hit online drops, and in-person releases vanish in minutes. Buyers cannot get packs at MSRP [F, Wikipedia TCG page].
- **Fraud in streamed pack breaks.** Documented scams include "resealed packs with valuable cards already taken out," counterfeits, and no shipment at all [F, same source]. This is a trust problem in the fastest-growing channel.
- **Flipper economics are cracking.** ICv2's front page (Sep 2026) headlines "Pokémon TCG Flippers Melt Down on Discord" and wild post-release secondary numbers for the **30th Celebration** set [F] https://icv2.com/. Margins on sealed-product arbitrage look volatile, and flippers are the main customers of a lot of hobby tooling.
- **Grading bottleneck [U].** PSA and Beckett (both Collectors Inc.) have had multi-week to multi-month queues and repeated price and tier changes. I could not verify current turnaround (psacard.com returned 403).
- **Court scarcity and injury (pickleball).** A 2022 SFIA report cited about **$900M** of court investment needed, and the Middle Atlantic had **1 dedicated court per 1,000 participants**. Fracture injuries rose **90x from 2002 to 2022**, mostly ages 60-69 [F, Pickleball page].
- **Faith demand without attendance.** Only **33%** of Americans attend monthly (flat since 2020). Yet **40%** of parents with minor children pursue religious education, and only **26%** of parents attend weekly. **35%** of adults have switched religions [F, Pew]. Many people want religious content, education or community and do not want a weekly pew slot.

## 3. Where the money is flowing, and what is crowded

- **Crowded, well-funded:**
  - Live commerce and collectibles marketplaces: Whatnot ($11.5B), plus eBay live and [U] Fanatics Collect and TikTok Shop.
  - Grading: PSA, Beckett, SGC and CGC. [U] The big graders are consolidating under Collectors Inc.
  - Price-tracking and collection apps for cards.
- **Mature public winner:** Games Workshop reports FY2026 revenue of £626.8M, operating income £275.0M and net income £206.0M, and joined the FTSE 100 on 20 Dec 2024 [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_Workshop. Treat the figures as medium confidence.
- **Faith apps:** Hallow (Catholic prayer app) raised $12M (Apr 2021) and $40M (Nov 2021), reached #1 on the US App Store (Feb 2024), and charges $69.99 a year. An outside commentator estimates about **$51.4M** annual revenue (May 2025) [F, weak source] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallow_(app). It also had a partnership scandal (Russell Brand, cut Apr 2025). Prayer, Bible and meditation apps are crowded in Christianity. **[U]** Muslim, Jewish, Hindu, Buddhist and Orthodox equivalents look far less served.
- **Designer-toy IP:** Pop Mart doubled revenue in 2024 and grew 116.8% then 60.3% in earlier periods. Labubu is a flagship character and the Rmb1,000+ resale market has attracted VC [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Mart. [U] 2025 growth was larger still, with the Labubu craze peaking mid-2025.
- **Vinyl:** one fetched source says US vinyl passed **$1B** with about 46.8M units (up 7.9%). The source conflicts with what I recall from RIAA, so **treat as low confidence** [F, low] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_revival

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps

- **Trust infrastructure for live-stream and raw-card sales [U/inference].** The platform owns the stream but not the physical chain of custody. Neither Whatnot nor eBay can verify pack integrity or card condition before a break without slowing the show. Gap: neutral escrow, authentication and insurance layers.
- **Grading is manual and centralized.** Graders sell scarcity and turnaround, so they cannot easily offer instant, cheap, pre-grading or a transferable "condition passport." Incumbents also have a conflict of interest, since their business needs the price gap between grades.
- **Publishers cannot sell direct at MSRP without hurting local game stores.** So shortages persist. That leaves room for allocation-fairness tools, store-level queues and lotteries, and vaulting.
- **Small and non-Christian congregations [U].** Church software is built for large evangelical churches. Small, aging congregations and mosques, temples and synagogues have thin tooling. Shrinking congregations also produce surplus property (a possible real-estate angle).
- **Seniors in racket sports.** Most pickleball fractures are in ages 60-69, and courts are scarce. Court booking, safety gear, coaching and physio for this cohort look underbuilt.

## 5. Weird signals

1. **$16.5M for a single card in Feb 2026** while a PSA 9 of the same card is $1.4M. The market pays roughly 12x for one grade point at the top [F].
2. **32 billion Pokémon cards were printed in three years**, yet the flipper community is publicly melting down (ICv2, Sep 2026). Oversupply is arriving before the hype has ended [F].
3. **Whatnot's Shopify integration (Mar 2026)** hints that live selling is becoming an omnichannel default rather than a marketplace, and 15 arbitration claims in one month hint at seller-side friction [F].
4. **Pew finds religious decline has leveled off** at about 62% Christian, while 18-24-year-olds are only 46% Christian and 27% pray daily versus 58% or more of older adults [F]. A flat total under a sharp generational split implies churn between traditions, not just exit.
5. **[U] Older-tech revivals:** film photography, cassettes, vinyl and mahjong clubs are led by under-35s. The one film-photography page I fetched had no data, so this remains a lead only.

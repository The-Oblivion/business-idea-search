# F04 Refined: Day-90 Aide Network (pay-on-retention aide recruiting, Florida)

Refined 2 Nov 2026 after two serious critiques. I re-checked the load-bearing facts myself. Evidence labels: VERIFIED = I opened the primary page; SEARCH = search-result summary only; RELAYED = from a critic or the dossier, not re-checked; NOT FOUND = I looked and found nothing.

## 1. Verdict: alive = false

This is dead as specified. I am not shrinking it into a boutique to keep it alive.

Four reasons, each enough alone:

1. **The insider edge is probably not there.** The F04 dossier waved off mhcorp.com as "a clinic group in other states." That was wrong, and it is almost certainly the family firm (section 3).
2. **The "open" pricing slot is more likely a rejected slot.** Every established seller picked flat monthly pricing. A $199/month retention product with a refund guarantee already exists. A $12.5M-funded competitor sits inside the largest home-care vendor network (section 4).
3. **The fee model collides with immigration law right now.** A contingency fee for placing workers is a federal "recruit for a fee" activity, and Haiti TPS work permits ended 27 July 2026. The clean compliance design removes the Creole-community edge the pitch rests on (section 5).
4. **The numbers do not reach "big."** By the dossier's own figures, $10M a year needs about 19% of all Florida aide replacement hires, at 6 to 35% gross margin (section 7).

Even if every gate below passes, the result is a local service business worth perhaps $1 to 2M a year gross. That fails criterion (5) in the brief. Passing the gate is a reason to keep a small experiment, not a reason to flip to alive.

## 2. The strongest honest version (what I would run if forced to)

Placement-only, W-2-only, Miami-Dade and Broward, non-medical and licensed-only agencies:

- **Fee.** $300 when a placed aide works a first paid shift. $600 at day 90, if the agency's payroll export shows the aide still employed above an hours floor. One free replacement.
- **Anti-gaming clauses.** The agency pays at least $17 an hour (HHAeXchange: 84.4% turnover at $15 to $17.99, 53.0% at $18 to $19.99). Terminating or cutting hours without cause before day 90 makes the $600 due. A 12-month non-circumvention clause covers rehiring the introduced aide directly. The agency supplies a payroll export.
- **Compliance posture.** The agency runs I-9, E-Verify and Level 2 screening. The service never asks for a specific document and never claims aides are "screened." There is no bench (a health care services pool trap), no 1099 or independent-contractor referrals (a nurse registry trap), and no check-in calls into client homes (an abuse-reporting trigger).
- **Dropped from the dossier:** the $500 to $900/month "retention concierge." CareKeep sells the software layer for $199 to $399. The human Creole and Spanish layer is the only gap, and Asher cannot staff it.

This is cleaner than the dossier version. It is also a $7K to $16K pilot business (section 7).

## 5. Critic 2: law and liability

- **Immigration (VERIFIED primary text, 8 CFR 274a.1).** "Recruit for a fee" and "refer for a fee" both cover remuneration "on a retainer or contingency basis." "Knowing" includes constructive knowledge inferred from facts and circumstances, including an improperly completed I-9. A start-triggered fee paid to a recruiter who "does not look" is the textbook bad fact pattern. The opposite error, demanding specific documents, is document abuse (RELAYED: DOJ IER settlements with staffing firms in 2024). The only clean design is "agency verifies first, we recruit only from already-verified pools." That removes the Creole-community edge and ties day-90 survival to immigration status. **Cannot be fixed. Conceded.**
- **Licensing (RELAYED, statute text not re-opened).** Nurse registry (s. 400.462(25), AHCA license under s. 400.506) versus health care services pool (s. 400.980) versus neither. Permanent W-2 placement is not squarely in either. No source confirms it. A $500 to $1,000 consult will not settle an open classification question. Level 2 screening belongs to the employing agency, so "screened" cannot be marketed. **Unresolved. Conceded.**
- **Check-in calls (RELAYED).** Fla. Stat. 415.1034 puts the reporting duty on "any person." Calls to aides at days 3, 14, 30 and 60 would surface reportable facts and discoverable records, and a teen LLC cannot price the tail risk. The check-in line is exactly the retention lever the model needs. Dropping the calls removes the legal risk and the survival lever together.
- **Delivery and hold-up (RELAYED).** Asher has no Creole. A paid community recruiter owns the trust and the contact list, can go direct, and is portable. Backdoor hires and day-85 terminations are the standard contingency-recruiting dispute, enforced by small-claims suits over $600. **Conceded.**
- **Platforms.** Meta's special ad category for employment and WhatsApp opt-in rules push volume to churches, radio and word of mouth. That is slow relationship work. **Conceded, least of the problems.**

## 7. Size, honestly

From the dossier's own figures:

- Miami metro: about 28,350 aides, about 21,000 replacement hires a year at 75% turnover, about $19M a year at $900. A 10% share is about $1.9M gross.
- Florida: 90,170 aides, so about 67,600 replacement hires a year, about $50M a year at $750 per slot.
- **$10M a year is about 13,000 retained hires, about 19% of all Florida replacement hires.** The brief's lessons file flags more than 20% share as heroic. The only other route is a multi-state field operation with a community recruiter per metro and language.
- Gross margin is 6% to 59% depending on survival (40% to 65%) and sourcing cost ($250 to $400 per start). $10M of revenue is about $0.6M to $3.5M of gross profit. That is a staffing business, not a venture outcome.
- **Pilot economics.** 10 to 20 starts gross about $7K to $16K at $684 to $826 per slot. After sourcing, a recruiter stipend and legal fees, roughly break-even to about $9K, for 100 or more hours.

## 8. What would flip it, and the cheapest test

All of these would need to hold, and the fourth still does not make it venture-scale:

1. Family ownership of the Florida home-health entities is confirmed, with at least 50 aide hires a year in Spanish or Creole (otherwise the family-pilot leg is gone).
2. A Florida health-care attorney confirms in writing that fee-per-retained-hire W-2 placement is neither a nurse registry nor a pool activity.
3. At least 3 non-family owners sign a non-binding LOI at $900 per retained hire, each hiring at least 10 aides a year, without a family introduction.
4. A Creole and Spanish community recruiter exists on revenue share who already has candidate trust.

### 30-day falsification sprint (cap 4 hours a week, about $550 total)

- **Day 1 (10 minutes).** Ask his mother three questions. (a) Is MHC, or any family entity, the owner of the "MHC Area N, LLC" Florida home-health agencies? (b) If yes, per agency: aide hires in the last 12 months, languages, and Medicare-certified or licensed-only? (c) If no, does the family know any owner-operator of a non-medical or licensed-only agency in Miami-Dade or Broward who would take a 20-minute call? Output: yes/no plus one hires-per-year number.
- **Day 2.** In his own browser, search Sunbiz and the AHCA provider locator for the MHC Area entities and read the officers and registered agents. Output: ownership confirmed or refuted from a primary record.
- **Day 3, gate.** If (a) is no and (c) gives zero names, the family leg is dead. Continue only if the freight-audit calls are already done. Otherwise stop and archive.
- **Days 4 to 7.** One paid 1-hour consult (up to $500) with a Florida health-care regulatory attorney. Three questions: registry or pool classification, an immigration-compliance script, and whether check-in calls trigger reporting. Output: a written answer to each.
- **Days 8 to 10.** Write a one-page term sheet and non-binding LOI (the section 2 terms) and a 25-row target sheet pulled from the AHCA locator, with owner names and phones. No code, no build.
- **Days 11 to 24.** Contact all 25. Complete at least 10 interviews of about 20 minutes, recording for each: aides hired in 12 months, current cost per hire, bounties paid, vendor used, wage band, license type. Ask each for an LOI at $900.
- **Day 28, decision (pre-registered).** At least 3 signed non-family LOIs, each with 10 or more hires a year: go to a 90-day pilot design. One or two LOIs: stop and write it up. Zero, or a median current cost per hire under $450, or most owners pointing to bounties and flat ad packages: dead, archive.

### Quit conditions

- Gate fails on day 3 (no family-owned aide-hiring agency and no warm path).
- Fewer than 3 signed LOIs from at least 10 non-family owners by day 28.
- The attorney says nurse-registry or pool licensing applies, or cannot clear W-2 recruiting for a fee.
- Pilot day-30 survival under 70%, or cost per start over $400 (the dossier's own kill lines).
- CareRecruit, Carework, Zingage or Indeed announces outcome-based pricing.
- Hours spent exceed 5 a week before any LOI exists.

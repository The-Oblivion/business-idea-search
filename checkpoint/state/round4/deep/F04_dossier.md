# F04 Dossier: Day-90 Aide Network (pay-on-retention aide recruiting, Florida)

Researched 2 Nov 2026. Evidence quality is flagged inline. "Inference" means my own reasoning, not a sourced fact.

## 1. Verdict

**Stays WEAK. Fast to first dollar, but it fails the "big" test and the "not crowded" test as written.**

- The exact pricing (pay at start plus day 90) is unoccupied, but I found no seller of it. That is a pricing wrapper, not a moat. Every vendor below could add it in a quarter (inference).
- The tri-county pool is about $19M a year at $900 per retained aide (section 7). A $10M business needs about 13,000 retained hires a year across five or six metros. That is an operations-heavy staffing business, not software.
- The first dollar is real. A family pilot could pay in roughly 8 weeks, and I found no license wall for W-2 recruiting (section 5).
- The fix is not a bigger fee. It is to sell the retention service first, because retention is the variable the fee depends on and the one nobody sells to non-medical agencies in Creole and Spanish (section 9).

**Free or captive substitutes**

- CareerSource boards give employers free recruitment help. Broward's flyer offers [on-the-job-training reimbursement of up to 75% of wages, free job posting and virtual job fairs](https://careersourcebroward.com/assets/uploads/documents/CSBD-Flyers-for-Webpage/Employer-Summary-Of-Services-PDF_rev1.pdf). The flyer is dated 2020. CareerSource South Florida's page says [up to 50%](https://www.careersourcesfl.com/training-incentives). I could not confirm that home health aide roles qualify.
- The employer-owned pipeline is already built. United HomeCare and Sunshine Health run [free 40-hour aide training with immediate placement at United HomeCare](https://www.sunshinehealth.com/newsroom/sunshine-health-and-united-homecare-join-forces-to-addres-home-health-labor-shortage-in-south-florida.html). [Florida Memorial University partners with United HomeCare](https://www.fmu.edu/?p=7620) on the same pathway. Large non-profits do not need a middleman. Schools such as [FVI](https://fvi.edu/home-health-aides/home-health-aide-training) are the independent supply.
- Employee referral bonuses are the default substitute. Miami postings show [HomeWell paying up to $300 after 90 days](https://career.com/job/homewell-care-services/caregiver-hha-cna-english-speaker/j202501212256486565642) and [ComForCare $250 after 250 hours](https://comforcarecareers.careerplug.com/jobs/2186144). The ComForCare link redirected when I opened it, so the posting may be closed.

## 3. What agencies pay today

- Home Care Pulse's 2022 study reported a median cost per caregiver acquired of [$561 via Indeed, $837 via referral programs and $167 via word of mouth](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2022/04/why-the-shifting-trends-in-home-care-recruitment-could-be-problematic/). The 2026 benchmark, as summarized by [HHAeXchange](https://www.hhaexchange.com/blog/reduce-aide-turnover-in-3-steps), puts word of mouth at $563. A $900 retained-aide fee is a premium over the average source.
- Boostpoint's vendor arithmetic puts ad-only cost per hire at [$34.70 to $173.50, or $104.10 at a 1-in-30 screening ratio](https://boostpoint.com/industries-healthcare/caregiver-staffing-agency-cost/).
- Larger skilled agencies pay more. HCS reports [average home care aide sign-on bonuses of $2,304 in 2025](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2025/11/home-health-worker-retention-improves-as-wages-bonuses-increase-in-2025/).
- The value of a retained aide is large. Florida aide services bill at a [median of about $68,640 a year at 44 hours a week](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20250301664457/en/Long-Term-Care-Costs-Increase-in-Florida-On-Par-with-National-Costs/), or about $30 an hour. The [Miami metro median wage is $15.81](https://wagedex.com/jobs/home-health-and-personal-care-aides/metro/miami-fort-lauderdale-west-palm-beach-fl). My estimate is a first-year gross spread of about $16K for a 25-hour-a-week aide, using an assumed 10% payroll burden. That makes $900 about 5% of one aide's first-year gross spread (inference).
- Demand is unmet. [63.3% of providers turned down cases in 2023 because of hiring shortfalls](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2024/08/caregiver-turnover-referral-rejections-continue-to-increase-in-home-care/), and only 12.8% of home care applicants were hired.
- I found no owner forum posts on willingness to pay for pay-on-retention pricing. That is a gap.

## 4. The retention problem, and the gate's number needs a caveat

- Turnover by an agency's top source (Activated Insights 2026, [via HHAeXchange](https://www.hhaexchange.com/blog/reduce-aide-turnover-in-3-steps)): own website 65.8%, employee referral 76.7%, word of mouth 77.1%, social media 107.0%.
- **Caveat on the 107%.** This compares agencies by their top source. It is not a survival curve for a candidate from that source, and it is confounded by pay. [Median turnover is 84.4% at $15 to $17.99 an hour and 53.0% at $18 to $19.99](https://hhaexchange.com/blog/recruiting-and-retaining-caregivers). I could not open the primary report. Social-heavy agencies are probably also low-wage (inference). The number is a warning, not proof.
- The older 2022 study found the reverse ordering: [word of mouth had 23.4% turnover and Indeed 84.5%](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2022/04/why-the-shifting-trends-in-home-care-recruitment-could-be-problematic/). Different years and methods, so the channel evidence is unstable.
- The first-90-days share is poorly sourced. Figures range from [more than 25% to 57% to over 80%](https://www.homecarepulse.com/articles/the-5-most-preventable-causes-of-90-day-caregiver-turnover/), and I could not trace any to a methodology.
- The type of agency matters. Skilled-agency aide turnover in HCS data is [34.17%](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2025/11/home-health-worker-retention-improves-as-wages-bonuses-increase-in-2025/), against [79.2% for home-based care](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2024/08/caregiver-turnover-referral-rejections-continue-to-increase-in-home-care/). The two surveys do not map cleanly onto each other. If MHC is a Medicare-certified skilled agency, its aide-retention pain may be much smaller, and it is a weak proof-of-need pilot (inference). The better targets are non-medical and licensed-only agencies.

## 5. Legal, licensing and platform issues

- **Immigration.** Federal law bars [recruiting or referring for a fee someone known to be unauthorized](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1324a), with a good-faith verification defense. The DOJ civil-rights office pursues staffing firms for document demands (see its [FY2024 letters](https://www.justice.gov/crt/ier-letters-resolutions-fy-2024)). I could not find the specific recruiter-for-a-fee case the gate cited. Rule: the agency runs I-9 and E-Verify, and the network never asks for a particular document. Florida requires [E-Verify at 25 or more employees](https://gunster.com/alerts/fl-bill-1718-mandates-e-verify-for-all-florida-employers-with-25-or-more-employees). The 2026 bill to extend it to all employers [died in the Senate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/197/ByVersion). Budget the $500 to $1,000 lawyer review.
- **Licenses.** Placing W-2 aides needs no state license I could find. A secondary source says [Florida has no state employment-agency license](https://www.harborcompliance.com/florida-employment-agency-peo-talent-license). Chapter 449, which the gate flagged, does not appear in the 2025 chapter list. I did not find the answer in the statutes themselves, so counsel should confirm.
- **Two traps.** Referring independent contractors to private homes is a [nurse registry, which needs an AHCA license](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/400.506). Supplying temporary aides to agencies is a [health care services pool, which needs AHCA registration](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/400.980). That blocks the "Surge Bench" extension.
- **Patient brokering.** Florida makes it a [third-degree felony or worse](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/817.505) to pay anything for patient or business referrals. Referral rewards to churches, radio hosts or aides must be for candidates only, never clients. I did not research the federal Anti-Kickback Statute.
- **Platforms.** Meta requires [employment ads to declare the Special Ad Category and disables age, gender and zip targeting](https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/). WhatsApp requires [opt-in before messaging](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in), so cold group scraping is out.
- **Age.** Asher is 18, so B2B contracts are not a blocker. HIPAA is mostly avoided because recruiting data is HR data (inference).

## 6. First customers and how to reach them

- **MHC is unverified.** I found no public record tying "MHC Healthcare" to Florida home health. The nearest hit, [mhcorp.com](https://mhcorp.com/about), is a clinic group in other states. Ask the family for the legal entity, services (skilled or non-medical) and annual aide hires before building anything.
- **The association.** The Home Care Association of Florida lists [6,056 in-home care providers: 2,305 home health agencies (1,154 licensed-only, 1,151 Medicare/Medicaid-certified) and 1,197 nurse registries](https://homecareassociationoffloridainc.growthzoneapp.com/ap/CloudFile/Download/pjM2N8gr). Its vendor guide lists [HomeCareCon sponsor fees of $5,000 to $15,000, exhibit fees of about $1,150, and $1,000 to $5,000 for the winter Warm Up](https://growthzonesitesprod.azureedge.net/wp-content/uploads/sites/2156/2024/01/HCAF-2024-Marketing-Guide.pdf). Its [events calendar](https://homecareassociationoffloridainc.growthzoneapp.com/events) showed the 2025 Private Care Symposium in October and the 2026 Warm Up in January. I found no 2027 dates.
- **Targets.** Licensed-only agencies and nurse registries are the pain. Examples with live bilingual postings: [Matrix Home Care](https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/bilingual-english-spanish-certified-nursing-assistant-cna-home-health-aide-hha-miami-fl-33125-33145-33133-area-miami-fl--eede42c2-5e2a-4a68-8a38-da387ae808ce) (Spanish preferred, $18 to $21 an hour), [BrightStar North Miami](https://careers.brightstarcare.com/job-detail-179895885-home-health-aide-hha-spanish-speaking-weekend-availably-required/) (Spanish-speaking HHA postings) and The Lakes Home Care (Creole-speaking HHA postings, per the gate report).
- **Supply.** FVI, FMU and workforce boards (above). The [Haiti TPS ruling](https://www.wlrn.org/light/immigration/2026-07-06/politifact-fl-will-the-end-of-temporary-protected-status-for-haitians-mean-a-caregiving-crisis) hit a community of about 158,000 Haitian TPS holders in Florida. Sources conflict on the end date (July 10, July 27 to 30, and an earlier February notice). The supply shrank, but it is also the pool most afraid of outreach.

## 7. Unit economics and size

Model: $300 at start, $600 at day 90, one free replacement (no new start fee, day-90 fee if the replacement survives). All figures are my arithmetic.

| Day-90 survival | Sourcing cost per start | Revenue per slot | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40% | $250 / $400 | $684 | 42% / 6% |
| 50% | $250 / $400 | $750 | 50% / 20% |
| 65% | $250 / $400 | $826 | 59% / 35% |

- Survival is everything. Below 50% with $400 starts, there is no business. The $250 to $400 cost is an assumption: the ads are cheap ($104 to $188 per hire), while screening, a paid Creole-speaking community recruiter, referral rewards and check-in labor are not.
- **Market.** [Florida has about 90,170 aides](https://wagedex.com/jobs/home-health-and-personal-care-aides/florida) (BLS OEWS May 2025), and the Miami metro about 28,350. At 75% turnover that is about 21,000 replacement hires a year, or about $19M at $900. A 10% share is about $1.9M gross, or about $1.5M after an assumed 20% haircut for missed targets and replacements, close to the gate's $1.2M median.
- **Nationally** the pool is large, but $10M means about 13,000 retained hires a year at about $750 of revenue per slot. That needs multi-metro operations and community recruiters in each language. Venture-scale analogs are software: [Incredible Health reached a $1.65B valuation](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/digital-health/incredible-health-reaches-1-65b-valuation/) selling nurse hiring to hospitals, and Carefam raised $14.5M. Neither is a $900-per-aide fee business.

## 8. Why now, and what cuts both ways

- **Tailwinds.** Authorized-worker supply shrank. Honor's CEO says ["we are, right now, today, short care pros"](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/08/staffing-gaps-turned-away-referrals-the-cost-of-ending-tps-for-haitian-home-care-workers/). [Immigrants are 46% of Florida's direct care workforce](https://www.phinational.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/PHI-Policy-Brief-Support-Immigrants-June-24-2026.pdf). Florida's minimum wage [rose to $15 on 30 Sep 2026](https://jaxtoday.org/2026/09/29/floridas-minimum-wage-increases-to-15-on-wednesday/), squeezing agency margins and raising the value of keeping each hire.
- **Headwinds.** AI voice recruiters and funded hiring platforms are commoditizing top-of-funnel screening. The shrinking pool lowers survival in the very channel Asher sells. Medicaid pressure may shrink the agencies' budgets (not researched).

## 9. Restructured offer (without shrinking the market)

1. **Concierge first.** Sell a bilingual new-hire retention line (Creole, Spanish, English check-ins at days 3, 14, 30 and 60 with escalation to the agency) to agencies' own hires. Proposed price: $500 to $900 a month per agency or about $200 per onboarded aide. The price comes from the benchmarks above (Carework from $720/mo, Home Care Marketing from $997/mo, replacement cost cited at [$2,600 per caregiver](https://enginehire.io/caregiver-retention-and-turnover-for-home-care-2026/)). This tests whether anyone pays for retention before taking sourcing risk.
2. **Placement second,** from schools and workforce boards, at $300 and $600 with hour minimums and payroll proof in the contract.
3. **Data third.** Survival curves by channel and agency. Peers will pay for evidence, not for a teenager's pitch.

## 10. 60-day test and kill criteria

- Weeks 1-2: confirm MHC's entity, services and aide hires; lawyer review; draft a two-page pilot agreement.
- Weeks 3-8: a pilot of 10 to 20 starts, tracking survival at days 14, 30 and 90. Take five owner meetings through the grandfather and the association.
- **Kill** if day-30 survival is under 70%, cost per start is over $400, or fewer than three non-family agencies sign a paid pilot within 90 days.

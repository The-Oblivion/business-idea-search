# F03 CareFund: deep dossier (researched late Oct 2026)

**Verdict: WEAK, not dead. Do not build the product yet; run a 3-week demand test first.** The pain is real and the exact combination (home-care intake funnel + non-advice funding map + attorney/advisor fulfilment) is empty. But every piece is free, bundled by franchisors, or being sold top-down by funded companies. The one payer with a rational reason to pay per consult (elder-law attorneys) sits behind Florida Bar rules that force a multi-firm panel and therefore county density. My arithmetic puts the sponsorship-plus-license model's ceiling near $3-5M a year, well short of $10M+. Three gate claims are corrected below: the "why now" reimbursement squeeze, the advisor lead price, and how large Dad's firm appears in public records.

## 1. Who already does it (assume yes; they do)

**Funded, selling top-down (all active in 2026):**
- **CareScout (Genworth).** Nurse-led Care Plans went national in Jan 2026, local care advisors doubled in H1 to 26 states, first LTC policy approved in 35 states, Seniorly acquisition planned ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260120255657/en/), [Coverager](https://coverager.com/genworths-carescout-expands-nationwide-with-care-planning-and-provider-network/)). Gate: providers pay on placements. It owns the carrier channel the "big" path needs.
- **Carefull.** $16.5M Series A, $19.7M total, plus CIBC debt in June 2025 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-financing-to-carefull-to-accelerate-expansion)). Sold to credit unions through [CUNA Strategic Services](https://www.cunastrategicservices.com/content/cuna/css/become-a-provider/all-providers/Carefull/carefull-for-wealth-advisors.html). In June 2026 Edward Jones invested and offered it free to all US clients across 20,000+ advisors ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/25/edward-jones-backs-carefull-with-venture-investment/)). Cetera advisors have had it since Jan 2023 ([Rethinking65](https://rethinking65.com/cetera-advisors-have-access-to-platform-helping-older-clients)).
- **Cariloop.** $20M Series C (2024); firm-level wealth deals with Raymond James (Dec 2025), Quotient Wealth (Apr 1, 2026) and Mariner (Jul 2026, $647B in assets) ([Cariloop](https://cariloop.com/news/cariloop-quotient-partnership), [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/retirement-planning/mariner-adds-caregiving-support-as-advisors-flag-rising-client-need/267293)). Wealth firms are buying caregiver support as an enterprise benefit, which closes the door on "individual advisor pays per consult" for any advisor at those firms.
- **Tendercare.** A free family platform with a vetted "Trusted Network" including a financial-advisor track; "membership is never sold; it's earned" ([advisor page](https://trytendercare.com/financial-advisors)). In Apr 2026 it named Assisting Hands (134 offices) a national homecare partner ([Home Care](https://www.homecaremag.com/news/tendercare-partners-assisting-hands-home-care-vivo-fitness)). Its monetization is not disclosed. This is the closest shape to CareFund and it already has a franchise-distribution deal.
- **Baba** (Feb 2026, $6.5M seed from General Catalyst, human-plus-AI advocacy for older adults) is adjacent; the only source is a weak aggregator ([BriefGlance](https://briefglance.com/articles/baba-exits-stealth-with-65m-to-transform-elder-care-with-ai-and-humans)).

**Free or bundled substitutes (these cap what anyone pays):**
- **Franchise agencies give the claim help away.** Amada brands itself "America's trusted resource for caregiving and long-term care insurance claims advocacy," offers free LTCI claims processing, and runs a preferred-partner network of agents, advisors, carriers and elder-law attorneys that swap referrals ([Amada](https://www.amadaseniorcare.com/partner/)). Home Helpers and CareWorks also offer free claim help ([Home Helpers](https://www.homehelpershomecare.com/bradenton/resources/long-term-care-insurance-assistance/), [CareWorks](https://www.careworkshealthservices.com/long-term-care-insurance/free-ltci-claims-processing/)).
- **The agency-to-attorney referral already flows for free.** A networking site ranks home health care the #1 referral source for elder-law attorneys and financial advisors #3 ([PTBN](https://ptbn.us/where-to-network/attorney-elder-law/); low-quality source, directional only).
- **NCOA BenefitsCheckUp** screens for 2,500+ programs; nearly 9.9M people found $42.7B since 2001 ([Federal Register 2022](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-05-11/pdf/2022-10094.pdf)). The generic funding map is a commodity.
- **CMS GUIDE** funds dementia care navigation for 8 years from July 2024 with up to $2,500 a year of respite per patient and 292 participants ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/guide)).

**Pay-per-lead price anchors:** SmartAsset charges advisors per lead with a monthly budget and no upfront fee ([SmartAsset](https://getleads.smartasset.com/smartadvisor-referral-sign-up/)). Third-party figures are $90-190 per lead (RIABiz 2018, repeated in [Kitces 2021](https://www.kitces.com/blog/the-latest-in-financial-advisortech-july-2021/)) and $100-200 per lead or $2-3k per acquired client ([WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/growth-strategies/top-qualified-lead-sources-for-advisors)). The idea's "$150-300+" is the high end. ElderLawAnswers lists an attorney for **$175 a month** ($2,100 a year), with no lead guarantee ([pricing](https://join.elderlawanswers.com/pricing/)); NAELA dues were listed at about $595 a year (possibly outdated). Caring.com bills home-care partners per lead or per start of care but publishes no price ([Caring.com](https://partners.caring.com/get-leads-caring-com)).

## 2. Payers, budgets and unit economics

**Agencies (the distribution, a weak payer).** Home-care marketing averages about 1.1% of revenue ([Home Care Pulse via HomeCareAtlas](https://homecareatlas.com/blog/home-care-marketing-spend-gap)); home health and long-term care average 1-2% ([Forvis Mazars via NDPAP](https://ndpap.org/resources/for-providers/digital-marketing-benchmarks-for-independent-hospice-and-home-health-agencies)). On HomeCareAtlas's illustrative $100K-a-month agency that is about $1,100 a month in total marketing, so the gate's **$300-900 a month license is 27-82% of the whole budget.** Typical lead cost is $80-150 and one admission $150-400, but those are vendor estimates (single-source Sagapixel; NDPAP labels its channel figures estimates).

The agency's real prize is large: the 2025 Florida median for non-medical caregiving is **$73,216 a year (about $6,100 a month, up 7%)**, with a national median of $35 an hour ([CareScout Florida release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260302523108/en/CareScout-Releases-2025-Cost-of-Care-Data-for-Florida); the monthly figure is my division). One retained or converted private-pay family is worth tens of thousands. So an outcome-priced pilot (pay if conversion rises) is more credible than a flat license. I found **no price comparable for "conversion and retention help"**; every comparable buys new leads, not existing census.

**Elder-law attorneys (the rational per-consult payer).** Secondary sources put Medicaid-planning engagements at $3-15k ([ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-a-medicaid-lawyer-cost/); an attorney-authored page guesses about $10k, [ElderLawAnswers](https://attorney.elderlawanswers.com/how-much-will-medicaid-planning-and-application-cost-12408)). A common law-firm rule is acquisition cost at about 10% of client value, and vendors cite $75 geo-targeted leads and $1-3k workshops converting 20-40% of attendees ([Marketing Lawcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1806835/episodes/15970318-learn-how-to-create-more-effective-ads-that-fit-your-budget); vendor claims). At a $5k fee and 30% close, 10% CAC is roughly **$150 per qualified consult, $300 at $10k.** That is the ceiling, below the idea's $200-350.

**Flat-fee advisors (Dad's roster, the wrong payer).** A 45-minute consult at hourly rates around $257-270 (2020 study) earns about $190-270 ([Wealth Briefing](https://www.wealthbriefing.com/html/article.php?id=189525); flat plans averaged $2,926 in 2026, [ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/amp/2026/05/04/average-financial-planning-retainer-fee-surges-52-in-3-years)). A $200-350 lead fee is 100%+ of revenue. Confirmed dead as a revenue engine.

**Funnel math (my model; the 10% and 15% rates are the gate's untested guesses).** 200 admissions x 10% complete the intake x 15% book = **about 3 consults per agency per year**, roughly $750 of lead value at $250. Agency license revenue, not leads, would have to carry the business, and the agency budget cannot. For a 4-attorney county panel to pay $300 a month each ($14.4k a year) at about $200 per consult, you need about 72 consults, so **about 25 participating agencies in one county.** Only Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Hillsborough, Orange and Pinellas are plausible. That is the chicken-and-egg: agencies must be nearly free to reach density, but then attorneys fund everything, which raises the patient-brokering question below.

**Size.** Agency counts are murky: HomeCareAtlas lists 15,565 and estimates 53,950 ([HomeCareAtlas](https://homecareatlas.com/guide/agencies-by-state)); about 12,000 are Medicare-certified (secondary). The Florida Bar Elder Law Section has about 1,800 members (undated, [Florida Bar](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-news/bars-elder-law-section-serves-an-often-vulnerable-clientele/)); NAELA has 4,200+. My arithmetic: 500 agencies x $3.6k + 10% of 4,200 attorneys x $3.6k is about **$3.3M**. This matches the gate's $0.7M year-5 median at lower adoption. Reaching $10M+ needs a payer class nobody has shown. The LTCI hook is narrow: about 6.9M people are covered and $16.9B was paid in 2024 ([AHIP](https://ahiporg-production.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/202511-AHIP_LTCStateData.pdf)), and carriers already have CareScout and Carefull.

## 3. Where the first customers are

- **Family agency (MHC).** Free 60-day pilot at the admission and discharge desks is the only no-search channel. It produces a favor-sale, not proof.
- **Agencies:** the Home Care Association of Florida (HCAF). HomeCareCon 2026 was the 37th edition in Orlando with 600+ professionals ([Simitree](https://simitreehc.com/events/homecarecon-37th-annual-conference-and-trade-show)); I found **no 2027 date**. HCAF runs monthly webinars (a Dec 15, 2026 Medicare-certified session) and the Home Care Association of America has a Florida chapter ([HCAOA](https://www.hcaoa.org/newsletters/category/florida)). Franchise owners (Amada, Home Helpers, Visiting Angels) already publish "how to pay" content and are the warmest peers.
- **Attorneys:** the Elder Law Section and AFELA, NAELA's Florida chapter. The Section's annual update has historically been in January ([2023 event page](https://www.floridabar.org/event/elder-law-annual-update/)); confirm 2027.
- **Advisors:** FPA, NAPFA and XYPN chapters, not Dad's roster. Cold outreach by an 18-year-old to attorneys or agency owners will be weak; plan on warm introductions only.

## 4. Legal and platform issues (none is a wall; each needs counsel)

1. **Fla. Stat. 817.505 (patient brokering).** It applies to "any person"; penalties run from a third-degree felony and $50k to a first-degree felony and $500k at 20+ patients. There is **no general exemption for flat advertising fees**. The closest fit is (3)(i), payments to an information service that provides free consumer information, does not steer to particular providers, offers no counseling, and charges advance, fair-market fees not based on patient value ([statute](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/817.505)). Design: CareFund is the payee; sponsorships flat; panel rotating; no per-start fees; nothing flows to the agency.
2. **Florida Bar Rule 4-7.22.** CareFund is a "qualifying provider": 4+ firms on the panel, no fee-sharing or percentage fees, annual lawyer list to the Bar ([rule text](https://floridajustice.com/rule/4-7-22/), [Bar guidance](https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etad/qualifying-providers-lawyer-referral-services/)). A 2018 Board proposal would bar lawyers from taking referrals from entities that also receive a benefit for referring to nonlegal professionals ([Bar News](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-news/amendment-would-prohibit-referrals-from-entities-that-also-steer-clients-to-nonlegal-professionals/)). **I could not confirm adoption.** If in force, one platform sending families to both lawyers and advisors is a problem.
3. **Unlicensed practice of law.** The Florida Supreme Court held that nonlawyers drafting Medicaid-planning documents (QITs, personal-service contracts) or advising how to apply Florida law to obtain Medicaid is UPL; preparing the application itself is allowed ([Bar News](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-news/court-oks-medicaid-planning-upl-opinion/), [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/2015/sc14-211.html)). **The part families most want, spend-down strategy, is exactly the line CareFund cannot cross**, so the funding map stays generic and overlaps the free NCOA tool.
4. **SEC Marketing Rule.** Paid referral from advisors makes CareFund a promoter: written agreement, client disclosure, no written-agreement exemption once compensation exceeds $1,000 in 12 months ([Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/sec-marketing-rule-advisers-promoters-solicitors-testimonial-endorsement-referrals/)). Workable (SmartAsset does it) but I found no evidence advisors will pay for a promoter-compliance rail.
5. **HIPAA marketing.** A communication encouraging a third party's product is marketing unless it fits the treatment and care-coordination exceptions, which do not cover lawyers or advisors; "financial remuneration" is direct or indirect payment from the third party whose product is described ([45 CFR 164.501](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/part-164/section-164.501)). My reading: the trigger is money flowing from attorneys or advisors to the agency. A sponsor-subsidized "free" tool may count as indirect; get a healthcare lawyer's view.
6. **VA.** Unaccredited parties cannot charge for VA claim help; the federal GUARD VA Benefits Act has not passed (H.R. 1732, [tracker](https://www.thelobbynews.com/congress/bills/119/house-bill/1732)). Keep VA content informational.

## 5. Why now: what holds up, what does not

- **CMS moratorium: holds, with a clock.** No new Medicare home-health agencies or branches since May 13, 2026, six months (expires Nov 13 unless extended); CMS promised about 60 days' notice and I found none ([HHCN](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/10/the-future-for-hospice-home-health-providers-under-cms-moratoria/), [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2146/2026-05-18-cms-imposes-nationwide-enrollment-freeze-home-health)). It is Medicare-only and blocks de novos and sales; I found no evidence it changes agency spending on private-pay conversion.
- **Reimbursement squeeze: overstated.** The gate cited a cut. The CY2027 proposed rule is a net **+2.4% ($420M)**: a 2.1% update, no new permanent adjustment for the first time since CY2022, and a one-year -3.0% temporary adjustment ([CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-home-health-prospective-payment-system-proposed-rule-fact-sheet-cms-1844-p)). MedPAC urged -7%, so the final rule (due about now) could still change.
- **Demand: holds.** 63M caregivers (AARP, via the Mariner article), and only 43% of caregivers have sought professional financial guidance (Edward Jones/Age Wave, same article). That supports need but not who pays.
- **Capital arriving in 2026 is both validation and crowding.** Edward Jones/Carefull, Cariloop/Mariner, Tendercare/Assisting Hands and CareScout/Seniorly all landed this year.

## 7. Scorecard against the six tests

1. **First paid customer in 60-90 days, under $10k: 6/10.** Likely via family pilot and a few advisors at $99, but counsel pushes cost to about $6-10k and revenue is favors.
2. **Reachable peers within 6 months: 5/10.** Warm intros to 2-3 agencies; no event in the window; no conversion proof.
3. **No license wall: 5/10.** None needed, but four regimes (patient brokering, Bar 4-7.22, UPL, SEC promoter) stack, plus HIPAA.
4. **Uses real edges: 7/10.** AI-speed build, rules database, family agency access. Gen Z distribution is not an edge here.
5. **Path to big: 2/10.** About $0.7M median and $3-5M by my math; $10M+ needs channels held by CareScout, Carefull and Cariloop.
6. **Not crowded: 3/10.** The combination is empty; every component is funded, free or franchisor-bundled.

## 8. Recommendation and kill test

Do not write software. Spend 3 weeks and under $1,000 on a paper test:

1. **Week 1:** verify MHC's licenses and ask Dad what MFA's advisors actually do. Interview 12 agency owners on one question: how many families a year leave or cut hours because of cost, and what they pay for conversion today.
2. **Weeks 2-3:** co-host one "Paying for Care" session at the family agency with one elder-law attorney, using a printed funding checklist and no software. Count families who book a consult.
3. **Kill if** by week 6 fewer than 2 attorneys will sign a written intent to pay at least $250 per qualified consult or $300 a month, **or** fewer than 3 of 12 agency owners rank conversion or retention in their top three problems. The gate's April 2027 test (3 of 10 agencies at $300 a month) is the second gate, after a build.

If it passes, sell the **attorney-sponsored, agency-distributed workshop and consult funnel in dense Florida counties** as a regional service. Treat anything bigger as unproven.

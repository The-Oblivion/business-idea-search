# Round 4 / MFA lens: advisor marketing and communications compliance (as of 2026-10-26)

Eight ideas, ranked best first. Written for Asher (18, ships software fast, 10-15 hrs/week). Insider edge: his father Ron Peremel's MyFinancialAdvice (MFA) and ~24 years of advisor relationships.

## The honest headline

AI marketing review for advisors is NOT an open gap. It is a hot, funded category: Hadrius (YC W23, $27M seed+A on 2026-07-14, led by CRV), Sedric ($22M total), Saifr (Fidelity-born), Archive Intel (AI Marketing Review add-on from $500/mo), FMG Overwatch, Red Oak, Smarsh, Hearsay/Yext. So a "yet another AI post checker" would be the first idea anyone has, and it would lose.

What the incumbents mostly do NOT do:
1. Sell to the long tail that has no compliance department (about 16.4k state RIAs, 84% with 0-2 employees, plus thousands of small SEC RIAs).
2. Sell to the human reviewers (outsourced CCOs and consultants) who actually approve content for most small firms. Schwab's 2025 benchmarking, as cited by SmartAsset, says 83% of advisors outsource compliance activities (second-hand figure).
3. Cover the third-party relationships the SEC keeps flagging (promoters, lead-gen, ratings, influencers).
4. Cover AI usage itself (chatbot prompts, AI-drafted client letters, Reg S-P) rather than the finished post.

Every idea below picks one of those gaps and uses MFA / Ron's network as the first pilot and the distribution path.

## 1. Sweep: the SEC marketing sweep, run on yourself

**One-liner.** A free scanner that crawls an advisor's public footprint (website, microsites, YouTube, public social, review sites), cross-checks the claims against their own Form ADV and IAPD/BrokerCheck record, and returns a ranked punch list for their compliance reviewer; paid tiers add change monitoring and an exam-ready binder.

**Customer.** Owner-operators of small RIAs (1-20 people) with no marketing-review software; the owner is usually the CCO. Second buyer in phase 2: RIA acquirers doing diligence on targets.

**Who pays.** The owner, from the compliance or marketing budget: free scan, then $99-199/mo monitoring and $495-1,500 one-time Exam-Ready Binder (dated snapshots, claim inventory, remediation log). Anchors: FMG $178/mo + setup, Archive Intel $99/mo + $500/mo AI review, outsourced CCO $8k+/yr, small-firm marketing penalties $20k-$430k. Acquirer diligence at $2k-5k per target is my estimate.

**Problem.** The SEC's sweeps were built from public websites. Draft-review tools only see content someone submits; nobody checks the live site against what the firm has filed. Typical hits: "no conflicts" claims versus Form ADV disclosures (the Meridian pattern), unsubstantiated superlatives, undisclosed testimonials and awards, stale third-party ratings, hypothetical performance on public pages, AUM figures that disagree with Form ADV, and "AI-powered" claims.

**Solution.** Crawl, extract claims, map to rule sections, cross-check against Form ADV/IAPD data, rank by exam risk, suggest redlines, send a weekly diff. One-click PDF to the firm's compliance consultant. Publish accuracy on a labeled test set of violating and clean pages (he already did this with his AI-text detector).

**Why now.** Dec 2025 Risk Alert; Jan 2026 FAQs; Sept 2025 proof the Atkins SEC still brings small technical cases; AI-written site copy multiplying; Reg S-P and AI claims drawing exam attention; investors are funding the category (Hadrius), so awareness is high while the long tail is unserved.

**Founder edge.** Fast crawler plus LLM pipeline build; benchmark-first habit; Ron's advisor network and MFA's CCO as the human reviewer; MFA advisors as the first scans.

**First 10 customers.**
1. Week 1: ask Ron to introduce 5 advisors and Kevin Condon; run live 20-minute scans on their sites for free.
2. Weeks 2-6: pull newly registered RIAs from the SEC's public Form ADV bulk data (1,478 SEC registrants in 2025 alone), scan about 300 sites, email each owner a one-page "3 things an examiner would ask about" note. Accurate, polite, opt-out, never impersonating a regulator.
3. Weeks 4-8: 30-day pilots with about 15; convert on the $495 binder or $99/mo monitoring.
4. Short posts breaking down each new enforcement action in plain English for small advisors; ask Ron to get him onto a state IAA or FPA chapter call.
Expect 3-5 paying by day 75, about 10 by day 120.

**Cost to first revenue.** About $2,500 (LLM and crawl about $300, LLC and insurance about $600, attorney review $500-1,500).

**Path to big.** Reachable long tail is about 25k firms with no marketing-review tool; 20% adoption at about $1,500/yr is roughly $7.5M. Add BD reps (639,723 FINRA reps; firm-level deals at $10-30 per rep per year), insurance agencies (FMG alone claims 3,500 P&C agencies), and acquirer diligence. Standalone ceiling about $15-30M ARR. The $100M outcome needs horizontal expansion (any regulated marketing claim) or a strategic sale; comps are Hearsay ($125M + up to $95M), FMG (PE-to-PE), Hadrius ($27M raised). Heroic share is not required for $10M.

**Who does it today.** Hadrius (reviews website changes, social, video; quote-priced; $27M), Sedric (enterprise; $22M), Saifr, Archive Intel (scans website content; AI review add-on), FMG Overwatch (inside FMG), Red Oak (workflow plus review service), Smarsh Web Archive (captures, does not judge), Wealthtender+Hadrius testimonial scanner (testimonials only, free to Wealthtender clients through year-end). I found no free outside-in scan that joins public claims to the firm's own Form ADV. A funded rival could copy it in a sprint, so the moat has to be the labeled violation dataset, the Form ADV join, and distribution through MFA and associations.

**Kill test (14 days).** If fewer than 3 of 10 advisors say the scan found something they would actually fix, or if a consultant says they would never trust a flag from an outsider tool, stop.

## 2. Reviewer Cockpit: marketing-review software sold to the consultants who sign off

**One-liner.** A multi-client review workspace for outsourced CCOs and small compliance firms: intake from clients, AI first-pass redlines using each client's approved disclosure library, a rule checklist, version-hashed approvals and a monthly review log. Same billable hours, 3-5x more clients per reviewer.

**Customer.** Independent compliance consultants and small compliance firms serving 20-200 RIAs each. Secondary seat: the advisors who submit content.

**Who pays.** The consultancy at $200-500/mo per reviewer plus $5-15 per client seat, funded from margin on retainers and hourly work (the consultant's constraint is capacity, not demand). Option: advisors pay $29/mo "submit and track" seats resold by the consultant. Anchors: 83% of advisors outsource compliance activities (Schwab 2025 via SmartAsset); outsourced retainers $8k-$15k/yr and up.

**Problem.** Reviews happen over email and Word. No consistent record of what was reviewed, which disclosures were required, which version was posted. The April 2024 Risk Alert found missing copies of social posts and no support for performance claims. Advisors complain compliance takes days; consultants cannot add clients without adding people.

**Solution.** Intake by email, Slack and a Chrome extension; auto-classify piece type and rule set (206(4)-1 or FINRA 2210); pre-fill redlines from the client's disclosure library; reviewer approves or edits; auto-archive with a hash and an approval stamp; export the exam log. Anonymized cross-client patterns improve suggestions over time.

**Why now.** AI is multiplying the volume of drafts per advisor; RN 26-14 pushes documented, risk-based review; Hadrius and Sedric are aimed at firms and compliance departments, not at the consultant channel.

**Founder edge.** He cannot pass as a compliance expert, so he sells picks and shovels to experts. Ron and Kevin Condon likely know several outsourced CCOs and law firms (the old Gust page lists a law firm and accountant, status unconfirmed).

**First 10 customers.** Ask Ron for 5 introductions to consultants he has used or whose clients he knows. Offer 2 of them a free 30-day custom intake-and-redline portal if each brings 10 clients; charge from month 2. Then NSCP events and LinkedIn. The first discovery question to ask all 5: "What do you review client content in today?"

**Cost to first revenue.** About $4,000.

**Path to big.** About 33k RIA firms (16.4k SEC + about 16.4k state). If 40% are served through consultants and 2,000 consultancies pay $400/mo, that is about $10M; client seats at $15/mo across 13k firms add about $2.3M. Larger prize: the system of record where small-firm compliance work flows (marketing, annual review, Reg S-P, AI governance), with a marketplace take rate of 15-20% on consultant retainers. Honest ceiling about $10-25M; $100M is heroic.

**Who does it today.** Red Oak (software and a review service; the closest incumbent), Smarsh, Saifr's SaifrReview, Hadrius, Sedric, COMPLY, plus consultants' email and spreadsheets. My searches found nothing sold as a white-label, multi-client tool for consultants, but that is absence of evidence from thin searching. Confirm in week 1 by asking the consultants.

**Kill test.** If 5 consultants all say Red Oak or their email is fine, or they will not put client content in a tool built by an 18-year-old, stop. Trust is the risk here, not tech.

## 3. Third-Party Marketing Ledger: promoters, ratings, awards and influencers (MFA as customer zero)

**One-liner.** One system for every outside party an advisor pays or relies on in marketing: written-agreement templates and e-sign, standard disclosure blocks, recurring disqualification screening, a payment ledger that flags the $1,000 threshold, and a diligence file for third-party ratings and awards. Promoters get a verified profile that any advisor can adopt.

**Customer.** RIAs that pay anyone for referrals, testimonials, lead generation, or use "Top Advisor" style awards. Also the promoters themselves: lead-gen sites, directories, CPAs, podcasts, creators.

**Who pays.** Advisors at $49-99/mo; promoters $500-2,500/yr for verification. Adjacent budgets: advisors already pay $90-190 per lead or about $5k/mo for lead gen, and pay rating providers.

**Problem.** The rule requires a written agreement over $1,000 in 12 months (non-cash counts), clear and prominent disclosure, and a screen the adviser "reasonably should know" is still clean, which means ongoing monitoring. The Dec 2025 Risk Alert and Jan 2026 SRO-order FAQ made eligibility more nuanced. GeaSphere was cited for missing agreements with compensated endorsers. Advisers also must keep rating questionnaires and have a reasonable basis for a rating. Today it is Word templates and memory.

**Solution.** Registry plus checker plus generator: promoter profile with disclosure text and e-signed agreement; advisor connects; payments imported by CSV, Stripe or QuickBooks; threshold alerts; monthly re-screen of IAPD, BrokerCheck and SEC orders; ratings file with methodology, date, criteria and disclosures and an expiry reminder; a records binder. API so a lead-gen site can inject the right disclosure automatically.

**Why now.** The Dec 2025 Risk Alert is squarely about this; influencer programs and referral networks are named as exam focus.

**Founder edge.** MFA itself connects consumers with advisors, so by the brief MFA is a promoter and its advisors are the advertisers. It is customer zero and the first registry entry. Ron can introduce peer directories and advisor networks; Asher's Gen Z network supplies creator-promoters.

**First 10 customers.** Day 1: build MFA's own promoter pack (agreement, disclosure, screening log) for free as the case study. Then Ron introduces 3 peer platforms or associations for verification at $1,000 each, and 10 MFA advisors onboard at $49/mo. Revenue by day 60: about $500/mo plus 2 verifications.

**Cost to first revenue.** About $3,000 (attorney review of templates is the real cost, $1.5-3k).

**Path to big.** Stand-alone this is about 10k firms at $600/yr = $6M, a modest business. It gets bigger by (a) becoming the standard that lead-gen platforms embed (they get paid per lead, so they want to be easy to pay), (b) extending to private fund placement agents and the 6,389 exempt reporting advisers with larger budgets, and (c) merging into Idea 1 or 2 as the third-party module. $100M not credible alone; honest standalone ceiling $5-10M.

**Who does it today.** No dedicated registry found. Compliance consultants and law-firm templates, generic CRMs (referral tracking in Salesforce), and lead-gen platforms' own boilerplate (SmartAsset, Zoe, Wealthtender).

**Kill test.** If MFA and 5 advisors cannot name a single promoter they currently pay, the ledger has no payers. Ask first.

## 4. Shadow-AI Ledger: record what advisors ask chatbots and what they send clients

**One-liner.** A browser extension and vault that logs prompts and outputs on approved AI sites into a tamper-evident archive, warns and redacts client identifiers before they are pasted, tags outputs that become client communications, and produces the AI-tool inventory and vendor-diligence file.

**Customer.** Small and mid RIAs (1-50 staff) whose people already use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and the CCO who must answer for it.

**Who pays.** The firm at $29/user/mo plus $99/firm. Anchors: Archive Intel $40/user text capture, $12/user connectors, $99 platform; Zocks-Archive Intel at $12/user.

**Problem.** Consumer chatbots leave no firm-side record. Regulators say firms are responsible for AI-generated communications (FINRA RN 26-14; the 2026 oversight report has a GenAI section). Reg S-P small-entity compliance date (June 3, 2026) has passed and covers incident response, service provider oversight and records. Meeting-assistant notes may be books and records; auto-emailed notes trigger archiving. Staff paste client details into chatbots today.

**Solution.** Extension plus admin console. Daily digest, per-user policy, client-identifier detection and local redaction, "became a client communication" tagging, AI-tool inventory, vendor questionnaire, export to a WORM archive partner or storage with object lock. Optional: sample outbound client emails for AI-sounding text using his detector, as a CCO sampling aid only, never as proof.

**Why now.** Reg S-P exam priority after the deadline; FINRA GenAI emphasis; the books-and-records status of AI output is unresolved and commentary expects stricter guidance, so firms want a defensible default.

**Founder edge.** Extension plus vault is a 2-3 week build for him; he already built an AI-text detector. MFA advisors are beta users. Cross-over: an HIPAA-flavored build (PHI detection) could be piloted at MHC Healthcare, but only after counsel and a BAA, and only if it processes locally.

**First 10 customers.** Beta with 8-10 advisors through Ron, free for 30 days; the hook is "your team pasted N client identifiers into ChatGPT last week." Charge from month 2. Ask the consultants in Idea 2 to recommend it, since the vendor-oversight file is something they must produce for Reg S-P anyway.

**Cost to first revenue.** About $2,000 (store fee, hosting, privacy policy and DPA review). SOC 2 comes later ($10-30k) and will gate bigger firms.

**Path to big.** Vertical wedge into a horizontal problem: about 33k RIAs plus 639k FINRA reps; 5% of reps at $300/yr is about $9.6M; then CPA firms, insurance agencies, mortgage and healthcare practices. Horizontal AI governance is crowded (enterprise DLP and AI-governance vendors, from memory, unverified), so the rule-mapped templates and the advisor channel are the wedge. $100M is a stretch.

**Who does it today.** Archive Intel (chat archiving, Zocks integration, FINRA 2026 AI recordkeeping content), Smarsh and Global Relay (capture integrations), the AI tools' own logs (Zocks, Jump), enterprise DLP tools. I found no advisor-specific browser-capture product, but my search was thin on this.

**Kill test.** If advisors will not install an extension that reads their chats, or if two CCOs say it creates more liability than it removes (you now hold sensitive content), stop. Security trust is the hurdle.

## 5. Creator-Advisor Desk: the compliance layer for advisors who post short video

**One-liner.** A $49-79/mo desk: paste a script or video link, get disclosures and risky claims flagged, required captions or disclaimers burned in, a dated archive (including TikTok, where capture has been a gap) and an approval packet for the firm or consultant.

**Customer.** Independent RIAs and next-gen advisors building audiences, dual registrants at small broker-dealers, advisor-creators.

**Who pays.** The advisor personally or from marketing spend. Anchors: Wealthtender $49-59/mo, FMG $178/mo, lead gen $5k/mo in one cited case.

**Problem.** Public video is generally an advertisement or retail communication; influencer programs are named exam topics; archiving TikTok has been manual (a 2020 article, so verify current capability). Most tools are sold to firms, not to the creator.

**Solution.** Web app and phone share sheet; AI first pass on transcript and caption; disclosure overlay; archive via export, API or screen-record fallback; approval packet that drops into Smarsh, Hearsay or a consultant's queue.

**Why now.** Finfluencer scrutiny; RN 26-14 would treat all social media alike; consumers increasingly discover advisors on social.

**Founder edge.** Gen Z native; he knows what hooks work and can build the content side too. MFA's historical product was social-media training for advisors, so this is the software layer for that training and Ron can co-brand it.

**First 10 customers.** MFA's roster for 10 advisors; Asher DMs 100 advisor-creators with a free clip review; convert at $49. 

**Cost to first revenue.** About $2,000.

**Path to big.** Honest weak point: the number of advisor-creators is thousands, and a broker-dealer rep cannot adopt a tool without the firm's approval. 1% of 639,723 reps at $300/yr is under $2M. To get bigger it must be sold to firms and networks as an approved creator program at $10-40 per rep per month. Ceiling $5-15M.

**Who does it today.** Hearsay, Smarsh, Archive Intel (YouTube archiving), Global Relay, Saifr and Hadrius (video review), PerformLine (finfluencer monitoring for firms), Marky-type schedulers with no archiving. Nothing found built for the individual creator.

## 7. Backtest Audit and Gated Delivery (from his Strategy Lab)

**One-liner.** Upload a model portfolio or strategy; an independent engine reproduces the backtest from raw data, produces net-of-fee results and the required assumptions and risk disclosures, and delivers them behind a gate (qualified viewer plus attestation plus audit log), with the policy-and-procedure text the SEC fined firms for lacking.

**Customer.** RIAs, strategists, TAMP model providers and newsletter publishers who show model or backtested performance.

**Who pays.** The owner or CCO, $149-399/mo or $1,500-3,000 per audited strategy. Penalty anchors: $175k (Atlas), $430k (Pacific Financial), $50k-175k each in the 2023 sweep.

**Problem.** Hypothetical performance includes model, backtested and projected returns. The SEC's position is that it generally cannot go to a mass audience; firms still post it. The 2023 cases alleged no wrong numbers, only missing audience-relevance procedures. Atlas claimed Morningstar verified numbers it did not; its models also did not follow its advertised formulas.

**Solution.** Reproducibility report, net-of-fee computation, disclosure pack, gated page, audit log, and a template policy for the CCO to adopt. It does not make content legal; it produces the evidence the CCO needs.

**Why now.** Continued sweeps; AI-generated "strategies" and "AI-powered" claims (the March 2024 AI-washing cases cost two advisers $400k combined).

**Founder edge.** His Strategy Lab (25 pre-registered strategies, 1927-2025, with holdouts) is exactly the discipline this needs.

**First 10 customers.** Find advisors in Ron's network who show model portfolios; give one a free audit as a case study; approach 5 newsletter and model providers.

**Cost to first revenue.** About $1,000 (price data subscription).

**Path to big.** Weak: about 3,000 firms at $2,400/yr is about $7M and the market may be smaller. It is the idea most at risk of being a boutique. Venture-scale only if repositioned as claims-verification for TAMPs and model marketplaces that must vet hundreds of strategists. Otherwise keep it as a module of Idea 1.

**Who does it today.** Performance systems (Orion, Black Diamond, Morningstar) compute numbers; GIPS verifiers serve large managers; Hadrius and Saifr flag language but do not reproduce numbers. I found no vendor that audits and gates backtests for small RIAs.

**Kill test.** If five advisors who currently post model performance say they would rather delete it than pay, the demand is just avoidance.

## 8. Cleared Claims API: pre-substantiated facts for AI-written content

**One-liner.** A licensed library and API of atomic, cited, dated, rule-tagged facts (tax limits, historical market statistics, fee benchmarks, product facts) so AI drafts cite cleared facts instead of hallucinated numbers; each use writes a substantiation record.

**Customer.** AI writing and meeting tools serving advisors, content platforms, and advisors directly.

**Who pays.** API license $500-5,000/mo from vendors; $29/mo direct. Budget line exists: content libraries (FMG at $178-$1,044/mo).

**Problem.** The Marketing Rule needs a reasonable basis to substantiate material facts on demand; AI invents numbers; reviewers re-verify by hand.

**Solution.** Agents keep about 5,000 atomic facts fresh from primary sources (IRS notices, SSA, Fed, BLS); a credentialed reviewer verifies each; every fact has a citation, as-of date and expiry; the API returns facts plus disclosure snippets and logs usage.

**Why now.** AI-written content volume; Dec 2025 Risk Alert on substantiation files; distribution partners (FMG, Hearsay, note-takers) exist.

**Founder edge.** His multi-agent research pipelines and his own lesson that search snippets have returned fabricated quotes, so verification is built in; Kevin Condon's CFP and PhD as reviewer credibility.

**First 10 customers.** MFA's content as customer zero; 10 advisors at $29/mo; 1-2 small AI-tool vendors via Ron (Hadrius's investors include founders of Altruist, Jump AI and FINNY, so those founders are reachable).

**Cost to first revenue.** About $4,500 (part-time credentialed reviewer).

**Path to big.** If it works: API licenses to thousands of financial AI vendors at $1-5k/mo, $10-30M, and it is a real data asset. Biggest risk: LLMs with search commoditize facts, and incumbents' pre-approved libraries already cover the common ones. Unproven; ranked last for risk, not size.

**Who does it today.** FMG and Hearsay pre-approved content and AI coaches, Advisor Perspectives and Kitces content, Morningstar. I found no atomic-fact API aimed at AI drafts, but could not confirm either way.

**Kill test.** If no AI-tool vendor will pay or even sign an LOI within 45 days, stop.

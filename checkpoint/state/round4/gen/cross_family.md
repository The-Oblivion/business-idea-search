# Round 4 / Lens: combinations across the family businesses (cross_family)

Written 2026-10-26. Founder: Asher, 18. Ideas ranked best first. Every idea combines at least two of: Grandfather's MHC Healthcare (UNVERIFIED type; a search found no Florida home-health company by that name, so confirm the legal entity and service lines with his mother before leaning on any "family agency pilot" claim), Dad's MyFinancialAdvice (advisor network plus compliance tech), Dad's Baygrass festival, and Asher's Gen Z reach and AI build speed.

## What the research grounded (about 28 searches and fetches)

- **Home health is squeezed and frozen.** CMS cut CY2026 Medicare home-health payments about 1.3% in aggregate. The CY2027 proposal is +2.4% but keeps a 3.0% temporary cut. On May 13, 2026 CMS imposed a six-month nationwide moratorium on new Medicare home-health and hospice enrollments, including new branches. It is extendable, and existing agencies keep operating. So growth has to come from existing census and private pay, and certified agencies are scarcer. Roughly 12,234 Medicare-certified agencies (MedPAC, 2024). Florida had 1,948 licensed HHAs in 2017.
- **The caregiver labor bill is large.** Home-care aide turnover is 34% in the HCS survey (1,100 agencies) and 75-79% in vendor blogs. The average sign-on bonus is $2,304. A replacement hire costs $2,600-5,000 (vendor-sourced). For-profit agency aide pay is $18.75/hr. About 80% of turnover happens in the first 90 days (vendor claim).
- **Paying for care is the hole.** 63M family caregivers (AARP/NAC 2025, +45% in a decade). LTC insurance paid $16.9B in 2024 to 6.9M covered people, and Milliman projects a $42B peak by 2041. Agency nonmedical care is about $35/hr. Genworth's CareScout network (1,100+ home care locations, about 2,950 matches in H1 2026) is funded by provider-paid placement fees. Caring.com sells home-care leads per lead or per start. NCOA BenefitsCheckUp is free but covers benefits only.
- **Advisor-side budgets exist.** SmartAsset leads reportedly cost advisors $150-300+ each. Wealthtender is $59-92/mo flat, and XYPN (2,200+ fee-only advisors) partnered with it on 2026-09-10. Cerulli: about 106k advisors retiring over a decade, with McKinsey estimating a 100k advisor shortfall by 2034. Rookie advisor failure rate is 72%, and 64% of new-advisor recruiting is word of mouth. The 2025 Harris poll: 43% of heirs plan to leave the parents' advisor, 42% stay. Leavers cite values and "didn't know the advisor". Under-40 heirs: 82% prefer in-person.
- **Elder fraud has bank budgets but no in-home signal.** The CFPB found non-family caregivers were the perpetrator in 1 of 9 known-perpetrator exploitation cases, with an average loss of $57,800. Carefull ($12.99/mo consumer) is being distributed through Synovus and CSI's bank and credit-union clients. EverSafe is distributed through Sompo. FINRA Rule 4512 requires trusted-contact requests, and Rule 2165's safe harbor requires documented training (Supp. Material .02, verified on finra.org).
- **Marketing Rule trap.** Paid referrals to advisers make the referrer a "promoter" under SEC Rule 206(4)-1: written agreement, specific disclosures, ineligible-person checks. The SEC's December 2025 risk alert targeted exactly this. Wealthtender sidesteps it with flat monthly fees and no per-lead pricing.

## Killed combinations (do not build)

- **Live music for senior living and memory care.** Activity-director budgets are about $350/month, and the free nonprofit Play it for Seniors lists 90,000+ entertainers.
- **Independent-festival software or sponsorship exchange.** Eventeny covers vendors, volunteers, ticketing and sponsor tracking. Small festivals are cost-squeezed (about 40% above 2019 costs; Okeechobee paused, Michelada cancelled), so the payer has no budget.
- **Festival medical or mental-health tents staffed by agency nurses.** Event-by-event quotes, no published price, small total pool, and a weak path to $100M.
- **GUIDE dementia respite network.** Only about 330 participants, traditional Medicare only, $2,500 cap per beneficiary, and CMS says family unfamiliarity is the barrier. The pool is far below $100M.
- **Agency exit-readiness or M&A origination.** Only about 134 certified home-health, hospice and private-duty deals a year. Florida likely requires a license for business brokerage.
- **Compliance-safe AI video or social for advisors.** Hearsay, Smarsh, Theta Lake and Saifr already sit on this. It is Dad's old territory, so there is no wedge.
- **Referral-intelligence CRM for agencies.** Trella already does market intelligence plus CRM for home health.
- **Aging Well workshop series as a standalone.** Roughly $1,750 per event across three co-hosts means about 57k events a year to hit $100M. It survives only as a distribution channel inside idea 1.

## 1. CareFund: the paying-for-care desk at the home-care front door

**One-liner.** A 12-minute family intake that turns "how do we pay for Mom's care?" into a Care Funding Map plus a flat-fee advisor or elder-law consult, handed out by home-care agencies at admission and discharge.

- **Customer / signer.** Owner-operated home-health and private-duty agencies (administrator or owner signs), plus fee-only or flat-fee advisors and elder-law attorneys in the same county (the principal signs). Later: LTC carriers, credit unions, employers.
- **Who pays.** Agencies pay a flat license ($300-900/mo) for the intake tool and funding-map handouts. Advisors pay $299/mo for an exclusive county, or $200-350 per qualified consult. Per-start fees to agencies are the Caring.com model, but see the legal premise below.
- **Problem.** Agency private-pay care at about $35/hr, 40 hr/wk, is roughly $6,000 a month. Families find this out in a crisis. They often do not know a parent holds an LTC policy or how to claim it. They do not know the VA Aid & Attendance or Florida Medicaid waiver rules. They end up cutting hours or never converting from a Medicare episode to private duty. Agencies lose the conversion, and advisors meet the family only after the damage is done.
- **Solution.** A non-advice, software-first funding map for the family:
  - local cost at current rates and months of runway
  - LTC-policy lookup plus claim-start checklist
  - VA, Medicaid waiver, Medicare Advantage extras and state programs screened with a live rules database (his multi-agent research pipeline keeps it current)
  - family cost-sharing
  - a booked 45-minute flat-fee advisor or attorney consult as the paid next step
- **Why now.**
  - CY2026 Medicare cut plus the 3% temporary cut proposed for 2027 push agencies to private pay.
  - The moratorium means agencies cannot grow by adding branches.
  - LTC payouts are climbing ($16.9B toward $42B).
  - CareScout proves carriers and providers will fund navigation.
  - SEC 2026 exam priorities put older investors in the spotlight.
- **Legal premises to clear first.**
  1. Promoter rules (206(4)-1) for any per-consult advisor fee. Default to flat monthly placement fees plus a written agreement and specific disclosures. Dad's firm already lives in this world.
  2. Florida's patient-brokering statute (my recollection is s.817.505; unverified here). Per-start fees paid by an agency may be treated as paying for referrals. Default to a flat subscription and get Florida counsel to bless anything per-start.
  3. VA claim-prep fees are barred unless accredited, so only screen and route.
  4. HIPAA: the family enters their own data via QR or link; the agency receives only "completed, consented." No PHI flows from the agency, so no BAA at the start.
- **Founder edge.**
  - Dad's flat-fee advisor roster and the MyFinancialAdvice compliance toolkit.
  - Agency intake workflows shadowed in the family company.
  - His freight-audit pattern (free or cheap diagnostic first, paid action second).
  - AI-speed build of the intake and rules database.
- **First 10 customers.**
  - 3-5 advisors from Dad's roster at $99 founding rate, then $299.
  - The family agency pilots the QR handout at admission and discharge. It is free for 60 days, then flat fee.
  - 2-3 peer agencies from grandfather's introductions or Home Care Association of Florida events.
  - 2 elder-law attorneys.
  - 2 ALF discharge planners as free referral sources.
  - A county "Money and Care" lunch hosted jointly by the agency and advisor as lead gen (the one place the workshop idea survives).
- **Cost to first revenue.** About $4,000: LLC, E&O and cyber about $1,500, counsel review $2,000, hosting and SMS.
- **Path to big.** Agencies and advisors are the wedge. Then:
  - Carriers: CareScout shows they pay to steer claims to lower-cost, higher-quality care.
  - Credit unions: Carefull and CSI show they will distribute caregiver tools.
  - Employer caregiving benefits: Cariloop and Wellthy are venture-backed at $20-25M rounds, so the pool exists.
  - Bottom-up to $100M: roughly 200k consults a year at $250 plus 100k agency starts at $400 is $90M. That is heroic share, so the realistic 8-year outcome is $20-60M unless one carrier or bank channel lands.
- **Who does it today.**
  - Genworth CareScout (provider matching, nurse assessment, LTC policyholders first)
  - A Place for Mom and Caring.com (lead sale)
  - Wellthy and Cariloop (employer-paid)
  - NCOA BenefitsCheckUp (free, benefits only)
  - Carefull and ElderFi (finance apps)
  - Everplans (document vault for advisors)
  - Nobody found combining a funding map, advisor fulfillment and agency-intake distribution.

## 2. Day-90 Bench: pre-health campus caregiver pipeline with a retention guarantee

**One-liner.** Pay-per-retained-hire recruiting of 18+ pre-health college students into part-time caregiver roles at agencies, free replacement if they quit before day 90.

- **Customer / signer.** Home-health and private-duty agencies (administrator, HR director, owner).
- **Who pays.** $1,000-1,500 per hire that reaches day 90. That is less than the $2,304 average sign-on bonus plus $2,600-5,000 replacement cost (both vendor-sourced).
- **Problem.** Aide turnover is 34% (HCS) to 79% (vendors), and about 80% of it happens in the first 90 days. Interviews no-show about half the time, and same-day interviews roughly double show rate (vendor claim). Agencies spend $2,000+ per head on bonuses alone and still churn.
- **Solution.**
  - Student ambassadors at pre-med, nursing, PA, PT and OT clubs run short-form video recruiting.
  - Same-day interview booking and text-based onboarding through the first 90 days.
  - A verified patient-care-hours letter for school applications.
  - Schedule-aware matching around exams.
  - The agency employs, trains and does competency evaluation. Bench only recruits and retains, so it is neither an employer nor a nurse registry (confirm with Florida counsel).
  - 18+ only.
- **Why now.** Medicare cuts and the moratorium make cost per aide and census-led growth the only levers. The 2025 HCS data show agencies are already paying up on pay and bonuses.
- **Founder edge.** He is the demographic and about to be a freshman. Gen Z distribution is the product. The family agency is a ready first customer. He ships onboarding and texting automation in days.
- **First 10 customers.** The family agency (Riverview/Tampa, Boca Raton, Fort Lauderdale "Area N" branches if confirmed) at $1,000 per day-90 hire. Then 3-4 peer agencies in the same metro through grandfather's introductions. Recruit from UF, USF, UCF, FAU, Nova and FIU pre-health clubs. Then add 5 private-duty franchise owners.
- **Cost.** About $2,500: LLC, video gear, ambassador stipends, ad tests.
- **Path to big.**
  - 12,234 certified HHAs at an assumed 25 aide hires a year is about 300k hires, before non-medical agencies.
  - A 10% share at $1,200 is about $36M from certified HHAs alone, and about $100M with private duty.
  - Then a shift marketplace and retention SaaS.
  - The same campus ambassador network also feeds idea 3.
  - Risks: seasonal student churn, thin margin per placement.
- **Who does it today.**
  - Papa (companionship, health-plan paid, about $1.4B valuation, relaunching direct to consumer)
  - Fountain Frontline OS and Paradox (generic frontline hiring)
  - Indeed
  - Clipboard Health, CareRev and ShiftKey (shift marketplaces)
  - Home Care Pulse content
  - No pre-health campus channel with a day-90 guarantee found.

## 3. Advisor Residency: Gen Z paraplanner and associate pipeline for independent RIAs

**One-liner.** A 10-week cohort that trains finance and econ students (and career changers) as paraplanners and associates, placed with independent RIAs for a fee, plus fractional paraplanning to the same firms.

- **Customer / signer.** Independent RIAs and fee-only planning firms (managing partner).
- **Who pays.** $6-12k placement fee after a 90-day hold, or $1.5-3k a month for fractional paraplanning from residents supervised by a lead planner.
- **Problem.** About 106k advisors retire over ten years, McKinsey sees a 100k shortfall by 2034, and 72% of rookies fail. 64% of new-advisor recruiting is word of mouth. Independent RIAs are invisible on campuses. The only matching tool, Schwab/TD RIAConnect, is for firms it custodies.
- **Solution.** Campus recruiting, a structured planning-software and meeting-prep curriculum, SIE-optional, supervised first clients' prep, and a placement guarantee. Residents do support work, not advice, so no license wall for Asher.
- **Why now.** The retirement wave, the 72% rookie failure rate, and SEC 2026 priorities that raise the supervision burden on thin teams.
- **Founder edge.** Dad's 24 years of peers. XYPN's partner program is a non-family door. The same Gen Z campus engine as idea 2. He is the recruit's peer.
- **First 10 customers.** 3 RIAs in Dad's circle take summer-2027 interns as the pilot. Then XYPN-adjacent firms, FPA chapters and Dad's introductions.
- **Cost.** About $3,000: LLC, E&O and cyber, cohort tooling, early recruiting spend.
- **Path to big.** About 5,000 placements a year at $10k is $50M. Add 2,000 fractional-paraplanning firms at $24k a year for $48M. That is about $100M if it becomes the default pipeline, which is heroic at about 10% of hiring. Risk: incumbents' free tools and recruiters' relationships.
- **Who does it today.**
  - Schwab RIAConnect NextGen
  - Kitces and FPA career centers
  - Specialist recruiters
  - Exam-prep vendors
  - Outsourced paraplanning shops (UK-style hourly)
  - AI notetakers (Jump, Zocks) are adjacent, not this

## 4. Heir Onramp: a next-gen program advisors give clients' adult kids, run by an actual heir

**One-liner.** A white-labeled 6-session cohort plus a play-money family-wealth simulator for 18-35-year-old heirs of an advisor's best clients, capped by an in-person "Heir Day" in festival hospitality.

- **Customer / signer.** Independent RIAs and trust companies with business-owner families (principal or head of client experience).
- **Who pays.** $6-15k per firm per year. Heir Day hospitality is $2.5-5k per advisor and shared with the festival.
- **Problem.** 43% of heirs plan to leave the parents' advisor and 26% do not personally know the advisor. 82% of under-40 heirs prefer in-person. Advisors know this but have no productized fix. Today it is consultants and workshops.
- **Solution.** Educational, non-advice modules on family-business ownership, trusts, taxes in plain English, and "what Dad's advisor actually does". A play-money family-office simulator based on the House Money economy engine he built. Asher hosts as a real third-generation business-family heir. Heir Day is a VIP festival tent that gives the festival hospitality revenue while costs run 40% above 2019.
- **Why now.** The Harris numbers, the SEC 2026 senior and fiduciary focus, and festival cost pressure.
- **Founder edge.** He is the target user, his family's second generation already works in the business, and he has built playable economy apps. Dad's Baygrass access provides the venue; Dad's advisors are the buyers.
- **First 10 customers.** 2-3 RIAs from Dad's network for a paid pilot cohort, with Baygrass as the first Heir Day. Then 5 RIAs from XYPN and FPA events.
- **Cost.** About $2,500: video, simulator polish, hospitality tent.
- **Path to big.** 3,000 firms at $10k is $30M. Enterprise trust-company and custodian licensing could add $10-20M. It is honestly a $20-50M business unless a custodian licenses it.
- **Who does it today.** Legacy Capitals and Cannon Financial workshops, Nitrogen and Hearsay client-engagement tools (general), in-house programs at big wealth managers. No productized heir cohort plus simulator found.

## 5. Open Door: a financial-safety layer for in-home care

**One-liner.** A 5-minute protocol and mobile checklist that lets agencies' in-home staff flag financial-exploitation red flags to a consented trusted contact, with escalation packets for the client's bank, advisor and Adult Protective Services.

- **Customer / signer.** Agencies (owner or DON) for the pilot. Later credit unions, banks and LTC carriers (head of fraud or member experience).
- **Who pays.** Agencies $3-6 per active client a month as a risk and differentiation tool. Institutions $1-4 per enrolled member a month.
- **Problem.** Non-family caregivers are the perpetrator in 1 of 9 known-perpetrator cases, with an average loss of $57,800 (CFPB). Banks see only transactions and cannot see the stack of past-due mail or the new "friend".
- **Solution.** Micro-training, an observation checklist, consent and trusted-contact capture (FINRA 4512-compatible), a bank handoff, and exploitation-reporting workflows. Non-clinical.
- **Why now.** Banks are adopting Carefull and EverSafe, Rule 2165 and 4512 push firms to build workflows, and the moratorium plus fraud task force make "clean agency" signaling valuable.
- **Legal and HIPAA.** HIPAA authorization from the client is required for each flow. Florida mandatory-reporting rules apply to staff. Minimum necessary. A BAA when connected to the agency's records. Counsel needed.
- **Founder edge.** Family agency shadowing, plus Dad's advisor trusted-contact conversations.
- **First 10 customers.** The family agency pilot, 2 peer agencies, 1-2 advisors through Dad, and one local credit union via the advisors.
- **Cost.** About $5,000 for counsel, insurance and tooling. First revenue is slower than ideas 1-4.
- **Path to big.** 3M enrolled members at $4 a month is $144M. That needs credit-union and bank channels with 6-12 month sales cycles. Whether the agency itself pays is unproven.
- **Who does it today.** Carefull (Synovus, CSI), EverSafe (Sompo), AARP BankSafe (free training), Aura-style consumer monitoring. Nobody uses in-home staff as the sensor.

## 6. Cash Desk: contingency recovery for LTC-insurance and private-pay home-care claims

**One-liner.** The freight-invoice-audit playbook applied to home-care billing: compare each LTC-insurance and private-pay claim to the policy schedule, find short-pays, rejections and missed benefit days, and recover them for 15-25%.

- **Customer / signer.** Agencies with private-duty lines (owner or controller). Depends on whether the family agency has one.
- **Who pays.** Contingency of 15-25% of recovered dollars, or a monthly SaaS tier.
- **Problem.** Documentation gaps, ADL proof, elimination-period math and insurer slowness leave money uncollected. Existing tools (AssuriCare LTCfastpay, AxisCare, AlayaCare) mostly submit claims. I did not find a recovery-first service.
- **Solution.** Ingest claims and EOBs, parse the policy schedule, flag underpayments and fixable rejections, and prepare appeal packets. Flat diagnostic first, like his freight offer.
- **Why now.** LTC payouts $16.9B in 2024, rising toward $42B in 2041. Margins at agencies are squeezed.
- **Legal and HIPAA.** BAA required, minimum-necessary claim fields only. Contingency fee rules vary and counsel is needed.
- **Founder edge.** He already has a built audit service with a diagnostic plus contingency structure.
- **First 10 customers.** The family agency if it bills LTC insurance, then peer agencies through grandfather's introductions.
- **Cost.** About $6,000: BAA, counsel, cyber and E&O, secure infrastructure.
- **Path to big.** Assume 45% of LTC benefits go to home and community care (unverified). At 4% recovery fees on $7.6B that is about $300M of fee pool today. The share needed for $100M is heroic, so call it a $15-40M business, and a feeder to idea 1's data.
- **Who does it today.** Claim-submission vendors above, in-house billers, and generic medical-bill advocates. No recovery-first player found, but only a light check.

## 7. Dementia-Ready Advisor: clinician-built senior-client training and trusted-contact workflow

**One-liner.** Scenario-based, AI role-play training written by home-health clinicians that gives broker-dealers and RIAs the documented training Rule 2165's safe harbor requires, plus ready-to-use trusted-contact and escalation templates.

- **Customer / signer.** Broker-dealer and RIA chief compliance officers or heads of training.
- **Who pays.** $50-200 per advisor per year, or $3-10k per firm per year.
- **Problem.** Rule 2165 requires documented training for firms relying on the safe harbor. The SEC watches older-investor recommendations. Existing material is generic.
- **Solution.** Clinician-written dementia and capacity scenarios, a voice role-play practice tool, and quick-reference scripts. It is education, not legal advice.
- **Why now.** SEC 2026 priorities, the FINRA senior investor focus (May 2026 conference), and heightened fraud enforcement.
- **Founder edge.** Access to clinical subject matter experts at the family agency and Dad's compliance network.
- **First 10 customers.** Dad's CCO contacts, 3 RIAs for a $2-5k pilot, then XYPN and FPA webinars.
- **Cost.** About $3,500 for clinician stipends and build.
- **Path to big.** 300k advisors at $150 is $45M if it wins, but free substitutes (AARP BankSafe, FINRA materials) cap pricing. Honestly a $10-30M business and better as a module inside ideas 1 and 4.
- **Who does it today.** FINRA and SIFMA materials, AARP BankSafe (free, 1,500 institutions), the Certified Senior Advisor credential ($395 exam, $180 a year), generic compliance-training vendors.

## 8. Age-in-Place Funding Navigator: from the home-safety evaluation to a funded modification

**One-liner.** When the agency's PT or OT recommends grab bars, ramps or a bathroom change, the family gets a funding stack (VA HISA, Medicaid waiver, Medicare Advantage extras, local programs, advisor-run family budget) and a vetted bid from a remodeler paying a flat membership.

- **Customer / signer.** CAPS-certified remodelers and accessibility contractors (owner). The agency's PT/OT director is the channel.
- **Who pays.** Contractors $300-900 a month flat, not per sale. No success fees from seniors.
- **Problem.** Medicare does not cover modifications. Grants are narrow (VA HISA $6,800 service-connected or $2,000 otherwise; some MA plans give about $500). Families stall. Third-party lead sites charge contractors $150-600 per lead, shared among 3-5 bidders.
- **Solution.** A funding-first checklist, no pressure selling, exclusive qualified projects for a monthly fee, and an ethics code against stairlift-style high-pressure sales.
- **Why now.** 63M caregivers, the Joint Center's $13B aging-in-place renovation market, and Medicare cuts that push more care into the home.
- **Legal and HIPAA.** The family consents and initiates, since PT/OT evaluations are PHI. Florida patient-brokering and kickback rules matter if the agency receives anything for referrals, so the agency gets nothing and the family gets a free tool.
- **Founder edge.** The multi-agent rules database from idea 1, and the family agency's PT/OT access.
- **First 10 customers.** 3 local remodelers from Dad's and grandfather's contractor networks, the family agency's PT/OT team, and 2 senior-center partners.
- **Cost.** About $4,000.
- **Path to big.** 50k projects a year at $400 equivalent revenue is $20M. This is a feature of idea 1 more than a standalone company, and the ethical risk of selling to older adults needs guardrails.
- **Who does it today.** Angi and HomeAdvisor, stairlift and walk-in-tub direct-response sellers, Rebuilding Together and local Area Agencies on Aging (grants and free help). Nobody uses the PT/OT evaluation plus funding-first as a channel.

# G14 Note Forensics (G:insider_personas:3): gate verdict KILL (as pitched)

Checked 26 Oct 2026. About 25 searches and 10 page fetches. Searches that only gave summaries are flagged below.

## Bottom line

The two halves of the product are already sold by funded companies with real customers.
- **Provider side:** Brellium's hospice page claims it detects "boilerplate decline narratives and copy-paste language", across 100% of notes pre-bill. It has a $16.7M Series A, HCHB/Kinnser/Axxess/MatrixCare integrations, a clawback guarantee, and Gentiva, Maxim and Interim logos. Its logos also include the Florida Hospice & Palliative Care Association and the Texas Association for Home Care & Hospice, the two channels this plan meant to use.
- **Payer side:** Codoxo launched "Deepfake Detection" on 11 Mar 2026. It does cloning and duplication detection across patients, partial-AI-generation detection, and behavioral cross-referencing, and says U.S. health plans are deploying it. That is the "cross-vertical authenticity layer for payer SIUs" from the pitch, and it already exists.
- **The part nobody sells** is AI-authorship detection for a provider. Nobody sells it because the provider has no use for it. The agency's own scribe (Enzo Scribe, WellSky Scribe) knows which notes it wrote, and EHR audit logs show it. No rule I found bans scribe-assisted notes if the clinician reviews and signs.

## Does the problem match the pitch?

- The "RCD specialist's secret" is not supported by what I found. Palmetto's lists of top denial reasons are dominated by face-to-face problems, missing records and missing plan of care or certification. One 2021 RCD-specific ranking had F2F in ranks 1 and 2. A Q2 2025 figure from a search summary had no-response at 38%, missing plan of care or certification at 29.5%, and F2F at 15.7%. I could not fetch the Palmetto page to confirm (403). I found no RCD denial reason about cloned visit notes.
- The pitch itself admits there is no OIG or UPIC statement about AI-written home health notes. I found none either. OIG has long called cloning a "documentation vulnerability", meaning claims are recouped for lack of support, not prosecuted as fraud by default. Law-firm commentary on AI cloning exists, but it is commentary.
- The RCD facts are real. It runs in IL, OH, TX, NC, FL and OK, extended five years from 1 Jun 2024. The affirmation threshold is 90% on a 6-month cycle, and only full affirmations count.
- The nationwide HHA and hospice enrollment moratorium is real. It began 13 May 2026 for six months and can be extended in 6-month steps. So it lapses around 13 Nov 2026 unless CMS extends it, and the "licenses are more valuable" argument is time-limited.
- The CY2027 proposed rule (1 Jul 2026) adds retroactive revocation and new revocation grounds, but it is still only a proposal.

- **A family pilot proves little.** A paid $2,500 diagnostic from a related party is not evidence the market will pay.
- **Peers through the grandfather is the real channel.** Florida has about 1,079 HHAs and 55 Medicare-certified hospices (KFF, 2023). Texas has 1,935 HHAs and 988 hospices. The U.S. total is 12,234 HHAs (MedPAC, 2024).
- **The mentor link is weak.** He founded an audio-visual integration company, not a post-acute or PE-healthcare one. The "PE platforms through the mentor" step is unproven.
- **Buyers will not hand charts to a teenager easily.** They face PHI, False Claims Act exposure and the 60-day rule.

## Legal and age

- No licence is needed to sell software or QA to a covered entity, and 18 is not a legal barrier. A HIPAA business associate has no age rule.
- A BAA is needed with every customer, MHC included. Using Claude on charts needs Anthropic's BAA plus a zero-data-retention organization arranged through sales. One third-party source says Claude Code is not covered, which I did not confirm on Anthropic's own page. Running the similarity and stylometry locally avoids this.
- The legal premise holds. Under the 60-day rule, credible information of an overpayment triggers a duty to investigate with reasonable diligence (CMS commentary reads this as up to about 6 months). Overpayments must then be returned within 60 days, with a 6-year lookback. "Coaching, not audit" does not remove that duty if the tool finds unsupported claims. Buyers' counsel will want to retain him for privilege. I found no authority on whether his work product would be privileged; that is a question for a healthcare lawyer.
- The risk of false accusations is real. No study I found measures AI-text detector false positives on short, templated clinical notes. In studies of medical writing, human reviewers misclassified about 12% of human-written articles as AI. A per-clinician "likely machine-authored" list invites HR and defamation problems.

## Money and size

- **Cost to first revenue:** about $4,000, my estimate. That covers an LLC, tech E&O and cyber insurance, a lawyer to draft the BAA and services agreement, and a security risk assessment. This is not verified.
- **Time to first revenue:** about 8 weeks, assuming MHC is a home health agency and the grandfather agrees.
- **$10M a year** needs about 830 agencies at $12k, which is about 7% of all 12,234 Medicare HHAs. That is the segment where Brellium, Enzo and the EHR vendors already sell.
- **Median case:** about 25 to 40 agencies at about $12k, or roughly $0.3 to 0.5M a year by year 3.
- **Upside:** about $4M, roughly 330 agencies, or a small acqui-hire. Getting beyond that means selling to payers, where Codoxo is already in place.

## Sharpened version (a pivot, not a rescue)

**Title:** Chart Provenance: independent documentation-authenticity diligence for post-acute deals and audits.

**One-liner:** A neutral third-party forensic check, sold to people who must rely on an agency's records but do not control its EHR.

**Pitch:** Brellium sells to the agency and Codoxo sells to the payer. Neither sells to the third party in the middle, who needs an opinion from someone other than the agency or the agency's own vendor. With new Medicare enrollments frozen, the only way into home health or hospice is to buy an existing licence. Acquirers, their lenders and their R&W/E&O insurers then need to know whether the target's charts show real, individualized care or cloned and machine-filled documentation. Healthcare counsel defending an ADR or UPIC request needs the same check. He sells a sampled forensic report, 300 to 500 charts, at roughly $5 to 15k per deal. It combines corpus-level clone statistics, his gestalt and compare-drafts method, scribe and EHR provenance logs, and timestamp and mileage reconciliation. Counsel retains him for privilege, and MHC is the lab. The path to something large is a benchmark: a cross-agency integrity baseline that lenders and insurers use as a standard. That is unproven. Before building anything, he should call 10 healthcare M&A advisors and attorneys, and kill the idea if fewer than 3 say they would pay $5k or more.

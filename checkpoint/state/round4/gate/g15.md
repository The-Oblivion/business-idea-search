# G15: Triple-Match, Florida Medicaid home care (G:mhc_revenue:3): gate report, 26 Oct 2026

**Verdict: KILL (as written).** The engine of the freight model, a percentage of recovered dollars, is barred for Medicaid billing help in Florida and federally. What is left is a flat-fee tool in a niche where HHAeXchange (the EVV gatekeeper), GEOH ($30M+ raised) and Claim Health (YC, $4.4M seed) already sell the same thing. Also, the "MHC Area N, LLC" agencies are not what the brief assumed.

## 1. The legal wall (verified in primary text)
- **Fla. Stat. 409.913(10):** "Payments for the services of billing agents or persons participating in the preparation of a Medicaid claim shall not be based on amounts for which they bill nor based on the amount a provider receives from the Medicaid program." An appeal packet is arguably "participating in the preparation of a claim."
- **42 CFR 447.10(f):** a billing agent's pay must be related to processing cost, "not related on a percentage or other basis to the amount that is billed or collected," and "not dependent upon the collection of the payment."
- So the 15-20% of recovered dollars fee is out. The fallback ($8-15 per "resolved" visit) is still risky if "resolved" means paid. Only a flat monthly fee or a fee per packet filed, whatever the outcome, is clean. That is a software subscription, not a recovery business. Get a healthcare attorney's opinion before any pricing.
- Not an age or licence problem: he is 18 and can sign a BAA. HIPAA, a BAA, cyber/E&O cover and an agency's compliance questions are real costs but clearable.

## 2. Someone already does it
- **HHAeXchange** is the EVV aggregator for Florida Medicaid (state FFS EVV free to providers since 1 Oct 2024; Sunshine says its HHAX portal is the channel for authorizations and billing). Its Florida page claims "99.9% first-pass acceptance"; its billing page sells claim scrubs, built-in authorization/eligibility checks and denial dashboards. It owns the data a third party would need, and no public Florida provider API was found (only TX/IL/MI/MN specs; per-agency app keys).
- **GEOH** (Indianapolis): home care software plus RCM services, "$275M" collected for agencies, $30M+ from Council Capital (Sept 2025), revenue "roughly doubled each year for three years." Built around Medicaid EVV denials.
- **Claim Health** (YC X25, $4.4M seed Jan 2026): AI billing for home care, home health and hospice, with denial appeal drafting, payment reconciliation and eligibility. See also G09 for three more funded MA home-health authorization startups.
- Others: Sandata (EVV plus claims), Aegis (YC, appeals engine), AutomationEdge "CareFlo AI" denial bots, RaftLabs custom EVV-to-claim builds, AAFCPAs authorization-extraction case (Boston Senior Home Care, denials $65k to $6k a month, self-reported), traditional billing firms (3-10% of collections, or $3-12 per claim; e.g. ASPR CMS Solutions lists Florida).
- **Free public substitute:** the AHCA claim dispute program (Capitol Bridge). Initial review is $127.59 per dispute plus hourly review fees; thresholds are $500 aggregate for professional services, none for "other providers"; must file within 12 months of the final determination; interest-only claims excluded; Medicaid plans must participate once a provider requests arbitration. Only providers and plans are listed as filers, so a third party filing is not confirmed.

## 3. The recoverable pool is thin
- Florida SMMC is $27.3B in FY25-26 (Dec 2025 estimating conference). SMMC long-term care had about 160k enrollees (Nov 2025), and HCBS was ~70% of Florida LTSS spend in 2023 (NORC). I found no published line for personal-care/attendant/home-health payments, so the next figures are my estimates.
- Agency-facing EVV-covered Medicaid home care in Florida: roughly $2-4B a year. Payer-fault leakage (underpay, wrong rate, wrongful denial, late interest) maybe 0.5-1.5% of that: **$10-45M a year recoverable**. Much of the pain is the agency's own fault (no EVV, edited times, units past authorization). Plans' appeal rules are narrow (Molina honors exceptions only for a temporary provider or a declared emergency; time sheets are not proof), and helping appeal visits with no EVV invites False Claims Act risk.
- Legal fee pool at flat or per-packet pricing: about $1-4M ceiling in Florida; a realistic 10-15% capture is $0.1-0.6M. A multistate flat-fee SaaS (say 500-800 agencies at $1,000-1,500 a month) is the upside: **$6-10M**, against funded rivals. No credible $100M path (the brief itself said it does not see one).
- The cited "20-30% denial rate" is a trade-press claim with no source; Connecticut reported 3% vs 9% before EVV.

## 4. Can he reach and close customers?
- **The family pilot is not established.** The CMS NPI registry (primary source) lists MHC Area 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, LLC as "Home Health" with the other name **Choice Health at Home**, one authorized official (Katrina Lanier, Secretary) and a mailing address in Tyler, TX; MHC Area 7 is "Haven Home Health Care" (Orlando; manager in Michigan). Choice Health at Home is a PE-backed national company (Trive Capital, Coltala) that grows on Medicare Advantage. That does not look like an owner-operated Florida family firm. Either the family's MHC Healthcare is a different company, or it sold. Check Sunbiz and AHCA before relying on anything.
- Even if it is a skilled Medicare/MA home health agency, its SMMC Medicaid book is likely small, so it cannot serve as the pilot for this product (the pitch itself says so).
- Channels exist: Home Care Association of Florida (HomeCareCon, Orlando, July), nurse registries, AHCA HealthFinder listings, plan LTC network lists. But owners hand a teenager PHI and portal logins only after a BAA, references and proof; HHAeXchange terms probably forbid credential sharing.
- Time and cost to first paid revenue: 14+ weeks (BAA, data access, a 90-day scan, then a paid month) and about $6k (attorney hour, BAA template, E&O/cyber, LLC), not $3k.

## 5. Sharpened version (the only thread that survives)
Move the match to where the fee is legal and the payer is the one at fault: a post-payment audit of private-contract home care payers (Medicare Advantage, commercial, VA community care): authorization vs visits delivered vs contract rate vs remit, contingency on underpayments. Medicaid stays a flat-fee add-on. Untested: G09 shows funded startups on MA authorization and denials, and I did not search post-payment rate audits. Gate it in three weeks: (1) confirm what MHC is; (2) get 90 days of remits from 3 agencies and compute payer-fault dollars (stop if under 1.5% of revenue); (3) get the attorney's opinion on contingency for non-Medicaid payers.

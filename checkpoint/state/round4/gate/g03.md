# G03: Heir Bridge (G:mfa_growth:0): gate report, 26 Oct 2026

**Verdict: WEAK.** The pain is real and the first $1,200 pilot is reachable. But a funded incumbent already sells nearly this exact thing to advisors, the price ceiling is far below the pitch, and the median case is well under $1M a year.

## What the search found

### 1. Someone already does it (direct, funded)
- **Goalsetter** (Moneylingo, "Firm Family Plan"). It sells co-branded financial education to advisors for clients' children and beneficiaries.
  - The pitch is to connect beneficiaries with advisors before a transfer.
  - Envestnet partnership (July 2023): advisors reach clients' kids under their own brand.
  - Funding: $15M Series A (2021); $9.6M extension (Mar 2024) led by an Edward Jones affiliate, with MassMutual and Fiserv. Edward Jones Ventures now holds it, and Edward Jones has 20,000+ advisors.
  - Pricing is not published. The consumer app is $3.95-5.95 a month.
  - Gap: it is kid-oriented (kindergarten to college) and game-based. It is not an 18-30 heir path ("what your parents' plan means for you").
- **Everplans Network Amplifier.** Advisor-branded invites to a client's "deputies" (heirs). Included in the paid Pro tier. Older advisor pricing was about $196 a month (undated).
- **EverFi NEXT** (Blackbaud). A private-wealth next-gen education and engagement platform. The PDF I found is from 2015, and I could not confirm whether it is still sold.
- **Select Advisors Institute** sells "heir readiness" programs and bootcamps to RIAs and private banks. **Risen Next Gen** sells heir education directly to inheritors.
- **Adjacent and funded:** Luminary ($22M Series A, 10 Sep 2026; wealth transfer as an ongoing service), Wealth.com ($30M, 2024), Genivity (family engagement), Greenlight for Banks (Northern Trust, Morgan Stanley perks).
- **Analog:** Zogo licenses a co-branded Gen Z money-education app to 200+ banks and credit unions. It uses $5 gift-card rewards to get engagement, and it has not moved into wealth advisors that I could find.
- **Free and in-house substitutes:** Fidelity's young-investor eBook, Cetera's Gen Z playbook, and Hearsay and Snappy Kraken content libraries. Firms also build their own programs (Performance Wealth Next Gen Education, Prepared Heirs since 1999).

### 2. The pain is real, and the idea's stats are if anything conservative
- Cerulli (Sep 2025): only 27% of future beneficiaries plan to keep the benefactor's advisor, and 20% of those who have already inherited do. The idea cites 52% from an older Cerulli study.
- Dynasty (2024): 34% of children stay with the parents' advisor.
- Protective/Greenwald (2025): only 6% plan to use the bequestor's advisor.
- Everplans/Cerulli (2016, older): only 7% of clients' children are established clients of the advisor.
- SIFMA/KPMG (19 Oct 2026): Gen Z trust in AI-only advice fell from 20% to 10%. I confirmed this only through secondary coverage.

### 3. But the buyer's felt need and budget are weak
- Natixis (2024): 81% of advisors say they already include heirs in planning. Advisors think they are covered.
- Schwab: only 14% of advisors rank finding the next generation as a top priority. Older figure: only 16% are in routine contact with clients' kids.
- Price anchors:
  - Yourefolio: $99-179 a month.
  - Everplans: about $196 a month.
  - A client portal: $50-200 a month.
  - A whole core RIA tech stack: $400-800 a month (a compliance consultancy's estimate).
  - **$499 a month for an optional heir tool is 2.5x the nearest analogs.** Realistic: $150-300 a month.
- There is no proof anywhere that a content path changes retention. Without that proof the spend is discretionary.
- Heirs have to open the link. Zogo needed gift cards for engagement, and the pitch itself says heirs "ignore advisor email."

### 4. Legal and age: clearable, not a wall
- At 18 he can contract. A vendor publishing generic education, with no personalized advice, needs no license.
- The SEC Marketing Rule still applies to the advisor. A "15 minutes with [Advisor]" button turns the content into advertising to prospects. Each RIA's CCO must approve the content, and the advisor owns it.
- A flat SaaS fee avoids promoter-compensation problems. Do NOT add per-converted-heir pricing.
- Amended Reg S-P (smaller RIAs since 3 Jun 2026) requires vendor oversight and a breach notice within 72 hours. The SEC withdrew its broader vendor due-diligence proposal in June 2025.
  - Keep the data footprint to heir email and consent, which keeps diligence light.
  - SOC 2 is not a rule, but expect questionnaires.
- Get a lawyer to review disclaimers, the heir-consent flow, and TCPA/CAN-SPAM for texting. Budget $2-3k.

### 5. Reach: the edge is narrower than the pitch
- **Ron's rolodex is probably the wrong segment.** MFA's world is flat-fee, hourly and subscription planners. The idea targets AUM-model RIAs with 55+ clients and $100M-$1B. Flat-fee planners rarely have an "inherited AUM walks out the door" problem.
- A directory lists MyFinancialAdvice, Inc. in Boulder as an SEC-registered adviser with about 2 employees. I could not load myfinancialadvice.com (connection failed). Wayback returned 410. **Ask Ron whether MFA is live and what the "compliance workflow" actually is** before leaning on it.
- **XYPN** (about 2,400 members) is mostly younger planners whose clients are Gen X and millennials, so it is the wrong segment for this pitch.
- **FPA** has about 19-24k members and 80-90 chapters, and FPA of Colorado holds an annual forum. **NAPFA** has about 4,600 members. These are better reach channels than XYPN.
- **Best pilot #1:** the advisors who serve Asher's own family. He is literally the persona, and the case study is authentic. Ask Ron and his grandfather who they use.

### 6. Size
- Verified: 16,544 SEC RIAs (IAA/Comply Snapshot 2026); 67% manage under $1B; the median firm has about 8 staff and about $447M (secondary source).
- The idea's path to big ($19M from 8,000 individual-focused SEC RIAs x 40% adoption x $6k) is not credible. It needs 3,200 firms paying $500 a month against a funded incumbent.
- Realistic:
  - 4-5k relevant SEC RIAs x 5-10% adoption x about $3k = **$0.6-1.5M a year**.
  - Only a channel deal (Envestnet-, Orion-, Altruist- or Schwab-type, or an IBD or aggregator) could reach $5-10M. Goalsetter already has the Envestnet and Edward Jones doors.
- First customers: a 90-day pilot at $1,200 is plausible within 8 weeks if Ron makes the intros.
  - Expect a favor-sale, not market validation.
  - Renewal at $300-500 a month without Ron on the call is the real test.

## Sharpened version (bigger, not smaller)
**Heir Bridge becomes "the next-gen relationship engine" for advisory firms and the platforms that serve them.**
1. **Sell the outcome, not the videos.** The advisor's real bottleneck is getting clients to bring their kids in. The product is a parent-side invite flow plus a heir-consented path, a quarterly Gen Z-hosted live cohort (the human part), and an engagement-to-meeting dashboard. Price flat by tier ($199 / $399 / $799 a month), not per converted heir.
2. **Make the dataset the asset.** Use the first 5 pilots to publish the first benchmark of 18-30 heir activation and meeting-booked rates. That answers the "no proof" problem and becomes the credential for sales to aggregators and platforms.
3. **Go channel by month 6, not firm by firm.** Take the case study to RIA aggregators, TAMPs and trust companies (one signer, hundreds of advisors). Use Marty Schaffel for introductions. Position against Goalsetter as the 18-30 heir path, not kids' education.
4. **Keep it neutral.** Do not hand off declining heirs to flat-fee planners through MFA on the same product. It conflicts with the RIA that is paying.
5. **Pre-commit a kill test.** If fewer than 3 of 5 pilots hit at least 30% heir activation and at least 1 booked meeting per 10 heirs, or none renews at $300 a month or more without Ron, stop.

# G04: Ground Truth Guild (vetted expert bench selling graded evals)

Gate date: 2026-10-26. Key: G:insider_big:1. About 30 searches and fetches. **Verdict: WEAK.**

## Bottom line

The vertical wedge is real and unoccupied: nobody sells an independent, expert-graded benchmark for home-health OASIS AI or for advisor-tech AI. But the idea as written is two things.

1. **A thin vertical eval shop.** It has no wall, but its bottom-up ceiling is about $1-3M a year.
2. **A general expert marketplace.** That is the only route to "big", and it is the exact fight Mercor is already winning.

The pitch's own base case of $5-15M a year is not supported by a bottom-up count. The first-90-days plan is feasible. The $2,500 price and the $1,500 cost are both wrong.

**Confirmed gap:** I found no independent third-party accuracy study of any AI OASIS vendor. Vendor claims are self-audited (Olli 98%+, Enzo about 95%). CMS says the assessing clinician stays responsible for OASIS coding, so error liability sits with the agency.

## Can he reach and close the first customers?

- **Reachable:** yes, cheaply. There are about 7 named home-health AI vendors (Enzo, Olli, Brellium, Apricot, Lime, Healthorbit, SimiTree SARA). There are about 6 named advisor-AI vendors (Jump with an $80M Series B, Zocks with $45M, Zeplyn, AdvisoryAI, Advisor X, Range). Founder DMs cost nothing.
- **Closable at $2,500:** probably 0-2 pilots in 60-90 days. The harder problem is the incentive:
  - Vendors want a marketing badge, not an honest independent score.
  - CHAI's national assurance-lab idea collapsed partly because nobody settled who pays.
  - No source shows any health-AI startup buying third-party evals. Hippocratic and Ada do it in-house.
- **The better payer is the buyer.** Only 1.3% of 160 agency respondents were "very confident" in their agency's OASIS accuracy. Agencies are told to demand vendor evidence, and none exists. KLAS (est. about $42M revenue) shows the precedent: independent ratings funded largely by vendors, with providers as the audience.

## Economics (my own arithmetic, flagged as such)

- **Cost per gold set.** A 100-chart gold set takes about 2.25 expert hours per chart (write, second-code, adjudicate). At $40-80/hr that is $9-18k. LLM pre-grading could cut it to about $5-6k.
- **The $2,500 price does not cover the build.** The first pilots are loss leaders unless the set is reused. Reuse contaminates the "blind" test, so the set needs a hidden split and rotation.
- **Honest cost to first revenue:** about $6k (range $4-12k), not $1,500.
- **Bottom-up ceiling.** About 80 named targets (home-health AI plus advisor AI).
  - At 15-25% buying and $30-50k a year each, the vendor side is $0.4-1.0M a year.
  - Add a buyer-side scorecard for agencies (SimiTree alone says it has reached 16,000+ agencies; assume $1.5k × 5%) and the total is about $1-2M a year.
- **The Mercor path.** $50M gross billings at a 35% take needs about 2,000 part-time experts, expert pay paid weekly before clients pay (float of millions), and a head-on fight with Mercor, Handshake, Surge and micro1.

## Teen blockers

| Blocker | Real? |
|---|---|
| Age / contracts / LLC | No. He is 18. |
| Credibility with vendors | Yes, but fixable. He needs a named credentialed clinical lead (an MHC director of nursing or an HHCS-O auditor). The experts' credentials are the product. |
| HIPAA | Low at the start (synthetic charts, no PHI, no BAA). It becomes real the moment he runs retainer evals on production traces, or an MHC employee writes charts from memory of real patients. |
| Independence conflict | Yes. If MHC is a customer or user of a vendor he grades, "independent" is compromised. See the MHC note below. |
| Worker classification | Not for the first 6 months. It becomes real at scale: Mercor (Texas), Handshake (N.D. Cal., July 2026) and Scale (SF) are all being sued over AI-trainer misclassification. |
| Expert-data security | Real as a trust issue. The Mercor breach (March 2026) led to at least 4-7 class actions, and Meta paused its work. |
| Advisor side-gigs | Minor. FINRA 3270 requires registered reps to give prior written notice to their firm. Flat-fee RIAs face lighter rules. |
| License wall | None. Grading AI outputs is not giving regulated advice. |

## Sharpened version

**Title:** Ground Truth Ledger: the independent accuracy scoreboard for back-office AI in regulated services.

- **Keep the bench, change the payer and the product.**
- **Wedge (days 0-90):** a $5,000 blind diagnostic on a 100-chart synthetic OASIS gold set. It has a hidden test split, three credentialed coders, a reported inter-annotator agreement and an error taxonomy. Sell the first two at $2,500 as design partners, with a testimonial and the right to publish anonymized results.
- **Buyer-side bake-off:** agencies pay $3-10k for a pre-purchase vendor comparison. MHC or a peer agency goes first, if the conflict check clears.
- **The standard (months 6-24):** publish an anonymized industry index. Vendors that score well license a "verified" badge. Sell the gold sets and rubrics as subscriptions, which are software-like margin, not take-rate on hours.
- **Second vertical only with a signed design partner:** advisor-AI marketing-rule and client-communication review, via his father's network.
- **Keep the bench small:** 10-20 credentialed people on per-project contracts through a payroll partner. Do not build a marketplace.

**What would move this to strong:** one agency paying for a bake-off before any vendor pays, and a vendor agreeing to publish a score.

# G:healthcare_ops:0 — MA Rate Truth (gate verdict: WEAK)

Date: 2026-10-26. About 30 searches plus two public-API pulls (NPPES, CMS Care Compare data).

## Verdict
**Weak.** There is a real vertical gap (no post-acute-specific MA remit-vs-contract tool found; no public MA home-health rate data). But three things stop it from being strong:
- The audit leg alone is small.
- Funded horizontal competitors launched this month.
- The family-insider premise (MHC) is unverified and the entity evidence is ambiguous.

## What I found

**Competitors (all hospital/physician-first; none post-acute-first)**
- Tenix Health: $4M seed announced Oct 20, 2026. Contingency-only. Upload contracts plus billing data, no EMR integration. This is the same product and pricing as this idea.
- Anomaly: $17M raise in May 2026, $34M total, 20+ health systems. Expanding into "managed care operations and payer contract strategy", which is the benchmark layer.
- Fuse (YC): LLM contract term extraction, remit reconciliation and auto-appeals.
- Lido: ingests 835s, matches against contract rules (sequestration, MPPR, etc.); demo-led.
- Rivet Health (acquired by Zelis, Jan 2026), RevFind, TriZetto, Revecore, R1, BESLER (MA/Medicare specialty), Domo payer-contract app.
- Contract Logix markets to home health/hospice for contract management (PDGM/LUPA obligation tracking). It does no repricing.
- SimiTree: incumbent for home health MA contracting plus billing. Trella Health sells MA volume/referral data, not rates.
- Turquoise/Serif: hospital and commercial rates only (MA is outside Transparency in Coverage).
- Fee norm for underpayment recovery: 20-35% of recovered dollars. Hospital underpayment is 1-3% of net revenue (HFMA, via vendor page) or 0.5-1% (R1). Both are vendor-reported, and neither is home-health-specific.
- I found NO published case of MA plans underpaying home health agencies against contract at scale. The known MA-home-health pain is low rates, denials, auth friction and late pay, not rate-variance errors.

**Facts that change the idea**
- Hospice is carved out of MA (paid by Original Medicare). The hospice carve-in demo ran 2021-2024. Drop hospice from the customer list. Real MA contract customers are home health, SNF, therapy and DME.
- Dispute windows for contracted MA providers run roughly 60-125 days (plan-specific; Kaiser is 24 months). A 12-month retro sweep is partly time-barred. The durable product is continuous monitoring, i.e. software, not a one-time audit.
- Antitrust: DOJ withdrew the health-care information-exchange safety zones in Feb 2023 and FTC followed in Jul 2023. A peer rate benchmark needs counsel-designed aggregation. MA contracts often have confidentiality terms. Both are Phase 2 legal costs ($10-25k+, my estimate).
- Percentage fees: OIG has long flagged percentage billing-company pay. Florida/Medicaid commentary says some states bar it. This is not a wall for contract-variance work (no coding or claim submission), but have a health lawyer bless the fee structure; a flat or hybrid fee is the fallback.
- Bargaining power is weak. LeadingAge (2022) reported plans unwilling to negotiate in saturated markets, and plans are building captive home health (Humana/CenterWell, UnitedHealth/Amedisys). This weakens the willingness to pay for a rank-versus-peers benchmark.

**Market size (CMS Care Compare pull, Oct 2026)**
- 12,409 certified HHAs nationally and 1,157 in Florida.
- Florida's distribution is very skewed. Of 879 agencies with episode data (median 380 episodes in CMS's window), the 257 with 1,000+ episodes hold 79% of volume, and the 110 with 2,000+ hold 53%.
- Fee math for a 1,000-2,000 episode agency: MA revenue of about $1.5-4M, 1-2% leakage, 20-25% fee gives roughly $3-20k a year. This assumes about $3k per episode and a 12-24 month window, so treat it as rough. Below that size it is a boutique.
- Reachable audit pool is about 100-250 Florida agencies and perhaps 1,500-3,000 nationally. At about $12k average that is $20-35M at 100% share. Realistic share is 5-15%.
- So $10M+ needs the data asset, the 2nd and 3rd verticals (SNF, therapy, DME), or both.

## Founder-fit blockers
- Age: not a legal blocker. He can sign BAAs. HIPAA business-associate duties apply regardless of age (security risk analysis, breach duties, cyber insurance).
- Credibility: real. A CFO handing an 18-year-old 835s is the true wall. Family sponsorship plus agency-run de-identification (minimum necessary) is the mitigation.
- License: none needed for variance analysis. Do not submit claims or code.
- Capital: under $10k to first revenue is feasible (about $6k with lawyer review, cyber policy and a HIPAA risk analysis).

## Sharpened version
Flip the funnel. Free first-plan "MA X-ray" computes realized yield per plan (paid vs the same claim repriced at Medicare PDGM, net of denials, short-pays and auth haircuts) from 835s the agency already owns. That avoids sharing contract documents. Contingency applies only to true contract variances. Yield data from audited remits (not self-reported terms) becomes the peer benchmark and the diligence dataset. Cover SNF and therapy, not hospice.

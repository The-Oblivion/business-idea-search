# G13: Retained-Hire Network (G:mhc_workforce:2): gate report, 26 Oct 2026

**Verdict: WEAK.** Reachable, legal, cheap to start, and a family pilot can pay within about 8 weeks. But the fee is tied to 90-day retention, a variable the recruiter does not control. The plan sources from the channels with the worst retention in the 2026 benchmark. The Haitian TPS ending on 27 Jul 2026 shrinks the Creole-speaking work-authorized pool that the funnel draws on, which is the same shortage the idea cites as its why-now. Bottom-up, the realistic market is $1-2M a year, not $7M.

## 1. Who already does it

No one found prices on retained hires for aides. Everyone found sells the same job (more applicants for agencies) on other terms.

| Player | Model | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| CareRecruit (carerecruit.io) | Done-for-you Meta and Google ads, AI pre-qualification, flat monthly retainer per location. One agency per market. Florida testimonials. | Own page. No guarantee. Spanish and Creole not mentioned. |
| Carework (careworkus.com/learnmore) | AI voice recruiter from $720/mo, Pro about $1,420/mo, AI+Scale from $1,865/mo. Claims 35,000 caregivers hired. | Own pricing page. No guarantee stated. |
| Boostpoint | Meta recruitment-ad software, $750/mo minimum ad budget. | Own data: $3.76 median cost per applicant, about $113 of ads per hire at 1 hire per 30 applicants ($173 at 1 per 50). |
| EngineHire, Home Care Marketing ($1,499-2,999/mo bundles), Truffle (from $49/mo), Hireology, Apploi, Indeed | Software, bundles, job boards. Indeed is a top-two source for 43.3% of agencies. | Vendor pages, Activated Insights via HHAeXchange. |
| Traditional contingency recruiters | Fee on placement with a 3-6 month replacement guarantee (one LA firm: 20% of annual pay, 3 months). | So the retention-guarantee structure is standard in recruiting. It is just not applied at $900 to aides. |
| Staffing agencies | 45-60% markup over aide wages (about $17-22k per worker per year). | Boostpoint cost page (vendor figure). |

The pay-on-retention pricing for aides is genuinely unoccupied, but it is a pricing change, not a moat. CareRecruit or Carework could add it in a quarter.

## 2. Checks on the idea's own premises

- **Retention by source is the central problem.** Activated Insights 2026 (via HHAeXchange): turnover is 65.8% for an agency's own website, 76.7% for employee referrals, 77.1% for word of mouth, and **107.0% for social media**, the worst source. The plan sources from WhatsApp, Facebook groups, churches and radio, which is social media plus word of mouth. The idea assumes about 60% 90-day survival. That is an agency-average number, not this funnel's.
- **Fee parity is shakier than the idea says.** Agencies get applicants for about $4 each and hires for roughly $113-250 of ads, plus staff time. The reported median acquisition cost of a word-of-mouth hire is $563. A $900 fee is a premium over what agencies already pay, justified only by the guarantee.
- **Cash per start is below $900.** At 50% survival, cash per start is $300 + 0.5 x $600 = $510. At 65% it is $690. With one free replacement per slot, delivering a retained aide takes about 2 starts at 50% survival. At roughly $250-400 sourcing cost per start (ads, screening, a community recruiter), gross margin per slot is about 10-45%. Survival is everything.
- **Adverse selection and gaming.** The agencies that buy this have the worst onboarding, schedules or pay. Agencies also control hours, so they can cut shifts so aides quit before day 90. The contract needs hour minimums and payroll proof.
- **Why-now cuts both ways.** USCIS ended Haiti TPS effective 27 Jul 2026 after a 6-3 Supreme Court ruling on 25 Jun. About 158,000 of the roughly 350,000 holders live in Florida. The Florida Health Care Association estimates up to 35,000 Haitian health workers are affected, and 1199SEIU says about half of South Florida's mostly Haitian CNAs are TPS holders. Attorneys disputed whether work permits ended before an injunction was lifted, and E-Verify and SAVE showed 27 Jul. Agencies will bid harder for authorized workers, but the Creole-speaking pool a recruiter reaches in community channels is the pool that lost authorization. Florida requires E-Verify for employers with 25 or more employees (SB 1718), so unauthorized candidates fail at the agency and the start fee is never earned.
- **Legal: the pre-screen step is the risk.** Federal law bars recruiting or referring for a fee someone known to be unauthorized (8 USC 1324a). Florida 448.09 mirrors that. DOJ's Immigrant and Employee Rights Section (IER) has settled a case where a recruiter in a "referral for a fee" process demanded a specific work-authorization document (cited under 8 USC 1324b). I could not load the full agreement, so I do not know the respondent or date. A teen-run "document-level pre-screen" invites that exposure. The fix is to let the agency run I-9 and E-Verify and to tell candidates the requirement upfront.
- **Licenses.** Recruiting for W-2 employees needs no license that I found (employment-agency licensing chapter 449 could not be confirmed, so verify with counsel). Two traps: referring independent contractors to private residences is a nurse registry (AHCA license, s. 400.506). Supplying temporary aides is a health care services pool (AHCA registration, s. 400.980). The "Surge Bench" extension would trigger the pool rule. HIPAA is mostly avoided because recruiting data is HR data, not patient data.
- **MHC is still unverified.** Directories show "MHC Area 6" (Riverview) and "Area 10" (Fort Lauderdale) as Medicare-certified home health, with no ownership shown. Check Sunbiz, AHCA and Medicare enrollment records. Skilled agencies employ fewer aides than non-medical home care, so ask how many aides MHC hires a year.

## 3. Can Asher reach and close the first customers?

- **MHC pilot (family): yes.** At 20 hires and $900 each, that is $18,000 of fees, with the first $300 start fee about 8 weeks out and the first $600 about 13 weeks after the first start. This satisfies the 60-90 day test. It proves the data, not willingness to pay.
- **Peers: plausible but untested.** Owner-operators take a meeting through the grandfather. They will buy on a survival curve, not on a teenager's pitch. Warm targets with live Creole or bilingual postings: Homewatch Care Givers (North Miami), The Lakes Home Care, Home Instead and Right at Home Miami franchisees, ComForCare Miami Lakes (it pays a $250 referral bonus after 250 hours, a useful price anchor), and United HomeCare, which hires HHAs from Florida Memorial University training cohorts.
- **Real blockers:** no Creole fluency (he must hire a paid community recruiter on per-start pay), no track record on survival rates, and trust in a community now afraid of immigration enforcement. Age is not a legal blocker for B2B work. Capital is not a blocker.

## 4. Size

- BLS OEWS May 2025 (via Wagedex): about 90,000 home health and personal care aides employed in Florida, median $16.25/hr. At about 75% turnover that is about 68k replacement hires a year. The idea's "80k" is plausible, and $72M follows.
- The tri-county area is about 27% of the state's population, so about 18-20k hires a year, an $18M pool at $900.
- Realistic share is 8-15% of tri-county in 3 years: 1,500-3,000 retained hires, **$1.4-2.7M** at the full $900 each. Net of free replacements and missed targets, the median case is about **$1.2M**.
- A $10M+ business needs about 11-13k retained hires a year, which is roughly 0.4% of US aide hires (about 3.3M a year), across 5-6 metros and language communities. That is an operations-heavy staffing business at thin margin, not software.

## 5. Sharpened version (does not shrink the idea)

**Day-90 Aide Network.** Florida home-care agencies pay $900 per aide only if the aide is still working at day 90. We source credentialed candidates mainly from HHA and CNA training programs, adult-ed and workforce boards, with community channels as the top of the funnel. We run a Creole, Spanish and English check-in line during the first 90 days. We own the candidate record.

1. **Source from the best-retention channels.** Add training schools (FVI, FMU-type programs) and workforce boards (for example CareerSource South Florida). They produce credentialed candidates and want placement statistics. Use social and WhatsApp for reach, not as the only source.
2. **Control the variable you are paid on.** The concierge (check-ins at day 3, 14, 30 and 60, with escalation to the agency) is the product. The contract requires a minimum offered weekly hours (for example 20), payroll proof, and no fee dispute if the agency cuts hours or fires without cause.
3. **Price-test in the pilot.** Run $300/$600 against a higher credentialed-bilingual tier ($1,200-1,500) with MHC and two peers, and record survival by channel.
4. **No document screening.** The agency runs I-9 and E-Verify. Candidates are told the requirement upfront. Spend $500-1,000 on an employment and immigration lawyer before the first ad goes live.
5. **Pilot as a measurement tool.** The survival curve by channel and by agency is what peers will buy and what the scaling asset is. The opted-in candidate base feeds Day-One Desk. Surge Bench waits until the AHCA registration is filed.

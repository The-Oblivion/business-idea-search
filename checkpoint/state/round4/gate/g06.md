# G06 gate: Proving Ground (home-health AI bake-off)

**Verdict: WEAK.** Nobody has published a measured, vendor-named benchmark. But the buyers who would pay are not reachable at the claimed price, the vendors who would pay are conflicted, and the honest size is about $1M (ceiling about $6M). That falls short of the $10M+ bar unless the idea is rebuilt, which the sharpened version below does.

## What the search found (about 25 searches and fetches, 26 Oct 2026)

**No direct measured competitor, but there are substitutes that cover most of what operators would pay for.**
- KLAS ranks home-health EHRs by customer satisfaction, and its research is shared at no charge with verified provider organizations. The pitch's "KLAS-style, 3,000 subscribers x $2,000" is wrong about KLAS's own model. Providers get it free, and the money comes from the vendor side.
- CHAP, the CMS-approved accreditor agencies already trust, runs a "CHAP Verified" seal for software. Olli, Brellium and Axxess hold it. It is a product review, not measured accuracy, but CHAP could extend it.
- Olli publishes its own quarterly Accuracy Report (98%+, "internal audits"). Vendors already self-benchmark, and nobody checks them.
- Free buying aids already exist: the QAPIplus "AI vendor evaluation checklist" and webinar, the Health AI Partnership vendor-disclosure framework, and a SimiTree blog. Affiliate-style rankings also exist (Clinical AI Report, With Andy, Lime Health). They are feature rankings with no testing method disclosed.
- Vals AI (reported $40M Series A, a16z) already benchmarks healthcare coding and scribing with certified coders (MedCode, MedScribe). It tests foundation models, not home-health vendor products, so the move into home-health products is short.
- CHAI's plan for vendor-paid "assurance labs" was scrapped, and who pays beyond the vendor was never settled.
- Large buyers run their own bake-offs. Cleveland Clinic tested five AI scribes itself.

**The vendors exist and have money.** Enzo raised $20M in May 2026 ($26M total) and Olli $10M in Nov 2025. Brellium raised $16.7M. Others are SimiTree SARA, Mosai, Lime, IO Health, Basalt and Apricot. That is roughly 8-12 vendors at $5-15k each, or about $50-150k a year, only if they win.

## Problems with the premise
1. **The "why now" is overstated.** The proposed CY2027 rule moves the HH QRP measure-data deadline from 4.5 months to about 45 days after quarter end. That is not the 30-day OASIS assessment submission rule, it is still a proposal, and comments closed 31 Aug.
2. **Access and ToS.** SARA is not sold standalone, only through SimiTree services. Olli and SARA are human-in-the-loop, so you would be testing their coders. Enzo is a full EHR. Many SaaS terms ban benchmarking or publishing results, and the legal risk (contract, trade libel, Lanham Act) lands on a founder with no insurance.
3. **Payer conflict.** Operators have free alternatives and make the selection decision once. Vendors will pay only if they rank well, and that pay-for-rank problem is exactly what the pitch forbids. SimiTree, the biggest consultancy named as a customer, is also a tested vendor.
4. **Cost.** The $2,500 estimate is too low. Double-coding 100 charts takes about 130 coder-hours (about $5k), plus nurse realism review, legal and media insurance. A realistic free teaser costs $7-10k.
5. **MHC is unverified.** I found "MHC Area N, LLC" Florida home-health entities, but no source tying them to the family, and the Boca entity lists a Texas mailing address. The warm-intro channel falls apart if MHC is not a Medicare-certified home-health agency, so ask the grandfather first.

## Reachability, teen blockers, numbers
- **Age:** not a legal blocker at 18. The credibility gap is real, and it is solved by a transparent method, hired credentialed coders (HCS-O/COS-C) and a named advisory panel.
- **License:** none is needed to grade or publish.
- **HIPAA:** avoided with synthetic charts. Any real MHC chart needs a BAA and de-identification.
- **First revenue:** about 14 weeks and about $7k. That is outside the 60-90 day target, unless charter memberships are pre-sold off the family network.
- **Median case (year 3):** about $0.8M. That is 150-400 operators at $1,500, 5-10 PE or consultancy reports, and 6-12 vendors at $8k.
- **Upside:** about $6M, which needs the post-acute standard across home health, hospice and SNF.

## Sharpened: "Post-Acute AI Proving Ground"
Run a locked, versioned test of chart-coding AI (OASIS first, then SNF MDS and hospice). Start with a public, vendor-free baseline of plain frontier LLMs, then let vendors opt in to beat it. Operators get the scorecard free, as KLAS does, and contribute pilot charts. Revenue comes from vendor testing and regression diagnostics, with a firewall so payment never changes rank, plus PE and payer custom bake-offs and a gold-label dataset. Gate: three vendors and five operators commit within 3 weeks, or kill it.

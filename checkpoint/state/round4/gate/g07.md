# G07: Founder Transfer Kit (G:insider_big:5): gate report, 26 Oct 2026

**Verdict: WEAK.** The pain is real and the first free pilot is reachable. But the generic kit (AI interview, SOPs, owner-dependence score, data room) is already sold by at least five live products, two of them through the same advisor channel the idea plans to use. The one open gap (certified home health) is a niche of roughly 100 deals a year. Ron's network is probably the wrong segment. A kit is bought once per owner lifetime.

## 2. Is the why-now real?

- The "+41%" is Braff Group's annualized first-half 2026 count for certified home health: 48 deals, up from a four-year low. LevinPro's count is flat (42 vs 43 announced through 11 May). The two are not comparable, and the 41% is thinner than the pitch implies.
- The **home health + hospice deal count is about 92 a year** (48 + 44, annualized). Even if three times as many owners prepare as sell, that is about 300 prospects a year.
- CMS imposed 6-month nationwide Medicare enrollment moratoria on home health and hospice on 13 May 2026. Many expect an extension in November. It does not block ordinary changes of ownership, but the 36-month rule turns a young agency's sale into a new enrollment, which the moratorium blocks. If MHC's "Area N" LLCs are recent, a sale may be blocked. Check each entity's enrollment date.
- AGG reports that LOIs closing fell from about 80% to about 50% because of diligence. That is the real pain, and it belongs to the **sell-side advisor and lawyer**, not only the owner.
- Owner-dependence discounts are real: 2.9x vs 3.9x (Value Builder) and a case study of 2.5x vs 3.3x SDE for 68% hospital-referral concentration (broker blog; the case is illustrative). Home health multiples are reportedly 7-9.5x EBITDA for skilled agencies. One turn on $1M EBITDA is worth $1M, so a healthcare owner could pay far more than $2,500.

## 3. Can Asher reach and close the first customers?

- **Grandfather (MHC):** easy to reach, but free, so it proves nothing about willingness to pay. I could not verify what MHC is. Directory listings show "MHC Area 6 LLC" (Riverview) and "Area 10" (Fort Lauderdale) as Medicare-certified home health, but no ownership. The name also collides with an Arizona health center nonprofit and Medical House Calls. Confirm through Sunbiz, AHCA and PECOS before building anything on it. Also confirm that he is actually planning a transition. If not, the "case study" is a pre-sale documentation exercise, which is weaker.
- **Ron's advisor network is probably the wrong segment** (same finding as G03). MyFinancialAdvice is a flat-fee advisor matching platform (a directory lists it in Boulder, 1-10 staff). Flat-fee planners mostly serve individuals, not owners 55-75 with a business to sell. Ask Ron for five advisors with owner-clients 55+, not for "his network".
- **Mom and grandfather give the better warm channel**: other home-health owners, their sell-side advisors, attorneys and CPAs, state and national home-care associations (NAHC, Florida home-care association: verify the name).
- **Three CPAs:** start with the family's own. CPAs who put a teen's tool under their own brand carry the reputational and E&O risk, so expect "interesting, come back with five case studies".
- **Mentor (AVI-SPL founder):** a second vertical through founder-owned AV integrators, which are consolidating. Untested.
- Gen Z distribution is not an edge here: buyers are 55-75 owners and their advisors.

## 4. Price and willingness to pay

- $2,500-7,500 per kit sits inside the consultant range ($3,500-8,000 assessment) but far above software ($50-400/mo). Owners compare it with IQExit (free) and Value Builder (a questionnaire most CPAs already run).
- The advisor wholesale price of $1,500 only works if the CPA resells at $2,500+. The evidence that a CPA will do that for a teen vendor is nil.
- Healthcare sellers can be priced on EBITDA ($7,500-15,000). Generic main-street owners cannot.
- No proof anywhere that a binder moves the sale price. Documentation is not independence: the owner still has to hand off relationships. Rowan uses human Guides for this reason.

## 5. Legal and age (clearable, with two real fences)

- At 18 he can form an LLC and contract. Selling software and fixed-fee documentation needs no license.
- **Fence 1 (real): brokering.** Florida treats selling a business for another as real estate and requires a real estate license (law firm summary; I did not confirm the statute number). The 2023 federal M&A-broker exemption does not preempt state law. So the idea's "buyer matching and financing referrals" step toward $50M is license-walled. Keep it to software and fixed fees, partner with licensed brokers, and never take success fees or referral splits.
- **Fence 2 (moderate): valuation and legal advice.** Do not label anything a valuation. Have a healthcare attorney review the "regulatory calendar" templates and disclaimers ($2-5k, not in the $800 budget).
- **HIPAA:** design the kit to contain zero PHI. Interviews about process, referral relationships and staffing are not PHI, but referral and billing data can be. If Asher touches it, sign a BAA with the family company, use minimum-necessary data, and use an LLM vendor with a BAA or zero-retention terms. Owner interviews also hold confidential employee and sale-intent data. Florida is an all-party consent state for recording, so get written consent.
- Family-company conflict: get the grandfather's written OK and confirm Mom's employment terms do not forbid it.

## 6. Size (arithmetic mine, sources noted)

- McKinsey (via IQExit): about 6M firms face a transition by 2035, and about 1M are expected to be sold. That is roughly 660k transitions a year but only about 110k sales a year, and many are micro-businesses and closures.
- The idea's $18M = 600k x 1% x $3,000 uses the 6M figure. Using sales instead gives 110k x 1% x $3,000 = **$3.3M**. $10M needs about 3% of all sales against Rowan, IQExit, Value Builder, Maus and Sell Ready.
- Home-health-only ceiling: about 300 prospects x $10k x 15% share = **$0.45M a year**.
- Year-3 median: 100-150 kits at $2,000-3,000 blended = **about $0.4M**. Upside: a vertical-by-vertical regulated-care file with an annual subscription and a buyer-paid second line = **about $10M**.
- Year-1 channel math: 5 advisors x 1-3 kits x $1,500 = $7-22k, plus a few direct kits.

## 7. Sharpened version (does not shrink)

**Sale-Ready File: the regulator-aware diligence file for owner-run care businesses.** Do not fight Rowan and IQExit on the generic kit. Take the part that is a compliance audit.

1. **Vertical, not niche.** Start with Medicare-certified home health and hospice. Then dental, vet, behavioral health, therapy and similar regulated practices that roll-ups buy, and founder-owned AV integrators through the mentor. Each vertical brings its own calendar: enrollment, license, accreditation survey, CHOW and 36-month status.
2. **Recurring, not one-shot.** Annual "stay sale-ready" at $1,200-2,400 (refresh, re-score, calendar) so revenue does not stop after one kit.
3. **Payer = whoever loses when the deal dies.** Sell-side M&A advisors and healthcare transaction lawyers (wholesale $1,500-2,500 or owner-funded at engagement). Test a second payer: search-fund and roll-up buyers (about 850 search funds; median purchase price $16M) buying a standard 90-day post-LOI knowledge handoff.
4. **EBITDA-based pricing** for healthcare: $7,500-15,000, and $2,500-5,000 for the base kit.
5. **No brokering, valuation or buyer matching.** License partners only.

## 8. 60-90 day test (kill gates)

1. Week 1: verify what MHC is and who owns it (Sunbiz, AHCA, PECOS). Ask Ron for five advisors who serve owners 55+.
2. Weeks 2-5: build the kit on MHC. Insist on a paid token invoice ($1,500) to prove the billing path.
3. Weeks 3-8: 10 conversations with home-health sell-side advisors, attorneys and CPAs through family introductions. **Pass: 2 agree to a paid pilot on a live seller.**
4. Week 10 kill gate: no paid non-family pilot means kill it.

Cost to first revenue: about $1,500 (LLC, LLM and voice costs, attorney review of the engagement and confidentiality terms), not $800. Weeks to first non-family revenue: about 10.

# G12: Referral Ledger (kickback-safe marketing supervision for referral-driven agencies)

Key G:mhc_coo:4. Gate run 2026-10-26, about 40 searches. **Verdict: WEAK, leaning kill as pitched.** It survives only as a cheap 60-day test, and only if widened (see "Sharpened").

## Deciding reason
The gap is real, but it looks empty because the market is small and already served by substitutes. Findings:
- The reachable pool is about 19k Medicare-certified agencies.
- Whole compliance suites sell for $99-$519 a month, so a single-function tool at $400-$900 a month is priced above them.
- The thresholds are set by each client's counsel, not by the software.
- The money in the enforcement cases is in medical-director contracts, not meals.

Median case is under $1M a year and the upside is about $8M, which is below the $10M bar. Under the round 2 rule ("can't be both defensible and big, call it dead"), the standalone gifts-and-meals ledger fails. The widened version has a chance but is unproven.

## What the searches found

### Competitors and substitutes (assume someone does it: they do, just not under this label)
No product built specifically for gifts, meals and free in-kind support to referral sources at home-health or hospice agencies turned up in about 10 searches. Everything adjacent exists:
- **Hospital-side non-monetary-compensation trackers.**
  - MedPro Systems sells Stark non-monetary compensation tracking with a SAP Concur link (https://www.medprosystems.com/?p=2821).
  - symplr Contract lists "record gifts or other non-monetary compensation" (https://www.symplr.com/hubfs/CSM%20Product%20Brochures%202023/symplr%20Contract%20Overview%20(1).pdf).
  - RLDatix markets gift tracking inside its contract-lifecycle management product (vendor blog; the page redirected and I did not read it, so treat as unverified).
  - Hospitals such as Vanderbilt run in-house trackers.
- **General compliance (GRC) gift and hospitality registers.** SAI360 (https://sai360.com/solutions/compliance-management/gifts-and-hospitality), Ethico (https://ethico.com/disclosures/gift-travel-entertainment), Archer (https://community.archerirm.com/hc/en-us/articles/50661726474003) and ConvergePoint. They offer threshold checks, approval routing and exception flags, which is roughly the pitched feature set, but they are generic and priced for enterprises.
- **Expense tools with a policy engine.** Ramp's Policy Agent checks receipts, meals and memos against a written policy (https://support.ramp.com/policy-agent-full-expense-checks). Concur and Expensify support custom fields. Adding a "referral-source ID" field plus a flag rule covers part of the idea for the cost of a card program the agency already pays for.
- **Referral CRMs.** Trella/PlayMaker serves 2,300+ post-acute organizations (https://www.trellahealth.com/solutions/customer-relationship-management-crm/), and Axxess, WellSky and MatrixCare have referral modules. I found no gift or expense logging in any of them. This is the "ships it as a feature" kill risk. They hold the buyer relationship and the marketer's daily workflow.
- **Compliance suites.** Compliancy Group, which acquired Healthicity in June 2026, starts at $99 a month, with tiers reported at $299 and $519 (third-party figures). MedTrainer is similar. A "gift module" would be a small add for them.
- **Free substitutes.** Hospital and Encompass Health policy templates plus an Excel log. Encompass publishes caps of $75 per event and $200 per year for non-physician sources. The pharmacy-industry trade press says few referral-driven companies track spend per source at all (https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/the-need-for-specialty-pharmacies-to-track-spending-on-referral-sources). That shows weak demand as much as a gap.
- **Services.** Healthcare attorneys and consultants (SimiTree and others) run marketing compliance reviews.

### Legal premise (mostly holds, with two corrections)
- Fla. Stat. 817.505 covers "any person", not just federal payers. It carries a third-degree felony and a $50,000 fine, rising to $100,000 for 10-19 patients and $500,000 for 20 or more (https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/817.505). The penalty scales with patient count, not per gift.
- State v. Kigar (4th DCA, Aug 2019) held that advice of counsel is not a defense. Verified.
- **Correction 1:** the statute exempts conduct that is not prohibited by the federal Anti-Kickback Statute. Florida therefore adds little for gifts and meals beyond the AKS. The AKS has no gift safe harbor and no de minimis rule. The only hard number is the Stark cap of $535 for 2026, which covers physician referral sources only (https://hallrender.com/2025/12/29/2026-non-monetary-compensation-to-physicians-and-chance-to-review-2025). Home health is a Stark designated health service, but hospice is not. For discharge planners and facility staff, every cap comes from the client's own policy. The "policy engine" is therefore client-entered thresholds, a yearly cap update, a log and a sign-off, which is close to a spreadsheet plus a workflow.
- **Correction 2:** recent Florida patient-brokering prosecutions I found are mostly sober homes, rehab and labs. I found no 2025-26 Florida home-health or hospice patient-brokering case. Hospice and home-health settlements cluster on **medical-director payments**: Creative Hospice $9.2M (June 2025) and Traditions Health $34M (Jan 2026). A 2024 DOJ settlement did involve meals, sports tickets and lease payments to assisted living facilities: three agencies, $4.5M.
- The pitch's "CMS letter to Florida" is unverified, and I found none.

### Why-now
- CMS froze new Medicare enrollments from May 13, 2026, for six months (to about mid-November), with six-month extensions possible. Sources conflict on whether hospice is covered: Greenberg Traurig says home health only, JD Supra says both. It makes existing licences more valuable, but it also stops new-agency formation, which means no new customers.
- Margins are squeezed. An advocacy coalition says about 48% of home-health agencies were projected to run negative margins in 2024 and more than 1,000 have closed since 2019. That is a weak backdrop for discretionary software.

### Size
- 12,234 Medicare-certified home-health agencies and 6,706 hospices in 2024, about 18,900 in total (MedPAC and CRS figures). The pitch's 11,925 is close. The "25,000 including DME, labs, ABA and senior living" is unsourced.
- Florida: about 1,099 home-health agencies and about 42-55 hospices. The Florida hospice association channel reaches roughly 45 organizations.
- Price benchmarks are $99-$519 a month for full compliance suites and $49-$490 a month for a CRM (third-party estimates). A realistic price for a single-function ledger is $200-$400 a month, or $2.4k-$4.8k a year. The pitch's $9k a year is 2-4x that.
- Bottom-up:
  - Median case: 150-250 customers at about $3.5k a year, giving about $0.7M a year by year 5.
  - Upside case: 1,500 customers at $5k a year, giving about $8M. That is about 8% of all certified agencies, and it requires the widened product.
  - The pitch's $10M needs about 1,100 organizations at $9k, which is not supported.

### Can this founder reach and close
- **Family company:** MHC is unverified. The public record shows "MHC Area 6/9/10 LLC" Medicare-certified home-health agencies (Riverview, Boca Raton operating as Choice Health At Home, Fort Lauderdale). The numbering suggests a multi-agency operator, which would suit a pilot, but ownership and the grandfather link are unconfirmed.
- **Father's tech:** the "borrowed architecture" is weak. MyFinancialAdvice has a Gust profile (8 employees, no customers or revenue listed), the company site is currently unreachable, and archive plus lexicon plus supervisory sign-off is commodity technology in financial services.
- **Channel:** the Home Care Association of Florida (HomeCareCon, July 2026) and the Florida Alliance for Home Care Services reach home-health owners. The Florida Hospice and Palliative Care Association (FHPCA) affiliate membership is $600 (Associate) or $3,200 (Patron) a year. It is cheap, but the hospice base is only about 45 organizations. FHPCA's last compliant-marketing webinar I found was in 2017, and the 2025 one was about benefits.
- **Credibility:** an 18-year-old selling felony-exposure software to compliance officers and attorneys will not close cold. A grandfather's introduction is the only real route. Chains will ask for security questionnaires and SOC 2 (roughly $10-25k), which arrives around month 6-12.
- **Design problem:** a gift ledger that can be joined to referral counts creates evidence that gifts "took into account" referral volume. Counsel will insist the ledger be firewalled from referral data, which removes the CRM integration the pitch relies on. The ledger itself is also a discoverable record. If MHC's past practice was unlogged, its counsel may resist starting one.
- **Legal and age:** no licence wall. He is an adult. The unauthorized-practice-of-law risk is manageable if the tool only applies client-set rules and never says "compliant". Counsel review of the template and terms is needed (about $1.5-2.5k). Avoid PHI so no BAA is needed.

## Numbers
- Cost to first (family) revenue: about $3,000. This covers the LLC, hosting, counsel review of the policy template and terms, and one association membership.
- Weeks to first revenue: about 10 with MHC. A first non-family customer would take 20-30 weeks, with roughly a 25% chance within six months.
- Median year-5 revenue about $0.7M. Upside about $8M.

## Sharpened version (widened, not shrunk)
**Referral Remuneration Ledger for post-acute care.** One system of record for everything of value that flows to anyone who can steer a patient:
- gifts, meals, events, free in-services and free staffing
- medical-director agreements, with time logs and third-party fair-market-value opinions on file
- marketer pay structures and sponsorships

Each item is logged and checked against the client's counsel-set policy and the current Stark cap, then routed to review and attested monthly. Everything goes into an audit-ready archive. It is firewalled from patient and referral-volume data.

Sell it to owner-operators and multi-location chains through healthcare attorneys and the Florida associations, priced per location (about $250 a month, with chain pricing), with MHC as the design partner. The widening goes where the settlements are (medical directors, assisted living inducements) and into a bigger budget line (hospice medical-director arrangements). It competes with symplr, RLDatix and MedPro in hospitals, but none of them target post-acute.

## Kill test (30 days, about 10 conversations)
- Run 10 calls: MHC marketing and compliance, 5 owner peers, 3 attorneys.
- Pass: at least 3 compliance leads say they would pay at least $250 a month, at least 1 attorney will recommend it, and MHC marketers log more than 80% of spend for four weeks.
- Kill if marketers will not log, if attorneys do not want a discoverable record, if buyers say Excel plus Ramp is enough, or if a CRM vendor announces the feature.

# G11: Clean-Agency Shield (G:mhc_coo:1) - gate report, 2026-10-26

**Verdict: WEAK.** The insider wedge is real and legal, and first revenue is plausible. The product as pitched is mostly a feature of funded incumbents, the real price per agency is about a third of the pitch, and "proof of visit" attacks the smallest slice of what actually gets Florida home-health claims denied.

## What I found (about 26 searches and fetches)

**1. Two of the three pillars already ship elsewhere.**
- **Brellium** (https://brellium.com/hospice): audits 100% of notes pre-claim. The hospice page says it detects "boilerplate decline narratives and copy-paste language." It also checks signatures and timing gaps.
  - Standard EMR integrations: HCHB, Kinnser/WellSky, Axxess, MatrixCare.
  - Customer logos: Gentiva, Maxim, Interim, Florida Hospice & Palliative Care Association, Texas Association for Home Care & Hospice.
  - It pays clawbacks on charts it approved ("Confidence Coverage").
  - Raised $16.7M (Apr 2025) and $23M (14 Oct 2026). The idea's "forensics is adjacent, not the same" is overstated for similarity detection.
  - I could not fetch its home-health page (404). Search results show one exists.
- **Referring-physician check:** Axxess already validates PECOS status at admission and re-checks it on a schedule. This is commodity. OIG exclusion screening is also cheap and mature.
- **GPS and timestamps:** the EMRs and EVV systems already capture them. Florida Medicaid fee-for-service home health EVV (HHAeXchange) went live 2024-10-01.
  - EVV is a Medicaid mandate. Medicare home health has no EVV requirement.
  - What is left is the cross-source reconciliation and the packet. I found no vendor selling that for Medicare home health.
- **Others:**
  - Enzo QA: pre-bill OASIS/coding QA, claims about $200 per episode recovered. Its AI Scribe is also a note generator.
  - AutoMynd: AI-native EMR with an ADR-readiness check and a downloadable pre-claim packet.
  - ABILITY EASE: ADR alerts and e-submission.
  - SimiTree: audit-prep consulting.
  - Charta Health: $22M Series A, adjacent chart review.
- **Substitute for "proof":** fraud-defense counsel (Bass Berry, Husch Blackwell, Oberheiden) win UPIC extrapolation fights with their own statisticians and clinical experts. They claim reversals of 90% or more of the extrapolations they contest (firm self-reports).
  - A vendor-generated "originality score" from the agency's own data has little evidentiary weight with a UPIC or ALJ.
- **Traction signal:** Enzo, a funded home-health-only QA vendor, reported **57 agencies** on its platform as of 2026-06-01. Selling software into this segment is slow.

**2. The demand story is partly misframed.**
- The Oz letter of 2026-03-17 to DeSantis was about **Medicaid** fraud, not Medicare home health.
- A 2026-03-31 law-firm alert on Florida fraud priorities listed DME, pharmacy, telemedicine, genetic testing, ABA, adult day centers and personal care. Home health was not on that list.
- The CMS moratorium (announced 2026-05-13, six months, extendable) blocks **new** enrollments, which is the fraud entry vector. It does not itself create audits of existing agencies. The initial period ends about mid-November 2026, so check for an extension.
- Palmetto's latest denial table (Apr-Jun 2026):

| Denial reason | Share of denials |
|---|---|
| No plan of care or certification | 34% |
| Records not submitted | 24% |
| Face-to-face not met | 14% |
| Medical necessity | 11% |
| Visits billed but not documented | 5.5% |
| No physician orders | 3.6% |

  - About 75% of denials are completeness failures. Authenticity or proof-of-visit touches the 5.5% row, plus fraud investigations, which a clean agency rarely faces.
- Florida runs the Review Choice Demonstration. The pre-claim and ADR tests are F2F, POC, homebound and orders, not visit authenticity.

**3. Size is smaller than the pitch.**
- MedPAC: 12,057 home-health agencies, 8.3M thirty-day periods, $15.7B paid (2023). At 8.6 visits per period (the 2022 figure) that is about 71M visits, or **about 490 visits per agency per month on FFS Medicare**. The all-payer average is probably 700-1,000 (my estimate). The pitch assumed 3,000.
- At $0.30-0.60 per visit, a typical agency is **about $3-7k a year**, not $16k. $16k only fits the top decile.
- Florida has about 1,079 agencies (KFF, 2023). The US has about 6,700 hospices (MedPAC, 2024).
- Reaching $10M of annual revenue takes:

| ACV | Customers needed | Share of ~18,800 home-health + hospice providers |
|---|---|---|
| $16k (pitch) | 625 | 3.3% |
| $6k | 1,670 | 8.9% |
| $4k | 2,500 | 13% |

  Large chains have in-house compliance teams, so the reachable pool is smaller than the total.
- Median case: 60-90 providers plus some counsel-channel engagements, about **$0.5M a year** by year 3-4. Upside: about **$6M**, if it becomes an EMR-partnered or counsel-standard evidence format.

**4. Legal and structural issues.**
- No age or license wall. An 18-year-old can form an LLC, sign contracts and be a HIPAA business associate. The costs:
  - BAA and a risk analysis.
  - Cyber/E&O insurance.
  - Non-family buyers will ask for SOC 2 or a security questionnaire. SOC 2 is my estimate at $10-25k, not verified.
  - Realistic pre-revenue spend is about $6-7k for the MHC pilot, and over $10k before non-family sales.
- **Adoption paradox:** since 2025, an overpayment is "identified" on actual knowledge or reckless disregard (FCA standard), which starts a 60-day return clock. A tool that surfaces cloned notes creates that knowledge, so owners may avoid looking.
  - Run it under counsel (privileged), which makes the law-firm channel the real front door. That channel is service-heavy.
- **Falsification risk:** a "make notes look more different" use would facilitate record falsification. Design against it.
- **Moat claim is weak.** A cross-agency originality corpus needs pooled PHI. BAAs and owners resist secondary use. His AI-text detector skill transfers loosely: clone detection is standard near-duplicate math, and authorship scoring on templated clinical notes is the hard, unproven part.
- **MHC is unverified.** Web search found only Medical House Calls (Tennessee/Florida Panhandle) and an Arizona MHC Healthcare, neither matching. Whether MHC is a Medicare-certified home-health agency, hospice or something else is unknown. It changes the first customer, the labeled set and the EMR.
- **Family conflict:** his mother is an MHC employee and his grandfather is the covered entity. Written data authorization is needed, and peers will discount a family reference.

## Reachability
- **Pilot customer:** MHC, only if it is a Medicare HHA. Free retro scan, then a paid pilot of $2-4k. First revenue is about 14 weeks out (BAA, EMR export, DON labels, calibration). Axxess integration setup alone is reported at 2-8 weeks, and broad API access is negotiated, so start with manual C-CDA/PDF exports.
- **First 10 named customer types and communities:**
  1. MHC.
  2-3. Two or three independent Florida Medicare HHAs run by grandfather's peers (500-1,500 visits a month).
  4. One hospice owner from the same network.
  5-6. Two or three Florida healthcare fraud-defense and compliance firms, as paid consultant-channel (privileged).
  7. A Florida billing or RCM company that serves 20-50 agencies.
  8. Home Care Association of Florida members (HomeCareCon is each July: 2026 was 21-24 Jul in Orlando; the next fits his summer).
  9. Florida Hospice & Palliative Care Association owners, where Brellium is already present.
  10. Independent OASIS/coding audit consultants as resellers.
- Non-family customers within 6 months look plausible via warm intros. Closing them at $4-7k a year, against Brellium and the EMRs, is the hard part.

## Sharpened version (not shrunk)
**Audit Packet: the EMR-agnostic, counsel-ready evidence layer for Medicare home health and hospice.**
- Lead with what gets claims denied: a continuously maintained audit-ready file per billed period. It checks:
  - certification, plan of care, face-to-face, orders, and signature and date order;
  - that records can be produced inside the 30-45 day ADR window.
- Visit-presence evidence (EVV, GPS, mileage, duration versus note length) is reconciled to the notes and attached.
- The authenticity or similarity queue is one module, not the product. Output is a needs-review queue, never an accusation.
- Sold through counsel (Kovel-style, privileged) and directly to agencies.
- Price on revenue at risk, about 0.5% of Medicare revenue or $8-12 per paid 30-day period, so about $6-9k a year for a typical agency (assumption). Add $2-10k per UPIC/TPE response engagement through counsel.
- Moat: a published, pre-registered validation of false-positive rates per agency baseline. Add a credentialed statistician and a certified coder as advisors, so UPIC and ALJ readers give the packet weight. Be the evidence format counsel trusts, not a corpus.
- Expand to hospice, Medicaid HHCS EVV reconciliation, then the payer and auditor side (a different business).
- Kill tests:
  - Precision of 70% or better on the DON-labeled set.
  - The tool surfaces at least one issue that MHC's real ADR/TPE history confirms.
  - Three of five non-family owners or counsel pay $2-5k for a retro scan within 30 days of the pilot. If fewer than two do, stop.

## Bottom line
Worth doing only as an MHC-based learning pilot and a wedge into counsel and owner networks, after verifying MHC's type. Not a $10M company as scoped.

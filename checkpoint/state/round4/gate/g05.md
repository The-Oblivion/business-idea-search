# G05 gate: CareFund (G:cross_family:0)

**Verdict: WEAK.** Real wedge and reachable first customers, but the payer math as written does not close, the legal stack is heavier than the pitch admits, and the "big" path runs through channels (carriers, banks, employers) an 18-year-old cannot open. Salvageable only by changing who pays and what is sold (see Sharpened).

## Numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Cost to first revenue | ~$6,000 (idea said $4,000; add limited-scope healthcare, securities and Bar-rule counsel, E&O/cyber) |
| Weeks to first revenue | ~8 (but the first dollars are favors from Dad's advisors and the family agency, not market proof) |

## Competitors and substitutes (assume someone does it)
- **CareScout (Genworth)**: providers pay on placements (25% of the monthly discount in the 2025 home-care example), ~2,950 matches in H1 2026, 1,100+ home-care locations, "local care advisors" in 26 states, bought Seniorly (Oct 2025). Nurse-led Care Plans went nationwide Jan 2026. Has a financial-professional partner hub (sales materials, not a referral tool). The strongest incumbent, and it owns the carrier channel.
- **A Place for Mom / Caring.com**: agencies already buy leads (about $55-150 per lead, bid pricing, per-lead or per-start). Agency budget line exists but is ~1-2% of revenue for marketing overall.
- **Tendercare (trytendercare.com)**: free-to-family advisor matching for elder care ("Verified Members"). Early, but it is the same advisor-fulfilment idea.
- **Cariloop** ($20M Series C, 2024) partnered with an RIA, Quotient Wealth Partners, in April 2026 to push caregiving help to advisor clients; **Wellthy** (employer-paid, ~$77M raised, merged with Cleo).
- **Carefull** ($19.7M plus CIBC debt 2025): sells through banks and credit unions (CUNA alliance), the exact channel the "big" path needs.
- **SmartAsset**: the advisor lead-gen incumbent (~$100-200 per lead, about 1 in 10 close, per third-party estimates).
- **Free**: NCOA BenefitsCheckUp (benefits screening), Area Agency on Aging / SHIP counselors, Medicare GUIDE (CMS dementia care-navigation model, 2024-2032), and franchise agencies (e.g. Amada) that already help families file LTC claims as a free sales aid. The "funding map" itself is a commodity.
- Nobody found combining funding map + advisor/attorney fulfilment + agency-intake distribution. That gap is real, but each piece is weakly monetised on its own.

## Why the money does not close
1. **Flat-fee advisors cannot pay $200-350 per consult.** The product is a ~45 minute flat-fee consult (hourly planners average ~$268/hr, flat plans ~$2,554 per Envestnet 2024). A lead fee of $200-350 is roughly 100% of consult revenue. Dad's roster is the wrong payer. The payers who can afford leads are elder-law attorneys (Medicaid planning $3k-15k; leads $75-400) and AUM/hybrid RIAs (SmartAsset-style), which carry the heaviest rules.
2. **Volume per agency is thin.** A 100-300-admission agency at ~10% intake completion and ~15% consult booking yields ~2-5 consults a year (a guess to test, not a finding). That cannot carry $6-10k a year in agency license fees against a total marketing budget of ~1-2% of revenue.
3. **The $90M bottom-up needs 200k consults, about 2M completed intakes**, and 100k agency "starts" at $400, which is the per-start fee the idea itself flags as restricted.
4. **The LTC-policy hook is narrow.** Only ~14% of people 60+ hold LTC insurance (TIAA 2025); ~6.2-6.9M covered lives. LTC insurance is ~8.7% of LTSS spending (all private insurance); Medicaid is 45.6%, out-of-pocket 14.4%.
5. **Why-now has a clock.** The CMS moratorium on new home-health enrollment began May 13, 2026 for six months (about Nov 13, 2026) and is extendable. Existing agencies are protected, but the argument is not permanent.
6. **The agency's real prize runs the other way.** One agency analysis put advisor-referred clients at ~$184k lifetime value vs ~$4.6k for guardian-referred ones. Advisors referring families IN is what agencies value; CareFund's flow is agency to advisor.

## Legal stack (the premises checked)
- **Fla. Stat. 817.505 (patient brokering)**: covers anyone licensed/registered with AHCA (home health agencies). Penalties: 3rd-degree felony and $50k per violation, up to 1st-degree and $500k for 20+ patients. Per-start fees to agencies are the exposure; a flat fair-market fee to a "consumer information service" that does not steer is the closest exemption (3)(i). Drop per-start fees. Real but avoidable.
- **Florida Bar Rule 4-7.22**: CareFund is a "qualifying provider" for lawyers. Participation requires, among others, no fee-sharing, a panel with lawyers from at least 4 firms, annual lawyer list to the Bar, written response in 15 days. "2 elder-law attorneys" and "exclusive county" both conflict with this. Real; clearable by design.
- **SEC Marketing Rule 206(4)-1**: any advisor paying CareFund for referrals makes it a paid promoter: written agreement, disclosure at the time of the referral, eligibility checks. SmartAsset proves it is workable. This is where Dad's compliance toolkit is a genuine edge.
- **HIPAA marketing**: an agency handout promoting a third-party advisor where the advisor funds it directly or indirectly likely needs patient authorization. Keep no remuneration flowing from advisors to agencies, and no PHI.
- **VA**: no claim-prep fees unless accredited; keep VA content informational.
- Not a wall: LLC formation and contracts (he is an adult); no license needed while non-advice and non-brokering.

## Reachability
- **First customers: yes.** Family agency pilot plus Dad's advisors within ~8 weeks. **Caveat:** nothing public confirms what MHC Healthcare is (only an unverified "MHC Area 6 LLC" Riverview listing on a low-quality directory), and MyFinancialAdvice appears to be a small Boulder, CO firm founded 2001 (~8 employees per one profile, last detailed coverage 2004). Verify both before building: AHCA license lookup, Care Compare, Sunbiz; ask Dad how many advisors are active and what they charge.
- **Non-family customers in 6 months: possible, not proven.** Grandfather's peer introductions work; the HCAF annual event (HomeCareCon, 37th edition) was July 21-24, 2026, so the next one is outside the 6-month window. A family pilot is weak proof to peers; they will want conversion data.

## Sharpened version
**CareFund Rail: the compliant funding-and-referral desk for home care.** Keep the free family intake and the non-advice funding map. Change three things:
1. **Payer**: elder-law attorneys (rotating panel of 4+ per county, flat monthly sponsorship) and hybrid/AUM advisors under written promoter agreements. Flat-fee planners become the low-cost tier, not the revenue engine. Agencies pay a small flat fair-market license, no per-start fees, no free-in-exchange-for-referrals.
2. **Proof asset**: instrument the family-agency pilot (plus 3-4 free peer pilots using de-identified aggregates) to measure private-pay conversion and length-of-stay lift. That number is what peers and, later, ALF/hospital discharge teams pay for.
3. **Moat and big path**: sell the compliance rail itself (promoter agreement, disclosure delivery, Bar-panel rules, record keeping, built from Dad's tooling), which is what carriers, banks and caregiver-benefit vendors lack, instead of selling a lead list.

**Kill criteria**: by ~April 2027, if fewer than 3 of 10 peer agencies would pay $300/month after seeing pilot data, or fewer than 5 attorneys/RIAs pay for a county slot, stop.

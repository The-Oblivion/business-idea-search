# G:mhc_coo:7 — Florida Independent Operators Alliance (gate report, 2026-10-26)

**Verdict: WEAK.** Best access of any idea in the round, thin economics. Lean toward "kill as written", "weak" only because the sharpened version below keeps the family edge and fixes the sizing and antitrust problems.

## Deciding reason
The product as written is a dues-and-fees association. Its own math caps near $0.5-1M a year in Florida and about $6M multi-state. The only route to $10M+ is payer contracting, which is the most antitrust-exposed step. It is also the step payers are routing around with delegated network managers (Tango for BCBSM, PHCN for Humana, Dina for MA/SNP/PACE).

## What searching found

**Every layer already has an incumbent (the "gap" is only the bundle).**
- Roundtable/peer group: Home Care CEO Forum masterminds, tiered by revenue, met in Florida (Orlando 2023, Marco Island/Naples). Private-duty focused, not Medicare home health.
- Benchmarking: Home Care Pulse / Activated Insights report ($699-$999), free Florida state report; SHP (OASIS benchmarking, from ~$1,000, 7,700 providers); BerryDunn free benchmark portal with a Medicare rate calculator; HCAOA/Careswitch free pay report. Nobody found publishing MA rates.
- Purchasing: HCAF already runs a "Member Discounts" affinity program; CAHSAH (California) runs a vendor-royalty GPO; Independent Home Care Alliance (IHCA) launched 2019 with 200+ members at $200-300 dues.
- IHCA is the cautionary precedent: in 2021 its founder put in $1.26M to remodel it as a back-office BPO. A dues + purchasing alliance did not stand on its own.
- HCAF is both channel and competitor: ~$1.73M revenue (2025, ProPublica), HomeCareCon 600+ attendees and 90+ vendors, reimbursement surveys for advocacy. The four HCAF founding members need HCAF's blessing.
- Contracting vehicle: no independent home-health CIN found contracting with MA, but payers are going the other way. BCBSM outsourced its Michigan home-health network to Tango (episodes from 2026-03-02; agencies must contract with Tango). Humana uses PHCN. Dina manages in-home networks for MA/SNP/PACE. Humana's CenterWell is vertically integrated (Trilogy, 11 FL offices).

**Antitrust (hardest wall, and it is not an age issue).**
- DOJ withdrew the health-care safety-zone statements in Feb 2023; the 2000 collaboration guidelines were withdrawn Dec 2024. Replacement guidance was opened for comment Feb 23, 2026 (comments closed Apr 24); none issued as of the Q1 2026 roundup.
- DOJ statements of interest in Frozen Potatoes (Feb 27, 2026) and Turkey (Mar 25, 2026): information exchanges are not presumptively lawful, a third-party facilitator does not shield them, and aggregated backward-looking data can still harm competition. FTC launched a Healthcare Task Force Mar 20, 2026.
- Exchanging MA rates among direct local competitors, chaired by an owner whose own company contributes data, is the highest-risk shape. Joint payer contracting without clinical or financial integration is the classic FTC messenger-model violation (older cases, 2002-2010, but the doctrine stands).
- The "retired safety zone" is a floor, not a defense. Counsel for the protocol is a gating cost. My estimate is $15-30k (unverified), well above the $6k budget.

**AKS group purchasing safe harbor (verified, 42 CFR 1001.952(j)).** Needs a written agreement with each member; vendor fee of 3% or less, or a stated amount in writing; annual written disclosure of vendor payments to provider members; the GPO cannot be wholly owned by its members. Doable without a license. GAO found hospital GPO fees averaged 1.22-2.25% (2008).

**Why-now is real but cuts both ways.**
- CMS froze new Medicare HHA enrollment and new branches nationwide on 2026-05-13 for six months (about Nov 13, 2026, extendable). Growth has to come from conversions and acquisitions.
- CY2027 proposed rule: +2.4% net, but a temporary -3.0% adjustment; permanent cuts of -3.925%, -2.890%, -1.975%, -1.023% in CY2023-26.
- MA rates are not covered by Transparency in Coverage, so a rate benchmark is a real information gap. No vendor found that reconciles MA remittances to contract rates for home health (WellSky has a generic 835 tool; D3rx excludes MA).

**Market size.** Medicare-certified HHAs (2023, KFF/CMS): US 11,925, CA 2,702, TX 1,882, FL 1,079.

## Numbers
- Assumed $12k per member per year is too high. Realistic is $4-9k (about $6.5k). Dues $3-6k is unvalidated (comparators: IHCA $200-300, HCP report ~$700-1k, private masterminds $3k+). Vendor fees are about 1.2-3% of the 30-50% of $150-300k addressable spend that moves, so $0.5-4.5k.
- Florida addressable (about half of 1,079 are independent owners, unverified) is ~500. At 10-20% in 3 years that is 50-100 members, or $0.3-0.9M a year.
- $10M at $6.5k needs about 1,500 members (12.6% of every US agency). Not credible for an association.
- Median case ~$0.7M a year (Florida plus a second state). Upside ~$6M (five states via state-association partners, about 550 members at ~$8-10k, or a CIN-partner data fee).
- First revenue: roundtable dues from founding members in about 10 weeks for about $6k (counsel review of ground rules, LLC, venue). First data exchange needs more (counsel).

## Sharpened version (does not shrink the vision; re-sequences it)
**Title:** MA Rate Exchange: a Florida owners' alliance built on a contract-audit data asset
1. Weeks 1-4: confirm MHC's type; retain antitrust counsel; write ground rules (no rate talk in the room).
2. Weeks 4-10: quarterly roundtable chaired by the grandfather; founding dues from 4-6 owners; partner with HCAF rather than compete.
3. Weeks 6-12 pilot: audit MHC's own MA remits vs contracts (reuse the freight contract-vs-invoice engine). Kill if leakage is under about 0.5% of MA revenue.
4. Month 4+: sell the private audit to members (flat fee plus share of recovered underpayments). No cross-competitor sharing yet.
5. Month 9+: counsel-governed anonymized rate cube (at least 5 contributors per cell, no contributor over 25%, data at least 3 months old, no price recommendations).
6. Then GPO under the 3% safe harbor; payer contracting only through a licensed CIN partner; replicate in other state associations and extend to hospice.

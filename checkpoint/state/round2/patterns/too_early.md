# Too-early startups whose blocker was removed (2025-2026)

Round 2 pattern mining. Brief: startups from 2012-2022 (plus a few older anchors) that failed or stalled because they were too early, where the specific blocker was REMOVED in 2025-2026. Written 2026-10-14. About 34 searches and fetches; the raw log with every number and URL is in the appendix at the bottom (nothing was dropped).

Evidence tags used below: [PRIMARY] = company filing or press release I fetched; [PRESS] = reputable press or a regulator/court document; [SECONDARY] = aggregator, analyst estimate or blog; [UNVERIFIED] = from my background knowledge or a single weak source, to be checked before anyone relies on it.

---

## 0. Headline findings

1. **Most "too early" stories are not too early.** I checked about 25 post-mortems. Only about half were really killed by a blocker that later lifted. The rest were bad execution or bad economics wearing a timing excuse: Olive AI (CEO blamed "lack of focus"), Transatomic Power (overstated design claims), Zume (two capital-heavy businesses in one), Kozmo and Webvan (no delivery density), Rethink Robotics (product did not fit buyers). Treat any founder's "we were early" with suspicion. See the case ledger.
2. **The prize almost never goes to the original too-early company.** Waymo is Google's, Starlink is SpaceX's, Kalshi was founded in 2018 (Intrade died in 2013), Circle (not Basis or Diem) holds the stablecoin prize, Meta plus EssilorLuxottica (not Google Glass, Intel Vaunt or North) sold 7M smart glasses, and Anduril is new. The winner is whoever starts building the day the blocker lifts, or a deep-pocketed platform that can survive the wait. For a new founder the lesson is: watch for dated blocker-removal events and build immediately after, rather than reviving an old idea.
3. **Where the money is already visible (hard numbers, mid-2026):** Starlink $11.4B 2025 revenue, $4.4B operating income [SECONDARY summary of the S-1]; Circle $2.7B FY2025 revenue [PRIMARY-derived]; Bloom Energy $2.02B in 2025 and a $3.9-4.2B 2026 guide [PRIMARY]; Anduril roughly $2.1-2.2B 2025 revenue [SECONDARY]; Kalshi $1.5B+ (company) to $3.5B (Sacra estimate) annualized [SECONDARY]; Cerebras $510M 2025 revenue and $25.4B backlog [PRIMARY for backlog]; Sierra about $150M ARR in 2 years [SECONDARY]. Where revenue is still tiny despite huge valuations: Aurora ($2M per quarter), Fervo ($113k in Q2 2026), Zipline (not disclosed), humanoid robots (sector under $1B), Persona (not disclosed).
4. **Six kinds of unlock appeared repeatedly,** and they have very different risk: (a) a regulator or court says yes; (b) a cost curve falls about 10x because someone else scaled; (c) a deep-pocketed buyer's demand shock (AI data centers) re-rates a stalled supply technology; (d) a government or infrastructure-capital backstop makes a capital-heavy industry bankable; (e) a closed buyer reforms its purchasing; (f) a new input (LLMs) lets software absorb a labor line that humans used to do at a loss.
5. **Round-2 fit (lessons.md):** the patterns that clear the "$100M bottom-up and a named payer" bar best are: regulator-yes categories with proven demand (1.2% take rate on event contracts, reserve income on stablecoins), AI-buildout bottlenecks (hyperscaler capex is the payer), and labor-line conversion with outcome pricing. The weakest on evidence: payer-code unlocks (Medicare digital therapeutics), age-verification mandates, humanoids, drone delivery (rule still unpublished).

## 2. Patterns

### Pattern 1. Autonomy crosses the labor-substitution threshold (safety proof and capital, not just the algorithm)

**Mechanism.** A task that costs a lot of wages becomes automatable only when two things are true at once: the model is good enough in a bounded environment, and the operator can prove safety to regulators, insurers and buyers. Early companies had the first idea but not the proof or the capital to wait for it. Winners geofence, validate relentlessly and launch driverless operations only in places where the proof exists.

**Evidence (strength: MEDIUM for robotaxi, WEAK for trucking).**
- Waymo: about 500,000 paid rides per week across 10 US cities in March 2026, 14-15M trips in 2025 vs 4.5M in 2024, fleet about 3,600 vehicles, Series D $16B at $126B (Feb 2026). Revenue is an estimate (Sacra: about $355M annualized, about $15-17 per ride), not audited. Goal of 1M rides per week by end of 2026 vs a forecaster's median of 775k. [SECONDARY] sacra.com/c/waymo ; futuresearch.ai/waymo-financial-forecast
- Aurora: second driverless fleet in 2026, about 440k driverless miles, Q2 2026 revenue about $2M, operating loss $266M, 200 driverless trucks planned by year-end, Roush ramping to 1,000 kits per year. [PRIMARY] Aurora Q2 2026 release (businesswire.com/news/home/20260729316480).
- Starsky Robotics (dead): cited timing and overhyped supervised ML. [PRESS] therobotreport.com/starsky-robotics-co-founder-on-self-driving-truck-shutdown

**Shift that enabled it.** A decade of simulation and fleet data plus a patient funder (Alphabet) and regulator-by-regulator permission, 2020-2025. Hardware kit cost halving per generation.

**Why incumbents failed.** Trucking and ride-hail incumbents did not build it; car makers (GM-Cruise) quit when the safety incident and the cost of scale collided. The ones that survived were funded by a cash-rich parent or by public-market money.

**Still open or crowded?** Robotaxi is consolidating (Waymo leads; one forecaster expects it to miss its own 1M target). Trucking is open but proof-heavy: Aurora's revenue is a rounding error against $266M of quarterly loss. Capital needed is very large; this is not a bootstrap pattern.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses, pool sizing is for the pools stage).** Bounded-environment labor pools where a driver or operator is the main cost and the buyer already pays by the hour, mile or load: ports and terminal tractors, rail and logistics yards, airport ground handling, quarry and mine haulage (OEMs already compete), farm field operations, long fixed-route middle-mile freight. The test: can the environment be fenced, and does the buyer have an hourly budget line that goes away?

### Pattern 2. A regulator's "yes" converts a banned or capped product into a legal one (and the first licensed player owns the category)

**Mechanism.** Demand existed and technology was easy. The only blocker was legal permission. Once a regulator, a court or a statute grants it, the first compliant entity with the license and distribution captures a category that already had customers. The risk is that permission stays contested.

**Evidence (strength: STRONG on revenue for stablecoins; MEDIUM-STRONG for prediction markets but estimates and legal risk; WEAK for drones because the rule is not out).**
- Kalshi (and Intrade, dead): Intrade peaked at about 82,000 members and over $200M wagered in 2012, then the CFTC sued and it shut in March 2013. Kalshi (founded 2018): $1B Series F at $22B (May 2026, Coatue), annualized revenue over $1.5B (company), $3.5B (Sacra estimate, June 2026) vs $735M in 2025; blended take rate about 1.2% (Polymarket about 0.67%); about 80% of volume is sports, parlays about 50% of volume during the World Cup; Robinhood about 25% of volume (but Robinhood launched its own CFTC-licensed exchange); Polymarket US reached about $1B annualized by June 2026. Several state lawsuits allege unlicensed sports wagering. Sacra's "+172% YoY" line is arithmetically wrong (735M to 3.5B is about +376%), so use the estimates with care. [SECONDARY] sacra.com/research/kalshi-at-3b-year ; [PRESS] reason.com/2013/11/25/the-death-of-intrade
- Circle (and Basis and Diem, dead): Basis raised $133M and shut in Dec 2018 because securities law would apply; Diem sold for $182M in Jan 2022 after regulators said no. After the 2025 GENIUS Act, Circle reported FY2025 revenue of $2.7B (+64%), net loss $70M (due to $424M of share-based pay), USDC $75.3B at year-end, Q1 2026 revenue $694M (+20% YoY) with reserve income falling as rates were cut. Most revenue is interest on reserves, so it is rate-sensitive. [PRIMARY-derived] bitcoinminingstock.io/earnings/calls/crcl/q4-fy2025 ; tikr.com Q1 2026
- Zipline (unlock pending): raised $600M+ at $7.6B (Jan 2026), over 2M deliveries, U.S. volume growing about 15% per week for seven months; revenue not disclosed; reportedly seeking $1B at $20B. The FAA Part 108 BVLOS rule (proposed Aug 2025) reached White House review on 10 Jul 2026 and was still unpublished in early September 2026, after missing a Jan 2026 statutory deadline. [PRIMARY] zipline.com newsroom ; [PRESS] lowaltitudeeconomy.aero/en/aam-events/faa-part-108-oira-review

**Shift that enabled it.** A statute (GENIUS Act, 2025), an exchange designation plus litigation wins (Kalshi), a rulemaking (Part 108).

**Why incumbents failed.** Banks and brokers could not offer products the law banned; Intrade was offshore and ran afoul of the CFTC; Libra/Diem's backers could not get regulatory sign-off.

**Still open or crowded?** Prediction markets: open but getting crowded (Polymarket US, Robinhood, others) and legally fragile because sports are about 80% of volume and states are suing. Stablecoin issuance is dominated by Circle and Tether; the open space is in the rails around it (payments, cross-border, treasury), not issuance. Drones: pre-unlock.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** Search for categories where a named regulator has signaled yes but only one or two licensed entrants exist: regulated event contracts on non-sports data; on-chain or tokenized versions of existing securities and treasury products; B2B cross-border payment flows moved to dollar tokens; anything waiting on a federal rule with a published deadline. Price the legal risk first (lessons.md item 4): a state-by-state fight can erase the pool.

### Pattern 3. A government payer code or compliance mandate creates the budget line a product never had

**Mechanism.** Products that worked but had no payer die. When a government creates a billing code or a legal duty, it creates a buyer with a budget line. Risks: the code is under-used, or the mandate is unenforced or delayed.

**Evidence (strength: WEAK; I found no revenue figure for either example).**
- Pear Therapeutics (dead, Ch.11 Apr 2023): only about half of prescriptions were covered; CMS then created digital mental health treatment codes G0552-G0554 in the CY2025 fee schedule and widened them for CY2026. G0552 still contractor-priced; analysts say claim volume is low. [PRESS] managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/pear-therapeutics-files-for-bankruptcy ; [PRIMARY] cms.gov CY2026 PFS summary (mm14315)
- Age assurance: the US Supreme Court upheld Texas's age-verification law 6-3 (Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton, June 2025); the UK Online Safety Act is being enforced (share of children meeting "highly effective" checks 25% to 43% between Jul 2025 and Jan 2026; GBP 1.35M fine in Feb 2026); Australia's under-16 social-media ban took effect on 10 Dec 2025 (fines to A$49.5M) but testers opened accounts on 9 of 10 platforms with no proof asked. Vendors: Persona $200M Series D at $2B (Apr 2025, no revenue disclosed); k-ID $45M Series A; Yoti revenue only GBP 6.2M in the year to March 2023. More than 50 firms bid for Australia's tech. [PRESS] thefire.org/supreme-court/free-speech-coalition-v-paxton ; lewissilkin.com Ofcom report ; [SECONDARY] thenextweb.com Australia study

**Shift that enabled it.** A dated administrative act (CMS final rule) or a court ruling or statute.

**Why incumbents failed.** Platforms had no duty to verify; payers had no code to pay.

**Still open or crowded?** Age assurance is already crowded with funded vendors, falling per-check prices and weak enforcement; payer-code products have shown little revenue. This is the weakest pattern for a $100M path unless the mandate comes with enforcement and a fixed fee per user.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** Other new mandates with enforcement and a payer: look for rules where a deadline, a fine schedule and a named compliance officer exist (for example the CMS prior-authorization API requirements that run into 2027 [UNVERIFIED], or e-invoicing mandates in Europe [UNVERIFIED]). Verify each rule's date, penalties and whether it has been delayed.

### Pattern 4. A scarce licensed asset is re-rated when a new use-case arrives

**Mechanism.** A license (spectrum, permit, slot, right-of-way) is worth little under the old use-case and much under a new one. The first owners fail or get stuck; the asset is later sold or leased at a multiple to whoever has the new platform.

**Evidence (strength: STRONG on the asset price, MEDIUM on downstream revenue).**
- LightSquared (dead 2012) to EchoStar to SpaceX: the FCC moved to block LightSquared in Feb 2012 over GPS interference; it filed Chapter 11 in May 2012. In 2025, EchoStar agreed to sell AWS-4 and H-block spectrum (65 MHz) to SpaceX for about $17B (about $8.5B cash plus $8.5B SpaceX stock, plus about $2B of interest payments); the FCC approved on 12 May 2026; closing expected by Nov 2027. [PRIMARY] EchoStar 10-Q (sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001415404/000110465926089370) ; [PRESS] fierce-network.com/wireless/spacex-scoops-echostar-spectrum-17b
- Ligado to AST SpaceMobile: AST got 80+ year rights to up to 40 MHz of L-band (about $550M at closing, about $80M per year, 4.7M penny warrants; court approved 23 Jun 2025). AST revenue: FY2025 $70.9M, Q1 2026 $14.7M, Q2 2026 about $31.5M, FY2026 guide $150-200M, commercial service slipped to 2027, and BlueBird 7 was lost to a too-low orbit in April 2026. [PRIMARY] AST 8-K (sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000178031226000005) ; [PRESS] businesswire.com AST-Ligado release

**Shift that enabled it.** Cheap launch and large phased-array satellites made space-to-phone viable; the FCC reframed the band's use.

**Why incumbents failed.** LightSquared could not make terrestrial LTE work next to GPS; EchoStar's own terrestrial wireless build did not pay off [UNVERIFIED, background]; the new use-case belonged to a different industry.

**Still open or crowded?** The big spectrum prizes are being taken (SpaceX, AT&T, AST). The pattern itself stays open for any scarce license whose best use changed.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** Stranded licensed or permitted assets whose best use shifted because of AI power demand or electrification: grid-interconnection positions at closed power plants, brownfield sites with transmission access, water rights near industrial clusters, rights-of-way and conduit, airport and port slots. A buy-side broker or aggregator of such assets with a data edge is a service-to-asset wedge. Needs pool sizing and a legal check on transferability.

### Pattern 5. A cost input falls about 10x because someone else scaled; the owner of the cost curve captures the category

**Mechanism.** A category looks absurd at 10x the cost, and the first startups (OneWeb, Teledesic) die on the capital and launch bill. A different player drives the input cost down for its own reasons and then enters the category already holding the cheap input.

**Evidence (strength: STRONG for Starlink, MEDIUM for storage, with a margin warning).**
- Starlink: 2025 revenue $11.4B (+49.8%), segment operating income $4.4B (39% margin), Starlink capex $4.2B, 8.9M subscribers at YE2025 and 10.3M at 31 Mar 2026 (from 2.3M in 2023), about 9,600 satellites. ARPU fell about 18% 2023-2025 as it expanded into cheaper markets. OneWeb filed Chapter 11 on 27 Mar 2020 with 74 of 648 satellites launched. [SECONDARY, S-1 summary] tomtunguz.com/spacex-s1-analysis ; [PRESS] engadget.com/2020-03-28-oneweb-declares-bankruptcy
- Grid batteries: BloombergNEF's Dec 2025 survey put average pack prices at $108/kWh (record low) and stationary-storage packs at $70/kWh (-45% vs 2024), driven by Chinese cell overcapacity (557 GWh of stationary cells made vs about half that installed). Grid battery installs were 112 GW (BNEF) or 108 GW (IEA) in 2025, about 10x in four years; BNEF forecasts 158 GW in 2026. [PRESS] about.bnef.com press release ; solarquarter.com IEA report

**Shift that enabled it.** Reusable rockets (SpaceX, 2015-2025); Chinese manufacturing overcapacity in cells (2023-2025).

**Why incumbents failed.** They had no control of the input; the cost curve belonged to a rival or to a foreign industry.

**Still open or crowded?** Satellite broadband is closed to new entrants for practical purposes (Starlink dominates; other large constellations such as Amazon's are a [UNVERIFIED, background] second player). Storage is open for integrators and software but price collapse driven by overcapacity thins margins for Western manufacturers; round 1 already found energy niches too small.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** The pattern says: look for a finished-goods category that is uneconomic only because one input is 10x too dear, where that input is already falling on someone else's curve (sensors, batteries, compute, actuators, satellite bandwidth), and then be the one who integrates and distributes. Candidate pools to size: storage-plus-load integration for commercial buildings and data centers, and any service that is priced per kWh or per GB and could be cut by 80%.

### Pattern 6. A deep-pocketed buyer's demand shock (AI buildout) re-rates a stalled supply technology

**Mechanism.** When a buyer with enormous capex budgets hits a hard bottleneck (power, inference speed), it pays a premium for any supplier that can deliver now. Technologies stuck in pilot mode for ten years suddenly have a customer whose problem is speed, not price.

**Evidence (strength: STRONG for Bloom and Cerebras on revenue/backlog; WEAK for Fervo on revenue, STRONG on contracted backlog).**
- Bloom Energy (founded 2001): 2025 revenue $2.02B (+37%), gross margin 29.0%; Q2 2026 revenue $1,065.4M (+165.5%), GAAP gross margin 33.4%; FY2026 guidance raised to $3.9-4.2B; CEO says all major US hyperscalers and over a dozen neoclouds and AI labs have approved its power systems. Backlog about $20B (about $6B product) at YE2025, of which about $14B is service contracts that customers can cancel annually. [PRIMARY] sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001664703/000162828026050150 ; bloomenergy.com Q4 2025 release
- Fervo Energy (geothermal): IPO 14 May 2026 raised about $2.2B; revenue is tiny (Q1 2026 $61k, Q2 $113k) but it reports 658 MW of binding PPAs worth $7.2B of contracted revenue, a 3 GW framework with Google, and Cape Station phase I (about 100 MW) declared in commercial operation on 30 Sep 2026 per one report (sources conflict on timing); 2027 revenue guided to $60-80M. [PRIMARY] sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001853868/000162828026056457
- Cerebras (founded 2016) and Groq (2016): Cerebras revenue $24.6M (2022), $290M (2024), $510M (2025), Q2 2026 revenue $180.1M GAAP (+74%) with cloud services up 281% and hardware down, RPO $25.4B at 30 Jun 2026, FY2026 core revenue guide $880-890M, IPO raised $5.55B. Customer concentration is extreme (named significant customers OpenAI, G42, MBZUAI, AWS; G42 plus MBZUAI were 86% of 2025 revenue per S-1 coverage). Groq licensed to Nvidia for about $20B (Dec 2025). Graphcore, the same vintage with a similar pitch, sold for about $600M in 2024. [PRIMARY] sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002021728/000162828026056186 ; [PRESS] w.media Nvidia-Groq ; computing.co.uk Graphcore

**Shift that enabled it.** The 2023-2026 AI buildout: hyperscaler capex made power and inference the binding constraints.

**Why incumbents failed (my inference, not sourced).** Utilities and turbine makers could not deliver on-site power quickly; GPU incumbents were not optimized for low-latency inference.

**Still open or crowded?** Open at the component and bottleneck level, but this is a capital-heavy, concentration-risk pattern. Cerebras's GAAP gross margin was 14.2% in Q2 2026 and one customer group dominated; Bloom's backlog is mostly cancelable service contracts; both could reverse if AI capex slows. Dependence on a single industry's capex cycle is the main risk.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** The payer is a hyperscaler with a named capex budget. Look at the next bottlenecks behind power and chips: electrical gear and transformers, cooling, on-site construction labor, interconnection studies and permitting, water, and fiber or optical links. For a small company, services and software for these bottlenecks (permitting, interconnection, commissioning, skilled-trade staffing for data-center builds) are the accessible edge. Verify with capex data in the pools stage.

### Pattern 7. A sovereign or infrastructure-capital backstop makes a capital-heavy, uninvestable industry bankable

**Mechanism.** Strategic or capital-intensive industries die from price risk or cost of capital, not from technology. A price floor, an offtake contract, an equity stake or a standardized financing structure turns the business into a financeable contract.

**Evidence (strength: MEDIUM-STRONG).**
- MP Materials: in July 2025 the Department of Defense bought $400M of preferred stock (about 15%, largest shareholder), set a 10-year NdPr price floor of $110/kg (China's benchmark was about $63/kg at signing; DoD pays the difference) and a 10-year offtake for the new magnet plant. Q2 2026: revenue $108.5M (+89%) plus $17.6M of price-protection income; NdPr sales 1,006 tons (+127%); adjusted EBITDA $28.5M. [PRESS] argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2708843 ; [PRIMARY] mpmaterials.com Q2 2026 release. (Molycorp's 2015 bankruptcy is the usual predecessor; I did not verify it in this run.)
- Bloom Energy and Brookfield: Brookfield's first AI-infrastructure investment, announced Oct 2025 at up to $5B, expanded to a $25B financing framework in Jun 2026 for onsite data-center power built with Bloom systems; the customer gets power as a service, not a capex bill. [PRESS] power-eng.com Brookfield-Bloom ; [PRIMARY] Bloom Q2 2026 8-K (Brookfield JV named)

**Shift that enabled it.** US industrial policy for critical minerals (2025); infrastructure funds seeking AI exposure.

**Why incumbents failed.** Pure-play commodity or equipment producers could not carry price risk against Chinese or cost competition, and customers could not fund the capex.

**Still open or crowded?** Open, but it rewards firms that can structure and operate, not idea founders. Revenue so far is modest (MP $108.5M a quarter).

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** Where a government or a large financier is publicly writing floors, offtakes or financing frameworks: critical minerals processing and magnets, grid equipment, defense munitions, shipbuilding inputs. For a small team: the origination layer (underwriting, project data, servicing, insurance) between equipment makers and infrastructure capital, since the capital providers now have mandates and few good deal flows. Check each program's budget and eligibility.

### Pattern 8. A closed buyer reforms its purchasing, and a product-first, self-funded vendor wins

**Mechanism.** Defense, government and other closed procurement systems favored cost-plus incumbents. When the buyer changes the rules (commercial-first buying, flexible contracting vehicles), a vendor that builds the product with its own money and sells it as a finished product can win contracts in months rather than years.

**Evidence (strength: MEDIUM; valuation far above revenue).**
- Anduril (founded 2017): $5B Series H at $61B (May 2026, Thrive and a16z); 2025 revenue about $2.1-2.2B, roughly double the prior year (secondary sources conflict on earlier years); valued at about 27.7x revenue vs about 1.6-2.7x for prime contractors. DIU brought it onto Replicator's autonomy software in 110 days. [SECONDARY] ocbj.com/technology/anduril-valuation-could-hit-60-billion-the-information ; newmarketpitch.com
- The reform itself: Hegseth's Nov 2025 memo "Transforming the Warfighting Acquisition System" favors Other Transaction Authorities and Commercial Solutions Openings over the standard FAR route; DIU has awarded over 500 OTAs since 2016, 88% to non-traditional vendors. Even so, startups get under 1% of Pentagon contract dollars (about $1.8B in FY2023 to $4.3B in FY2025, Bloomberg, Mar 2026), while PitchBook counts a record $16.8B of defense and aerospace startup investment in H1 2026. [PRESS] hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/war-secretary-announces-strategy-regarding-defense-acquisition-reform ; bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-03-25/defense-tech-gets-less-than-1-of-pentagon-spending-video

**Shift that enabled it.** Procurement reform (2025) plus a wartime-pace demand signal; the 2016-2025 DIU/OTA groundwork.

**Why incumbents failed.** Primes optimized for cost-plus programs and long timelines; earlier startups lacked a contract path.

**Still open or crowded?** Capital is pouring in (record H1 2026), so valuations are stretched and many founders are chasing the same primes' subsystems. The unserved edge is in the supply chain and operations layer behind the hardware.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** Other closed buyers with a reform or new vehicle and a large budget: border and homeland security technology, Coast Guard and shipbuilding, state and local public safety and emergency management, public transit and utilities procurement. The wedge for a small firm is compliance, program-capture and supplier services; check which budgets are actually appropriated.

### Pattern 9. A consumer hardware form factor works when a category-owning incumbent supplies the brand, channel and manufacturing

**Mechanism.** New devices fail when they are ugly, expensive, short on battery and short on a reason to exist. The unlock was packaging familiar objects with just enough new function and shipping them through the incumbent who owns the category's brand and stores.

**Evidence (strength: STRONG on units, but revenue is booked by EssilorLuxottica and Meta, not a startup).**
- Meta with EssilorLuxottica: more than 7M Ray-Ban and Oakley smart glasses sold in 2025, more than triple 2024 (about 2M cumulative in 2023-24); capacity planned at 10M per year by end 2026, with reported talks of 20M or more; Omdia counted 8.7M AI glasses shipped worldwide in 2025; the $799 Ray-Ban Display's international rollout was delayed in Jan 2026 by strong demand and supply limits. [PRESS] uploadvr.com/meta-essilorluxottica-sold-7-million-smart-glasses-in-2025 ; roadtovr.com
- The dead: Google Glass (consumer version ended about 2015: privacy backlash, $1,500, battery 3-5 hours, no clear use; Enterprise Edition sold until 15 Mar 2023); Intel Vaunt cancelled Apr 2018; North Focals (about $999, estimated fewer than 1,000 units) bought by Google in Jun 2020 for patents and staff. **Why incumbents failed.** Tech firms built gadgets for early adopters; no one with frames, fitting stores and brand was in the room.

**Still open or crowded?** Closed for a startup on glasses (Meta and EssilorLuxottica have the channel). Open in the sense of the template: tech plus a category owner. Counter-example from the same class of device: no verified revenue evidence in this run for standalone AI gadgets.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses).** Categories with a dominant physical-retail brand and a clear sensing or AI addition: eyewear and hearing (an adjacent EssilorLuxottica move I have not verified), fitness equipment, watches, dental and optical retail. A small company's route is to be the software and data partner to a category owner, not to ship its own hardware.

### Pattern 10. Labor-line conversion: human-in-the-loop services that died on labor cost become outcome-priced software once the language model exists

**Mechanism.** Services that needed humans to finish the job lost money at scale (Facebook M). Language models cut the marginal cost of the human part, so a vendor can price per resolved outcome against the customer's existing labor or outsourcing budget, which is a large, already-budgeted pool.

**Evidence (strength: MEDIUM; fast growth but frothy multiples and some figures unconfirmed; also in round 1's cliche zone for customer-service agents).**
- Sierra (founded 2023): $100M ARR announced about 21 months in (late 2025), about $150M by early February 2026; $950M Series E at $15.8B (May 2026); pay-per-resolution pricing; customers include ADT, Cigna, SiriusXM, Rivian, SoFi. [SECONDARY] trendingtopics.eu/sierra-raises-950-million-bret-taylors-ai-agent-startup-now-valued-at-over-15-billion ; tomtunguz.com/ai-harness-arr-multiples
- Decagon: valued at $4.5B in Jan 2026 (Coatue, Index) on about $35M ARR in October 2025 (3x YoY); a later report of over $100M ARR is not confirmed by the company (TechCrunch says it has not disclosed revenue since late 2024). [PRESS] news.bloomberglaw.com ; [SECONDARY] valueaddvc.com

**Shift that enabled it.** LLMs from late 2022 onward; outcome pricing as a business model.

**Why incumbents failed.** BPOs sell hours (automation cuts their revenue), and legacy contact-center software sells seats.

**Still open or crowded?** Customer service is crowded (Sierra, Decagon and others; roughly 100x ARR valuations signal froth). Other labor lines are less crowded but need domain depth.

**Transfer targets (hypotheses, need pool sizing).** Language-heavy, measurable-outcome, compliance-bounded back-office labor that is outsourced today and still staffed by people on phones and documents: insurance first-notice-of-loss and claims intake, healthcare denial appeals and prior-authorization work (the CMS API mandates give it a deadline), freight brokerage carrier calls and check-calls, property-management leasing and maintenance dispatch, collections and KYC operations. ### Pattern 11 (anti-pattern). False dawn: capital arrives before the paying customer

**Mechanism.** A claimed unlock (new models, new hardware) produces huge funding rounds, but customers are still piloting. Founders and press describe the pilot as demand. This pattern explains why "no revenue number" should lower the score.

**Evidence (strength: SECONDARY).**
- Humanoids and robot AI: Counterpoint says global humanoid sales revenue first exceeded $500M in 2025, with the top three firms about 56% of it and Unitree (China) about CNY 879M (about $129M) in the first nine months; Figure's 11-month BMW Spartanburg deployment is described by others as a pilot; Agility Robotics put 7 Digits at Toyota Canada (Feb 2026) and moved over 100,000 totes at GXO; Apptronik's mature product is targeted for 2027. - Rethink Robotics (Baxter) closed Oct 2018 after the sale fell through; the CEO said "We got out a little early," while analysts blamed product-market mismatch and precision complaints. [PRESS] spectrum.ieee.org (Rethink) ; bostonglobe.com

**Shift that enabled it.** Claimed: foundation models for manipulation; hard evidence of purchase orders is thin.

**Why incumbents failed.** Not applicable; the question is whether anyone has paying volume yet.

**Still open or crowded?** Unknown; revenue evidence is under $1B across the whole sector.

**Transfer targets / how to use it.** A scoring rule for the round-2 gate: an "unlock" counts only if (1) there is a dated catalyst, (2) a named buyer is already paying today at scale in a close substitute, and (3) revenue at least in the tens of millions within about 24 months of the catalyst. Score pilots, press releases, valuations and "orders" as zero.

## 3. Cross-cutting lessons for generating big ideas

1. **Hunt dated unlocks, not dead ideas.** Look for a regulator action, rule deadline, court decision, price crossing, or contract vehicle with a date, then ask which large pool it opens. The old dead startup is only a hint that demand exists.
2. **Prefer unlocks where the payer is named and already spends**: hyperscaler capex, Medicare codes, existing outsourced labor budgets, defense OTAs. Avoid those that depend on consumers adopting a new behavior unprompted.
3. **Beware unlocks owned by a cost-curve owner** (Starlink, Chinese cells, Meta). A small team wins by sitting downstream (integration, services, data, origination) not by competing upstream.
4. **Revenue lags unlock by 2-4 years.** Aurora and Fervo show it. Pair any early pattern with a service or software wedge that earns while the core ramps.
5. **Check the legal premise twice** for the regulator-yes class: sports contracts are about 80% of Kalshi volume and states are suing; the Part 108 rule is still unpublished; mandates may be unenforced (Australia) or paid by nobody (Medicare codes with low claims).
6. **Treat funding rounds and valuations as noise.** Persona, Decagon, Zipline, humanoid startups and Fervo all show valuations well ahead of disclosed revenue.

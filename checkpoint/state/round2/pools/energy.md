## Pool 1: US data centers' spend on onsite / behind-the-meter (BTM) power

- **Geography:** United States (global figure shown for reference).
- **Size:** ~$9B/yr US, DERIVED. Enverus forecasts 22.5 GW of BTM generation for data centers in 2026-2030, i.e. ~4.5 GW/yr; at an assumed blended installed cost of $1.5-2.5M/MW (reciprocating engines at the low end, turbines and fuel cells at the high end; my assumption) that is $7-11B/yr. Global market per MarketsandMarkets (paid report): ~$18B in 2026 rising to $87B by 2032; Baker Hughes: >$25B BTM/off-grid opportunity by 2030. Sources: https://www.enverus.com/newsroom/behind-the-meter-generation-forecast-skipping-the-queue/ ; https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/data-center-onsite-behind-the-meter-power-market-129738654.html ; https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/gigawatt-era-ai-power-demand-reshaping-turbomachinery-industry
- **Reality check:** Cleanview counts ~90 GW of BTM in the pipeline but only ~2 GW operating in mid-2026 (mostly xAI Memphis); cumulative capacity could be only 5 GW by end-2027 (13 GW if tenant-backed projects hold). So the pool is large on paper and slow in practice. https://www.cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers
- **Money flow:** hyperscalers, neoclouds and colos (or developers like Crusoe on their behalf) pay engine/turbine/fuel-cell OEMs, EPC and electrical contractors, gas suppliers and pipeline-lateral builders, and increasingly "power-as-a-service" owners who finance and operate the plant (Bloom/Brookfield model). Utilities lose the load or the rate-base growth.
- **Shock (2026-07):** GE Vernova's gas backlog plus slot reservations reached 116 GW at Q2 2026 with 2031 delivery slots being sold, and Caterpillar's power-generation segment grew 72% on data-center demand (per MarketsandMarkets), so buyers who cannot wait for heavy-duty turbines or a utility substation go onsite. FERC did not issue a final large-load rule by DOE's 2026-04-30 request and instead opened six FPA 206 show-cause proceedings on 2026-06-18 (I could not confirm more than that from the summaries). Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000147/gevpressrelease2q26.htm ; https://www.jdsupra.com/topics/rulemaking-process/data-centers/interconnections/ ; https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/11/potential-game-changing-rulemaking-for-data-centers
- **Incumbents:** Caterpillar, Cummins, Wartsila, GE Vernova (aeroderivatives), Siemens Energy, Solar Turbines, Bloom Energy; EPC firms; gas midstream. Bloom's Brookfield financing framework was expanded to $25B in June 2026 (a framework, not booked revenue). https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/13/bloom-energy-expand-ai-power-fuel-cell-stock/
- **Why fragile:** OEMs are supply-capped, not demand-capped, so they cannot absorb more orders; utilities cannot deliver power on a data-center timeline. The scarce things are permits, engineering hours, commissioning crews and fuel supply, not the money.
- **Funded startups already here:** Crusoe (Series E $1.375B Oct 2025; Series F ~$3B+ at ~$30B valuation, reported closed Sept 2026), Bloom Energy (public), ProEnergy (private; repurposed CF6 jet-engine cores, 21 turbines sold for >1 GW of data-center bridging power), Emerald AI ($150M Series A, 2026-08-25; makes data centers flexible loads), DG Matrix ($60M Series A, Feb 2026; ~90% of its pipeline is data centers). VoltaGrid is a named private provider but I could not verify its funding. Sources: https://theenergymag.com/news/2026-07-06/crusoe-3-billion-raise-ai-data-center ; https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/proenergy-offers-repurposed-jet-engines-to-data-cent/ ; https://www.esgtoday.com/?p=25347
- **Crowdedness:** CROWDED at the equipment/power-as-a-service layer. Open at the permitting, commissioning and controls layers.
- **Legal premise to check before building:** air permitting. EPA reportedly found (Jan 2026, secondary report) that xAI's "temporary/portable" Memphis turbines are not exempt from air rules; the Shelby County permit for 15 turbines expires 2027-01-02. Texas SB 6 (signed 2025-06-20) requires large loads (>=75 MW) to disclose backup generation and accept emergency curtailment. https://www.bracewell.com/resources/texas-senate-bill-6-ushers-in-major-overhaul-of-large-load-interconnection-and-grid-access-rules/
- **Wedge hints:** air-permit and emissions-compliance engineering for onsite turbines/engines; commissioning and controls integration (islanded microgrids with grid-forming batteries); gas supply and lateral procurement; bridging-power rental fleets; secondary market and refurbished-core brokerage; replacing diesel backup under tightening air limits.
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** a permitting + commissioning + controls business needs ~300 campuses x $300k = ~$90M; reaching $100M+ most plausibly means owning assets (power-as-a-service) or financing, which is the crowded layer.
- **Insider roles:** data-center development VPs of power; utility large-load account managers; air-permit consultants; OEM dealer application engineers; interconnection consultants; midstream gas marketers.
- **Hidden giant:** no.

## Pool 2: US purchases of power and distribution transformers

- **Geography:** United States.
- **Size:** ~$14B/yr. MarketsandMarkets (paid): $13.76B in 2025 rising to $19.0B by 2030; Arizton: $16.5B (2024); GMI: $12.2B (2024). Narrow-definition outliers ($0.7-3.4B) exist; I used the broad definition. Cross-check: EEI says transmission capex is $39.9B (2025 projected) and distribution capex was $50.9B in 2023, so $14B is plausibly 10-15% of grid capex. Sources: https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/transformer-market/us ; https://www.foley.com/?p=125261
- **Money flow:** utilities (close to half of distribution-transformer demand in one global forecast), IPPs/developers, data centers and industrials pay manufacturers and distributors; imports are ~80% of power-transformer and ~50% of distribution-transformer supply (Wood Mackenzie).
- **Shock (2025-08):** Section 232 tariffs took effect 2025-08-18 (50% on the non-US steel/aluminum content of transformers); Wood Mackenzie projected 2025 supply deficits of 30% (power) and 10% (distribution); generator step-up lead times passed 160 weeks by Q1 2026; DOE cites >30 months for distribution units; ~40M US distribution transformers are past expected service life; only one US grain-oriented steel producer (Cleveland-Cliffs). Sources: https://tedmag.com/trump-administration-increases-tariffs-on-certain-transformers/ ; https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/power-transformers-and-distribution-transformers-will-face-supply-deficits-of-30-and-10-in-2025/ ; https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/
- **Incumbents:** Hitachi Energy (>$1B US investment announced 2025-09-04; $457M Virginia large-power-transformer plant broke ground June 2026, operating 2H 2028), Siemens Energy, GE Vernova (closed its $5.3B buyout of Prolec GE on 2026-02-02; Prolec 2025 revenue ~ $3B), Eaton, Hubbell and regional makers. Sources: https://www.hitachienergy.com/jp/ja/news-and-events/press-releases/2026/06/hitachi-energy-breaks-ground-on-the-nation-s-largest-facility-for-the-production-of-large-power-transformers-in-south-boston-virginia ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000064/gev-20260331.htm
- **Why fragile:** new capacity does not arrive until 2027-2028; steel is single-sourced; every utility has custom specs, which blocks standard-unit mass production; utilities hold their own spares in silos. Manufacturers have cyclical-capex scars and no reason to over-build.
- **Funded startups already here:** Heron Power ($140M Series B, Feb 2026; a16z American Dynamism + Breakthrough; $38M Series A in 2025; 40 GW plant, production 2H 2027), DG Matrix ($60M Series A, Feb 2026), Amperesand ($80M Series A, late 2025), Hyperscale Power (EUR 5M seed, Mar 2026). All are solid-state-transformer hardware. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/18/heron-power-raises-140m-to-ramp-production-of-grid-altering-tech/ ; https://transformers-magazine.com/tm-news/amperesand-secures-80-m-for-medium-voltage-sst-rollout/
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED for new-build hardware (well funded, 2027+ revenue). Nothing funded found for repair/refurbishment, spares pooling or specification standardization (I searched solid-state and general terms only; treat as "none found", not "none exist").
- **Wedge hints:** transformer repair/rewind and life extension for the ~40M units past service life; mutualized spares pool or group purchasing for ~2,000 public-power utilities and ~800+ co-ops (counts are approximate, from general knowledge, not verified here); standard-spec consortium to aggregate orders into factory slots; condition-monitoring analytics; certified used-transformer marketplace with test data; tariff/import-compliance brokerage.
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** a consortium that brokers ~$2-3B/yr of co-op and muni purchases at a 3-4% fee is $60-120M; repair/refurb economics depend on rewind-shop capacity, which is the hard part.
- **Insider roles:** utility transformer asset managers; co-op and muni procurement directors; repair-shop owners; distributor reps (Graybar/Wesco type); transformer test labs; NRECA/APPA purchasing staff.
- **Hidden giant:** no (heavily covered), though repair/spares is overlooked.

## Pool 3: US utilities' vegetation management (line clearance)

- **Geography:** United States.
- **Size:** ~$7.5B/yr. Power Magazine (post-LA fires, Jan 2025): ~$7B/yr; Harris Williams (2022 report) via Middle Market Growth: ~$8B/yr, often the largest line item in utility O&M. Cross-check: Davey Tree's utility segment grew $54.2M (+5.4%) in 2025, implying ~ $1.06B (DERIVED); Asplundh is private (Wikipedia: ~ $4.7B revenue in 2021, single-source, low confidence). Sources: https://www.powermag.com/after-the-l-a-wildfires-why-vegetation-management-cant-afford-to-stay-on-a-fixed-cycle/ ; https://middlemarketgrowth.org/next-target-utility-vegetation-management-buzz/ ; https://www.davey.com/media/dhvn01qx/2025-annual-report.pdf
- **Money flow:** utilities pay line-clearance contractors (Asplundh, Davey, Wright Tree, Lewis, ACRT and others) by unit price, crew-hour or per-mile, recovered from ratepayers as O&M; inspection and LiDAR vendors sit on top.
- **Shock (2025-09):** wildfire liability is now existential and visible: Xcel agreed to pay ~$640M for the Marshall Fire (2025-09-24); SCE booked $1.6B of Eaton Fire losses by 2026-06-30 and exhausted self-insurance on 2026-02-11 (an outside Jefferies estimate puts exposure at $13.5B); PacifiCorp was found grossly negligent in Oregon's 2020 fires. California SB 254 (Sept 2025) requires cost-per-avoided-ignition reporting, which pushes utilities off fixed-cycle trimming toward risk-based trimming. Sources: https://www.news4jax.com/business/2025/09/24/xcel-energy-will-pay-640m-to-settle-claims-from-denver-area-wildfire-that-burned-1000-structures/ ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000827052/000082705226000059/eix-20260630.htm ; https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/california-enacts-new-electric-transmission-financing-programs-and-adds-to-its-wildfire-fund
- **Incumbents:** Asplundh (private), Davey Tree (public), Wright Tree, Lewis Tree, ACRT, plus engineering/LiDAR firms. They sell crew-hours, so analytics that cut crew-hours threaten revenue (business-model conflict); they have little software culture, and utility foresters often manage work in spreadsheets and GIS exports.
- **Why fragile:** spend verification is weak (who trimmed what, to which clearance, at what price) and cycle-based schedules are indefensible after Marshall/Eaton; regulators are asking for cost-effectiveness proof the incumbents' data cannot supply.
- **Funded startups already here:** Overstory ($43M Series B, Nov 2025, Blume-led with Energy Impact Partners, B Capital; $14M Series A in 2023), adjacent: Pano AI (camera detection, $44M Series B, total $89M, 15 utilities incl. Xcel) and Gridware ($55M Series B, 2025-11-17; $26.4M Series A, Jan 2025, Sequoia). Sources: https://www.overstory.com/blog/series-b-announcement ; https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/smart-grids/15-utilities-are-already-using-an-ai-powered-wildfire-detection-startup-that-just-raised-another-44m/ ; https://citris-uc.org/gridware-raises-55m-expands-to-uk/
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED. One well-funded satellite/AI risk-analytics pure play (Overstory) plus adjacent sensors. Nobody found funded on work verification, invoice audit, crew marketplace or small-utility foresters.
- **Wedge hints:** work-verification and unit-price invoice audit for utility VM spend; crew scheduling and subcontractor marketplace, with storm-surge staffing; hazard-tree identification for the highest-risk circuits; herbicide-program verification; co-ops and munis that have no staff forester.
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** audit/verification software at 1-2% of a $7.5B pool is $75-150M only if it reaches most large IOUs; a crew marketplace taking 5% of subcontract flow is a larger but operationally harder path.
- **Insider roles:** utility vegetation managers and ISA-certified utility arborists; line-clearance general foremen; contractor estimators; wildfire-plan regulatory staff; forestry GIS analysts; insurance risk engineers.
- **Hidden giant:** YES.

## Pool 4: US utility wildfire mitigation programs (undergrounding, covered conductor, sensing, inspection, PSPS)

- **Geography:** United States, California-led.
- **Size:** ~$9B/yr, DERIVED. PG&E alone: 2025 wildfire mitigation spending est. >$6B (PNNL, May 2025, citing PG&E). The two California IOUs' WMPs were reported to detail >$23B of expenditures through 2025 (about $7.7B/yr for 2023-25 combined, per a Buchalter title I could not open). Add Xcel ($1.9B proposed 2025-27 in Colorado alone), Berkshire Hathaway Energy (capex rising from $188M in 2022 to $797M in 2027), PacifiCorp, SDG&E and others to reach ~$9B. Capital is 55-86% of the spend. Sources: https://wildfire.pnnl.gov/mitigationPlans/content/analysis/Utility%20Investment%20in%20Wildfire%20Mitigation.pdf ; https://www.buchalter.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/PGE-and-SCEs-Wildfire-Mitigation-Plans-Detail-More-than-23-billion-in-Expenditures-through-2025.Hafez_-1.pdf
- **Money flow:** ratepayers fund utilities (capex into rate base); utilities pay EPC/line contractors, equipment makers (covered conductor, fast-trip protection), sensor/software vendors and vegetation contractors; the California Wildfire Fund backstops liability.
- **Shock (2025-09):** SB 254 (signed Sept 2025) adds an $18B Wildfire Fund continuation account (half ratepayer via an extended charge, half shareholders; PG&E 47.85%, SCE 47.85%, SDG&E 4.3%), bars $6B of wildfire-mitigation capex from earning a return (rate base), requires cost-per-avoided-ignition reporting and exempts undergrounding plan approvals from CEQA. Utilities lose return on part of the spend, so cheaper and provably effective measures win. Sources: https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/california-enacts-new-electric-transmission-financing-programs-and-adds-to-its-wildfire-fund ; https://www.nossaman.com/newsroom-insights-californias-ambitious-new-bill-overhauls-the-states-approach-to-wildfires
- **Incumbents:** the utilities themselves, EPC contractors (Quanta, MasTec), equipment OEMs, and engineering firms. They lack the data to compute avoided ignitions per dollar.
- **Why fragile:** regulation now demands a metric (cost per avoided ignition) that no one has a standard methodology for; return on $6B of spend was removed, which flips incentives toward cost-effective options.
- **Funded startups already here:** Pano AI, Gridware, Overstory (above); others exist in fire modeling but I did not verify them. Crowdedness at detection/sensing is high.
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED to CROWDED for detection and sensing; OPEN for ignition-risk economics, WMP compliance analytics and PSPS logistics.
- **Legal premise to check:** inverse condemnation (California) and state wildfire-fund rules decide who bears losses; an August 2026 tentative ruling declined to hold SCE liable without a trial (trial set for 2027), so liability rules can still move. https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2026/08/12/881157.htm
- **Wedge hints:** cost-per-avoided-ignition analytics and WMP reporting; PSPS customer notification plus medical-baseline backup-power logistics; inspection QA (drone/ground); covered-conductor install productivity; wildfire plans for Western co-ops and munis that must now file them.
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** analytics/compliance software at 1% of $9B is only $90M; services plus logistics (PSPS backup power) can extend it; it is hard to reach $100M without a hardware or services layer.
- **Insider roles:** utility wildfire-mitigation program managers; PSPS incident commanders; fire-weather meteorologists; state energy-safety regulators; insurance brokers; covered-conductor crew leads.
- **Hidden giant:** no.

## Pool 5: PJM capacity payments passed through to customers

- **Geography:** PJM region (13 states plus DC).
- **Size:** $16.4B for the 2028/29 delivery year (cleared at the $325/MW-day cap; 138,318 MW procured). Without the cap PJM estimated ~$555/MW-day, i.e. ~ $30B. The independent market monitor attributes $6.3B (38%) to data centers. Source: https://environenergy.com/2026/08/pjm-capacity-prices-2026/ ; https://www.industrialinfo.com/iirenergy/industry-news/article/report-data-centers-create-about-us63-billion-in-electric-costs-for-pjm-customers--360334
- **Money flow:** generators and demand-response (DR) providers are paid at the clearing price; load-serving entities (utilities and retail suppliers) charge customers. For large customers the share is set by their peak load contribution, which (from my knowledge of PJM rules, not re-verified here) is based on their load at the system's summer coincident peaks, so a customer that curtails at the right hours cuts its capacity charge.
- **Shock (2026-07):** auction results released 2026-07-14: third straight year at the cap, 6,831 MW short of PJM's reliability target; the collar (cap $325, floor $175) was extended through the December 2026 auction for 2029/30; PJM planned to ask FERC in September for a backstop procurement (outcome not confirmed). Sources as above; https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/PJM-Auction-Comes-Up-68-Gigawatts-Short-As-Data-Centers-Devour-Power.amp.html
- **Incumbents:** DR aggregators (CPower and others), retail energy suppliers, utilities, energy consultants/brokers paid on commodity commissions. Their DR products are mostly emergency-only, legacy-contract based; brokers earn on volume, not on demand reduction.
- **Why fragile:** capacity prices at the cap make every megawatt of verified flexibility worth ~$118k/yr (325 x 365; DERIVED), yet most mid-size customers (1-20 MW) do not manage their peak at all, and market rules on DR performance and ELCC derating keep shifting, which punishes incumbent aggregators with fixed portfolios.
- **Funded startups already here:** Voltus ($225M Series D, 2026, Vitol; claims 8.1 GW of flexible capacity managed, a 100 MW Google deal in PJM, acquired Brightfield AI in June 2026), Emerald AI ($150M Series A at $1.05B, 2026-08-25), others I could not verify (EnergyHub, Renew Home, Base Power). Sources: https://www.energy-storage.news/voltus-raises-us225-million-in-series-d-funding-round-to-expand-vpp-service-in-us-and-canada/ ; https://www.esgtoday.com/?p=25347
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED, with large funded players at the data-center and utility-scale ends; mid-market C&I peak management looks less served but I did not find a direct count.
- **Wedge hints:** mid-market C&I peak-tag management with batteries and controls; coincident-peak prediction and alerts; capacity-pass-through audits for large customers on supplier contracts; school, hospital and muni aggregation; data-center flexible-load contracting.
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** if 5,000 PJM customers each avoid $50-100k of annual capacity cost and the vendor keeps 25-30%, that is $60-150M; needs software plus storage financing.
- **Insider roles:** C&I energy managers; retail-supplier pricing desks; PJM market-monitor analysts; DR dispatch operators; utility rate analysts; data-center power procurement leads.
- **Hidden giant:** no.

## Pool 6: Gas-turbine fleet services and aftermarket (maintenance, repair, parts, upgrades)

- **Geography:** Global (the US share is ~$4.5B per GMI), with the US fleet central to the shock.
- **Size:** ~$30B/yr global in 2026. GMI (paid): $27.1B in 2025, $30.2B in 2026, US $4.5B; other paid reports range $10-25B depending on scope (Future Market Report $10.25B; MarkNtel all-turbine services $25.3B, gas ~46%). Cross-check from filings: GE Vernova's Power services revenue was $13.07B in 2025 (gas, steam, nuclear and hydro together; not split), against $6.42B equipment. Sources: https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/gas-turbine-service-market ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996810/000199681026000015/gev-20251231.htm
- **Money flow:** plant owners (utilities, IPPs, industrials, data-center BTM operators) pay OEMs through long-term service agreements (LTSAs), parts and outage services; independents (ISPs) and repair shops take a minority (top 5 firms hold ~39% of global services per MarkNtel).
- **Shock (2026-07):** GE Vernova's gas equipment backlog plus slot reservations rose from 100 to 116 GW in Q2 2026 (target >=125 GW by year-end), it is selling 2031 slots, and output is 20 GW/yr rising to 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030. New heavy-duty units are unavailable for years, so owners must keep old frames running, defer retirements and lengthen overhaul cycles; BTM data-center adds enlarge the fleet needing service. Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000147/gevpressrelease2q26.htm ; https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-climbs-to-116-gw/826039/
- **Incumbents:** GE Vernova, Siemens Energy (Gas Services ~EUR 1.6B in FY2025, unconfirmed whether revenue or profit), Mitsubishi, Solar/Baker Hughes, Ansaldo; a thin layer of ISPs.
- **Why fragile:** OEMs prioritize scarce factory and field-service capacity for new equipment, and they lock in hot-gas-path parts via proprietary designs; outage labor is short; parts lead times are long. Owners have little price transparency on LTSAs.
- **Funded startups already here:** none found focused on turbine MRO/parts after two broad searches. Adjacent: ProEnergy (private) selling repurposed CF6 cores. Treat as OPEN but confirm with a named-company check before building.
- **Crowdedness:** OPEN.
- **Wedge hints:** LTSA and parts-price benchmarking and audit for owners; condition-based outage planning (borescope AI, digital twins); a certified marketplace for surplus frames, aeroderivatives and spare parts including slot transfers; outage-technician staffing and credentialing; inventory pooling of rotables among IPPs and co-ops; independent hot-gas-path repair (capital intensive).
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** benchmarking/audit at 1% of a ~$20B accessible pool is $200M, but a realistic first-3-years capture is a fraction; marketplace/brokerage on surplus equipment (a few $B/yr of trades x 3-5% fee) is $100M+ only if turbine scarcity persists.
- **Insider roles:** plant O&M directors at IPPs/utilities; outage managers; combustion engineers; LTSA contract managers; ex-OEM field service reps; ISP shop owners; insurer engineering surveyors.
- **Hidden giant:** YES.

## Pool 7: US oil and gas operators' produced-water gathering, disposal, recycling and treatment

- **Geography:** United States (Permian is ~21 MMbbl/d).
- **Size:** ~$12B/yr, DERIVED. Rystad's 2022 outlook put US onshore produced water at 22.9B bbl in 2022 and 23.8B bbl in 2026; times a blended all-in cost of $0.40-0.65/bbl (my assumption; B3 Insights says Delaware-basin gathering plus disposal will reach $0.75-1.00/bbl; Aris reports $0.31-0.46/bbl margin on top of its own costs) gives ~$9-15B. Bluefield (Oct 2025) sizes the broader US midstream water market at $156B over 2025-2030 (~$26B/yr; Permian $101.8B), which includes freshwater sourcing and frac-water logistics. Sources: https://www.wateronline.com/doc/bracing-for-a-flood-us-shale-set-to-treat-less-produced-water-despite-earthquake-boom-0001 ; https://www.wateronline.com/doc/u-s-midstream-water-market-totals-us-b-from-anchored-by-surging-production-in-the-permian-basin-0001 ; https://pgjonline.com/news/2025/june/texas-tightens-wastewater-well-rules-raising-costs-for-oil-producers
- **Money flow:** E&P operators pay water midstream companies per barrel for pipeline gathering and saltwater-disposal (SWD) injection, and trucking firms for spot loads; a small and growing fraction goes to treatment and recycling.
- **Shock (2025-06):** the Texas Railroad Commission widened the area of review for new SWD permits from a quarter mile to half a mile (from 2025-06-01), added injection-pressure and volume limits tied to reservoir pressure, and barred deep injection below the top of the Strawn in the Gardendale seismic response area; at least one-third of Permian SWD wells have been losing injection capacity since 2016; B3 sees 20-30% cost increases in parts of the Delaware. In 2026, the escape valve (treated-water discharge) is still blocked: TCEQ has issued no discharge permits since accepting applications in 2024, and the early-2026 land-application rule drew ~450 comments with over two-thirds opposed (Aug 2026). Sources: https://pgjonline.com/news/2025/june/texas-tightens-wastewater-well-rules-raising-costs-for-oil-producers ; https://www.hppr.org/hppr-news/2026-08-24/pressure-mounts-to-allow-treated-oilfield-waste-in-texas-rivers-and-on-rangeland
- **Incumbents:** Aris Water, WaterBridge (raised $345M equity), Texas Pacific Water Resources, NGL, Select; many E&Ps run their own SWD systems. Business is acreage dedication plus fixed pipes and wells.
- **Why fragile:** disposal capacity is constrained by physics and regulation, not by capital; pipes and wells cannot be "software-updated"; treatment is energy-hungry (the Orla plant needs 5.5 MW for 10,000 bbl/d) and has no outlet permits. Wood Mackenzie projects $3-6 of water cost per barrel of oil over time.
- **Funded startups already here:** Axine Water Technologies ($5.6M Series A, Oct 2025), Sourcewater ($7.2M Series A, water data/market; date unverified), Clean Chemistry ($2.2M, date unverified), WaterTectonics + Clearvale's WT Oil & Gas (launched Oct 2025). All small compared with the infrastructure incumbents. Sources: https://www.wateronline.com/doc/watertectonics-clearvale-capital-launch-wt-oil-gas-delivering-high-volume-produced-water-recycling-and-reuse-across-the-permian-basin-0001
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED for treatment and infrastructure; OPEN for data, capacity brokerage and compliance software.
- **Legal premise to check:** induced-seismicity rules and who owns the produced water (Texas 2025 law moved land-application authority from RRC to TCEQ).
- **Wedge hints:** SWD pressure-front and seismic-risk analytics for operators and regulators; disposal-capacity and recycled-water marketplace; trucking dispatch and verification; SWD integrity monitoring; permitting service for reuse; offtake matching of treated water to data-center cooling or agriculture once discharge rules open.
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** a capacity marketplace/software layer taking 2-3% of a $12B pool is $240-360M at full share, but real traction needs operators to give up self-disposal; a data/compliance product alone is likely under $100M.
- **Insider roles:** E&P water and completions managers; water-midstream commercial managers; RRC/TCEQ permit consultants; SWD operators; hydrogeologists and seismologists; surface-use landmen; water-trucking dispatchers.
- **Hidden giant:** YES.

## Pool 8: US nuclear fleet operating, fuel and capital spend (and the supplier ecosystem behind it)

- **Geography:** United States (94 reactors, ~97 GW).
- **Size:** ~$26B/yr, DERIVED. 816 TWh generated in 2024 (World Nuclear Association) x $31.76/MWh total generating cost (NEI/EUCG 2023: fuel $5.32, capital $7.06, operating $19.38 per MWh) = ~$25.9B. (Operating cost, the largest piece, is ~ $15.8B of that.) Sources: https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/country-profiles/countries-t-z/usa-nuclear-power ; https://www.nei.org/getContentAsset/47fa8caa-9b0d-4029-932c-07f902e82f4f/8d8ff8d6-b2ae-401b-a63c-f6b108e809d2/2024-Costs-in-Context-final.pdf
- **Money flow:** ~two dozen owner-operators (Constellation, Vistra, Duke, Southern, Dominion, Entergy and others) pay fuel suppliers, O&M labor, outage-services firms (Westinghouse, Framatome, BWXT, Curtiss-Wright, Jacobs, Bechtel and specialists), engineers (Sargent & Lundy is ENR's #1 nuclear-plant design firm), compliance and NRC fees. Hyperscaler PPAs now fund uprates and restarts.
- **Shock (2025-05):** Executive Order 14300 (2025-05-23) sets 18-month limits for new-reactor decisions and 12-month limits for license renewals, with NRC final rules due ~November 2026 and an NRC reorganization. Follow-through: NRC approved TVA's Clinch River BWRX-300 construction permit on 2026-09-29 (14-month review), TerraPower's Natrium permit in March 2026, four DOE pilot test reactors reached criticality by 2026-07-04, and Palisades began fuel loading (~2026-08-31) after repeated slips. Sources: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/new-executive-orders-aim-to-accelerate-and-expand-development-of-us-nuclear-energy ; https://www.powermag.com/nrc-launches-major-reorganization-as-licensing-deadlines-and-reform-workload-intensify/ ; https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/news-releases/2026/26-079 ; https://www.ans.org/news/2026-07-08/article-8187/palisades-restart-projects-holtec-ipo-lawsuit-dismissal-but-no-restart-date/
- **Incumbents:** owner-operators' in-house staff plus the OEM/outage-services oligopoly; documentation, quality assurance (NQA-1) and condition-report workflows are manual.
- **Why fragile:** the workforce is aging; regulatory documentation (licensing responses, 10 CFR 50.59 screenings, condition reports) is mostly hand-written; very few shops hold nuclear quality certifications, so a new-build and uprate wave hits a supplier base that cannot scale; commercial-grade dedication is slow and expensive.
- **Funded startups already here:** Nuclearn ($10.5M Series A, Sept 2025, Blue Bear; claims >65 reactors; per-reactor annual subscription) and Atomic Canyon ($7M seed, May 2025, EIP; Diablo Canyon customer). Both target documentation/AI search, not supplier qualification, staffing or outage services. Reactor developers (Valar, Aalo, Antares, Deployable Energy) are funded but do not attack fleet spend. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/09/nuclearn-gets-10-5m-to-help-the-nuclear-industry-embrace-ai ; https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20250612-atomic-canyon-closes--7-million-seed-financing-round
- **Crowdedness:** OPEN overall (two small startups against $26B); CONTESTED only for AI documentation.
- **Legal premise to check:** NRC and NQA-1 requirements determine what can be automated or substituted; quality-assurance sign-offs cannot be waived by software.
- **Wedge hints:** nuclear supplier qualification and commercial-grade dedication marketplace; outage workforce staffing and credentialing (radiation-worker portability); counterfeit/suspect-item detection; procurement of nuclear-grade valves and pumps; uprate engineering at scale; restart/decommissioning services; licensing documentation (contested).
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** if outsourced services and supplies are ~40% of the $26B pool (~$10B), a staffing/qualification marketplace taking 5% is ~$500M at full share; first-$100M needs ~20% of that, so it is a stretch without a standard or certification role.
- **Insider roles:** plant licensing engineers; outage managers; procurement and commercial-grade dedication engineers; NQA-1 lead auditors; radiation protection technicians; fuel reload engineers; former NRC resident inspectors.
- **Hidden giant:** YES.

## Pool 9: Utilities' and developers' outsourced T&D engineering and line-construction contracts

- **Geography:** United States.
- **Size:** >= $31B/yr. Quanta Services' Electric segment alone booked $7.84B revenue in Q2 2026 (+44% y/y), ~ $31B annualized (DERIVED), and that excludes MasTec, MYR, Primoris, Henkels & McCoy, Asplundh's utility work and engineering firms. The payer budget is large: EEI projects $208B investor-owned-utility capex in 2025 (>$1.1T for 2025-2029), with transmission at $39.9B (2025) and distribution at $50.9B (2023). - **Incumbents:** Quanta, MasTec, MYR; Burns & McDonnell, Sargent & Lundy, Black & Veatch. They are capacity-limited by hiring, bill by the hour or unit, and design work still runs on utility-specific standards and desktop tools; automation reduces billable hours (business-model conflict).

- **Funded startups already here:** Piq Energy ($5M seed, 2026-07-08; automates interconnection/grid-planning studies), Pearl Street Technologies (acquired by Enverus, Mar 2025), envelio (German; Eversource uses it). Sources: https://kurrant.com/kurrantly-news/piq-energy-raises-5-million-seed-to-accelerate-grid-interconnection-studies/ ; https://solarquarter.com/2025/03/15/enverus-acquires-pearl-street-technologies-to-enhance-grid-reliability-and-resilience/
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED for interconnection-study automation; OPEN for distribution design, estimating, crew productivity and workforce.
- **Wedge hints:** distribution design automation for new-load service requests on utility GIS; estimating and bid tools for line contractors; crew scheduling, materials staging and yard logistics; mutual-assistance storm staffing marketplace; lineworker apprenticeship and credentialing pipeline (a network/standard asset); outsourced "engineer-in-a-box" for ~2,000 public-power utilities and ~800+ co-ops (approximate counts, unverified).
- **Bottom-up sketch (assumption):** design-automation plus engineering-as-a-service for 200 mid-size utilities at $500k each is $100M; a workforce marketplace taking 3% of $30B+ is ~$1B at full share but is a labor business with thin margins.
- **Insider roles:** utility distribution planners and designers; line-construction superintendents; contractor estimators; T&D engineering managers; storm-response coordinators; IBEW/NECA training directors; project-controls managers.
- **Hidden giant:** YES.

## Cross-pool notes (for the idea generators)

1. The same shock (load growth plus scarce equipment plus wildfire liability) shows up in every pool; the best wedges sit where a scarce resource (permits, qualified labor, slots, spares, certified suppliers, verified data) can be matched to a payer with a mandate.
2. Hidden giants where I found no or only tiny funded competition: gas-turbine aftermarket (pool 6), nuclear supplier/outage ecosystem (pool 8), vegetation-management audit/verification (pool 3), distribution design and crew productivity (pool 9).
3. Pools where "a generic AI copilot" is the only obvious play and should be avoided: nuclear documentation (already served by Nuclearn/Atomic Canyon), interconnection-study automation (Piq/Enverus).
4. Biggest verification gaps: no sourced engineering-share of T&D spend; US-only gas-turbine services figure comes from one paid report; produced-water spend is derived; nuclear spend is derived from 2023 cost data and 2024 generation; VoltaGrid and several DR competitors' funding were not verified; FERC's 2026-06-18 action was confirmed only as show-cause orders.

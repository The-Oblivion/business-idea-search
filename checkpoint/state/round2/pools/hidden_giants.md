# Round 2 pool map: HIDDEN GIANTS (boring, huge, ignored by tech founders)

Date of research: 2026-10-14/15. Method: ~45 searches and fetches (SEC filings, DOL, Federal Register, FCC, company releases, trade press). Anything marked **[unverified]** came from one secondary source or my background knowledge and should be re-checked before it carries weight.

Ranking by "how true is the shock + how fragile is the incumbent + how open is the field" (my read, not a score):

1. Uniform and linen rental (Cintas/UniFirst merger) - open, strong shock
2. Fire and life-safety ITM + POTS copper cutover - contested but the mandate is real
3. Community-association (condo/HOA) structural reserves, Florida first - finance layer open
4. Elevator/escalator service - open in US, demand surge, OEM labor squeeze
5. School transportation - contested, funding shock plus PE ownership churn
6. H-2A farm labor - contested by Seso, rule instability
7. Pest control - contested, Rentokil stumbling
8. Fines/fees and automated enforcement - ethically sensitive, vendor share well under pool size
9. Church giving stack - crowded, weak shock
10. Vending / unattended retail - crowded, weakest shock (included for completeness)

## 1. US uniform, linen and workwear rental (incl. healthcare linen)  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** ~1.5M+ US businesses (restaurants, auto shops, hospitals, factories, hotels) pay route-based rental companies weekly/monthly for uniforms, mats, towels, linen, restroom and first-aid supplies, usually under 3-5 year evergreen contracts with loss/damage charges and annual price escalators.

**Size:** TRSA/Oxford Economics put industry economic output at **$27.7B in 2024** (jobs 304,000). A narrower First Research definition gives ~$14B. Bottom-up check: Cintas Uniform Rental and Facility Services alone was **$8.62B** in fiscal 2026 (year to 2026-05-31); Vestis runs ~$2.6-2.7B (quarterly $659-662M); UniFirst ~$2.4B [unverified, from memory]. Top 3 are therefore ~$13.7B before Alsco, Elis, healthcare linen specialists and regional laundries.
- TRSA: https://www.trsa.org/news/new-data-highlights-the-industrys-economic-impact-employment-numbers/
- First Research: https://www.bvresources.com/articles/first-research-us-industry-profile/linen-uniform-supply-services
- Cintas 10-K FY2026: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000072325426000028/ctas-20260531.htm

**Shock (verified in filing):** Cintas agreed on 2026-03-10 to buy UniFirst (~$5.5B EV; $155 cash + 0.7720 Cintas shares per UniFirst share; ~$375M claimed synergies; combined ~1.5M customers). FTC issued a Second Request 2026-06-11; both certified substantial compliance 2026-10-02; timing agreement bars closing before **2026-12-11**. Meanwhile Vestis (Aramark spin-off) is shrinking: fiscal Q3 2026 revenue -1.8% with pounds processed **-4.5%**.
- Cintas 10-Q (quarter to 2026-08-31): https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000072325426000047/ctas-20260831.htm
- Deal coverage: https://www.owensborotimes.com/news/2026/03/cintas-announces-5-5b-deal-to-acquire-unifirst-parent-company-of-owensboro-facility/
- Vestis Q3 FY26: https://ir.vestis.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001628280-26-055485/vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm

**Incumbents and fragility:** Cintas, UniFirst, Vestis, Alsco, Elis NA. Business is plant-and-route density: huge sunk capital in laundries and trucks, customer lock-in by evergreen contracts. Fragility: (a) the two largest "challengers to each other" are merging, so for 12-24 months a large share of the base faces integration, route consolidation and repricing; (b) Vestis is losing volume and cutting cost ($75M plan) rather than hunting customers; (c) FTC review limits how aggressively Cintas can act before close; (d) customers hate auto-renewal and damage charges but have no neutral price benchmark. Filing does not mention divestitures.

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** none found attacking US route-based uniform rental. Adjacent and tiny: bnbkit (Geneva, $1.6M, short-term-rental linen), Quick Smart Wash (India, $5.15M), White Plains Linen (NY, PE-backed by Surge, Aug 2025, amount inconsistent across sources [unverified]). **Crowdedness: open** (but asset-heavy; a software-only entrant has no route).

**Wedge hints:**
- Buyer-side contract audit/exit and re-bid service for SMB renters (auto-renew traps, loss charges, escalators), monetized as % of savings, then upgraded to a marketplace/broker taking supplier-paid commissions.
- Roll-up or franchise of regional laundries inheriting customers during the Cintas/UniFirst route consolidation; healthcare and hospitality linen is the stickiest sub-segment.
- Garment/linen RFID tracking and billing-dispute software sold to regional operators who cannot match Cintas's tech.
- Any divestiture package the FTC forces would be a sudden supply of routes to buy.

**Insider roles:** route sales reps and district managers (ex-Cintas/UniFirst), laundry plant GMs and engineers, TRSA member operators, hospital linen/EVS directors, restaurant-group procurement heads, antitrust counsel.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 1.5M customers x ~$7k/yr average = ~$10B+ billed; a buyer-side service taking 10% of savings on 20% of that is ~$100M only at heroic share; a supplier-paid marketplace at 5% commission needs ~$2B of GMV (<10% of the pool). Flag: needs both sides' trust.

## 2. Fire and life-safety inspection, testing, maintenance and monitoring (ITM) - POTS copper cutover  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** Every commercial building must have fire alarms, sprinklers, extinguishers and emergency lighting inspected on code-set cycles (NFPA 25/72 and local fire code). Owners pay licensed contractors annual inspection/testing/monitoring contracts plus repair; fire marshals (AHJs) enforce.

**Size:** IBISWorld puts US fire protection and security system installation contractors at **$22.1B** (2026 label; the page's own text says 2025, treat year as uncertain); fire protection consultants $8.1B. No clean US ITM-only figure exists. My estimate: recurring ITM + monitoring + code-driven repair is $8-12B of that, but this split is an assumption. Fragmentation: >118,000 privately owned potential acquisition targets (Grata, 2026).
- IBISWorld: https://img2.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/fire-security-alarm-installation-services/6486/
- Grata: https://aimagazine.com/globenewswire/3352141

**Shock:** FCC adopted the Network and Services Modernization order on **2026-03-26** (FCC 26-19, WC Dockets 25-208/25-209): eliminates network-change disclosure filings, applies a 31-day automatic-grant period to all discontinuance applications, grants blanket authority to grandfather legacy copper voice, and preempts state/local rules that slow retirement. AT&T stopped taking new copper orders in Oct 2025 and targets retiring most copper outside California by end-2029 (vendor trackers). Fire alarm communicators and elevator cab phones still riding POTS lines must be replaced with cellular/IP communicators, accepted by the AHJ. Caveat: the FCC fact sheet I read does not name fire alarms; the fire-alarm link comes from telecom-vendor sources (self-interested). Also confirm each carrier's wire-center dates.
- FCC draft text: https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-419252A1.txt
- Trade summary: https://www.isemag.com/core-legacy/copper-to-fiber-migration/article/55367545/fcc-adopts-rules-to-accelerate-shift-from-copper-to-next-gen-networks

**Incumbents and fragility:** Johnson Controls (Tyco), Siemens, Honeywell (panels), Convergint, Securitas, Pye-Barker (57 acquisitions in 2025; 9,000 staff; 47 states), Summit Companies (BlackRock), PE-owned regionals. Fragility: roll-ups are integrating dozens of acquired shops with different paper workflows; licensing is state-by-state; technicians (NICET-certified) are scarce; inspection reports are still PDFs/paper filed to AHJs; the POTS cutover is a one-time forced retrofit across millions of lines that no incumbent has a standard program for.
- Pye-Barker 2025: https://www.sdmmag.com/articles/105203-pye-barker-fire-and-safety-accelerates-growth-strategy-with-57-acquisitions-in-2025
- Lincoln International M&A note (2022): https://www.lincolninternational.com/perspectives/articles/ma-activity-in-fire-and-life-safety-services-continues-to-heat-up/

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** Ember Software (formerly Fire Inspect Software; $10M led by 8VC and Menlo Ventures, Jan 2025); Inspect Point ($28M Mainsail growth equity, Jan 2022); Life Safety Inspection Vault (reported $28.7M, last round Aug 2025 [unverified]); ServiceTrade and other field-service tools. **Crowdedness: contested** at the inspection-software layer; the managed POTS-replacement/communicator program for multi-site owners looks open.
- Ember: https://www.embersoftware.com/resources/fire-inspect-software-rebrands-ember-software-announces-10million-seed-round-investment
- Inspect Point: https://mainsailpartners.com/inspect-point-announces-28-million-equity-investment-from-mainsail-partners/
- LIV: https://dealroom.co/companies/life-safety-inspection-vault/

**Wedge hints:**
- "POTS cutover as a service" for multi-site owners (retail chains, school districts, REITs, hospitals): inventory every copper line (fire panel, elevator phone, alarm, fax, POS), replace with listed cellular communicators, file AHJ paperwork, then bill monitoring monthly. Recurring revenue attaches to every device.
- Inspection-report filing network to AHJs (compliance records become the data asset).
- Small-commercial sprinkler/extinguisher ITM in metros where roll-ups have not reached.

**Insider roles:** NICET-certified alarm technicians, fire marshals/plan reviewers, central-station operators, telecom managers at national retail chains, insurance loss-control engineers, fire-sprinkler contractors.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 2M cutover devices x $40/month monitoring ($480/yr) = ~$1B/yr recurring at full saturation; $100M = ~210k devices (10%) plus install fees. Per-line price evidence is vendor marketing ($39.95-45/month); no independent unit count - verify.

## 3. Community associations (condo/HOA): capital repair and reserve funding, Florida first  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** ~358,000 US community associations (74.2M residents) collect assessments from owners; boards (volunteers, advised by a manager) spend them on operations and capital reserves. Florida's post-Surfside law now forces structural reserve studies and funded reserves, producing special assessments, loans and unit-owner financing.

**Size:** CAI/Foundation: ~**$96B/yr** assessments (2019-20 edition) and **$26.6B/yr** contributed to reserves (2021-22 edition). Pool defined as the reserve/capital stream: **$26.6B**. Florida estimate: ~28,250 condo associations with 1.2M+ units [unverified newsletter]; no statewide dollar shortfall found - a sourcing gap, not a zero.
- CAI: https://www.caionline.org/PressReleases/Pages/2019StatisticalReview.aspx and https://www.caionline.org/new-housing-report-shows-73-9-million-americans-reside-in-homeowners-association-and-condominium-com

**Shock:** Florida HB 913 (signed May 2025, effective **2025-07-01**) extended the Structural Integrity Reserve Study deadline to **2025-12-31** for existing associations, raised the SIRS non-specified item threshold to $25,000, and lets associations fund reserves via special assessments, lines of credit or loans. The deadline has now passed; realtor sources say 2026 bills to soften the mandate did not pass [unverified]. Result: catch-up bills (example: Orlando building proposing up to $22,105 per owner; a vendor claims 74% of associations are <70% funded).
- Bill analysis: https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/913/Analyses/h0913c.COM.PDF
- Miami-Dade special-assessment loan program (62+ priority): https://www.nbcmiami.com/responds/rising-condo-costs-miami-dade-loan-program/
- Reserve gap reporting: https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/condo-associations-short-on-repairs-like-surfside-109428

**Incumbents and fragility:** Managers (FirstService Residential, Associa), software (Vantaca, AppFolio), reserve-study firms (Reserve Advisors, Association Reserves), law firms, regional banks with association-lending desks. Fragility: boards are untrained volunteers with personal-liability exposure (SIRS affidavit); association loans are unsecured except assessment lien rights; owners on fixed incomes cannot pay lump sums; the licensed-engineer bottleneck delays studies.

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** Vantaca (community-association software; **$300M+ at $1.25B valuation, Oct 2025**, Cove Hill-led, JMI). I found **no** funded startup doing special-assessment financing, which is the gap (could be hidden incumbents: regional banks and Miami-Dade program). **Crowdedness: contested** (software saturated; finance layer open).
- Vantaca: https://pulse2.com/vantaca-300-million-raised-at-1-25-billion-valuation-for-community-association-management

**Wedge hints:** unit-owner special-assessment financing (installment loans paid through association billing, lender secured by the assessment lien right); association-level credit marketplace; SIRS/milestone-inspection and repair project coordination for boards; insurance premium finance; extend to every state that adopts reserve mandates.

**Insider roles:** community association managers (CAMs), Florida condo attorneys, structural engineers doing milestone inspections, association-banking officers, board treasurers, insurance brokers.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 1.2M FL units x avg special assessment $10k x 10% financed per year = $1.2B originations; 5-8% net revenue (spread + fees) = $60-100M. Needs credit performance proof and a funding source; lien-rights legal premise must be checked per state.

## 4. US elevator, escalator and moving-walk maintenance, repair and modernization  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** Building owners (CRE, condos, hospitals, hotels, transit) pay elevator companies monthly maintenance contracts ($3.5-5k/unit/yr, per an NEIA-attributed aggregator [unverified]) plus repair and modernization projects. Four OEMs (Otis, Schindler, KONE, TK Elevator) service most units; independents handle the rest. Licensing and union labor (IUEC) restrict entry.

**Size:** Installed base ~1.3M elevators/escalators in North America [unverified secondary]; US maintenance market estimates $5.6B (2023) and US installation+service $5.7B (2024). Adding repair and modernization (the fastest-growing lines) gives my estimate of **~$8B** for the US service stream. Cross-check: Otis alone booked **$4,997M** of Service sales in H1 2026 globally ($2,580M in Q2).
- Otis Q2 2026: https://s203.q4cdn.com/227649559/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2Q26-Otis-Earnings-Release.pdf
- PS Market Research: https://www.psmarketresearch.com/market-analysis/us-elevator-installation-service-market-report

**Shock:** Otis Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-22): organic Service +9% (matching the post-spin high), modernization backlog +24% (+26% cc), double-digit repair growth, but Service margin fell to 23.2% on labor/material cost and Otis cut its profit outlook; spending $50M on service quality. Otis took majority control of WeMaintain on **2026-04-13**, buying the leading venture-backed digital-maintenance challenger. Also: FCC copper order (see pool 2) hits elevator cab phones. I found no elevator right-to-repair bill in 2026 (CT SB 369 is about owner duties, not OEM lockout).
- WeMaintain/Otis: https://memoori.com/otis-majority-stake-wemaintain-connected-elevator-2026/

**Incumbents and fragility:** OEM service arms are capacity-constrained: aging installed base creates repair/modernization backlog but mechanics (5-yr apprenticeships, licensing) are the bottleneck; margins compress as they ramp; owners complain about service quality; proprietary controllers and diagnostics lock customers in but also invite independents. 

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** WeMaintain (Paris; ~$40M raised; now Otis-controlled), Mainteny (Berlin, seed), Uptime (France, EUR 7M), Datahoist (Austin, tiny). **No US venture-funded independent elevator service company or roll-up found.** **Crowdedness: open in the US** (the Otis deal signals the incumbents are watching).

**Wedge hints:** independent service roll-up in secondary metros with a software-led dispatch/parts layer; owner-side contract audit and benchmarking for CRE portfolios (OEM contracts are opaque); modernization financing and project management for condo boards (links to pool 3); cab-phone cellular conversion programs (links to pool 2); legacy-controller parts brokerage.

**Insider roles:** IUEC elevator mechanics, QEI-certified inspectors, state elevator inspection supervisors, CRE building engineers/property managers, parts distributors, elevator consultants.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 20,000 units x $5k maintenance = $100M. That is ~1.5-2% of US units; a roll-up needs ~100-200 mechanics (IUEC labor access is the constraint). Heroic part: mechanic supply, not share.

## 5. US K-12 student transportation (district spend; contractor-run share)  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** ~13k school districts pay for yellow-bus transport (own fleets or contractors on fixed-price multi-year contracts), plus special-ed and homeless-student transport (often by van/rideshare). State aid covers 30-40%; local districts pay 60-70%.

**Size:** national spend **>$28B/yr** (Getting Smart, Feb 2026; EdChoice pre-COVID >$25B). ~25-26M students ride daily. Contractors own/operate ~1/3 of buses (NSTA estimate; other counts say 6.5-35% depending on definition).
- https://www.gettingsmart.com/2026/02/26/the-need-for-investment-in-human-infrastructure-transportation/
- https://www.schoolbusfleet.com/articles/contracting-on-the-rise

**Shock (stacked):** (a) EPA on **2026-02-19** cancelled the 2024 Clean School Bus rebate program and is restructuring ~$2.3B of unspent funds (statutory money ends FY2026); (b) tariffs: Blue Bird fiscal Q3 2026 cites "cumulative bus price increases" and tariffs, legacy units -7.2%; (c) Mobico sold its North American school-bus arm (Durham, Petermann, Stock; 14,000+ buses) to I Squared Capital, closed **2025-07-14**; the successor (Summit School Services) lost 3,000 buses and fell to #3; (d) ESSER money expired in 2024.
- EPA: https://www.sustainable-bus.com/news/epa-clean-school-bus-program-restructuring-biofuels/
- Blue Bird: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001589526/000158952626000046/a2026q3resultsreleaseex991.htm
- Mobico: https://www.mobicogroup.com/media/news-releases/2025/completion-of-north-america-school-bus-sale/
- SBF 2026 ranking: https://www.schoolbusfleet.com/articles/the-20-largest-school-bus-contractors-in-2026

**Incumbents and fragility:** First Student (~52,000 vehicles, 1,300 contracts, 4.6M students/day; 22,000+ workers under a new 5-year Teamsters national contract), STA (~19,000), Summit (~18,500), Beacon Mobility. Fixed-price contracts + wage settlements + driver shortage + tariffs on $50-200k+ buses = squeezed margins; PE owners must show growth; districts face enrollment decline, so route consolidation and RFP churn are likely.

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** HopSkipDrive ($25M Series C 2021, $37M Series D; 400+ districts; special-needs niche); BusPatrol (GI Partners Jan 2024, Weatherford Capital Jun 2024; stop-arm cameras funded by violator fines); Via Transportation (NYSE IPO 2025-09-15 at $46; routing/microtransit); Zum (earlier large raises; no 2025-26 news found); routing incumbents Tyler, Transfinder, Edulog. **Crowdedness: contested.**
- BusPatrol: https://www.gipartners.com/news/buspatrol-announces-significant-strategic-growth-investment-from-gi-partners
- HopSkipDrive: https://www.schoolbusfleet.com/news/hopskipdrive-raises-37m-in-series-d-funding
- Via IPO: https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/113433/Public-transit-tech-platform-Via-Transportation-prices-IPO-at-$46-above-the

**Wedge hints:** equipment finance/leasing and pooled purchasing for small districts and independent contractors now unable to use EPA subsidies; contract benchmarking and RFP service for districts (signers: transportation director + business official); school-bus CDL training pipeline and driver staffing; maintenance-shop/parts network; special-ed/IEP transport coordination.

**Insider roles:** district transportation directors, contractor regional managers, bus-shop mechanics, school business officials, state pupil-transport directors, bus dealers.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 10% of the $28B pool is $2.8B; $100M = 0.35% of national spend, i.e. e.g. 100 districts x $1M/yr of contracted service or 3% of special-ed transport. Realistic only via a services+fleet balance sheet.

## 6. H-2A agricultural guest-worker labor (and farm labor contracting)  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** Growers (directly or via associations/contractors) pay H-2A workers wages at the state Adverse Effect Wage Rate, plus mandated housing, transport and recruiting; they pay agents/attorneys and foreign recruiters to file DOL and USCIS paperwork.

**Size:** DOL OFLC FY2025: **398,258 positions certified** of 415,496 requested (first year above 400k requested). My derivation: ~400k x ~1,000-1,100 hours x ~$17-19/hr = ~$7-8B wages; with housing, transport and fees **~$9B**. AEWR level and hours are my assumptions, not sourced.
- DOL OFLC FY2025 Q4 statistics: https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/oflc/pdfs/H-2A_Selected_Statistics_FY2025_Q4.pdf

**Shock:** DOL interim final rule **2025-10-02** replaced USDA Farm Labor Survey-based AEWRs with BLS OEWS state wages in two skill levels plus a housing adjustment (some workers' wages fall); UFW sued 2025-11-21. Demand is rising while the wage regime is unstable.
- Federal Register: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-10-02/html/2025-19365.htm
- Growth reporting: https://www.freshfruitportal.com/news/2026/04/29/h-2a/

**Incumbents and fragility:** Top filers in DOL data include contractors/associations: NCGA (10,398 positions), Foothill Packing (6,959), WAFLA (4,913), Temp.Labor LLC (4,891), Fresh Harvest (4,375), Ag Labor LLC (3,335), Empire Farm Labor Contractor. Agents/attorneys filed 92% of positions in Missouri (one-state sample). Filings are manual, long-lead (workers often late; a Seso claim says 72% of farms), and rule changes plus litigation create re-pricing risk.

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** Seso (agri workforce/HR platform; $25M Series A 2022 Index; $26M led by BOND Apr 2024; 27 of top-100 US ag employers claimed); Croft (H-2A software, $700k seed, undated). No 2025-26 rounds found. **Crowdedness: contested** (Seso leads software; compliance + placement + housing bundle open).
- Seso: https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/02/seso-is-building-software-to-fix-farm-workforces-and-solve-agricultures-hr-woes
- Croft: https://app.dealroom.co/companies/croft_2

**Wedge hints:** H-2A filing/compliance + payroll + worker banking/remittance bundle for mid-size growers (top 5 states = ~50% of demand); farmworker housing build-to-rent/leasing (the binding constraint in many states); H-2B (landscaping, seafood, hospitality) extension; workers' comp/bonding for contractors.

**Insider roles:** grower HR managers, farm labor contractor owners, H-2A attorneys, Mexican recruiters, DOL OFLC analysts, packinghouse managers, farm-housing inspectors.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 400k positions x $250/position/yr fees and software = $100M at 100% of positions, or $1,000/position all-in services on 10% of positions. Heroic unless housing/payroll float is included.

## 7. US structural pest control  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** Households and businesses pay recurring route-based service fees (quarterly/monthly) plus termite and wildlife one-off jobs. Commercial customers pay for audit-ready documentation (food plants, hospitals).

**Size:** NPMA/Specialty Consultants: **$12.654B in 2024** (+7.9%), 17,000+ firms. IBISWorld's broader view is ~$24B. Use $12.7B as the defensible floor.
- https://npmapestworld.org/your-business/latest-news/us-pest-control-industry-shows-remarkable-resilience-with-nearly-8-growth-in-2024

**Shock:** Rentokil H1 results on **2026-07-30**: North America pest organic growth slowed to 2.4% in Q2 (guidance 2.6-3.4%), management retired the 20% 2027 North American margin target, shares fell 16%, residential weakness continued into July; extra $47M provision for termite damage claims.
- https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/rentokil-shares-plunge-retires-margin-target-after-us-pest-miss
- Rentokil 6-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000930157/000165495426006999/a4551o.htm

**Incumbents and fragility:** Rollins (Orkin), Rentokil Terminix, Anticimex (EQT), PE platforms (Certus, Hawx, PestCo). PE bought ~60% of 2024-25 pest M&A (broker tracker, directional). Fragility: Terminix integration drag; technician churn; termite-warranty liabilities; PE platforms bought at 8-15x EBITDA and need growth; 17,000 small owners aging into succession.
- https://ctacquisitions.com/ct-pe-tracker-pest-control/

**Funded startups (2023-2026):** Pest Share (Boise; $28M Series A Sept 2025 per VCBacked [unverified]); Zepta (Germany, seven-figure 2025); Spotta (UK); software incumbents WorkWave/PestPac and FieldRoutes. **Crowdedness: contested.**
- https://www.vcbacked.co/company/pest-share

**Wedge hints:** termite warranty underwriting/insurance and claims management; commercial compliance monitoring-as-a-service for food plants; roll-up-financing and succession marketplace for owner-operators; technician training/recruiting.

**Insider roles:** branch managers, route supervisors, QA directors at food plants, state pesticide regulators, PMP owners, M&A advisors.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 100k residential customers x $600/yr = $60M, plus commercial; i.e. ~0.8% of the pool via acquisition roll-up. Exit multiples compress if roll-up is crowded.

## 8. US fines, fees and automated-enforcement citations (the citation lifecycle)  [HIDDEN GIANT]

**Money flow:** Drivers and defendants pay state/local governments fines, court fees and surcharges. Private vendors (camera operators, payment processors, collectors, probation providers) take a share via per-ticket fees, convenience fees or contingency.

**Size:** Tax Policy Center, citing Census: fines and fees **$17.8B in 2024** ($15.5B in 2023; $13.9B in 2022 per Fines and Fees Justice Center). **Honest caveat:** the vendor-captured slice is much smaller than the flow - Verra Mobility's whole 2026 revenue guide is $945-965M - so the monetizable pool is likely $2-4B.
- https://taxpolicycenter.org/taxvox/new-census-data-show-fines-and-fees-revenues-are-rising (fetch blocked; figures from search result)

**Shock:** automated enforcement is expanding fast. Verra Mobility Q2 2026 (released **2026-08-05**): Government Solutions revenue $128.5M (+20%); NYC red-light camera contract ~$998M over 5 years for 600 intersections; Los Angeles selected Verra for California's largest speed program (125 locations). New York authorized statewide work-zone cameras in its 2026 budget; ~300 related bills were introduced in 2025; Colorado SB26-152 would cut the speed threshold to 6 mph over (outcome unknown).
- Verra 8-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001682745/000119312526335111/vrrm-ex99_1.htm
- NYC: https://www.smartcitiesdive.com/news/nyc-expands-red-light-cameras-verra-mobility/804222/
- Legislative recap: https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/2/3/automated-traffic-enforcement-confronts-privacy-and-safety-debates-2025-legislative-recap

**Incumbents and fragility:** Verra Mobility, Conduent, Tyler Technologies (courts/payments), Paymentus-type bill-pay vendors, collection agencies. Fragility: 5-year municipal contracts, "revenue machine" political backlash, ability-to-pay reforms, legacy court case-management, and payment portals with high convenience fees; Verra flags NYC payment timing risk.

## 9. US giving to congregations: payments, software and treasury stack

**Money flow:** ~$151.6B of donations flow to US religious organizations each year, mostly via checks/cash; digital giving platforms take ~2-3% processing plus monthly software fees from churches.

**Size:** Giving USA 2026 (2025 data): religion **$151.58B** (23% of $617.2B total), +2.4% nominal, -0.2% real; only ~1% real growth since 2005. Addressable fee pool is far below $5B (my estimate: digital share of giving x ~3% take + software, roughly $1.5-2.5B) - treat the pool as the giving flow, with capture much smaller.
- https://givingusa.org/giving-usa-charitable-giving-rose-to-617-20-billion-in-2025-surpassing-the-600-billion-mark-for-the-first-time/

**Shock:** One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) changes charitable tax rules from tax year **2026**: a deduction of up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) for cash gifts for non-itemizers (direct gifts only, not donor-advised funds), a 0.5%-of-AGI floor and a ~35% benefit cap for itemizers. Net effect on church giving is unmeasured (no projection found); possible shift toward small direct cash gifts.
- https://pgcalc.com/insight-training/pg-calc-featured-articles/impact-on-giving-in-the-one-big-beautiful-bill

**Incumbents and fragility:** Pushpay (private since 2023 under Sixth Street and BGH; 53.6M gifts in 2025), Tithely, Subsplash, Planning Center (on Stripe), Givelify, Gloo (Nasdaq IPO Nov 2025 at $8, ~$73M raised, 57,000 paying customers, ~$41M LTM revenue). Fragility is low: bundled platforms with high switching cost; flat real giving limits growth.
- https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/115024/christian-tech-platform-gloo-holdings-prices-ipo-at-8-below-the-range
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushpay

**Funded startups:** many; see above. **Crowdedness: crowded.**

**Wedge hints:** treasury/banking, church loans and property sale/lease-back for shrinking congregations (not another giving app); tax-receipt workflows for new non-itemizer rule.

**Insider roles:** church administrators and business managers, denominational finance officers, church-lending credit unions, CPAs for churches.

**Bottom-up path to $100M:** 350k US congregations x $300/yr = ~$100M only at 100% share of software; not a good path without the lending/treasury layer. Included mainly so nobody re-discovers it.

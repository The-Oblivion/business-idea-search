## 1. US health plans' and self-funded employers' out-of-network payments through No Surprises Act arbitration (IDR)  [HG]

- **Money flow:** Health plans, and self-funded employers through their TPAs, pay out-of-network providers (emergency physicians, radiologists, anesthesiologists, hospital-based groups) the offer an independent arbiter picks. Providers and billing intermediaries start about 99.9% of disputes (H1 2025). The payer also pays an administrative fee ($115 per party, unchanged for 2026) plus the arbiter's fee.
- **Size:** about $15B of awards in 2025, more than triple the prior year ($4.08B in 2024), from a Wall Street Journal analysis of CMS data as reported by Healthcare Dive on 2026-07-23: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/no-surprises-act-disputes-increase-arbiters-progress-speed-backlog-cms/826011/ (I read this page). Georgetown/Health Affairs (Aug 2026) puts cumulative IDR cost 2022-2025 at $22.4B: $15.6B above-benchmark payments, $4.2B administrative, $2.7B arbiter fees (second-hand via Fierce Healthcare / TechTarget summaries; the Health Affairs page returned 403 to me: https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/forefront.20260821.888041). Volume: ~1.2M disputes in H1 2025 vs ~590k in H1 2024 and ~1.4M in H2 2025; government had expected ~17,000 a year (Georgetown CHIR: https://chir.georgetown.edu/the-no-surprises-act-idr-process-an-early-look-at-2025-data/, which I read).
- **Who wins, by how much:** providers prevail ~85% of the time; the winning offer exceeds the plan's qualifying payment amount (QPA, the median in-network rate) in ~87% of decisions; median prevailing award about 445% of QPA (Becker's); about 22% of H1 2025 determinations were default decisions (physician groups cite CMS data showing insurers defaulted on 24.5% of 2025 line items; that figure is one side's framing). Top initiators: HaloMD (billing intermediary), Team Health, SCP Health, Radiology Partners (56% of H1 2025 disputes). Most-disputed payers: UnitedHealthcare, Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas, Aetna (57%).
- **Shock:** (1) The en banc Fifth Circuit on 2026-08-11 (TMA III) largely affirmed the invalidation of the federal QPA rules: plans may not count non-negotiated "ghost rates" or categorically exclude bonus/risk-share payments in the QPA. Every payer's benchmark methodology is legally defective and whether corrected QPAs apply to past claims is open (Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor, 2026-09-08, which I read: https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/09/fifth-circuit-rules-en-banc-on-key-qpa-calculation-disputes-under-the-no-surprises-act/). (2) Agencies finalized an IDR Operations Rule in May 2026 (eligibility screening, standardized payer-provider communication; reports of fee changes not verified). (3) Insurers are lobbying Congress for an overhaul; physician groups oppose (ACEP 2026-10-08).
- **Incumbents:** Provider side: PE-backed physician groups and contingency billing intermediaries (HaloMD is the largest filer; insurers have sued it and several suits were dismissed). Payer side: Claritev (ex-MultiPlan) repricing, Zelis, Cotiviti, Fair Health benchmarks, big-carrier in-house teams.
- **Why fragile:** The payer side loses ~85% of disputes and, per physician-group analysis of CMS data, defaults on roughly 1 in 4 line items; the self-funded employers who ultimately fund awards have no defense tooling; the QPA, the legal anchor of the whole system, was just ruled defective; the arbiter market is throughput-constrained (backlog of 430k in mid-2025; two arbiters added Sept 2025).
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** Daffodil Health (payer-side OON pricing and payment integrity, $16.3-16.6M Series A, Flare Capital, LRVHealth, Maverick; ~$20.9M total; https://www.flarecapital.com/insight/page/4/), Radix Health (software-based IDR for providers), Pivotal Health (provider-side, success fee). HaloMD has no disclosed funding I could find. Payer-side IDR-defense startups for TPAs/employers: none found. Judgment: CONTESTED (provider side crowded; payer/employer side thin).
- **Wedge hints:** (a) IDR defense and default-prevention for the hundreds of mid-size payers and TPAs with no in-house team; (b) QPA recomputation/audit service after TMA III; (c) pre-IDR negotiation and out-of-network carve-outs for self-funded employers; (d) software for the arbitration entities themselves.
- **Insider roles:** payer OON/NSA compliance directors, TPA claims-operations VPs, emergency-medicine and radiology group revenue-cycle directors, IDR-entity reviewers, benefits attorneys.
- **Open risk:** a legislative reset (Congress could cap awards) or a regulatory fix could shrink the pool; the opportunity is partly a bet on continued volatility.

## 2. State Medicaid program administration: the H.R.1 work-requirement and six-month-renewal build  [HG]

- **Money flow:** States (federal match 90% for system builds, 75% for operations, 50% for general admin) pay IT integrators, eligibility vendors, call centers, enrollment brokers and data brokers. Medicaid managed-care plans separately pay for member outreach and retention.
- **Size:** state program administration was $41.3B in FY2024 ($25.3B federal, $16.0B state) out of $957B total Medicaid; I read the table (MACPAC Exhibit 16: https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/EXHIBIT-16.-Medicaid-Spending-by-State-Category-and-Source-of-Funds-FY-2024.pdf). The shock-affected slice is much smaller: CMS estimates ~$15M average systems cost per state, $660M nationally, excluding ongoing verification, notices and appeals (Foley Hoag, which I read); an AP analysis of 25+ states put technology plus staffing above $1B; New York alone estimated $500M; the federal implementation grant is only $200M.
- **Shock:** H.R.1 (signed 2025-07-04) requires 80 hours a month of work, community service or education for expansion adults 19-64 from 2027-01-01 (41 expansion jurisdictions), plus six-month redeterminations. CMS issued the interim final rule 2026-06-01 (effective 2026-07-31, comments due the same day); CMS projects ~2.3M fewer enrollees in FY2027 and CBO sees 5.3M more uninsured by 2034; states can seek a good-faith-effort exemption that expires no later than 2028-12-31. https://www.foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2026/june/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-imposing-medicaid-work-requirements-for-expansion-populations/
- **Incumbents:** Deloitte, Gainwell, Maximus, Accenture, Conduent, GDIT, Optum, Acentra, Merative (Curam), RedMane pledged $600M+ in no-cost or discounted services to states in Jan 2026 (Healthcare Dive: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicaid-vendors-trump-deals-cms-work-requirements/810839/). Equifax's Work Number is used by about half of states per a Feb 2026 Warren/Wyden/Sanders letter; its CEO called work requirements "a big positive" (https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-wyden-sanders-announce-new-investigation-into-equifaxs-plan-to-profit-from-trumps-so-called-work-requirements-in-medicaid-snap).
- **Why fragile:** state eligibility systems are patchworks never designed to pull and track data from many sources; integrators are discounting under White House pressure; the employment-data layer is a near-monopoly under political fire; prior state trials (Arkansas) dis-enrolled people for paperwork failure rather than non-compliance, so errors carry legal risk.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** none found specifically for Medicaid work-requirement verification (searched Nava, Propel, Benefit Kitchen, Truss, Gridwise, Truv, Argyle, Pinwheel). Judgment: CONTESTED because the incumbents are giants giving services away, though no venture-funded startup is in it.
- **Wedge hints:** (a) alternative income/employment verification (payroll APIs, state UI wage records, gig platforms) priced per verification; (b) plan-paid member documentation and retention outreach (plans lose per-member revenue for every disenrollment); (c) volunteer/community-service hour verification; (d) medical-frailty exemption documentation for FQHCs and providers.
- **Insider roles:** state Medicaid eligibility directors and MMIS project managers, managed-care government-programs VPs, county eligibility workers, FQHC enrollment staff, legal-aid attorneys.
- **Honest caveat:** the buyer is state procurement; a bottom-up $100M needs ~20 states at ~$5M, or per-verification pricing across 2.3M to 18M people.

## 3. The 340B Drug Pricing Program: covered-entity purchases, the spread, and the rebate transition  [HG]

- **Money flow:** manufacturers sell outpatient drugs at statutory ceiling prices to covered-entity sites (hospitals ~87% of volume); the entities bill Medicare and insurers at full rates and keep the spread, increasingly through contract pharmacies.
- **Size:** HRSA says covered entities purchased $100B in CY2025, up ~22% from $81.4B in CY2024 (I read the AHA summary: https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-07-14-hrsa-announces-340b-program-sales-reached-100-billion-cy-2025). The contestable money (spread, administration, compliance) is a fraction of this and I could not source it.
- **Shock:** the first HRSA rebate pilot (10 negotiated drugs, Jan 2026) was enjoined by D. Maine on 2025-12-29, upheld by the First Circuit on 2026-01-07, and withdrawn in Feb 2026. On 2026-07-21 the D.C. Circuit (Novartis v. Kennedy) held rebate models permissible but only with HHS approval. HRSA published a revised pilot on 2026-08-03 (2026 and 2027 negotiated drugs; covered entities pay full price, then submit claims data to get rebates; manufacturers cannot deny on suspected diversion or duplicate discounts; effective 2027-01-01). AHA and Maine Hospital Association sued again on 2026-10-09. Congress: Cassidy discussion draft (June 2026) would allow discounts, rebates or a claims-repository; House SECURE 340B Act (2026-07-06) would pause rebates; Senate SUSTAIN 340B Act (2026-08-05) would wind down the pilot and move to a data clearinghouse. All read at https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/08/second-times-the-charm-hrsa-revives-the-340b-rebate-pilot/
- **Incumbents:** third-party administrators and contract-pharmacy networks (Sentry/Wellpartner, Walgreens, CVS, Verity, Second Sight's 340B ESP), Kalderos (duplicate-discount and rebate platform).
- **Why fragile:** a rebate model forces hospitals and safety-net clinics to front full price and then prove claims-level eligibility, a working-capital and data problem for entities with thin balance sheets and weak data teams; the data-standard (clearinghouse) is unresolved and may be written by whoever shows up with a credible implementation.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** Proximity ($3M seed, Feb 2025, LRVHealth and Commonweal; 340B revenue automation), Pillr Health (Water Street Healthcare Partners investment, Jan 2026; split billing, contract pharmacy, rebate readiness; size undisclosed), Kalderos ($7M Series A 2019, $28M Series B 2020 led by Bain Capital Ventures; no 2023-26 round found). Judgment: CONTESTED.
- **Wedge hints:** advance financing against 340B rebate receivables; claims-data scrubbing and rebate-submission service for small covered entities and FQHCs; duplicate-discount prevention; a neutral claims clearinghouse if Congress picks that model.
- **Insider roles:** 340B program directors at DSH hospitals and FQHCs, hospital pharmacy directors, manufacturer 340B-integrity leads, TPA compliance staff, health-system treasurers.
- **Open risk:** outcome is split three ways (rebates, discounts-with-data, pause); the prize depends on legal and legislative resolution within ~12 months.

## 4. Medicaid home- and community-based services (personal care, home care agencies, fiscal intermediaries)  [HG]

- **Money flow:** state Medicaid agencies (and managed-LTSS plans) pay thousands of small home care agencies and fiscal-intermediary payroll firms per care hour; most revenue passes through as aide wages.
- **Size:** CMS data show 7.8M HCBS users and $129.4B of Medicaid spending in 2022 (+12.5% y/y); I read the figure at https://www.leadingageny.org/topics/data/datapoints-archive/datapoint-cms-releases-updated-ltss-user-and-expenditure-data . A narrower slice: personal-care spending ~$46.4B in 2024, nearly double 2019, with New York alone $15.67B (RealClearInvestigations 2026-05-14, read: https://www.realclearinvestigations.com/articles/2026/05/14/unbridled_spending_billions_for_medicaid_program_congress_never_approved_1182576.html).
- **Shock:** (1) H.R.1 cuts federal Medicaid by $911B over 2025-34 (KFF); states historically respond with fewer people served (40 states) and rate cuts (47 states): https://www.kff.org/medicaid/states-management-of-medicaid-home-care-spending-ahead-of-h-r-1-effects/ . (2) Immigration enforcement hits the workforce: foreign-born workers are over 40% of home health aides and ~30% of personal-care workers (US government data via Staffing Industry Analysts / American Immigration Council). (3) New York forced all ~600 fiscal intermediaries into one statewide FI (Public Partnerships LLC) on 2025-04-01 for ~250k CDPAP consumers; the Governor claimed over $1B in savings (Jan 2026), contested by the Fiscal Policy Institute; DOJ reportedly investigating the rollout. (4) The Access Rule's 80/20 wage pass-through (effective ~2030) is still on the books; I found no rescission.
- **Incumbents:** FIs (PPL, GT Independence, Acumen/Tempus), agency chains (Addus, BrightSpring, Amedisys), software (HHAeXchange for EVV, WellSky, AlayaCare, AxisCare, Axxess, ClearCare).
- **Why fragile:** thousands of agencies under $20M revenue on thin margins, paid late by states, squeezed by rate cuts, with a shrinking labor supply and electronic-visit-verification compliance burdens; no access to working capital.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** AlayaCare (C$225M Series D in 2021; debt financing Feb 2026), Enzo Health ($20M Series A, May 2026, home health/post-acute AI), MochaCare (~$1M accelerator round, Mar 2026). No venture-funded Medicaid FI or payroll challenger found. Judgment: CONTESTED on software, OPEN on financing, payroll and back-office for Medicaid agencies.
- **Wedge hints:** working-capital advances against Medicaid receivables (a balance-sheet business); fiscal-intermediary/payroll platform for states that will bid statewide FIs after New York; EVV-to-claims reconciliation; caregiver pipeline products for agencies.
- **Insider roles:** agency owners and administrators, state long-term-care program managers, FI operations VPs, EVV compliance leads, waiver case managers.

## 5. Clinical laboratory testing (independent, hospital-outreach and reference labs)  [HG]

- **Money flow:** Medicare, Medicare Advantage, commercial plans and Medicaid pay labs per test; Medicare's rate is set from a private-payer rate survey under PAMA.
- **Size:** US clinical lab services ~$99.7B revenue in 2024, projected $122.5B by 2029 (Hyde Park Capital, an investment bank; secondary: https://www.hydeparkcapital.com/insights/industry-reports/laboratory-and-diagnostic-services-market-insights-summer-2025/). Medicare Part B lab spend $8.4B in 2024 per HHS OIG as cited by MarkNtel (secondary), 43% genetic tests. Narrow market-research figures near $10B are not comparable.
- **Shock:** the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026 (signed 2026-02-03) delayed the PAMA cuts for the seventh time and moved data collection to 2025 (ACLA: https://www.acla.com/acla-welcomes-pama-relief-in-funding-package-urges-congress-to-advance-results-act-in-2026/). CMS's preliminary 2027 fee schedule (week of 2026-09-25) applies the maximum 15% cut to ~775 tests (almost 1,200 tests total per ACLA), with more cuts in 2028-29; CMS says it saves about $1B a year. TD Cowen expects Congress to punt again; the RESULTS Act would use a third-party claims database to set rates. House is in recess until after the midterms. https://www.medtechdive.com/news/lab-test-providers-want-medicare-payment-reform-will-congress-pass-it/831384/ (read).
- **Incumbents:** Quest, Labcorp (buying hospital outreach labs), thousands of regional and specialty/genetic labs; billing systems XIFIN, Sunquest, Orchard.
- **Why fragile:** independent labs run thin margins and depend on payer coverage decisions and the PAMA rate; hospitals are selling outreach labs; billing is mostly legacy or offshore shops.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** none found that are lab-specific revenue-cycle companies; general RCM (Candid Health $120M Series D, Silna Health $27M) is adjacent. Not verified beyond three searches. Judgment: OPEN, low confidence.
- **Wedge hints:** lab-specific underpayment and denial recovery on contingency; payer-rate benchmarking for labs in the 2025 PAMA data cycle; outreach-lab transition services for health systems.
- **Insider roles:** independent-lab CFOs and billing managers, hospital outreach-lab directors, MolDX/coverage consultants, lab sales managers.

## 6. Rural Health Transformation Program (state grant money)

- **Money flow:** CMS to 50 states (half split equally, half by CMS-scored plans), then to state agencies, providers, vendors and workforce programs; funded by H.R.1 at $10B a year, FY2026-FY2030.
- **Size:** $10B a year, $50B total. FY2026 awards run from $147M (New Jersey) to $281M (Texas), averaging ~$200M (CMS announcement 2025-12-29: https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/cms-announces-50-billion-in-awards-to-strengthen-rural-health-in-all-50-states.html). Use limits from the funding notice (via Texas DSHS summary and AHA's 2026-08-05 letter): provider payments capped at 15%, infrastructure/minor renovation at 20%, EHR spending at 5% of a state's award. So roughly 60% or more goes to programs, technology, workforce and care-model pilots.
- **Shock/timing:** states have ten months to obligate year-one money and a further year to spend it or face claw-back and redistribution (Manatt, 2026-02-09, read: https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/health-highlights/rural-health-transformation-program-what-s-next-considerations-as-states-begin-implementation). Workplans finalized Mar-Jun 2026; RFPs posting Jul 2026 through Q1 2027 (vendor-sourced, low reliability). Framed by KFF as an offset to Medicaid cuts rather than net new money.
- **Incumbents:** state agencies, large consultancies (Deloitte, Guidehouse, Accenture), EHR vendors, telehealth vendors, rural hospital associations.
- **Why fragile:** spend-fast pressure creates many isolated pilots; no standard vendor for outcome tracking and subgrant management; rural hospitals lack grant-writing and IT capacity (AHA is asking CMS for technical-assistance centers).
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** none found aimed at this program (searches returned only advisory content). Judgment: OPEN.
- **Wedge hints:** grant-management, compliance and outcome-reporting platform sold to 50 state RHT offices; subgrant application help for critical-access hospitals; shared IT/cyber/revenue-cycle services paid from program funds.
- **Insider roles:** state RHT program directors, rural hospital CFOs and CEOs, state hospital association staff, rural health clinic managers.
- **Honest caveat:** five-year grant then a cliff; the vendor-addressable share of $10B is not public; state procurement cycles are slow.

## 7. Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment (coding-intensity) payments

- **Money flow:** CMS pays MA plans risk-adjusted capitation; plans spend on chart review, coding vendors, in-home assessments, encounter capture, and pay value-based provider groups.
- **Size:** MedPAC (March 2026): Medicare pays ~14% more for MA enrollees than for the same people in fee-for-service, a projected $76B in 2026, from favorable selection (~11 points) and coding intensity (~4 points); MA plans were paid an estimated $537B in 2025. I read the MedPAC release (https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/March_2026_MedPAC_Report_Press_Release_SEC.pdf). The coding slice is ~$22B (4 points of ~$540B, consistent with an outside analysis). Vendor spend on risk adjustment is not publicly sized (my estimate: low single-digit $B).
- **Shock:** CY2027 final rate notice (2026-04-06): +2.48% base (about +$13B; about +4.98% with risk-score trend), but it excludes diagnoses from unlinked chart reviews (CMS-estimated -1.53%) and audio-only encounters, and keeps the 2024 risk model (McDermott+: https://mcdermottplus.com/blog/regs-eggs/the-final-course-for-2027-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-policies ; KFF: https://www.kff.org/quick-insights/medicare-advantage-insurers-will-see-higher-payments-as-cms-backs-off-a-key-payment-update/). RADV: CMS said (2025) it would audit every eligible contract yearly (~550 contracts, ~2,000 coders), but the N.D. Texas court vacated the 2023 extrapolation rule on 2025-09-25 (Humana); the Fifth Circuit (No. 25-11293) heard argument around 2026-10-05 and the panel reportedly seemed poised to affirm (STAT, paywalled).
- **Incumbents:** Optum, Cotiviti, Episource, Inovalon, Lumeris, plan in-house teams.
- **Why fragile:** retrospective, unlinked chart review is being excluded from payment; in-home assessment vendors face DOJ scrutiny; RADV enforcement is legally unstable.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** RAAPID (Series A extension Mar 2026 from UPMC Enterprises; M12 and CareFirst's Healthworx earlier), Keebler Health ($6M seed, $7.8M total, date unclear); list is partial. Judgment: CROWDED.
- **Wedge hints:** encounter-linked prospective documentation at the point of care; RADV defense and record retrieval for plans; compliance for value-based groups.
- **Insider roles:** MA risk-adjustment directors, value-based group CMOs and coding managers, RADV appeals attorneys, certified coders.

## 8. Hospital and post-acute claim denials, including MA prior-authorization denials

- **Money flow:** providers spend labor and vendor fees to appeal payer denials; the money at stake is revenue recovered.
- **Size:** hospitals spend about $19.7B a year fighting denied claims (Premier survey of 516 hospitals, claims from 2022: ~15% of private-payer claims denied, 54.3% eventually overturned; Becker's: https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/claims-denials-are-costing-hospitals-nearly-20b-per-year/). Unverified secondary figures: AHA estimate of $43B spent on payer collections in 2025 including ~$18B on denials; vendor claims of $26B. Cost per denial was $43.84 in the Premier method.
- **Shock:** HHS OIG report OEI-09-24-00331 (issued 2026-06-08): 19 MA parent companies denied 12% of skilled-nursing-facility admission requests (June 2024); 18% were appealed and 95% of appeals were overturned; UnitedHealth's naviHealth denied 14%; nursing-home residents were denied 40% of the time (https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2026/medicare-advantage-organizations-overturned-nearly-all-appealed-prior-authorization-denials-for-skilled-nursing-facility-admission-raising-concerns-about-initial-denials/, read). Senate HSGAC letters to Humana, UnitedHealth and CVS on 2026-07-14. A 2025 survey of 363 nursing-home providers found two-thirds face MA denials or delays daily or weekly (HIDA citing Plante Moran).
- **Incumbents:** R1 RCM, Waystar, Optum, Ensemble, Cotiviti; payers' automated utilization-management tools.
- **Why fragile:** a 95% overturn rate on appeal means initial denials are a volume game; providers under-appeal (11-18%); payers' denial systems face political scrutiny.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** Amperos Health ($16M Series A, Apr 2026, Bessemer; $4.2M seed Jun 2025), Candid Health ($120M Series D, Sixth Street), RapidClaims ($11M, Accel, Apr 2025), Basalt Health ($20M Series A, NEA; post-acute admissions and prior auth), ExaCare AI ($30M Series A, Insight; SNF referrals), Willow (YC, long-term-care RCM, Feb 2026), Clinware ($4.25M seed), Cascala ($8.6M seed, 2025); R1 bought Humata Health. Judgment: CROWDED for hospital denials; CONTESTED for nursing-home-specific MA denials.
- **Wedge hints:** contingency appeals for skilled-nursing and home-health operators against MA denials (the 95% overturn rate gives a high win rate); payer-contract compliance audit for long-term-care operators.
- **Insider roles:** hospital denial-management directors, SNF administrators and business-office managers, MA appeals nurses, payer medical directors.

## 9. GLP-1 obesity and diabetes drug spend

- **Money flow:** payers (Medicare Part D, Medicaid, employers, commercial plans) and cash-paying patients pay manufacturers via PBMs and pharmacies.
- **Size (conservative, sourced):** Lilly's US revenue in Q4 2025 was $4.1B Mounjaro plus $4.2B Zepbound = $8.3B in one quarter, a ~$33B annualized run rate for tirzepatide alone; Lilly's FY2025 worldwide sales were Mounjaro $22.97B and Zepbound $13.54B (Lilly Q4 2025 release: https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-reports-fourth-quarter-2025-financial-results-and-provides). Novo's US semaglutide sales add to this but I did not source them. IQVIA: GIP/GLP-1 drugs added $14B to US net medicine spend in 2025 (net spend $606B): https://www.iqvia.com/insights/the-iqvia-institute/reports/us-medicine-use-trends-2026 .
- **Shock:** Nov 2025 Trump-Lilly-Novo pricing deal; TrumpRx.gov live 2026-02-05 (oral semaglutide $149 a month cash; injectables at or below $350, trending to $245); Medicare GLP-1 obesity bridge from 2026-07-01 ($245 Medicare price, $50 copay) through Dec 2027, while CMS's longer-term BALANCE model stalled (80% of Part D enrollment threshold unmet); employer obesity-GLP-1 coverage fell from 72% (2025) to 60% (2026) in a BGH survey, with Cigna (staff, 7/1/2026) and Starbucks (Oct 2026) dropping coverage.
- **Incumbents:** Lilly and Novo (LillyDirect, NovoCare direct-to-consumer), PBMs, telehealth firms.
- **Why fragile:** manufacturers can now sell direct, shrinking PBM and middleman economics; employers are retreating, leaving a care-management gap.
- **Funded startups (2023-26):** eMed ($200M Series A, Mar 2026, led by Aon, valuation over $2B), knownwell ($25M, Oct 2025), Form Health ($38M Series B, 2024), 9amHealth, Waltz Health. Judgment: CROWDED.
- **Wedge hints (thin):** employer purchasing/aggregation of cash-price channels; navigation of Medicare bridge enrollment; weak because manufacturers can bypass intermediaries.
- **Insider roles:** employer benefits directors, PBM clinical pharmacists, obesity-medicine physicians, Medicare Part D plan product leaders.

## Considered but not scored as pools (so nothing is lost)

- **PBM compensation and employer pharmacy benefit:** the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026 (H.R. 7148, signed 2026-02-03) delinks Part D PBM pay from drug prices from plan year 2028 and requires 100% rebate pass-through and compensation disclosure for ERISA plans in 2028-2029 (Mintz: https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2146/2026-02-06-congress-passes-landmark-pbm-reform-2026-spending-bill); a DOL proposed PBM-reporting rule followed 2026-01-29.

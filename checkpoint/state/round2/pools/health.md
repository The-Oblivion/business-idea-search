# POOL: No Surprises Act out-of-network payments (IDR arbitration)  [hidden giant: yes]
(saved 2026-10-14, raw research notes; final polish pending)

- **Money flow:** Health plans and self-funded employers (via TPAs) pay out-of-network providers (emergency physicians, radiologists, anesthesiologists, air ambulance) the amount an arbiter picks between two offers. Providers and billing intermediaries initiate ~99.9% (H1 2025) of disputes; payer pays award plus $115 admin fee per party per dispute plus IDR-entity fee.
- **Size:** ~$15B of awards in 2025, "more than triple the year prior" ($4.08B in 2024) per a WSJ analysis of CMS data, reported by Healthcare Dive 2026-07-23: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/no-surprises-act-disputes-increase-arbiters-progress-speed-backlog-cms/826011/ . Georgetown CHIR/Health Affairs (Aug 2026) estimates cumulative IDR cost $22.4B for 2022-2025 incl. $15.6B above-QPA payments (2023-25), $4.2B admin, $2.7B IDR-entity fees; ~$16.5B in 2025 alone (as reported by Fierce Healthcare https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/costs-tied-no-surprises-acts-idr-process-hit-22b-2025-study-finds and TechTarget; primary Health Affairs page https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/forefront.20260821.888041 returned 403 to me, so figure is second-hand).
- **Volume:** ~1.2M disputes H1 2025 (vs ~590k H1 2024), ~1.4M H2 2025 (CMS data; 16% more than H1). Government expected ~17,000/yr. Georgetown CHIR: https://chir.georgetown.edu/the-no-surprises-act-idr-process-an-early-look-at-2025-data/
- **Win rates / award levels:** providers prevail ~85% (88% H1 2025); winning offer above QPA in ~87% of determinations; median prevailing award 445% of QPA (Becker's); 22% of H1-2025 determinations were default decisions (payer failed to participate).
- **Concentration:** HaloMD (billing intermediary), Team Health, Radiology Partners, SCP Health = ~56% of disputes H1 2025; top 3 (HaloMD, Team Health, SCP) ~38% of H2 2025; most-disputed payers UnitedHealthcare, BCBS Texas, Aetna = 57%.
- **Shock (2026):** (1) CMS/Departments finalized the IDR Operations Rule in May 2026 (eligibility screening, standardized payer-provider communication, fee changes); (2) en banc Fifth Circuit (TMA III, Aug 2026) held plans may not include non-negotiated "ghost rates" or categorically exclude bonus/risk-share payments in QPA calculation: every payer's QPA methodology is now wrong and past QPAs may need recomputation (Polsinelli https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/en-banc-fifth-circuit-rejects-key-qpa-rules-in-tma-iii ; Health Affairs https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/forefront.20260830.290386); (3) insurers lobbying Congress to overhaul IDR (ACEP 2026-10-08 opposing bill that gives insurers more control of OON payments).
- **Incumbents:** Provider side: PE-backed physician groups and HaloMD-style billing intermediaries (take contingency %). Payer side: Claritev (ex-MultiPlan) repricing, Zelis, Cotiviti, Fair Health benchmarks, big-carrier in-house teams. IDR entities (certified arbiters, few dozen; 2 new added Sept 2025; Physio Solutions, Medlitix added).
- **Fragility:** Payer side is poorly instrumented: payers lose 85% and default on ~22-25% of line items (missed deadlines). Self-funded employers/TPAs (who ultimately fund the awards) have no defence tooling. QPA methodology now legally defective.
- **Funded startups:** Daffodil Health (payer-side OON pricing/payment-integrity, $16.3-16.6M Series A, Flare Capital, ~$20.9M total, 2025-26: https://www.flarecapital.com/insight/page/4/ ); Radix Health (software IDR, provider-side); Pivotal Health (provider-side IDR, success fee); HaloMD (private, no disclosed funding found). Crowdedness: CONTESTED (provider side crowded, payer/employer side thin).
- **Wedge hints:** (a) payer/TPA-side IDR defence and default-prevention for the ~hundreds of mid-size payers and TPAs that lack in-house teams; (b) QPA recomputation/audit service post-TMA III; (c) self-funded employer OON claims "pre-IDR" negotiation and carve-out; (d) IDR entity software/capacity (arbiters are throughput-constrained).
- **Insider roles:** payer OON/NSA compliance directors, TPA claims ops VPs, ED/radiology group revenue-cycle directors, IDR-entity reviewers, benefits attorneys.

# POOL: State Medicaid administration, esp. H.R.1 eligibility/work-requirement/6-month-renewal build  [hidden giant: yes]
(raw notes saved 2026-10-14)
- **Money flow:** States (with federal match: 90% for system build, 75% for ops, 50% general admin) pay IT integrators, eligibility vendors, call centers, enrollment brokers, and data brokers. Medicaid MCOs separately pay for member outreach/retention.
- **Size:** State program administration = $41.3B in FY2024 ($25.3B federal, $16.0B state) of $957B total Medicaid, per MACPAC Exhibit 16: https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/EXHIBIT-16.-Medicaid-Spending-by-State-Category-and-Source-of-Funds-FY-2024.pdf (via search summary; state-by-state in Exhibit 31 https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/EXHIBIT-31.-Total-Medicaid-Administrative-Spending-by-State-and-Category-FY-2024.pdf). The shock-affected incremental slice is much smaller: Foley Hoag est ~ $15M/state average; AP analysis of >25 states put tech+staff cost >$1B; New York alone est $500M; federal implementation grant only $200M.
- **Shock:** H.R.1 (OBBBA, signed July 2025) requires 80 hrs/month work/community service/education for expansion adults 19-64 starting Jan 1 2027 (41 expansion states/DC), plus 6-month redeterminations. CMS interim final rule issued June 1 2026 (effective July 31 2026), CMS projects ~2.3M fewer enrollees FY2027. Foley Hoag: https://www.foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2026/june/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-imposing-medicaid-work-requirements-for-expansion-populations/ ; Healthcare Dive on vendor deals: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/medicaid-vendors-trump-deals-cms-work-requirements/810839/
- **Incumbents:** Deloitte, Gainwell, Maximus, Accenture, Conduent, GDIT, Optum, Acentra, Merative/Curam, RedMane pledged >$600M of no-cost/discounted services (Jan 2026; CMS says ~$600M savings through 2028). Equifax (Work Number, used by ~half of states per Warren/Wyden/Sanders Feb 2026 letter; CEO called work requirements "a big positive"): https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-wyden-sanders-announce-new-investigation-into-equifaxs-plan-to-profit-from-trumps-so-called-work-requirements-in-medicaid-snap
- **Fragility:** Legacy state eligibility systems are patchworks; contractors pledged discounts (price pressure on incumbents), but prior state work-requirement trials (Arkansas) dis-enrolled people for paperwork failures, not non-compliance. Equifax near-monopoly on employment data is politically exposed.
- **Funded startups:** Searched; found no VC-funded startup specifically for Medicaid work-requirement verification (searched Nava, Propel, Benefit Kitchen, Truss, Gridwise, Truv, Argyle, Pinwheel: no relevant 2026 hits). Crowdedness: OPEN for startups, but CROWDED with giant incumbents who are discounting. 
- **Wedge hints:** (a) wage/gig-income verification layer as Equifax alternative (payroll APIs, state UI wage records, gig platforms) sold per-verification to states/MCOs; (b) MCO-paid member retention/documentation outreach (MCOs lose PMPM revenue per dropped member); (c) volunteer-hours / community-service verification marketplace; (d) exemption (medical frailty) documentation for providers/FQHCs.
- **Insider roles:** state Medicaid eligibility directors, state Medicaid IT/MMIS project managers, MCO government-programs/member-services VPs, county eligibility workers, FQHC enrollment staff, legal aid attorneys.
- **Honest note:** sales cycle = state procurement; bottom-up to $100M requires ~20 states x $5M. Reachable only via MCO channel or per-verification pricing at scale (2.3M-18M people x several verifications/yr).

# POOL: Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment / coding economics  [hidden giant: no]
(raw notes saved 2026-10-14)
- **Money flow:** CMS pays MA plans ~$615B (2026, MedPAC) risk-adjusted capitation; plans spend on chart review, coding vendors, in-home assessments, encounter capture, and pay VBC provider groups. 
- **Size:** MedPAC March 2026: MA payments ~14% above FFS in 2026 = ~$76B extra; favorable selection ~11 pts, coding intensity ~4 pts (~$22B per one analysis); down from $84B/20% in 2025. https://www.medpac.gov/document/march-2026-report-to-the-congress-medicare-payment-policy/ . CMS improper-payment estimate: ~$17.2B/yr unsupported diagnoses (CMS RADV fact sheet https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cpi-radvfact-sheet.pdf). Vendor-spend slice (coding/chart review/HRAs) not publicly sized; I estimate low single-digit $B.
- **Shock:** CY2027 final rate notice (April 6 2026): +2.48% base (~+$13B; ~+4.98% incl. risk-score trend), but excludes diagnoses from unlinked chart reviews (CMS est -1.53% industry-wide) and audio-only (mod 93/FQ) diagnoses; keeps 2024 CMS-HCC model. (McDermott+ https://mcdermottplus.com/blog/regs-eggs/the-final-course-for-2027-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-policies ; KFF https://www.kff.org/quick-insights/medicare-advantage-insurers-will-see-higher-payments-as-cms-backs-off-a-key-payment-update/ ). RADV: CMS announced (May 2025) auditing every eligible contract annually (~550) with ~2,000 coders; but N.D. Tex vacated the 2023 extrapolation rule 2025-09-25 (Humana v. Becerra), appeal at 5th Cir (No. 25-11293), argued ~Oct 5 2026, panel seemed poised to affirm (STAT 2026-10-05).
- **Incumbents:** Optum, Cotiviti, Episource, Inovalon, Lumeris, plus plan in-house. Fragility: the whole chart-review business model (retrospective, unlinked) is being excluded from payment; in-home assessment (HRA) vendors face DOJ scrutiny.
- **Crowdedness:** CROWDED (AI coding/CDI startups abundant). I did not run a startup list for this pool: flagged as not verified.
- **Wedge hints:** encounter-linked prospective documentation at point of care; RADV defence/medical-record retrieval for MA plans' ~550 contracts; provider-group (VBC) risk-adjustment compliance.
- **Insider roles:** MA plan risk-adjustment directors, VBC group CMOs/coding managers, RADV appeals attorneys, certified coders.

# POOL: 340B Drug Pricing Program (covered-entity savings and the rebate transition)  [hidden giant: yes]
(raw notes saved 2026-10-14)
- **Money flow:** Drugmakers sell outpatient drugs to ~50,000 covered-entity sites (hospitals ~87% of volume) at statutory ceiling prices; entities bill insurers/Medicare at full rates and keep the spread. HRSA says 340B purchases hit ~$100B in CY2025 (up ~22% from $81.4B in 2024): https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-07-14-hrsa-announces-340b-program-sales-reached-100-billion-cy-2025
- **Shock:** HRSA's first rebate pilot (10 negotiated drugs, Jan 2026) was enjoined by D. Maine (2025-12-29), upheld by 1st Cir (Jan 7), notice withdrawn Feb 2026; D.C. Circuit (Novartis v. Kennedy, 2026-07-21) held rebate models permissible but only with HHS approval; HRSA published revised pilot Aug 3 2026 (IPAY 2026+2027 drugs, 90-day advance notice) targeting Jan 1 2027; AHA + Maine Hospital Assn. sued again Oct 9 2026. Sources: https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/08/second-times-the-charm-hrsa-revives-the-340b-rebate-pilot/ ; https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/hrsas-revised-340b-rebate-model-pilot-program
- **Incumbents:** Third-party administrators and contract-pharmacy networks: Sentry/Wellpartner, Walgreens, CVS, Verity, Kalderos (rebate/duplicate-discount platform). Kalderos raised $7M Series A (2019, Mercato) and $28M Series B (2020, Bain Capital Ventures): https://venturebeat.com/ai/kalderos-raises-28-million-to-automate-drug-discount-program-compliance . Hospitals' real exposure: if rebate model starts, they must front the full price then claw back, a working-capital and claims-data problem.
- **Fragility:** Claims-level data requirement (manufacturers want 340B-flagged claims data to validate rebates) breaks the current opaque contract-pharmacy model; covered entities (rural/safety-net) have thin balance sheets and weak data teams.
- **Crowdedness:** CONTESTED (Kalderos, 340B ESP by Second Sight, plus TPAs). 2023-26 startup funding not confirmed in my searches (no 2026 raises found).
- **Wedge hints:** rebate-claims working-capital finance (advance against receivable rebates); claims-data scrubbing/validation for small covered entities; state-law compliance for contract pharmacy; duplicate-discount (Medicaid) prevention for FQHCs.
- **Insider roles:** 340B program directors at DSH hospitals/FQHCs, pharmacy directors, manufacturer 340B integrity leads, TPAs' compliance staff, health-system CFO/treasury.

# POOL: Medicaid home- and community-based services (personal care / home care agencies + fiscal intermediaries)  [hidden giant: yes]
(raw notes saved 2026-10-14)
- **Money flow:** State Medicaid agencies (and MCOs in managed-LTSS states) pay ~tens of thousands of small home care agencies and fiscal-intermediary (FI) payroll firms per hour of care; most revenue is wage pass-through to aides.
- **Size:** CMS: in 2022, 7.8M HCBS users accounted for $129.4B Medicaid spend (+12.5% y/y) (CMS briefs of Nov 2024 via LeadingAge NY https://www.leadingageny.org/topics/data/datapoints-archive/datapoint-cms-releases-updated-ltss-user-and-expenditure-data , verified in text). RealClearInvestigations (2026-05-14): personal-care program spend ~$46.4B in 2024, nearly doubled since 2019; NY alone $15.67B (mostly consumer-directed CDPAP). https://www.realclearinvestigations.com/articles/2026/05/14/unbridled_spending_billions_for_medicaid_program_congress_never_approved_1182576.html
- **Shock:** (1) H.R.1 cuts federal Medicaid by $911B over 2025-34 (KFF) and states historically respond by cutting HCBS (40 states cut enrollment, 47 cut rates/benefits last time) https://www.kff.org/medicaid/states-management-of-medicaid-home-care-spending-ahead-of-h-r-1-effects/ ; (2) immigration enforcement: foreign-born are >40% of home health aides, ~30% of personal care workers (US gov data via American Immigration Council / Staffing Industry Analysts); (3) NY forced all ~600 FIs into one statewide FI (PPL) on 2025-04-01 for ~250k CDPAP consumers, Hochul claims >$1B savings (Jan 2026), contested by Fiscal Policy Institute; DOJ reportedly investigating the rollout (Home Health Care News 2025-06); (4) 80/20 wage pass-through rule (Access Rule, effective 2030) still law; no rescission found.
- **Incumbents:** Public Partnerships LLC (PPL), GT Independence, Acumen/Tempus (FIs); agency chains Addus, BrightSpring, Amedisys; software: HHAeXchange (EVV, dominant), WellSky, AlayaCare, ClearCare/WellSky, AxisCare, Axxess. 
- **Fragility:** fragmented (thousands of sub-$20M agencies on 3-6% margins), state payment delays, EVV compliance, no access to working capital; states squeezing rates while labor supply shrinks.
- **Funded startups seen:** AlayaCare (C$225M Series D 2021; debt financing Feb 2026), MochaCare (~$1M accelerator, Mar 2026), Enzo Health ($20M Series A, May 2026, home health/post-acute AI); no large VC-funded Medicaid FI/payroll challenger found. Crowdedness: CONTESTED on software (many mid-size vendors), OPEN on financing/payroll/back-office for Medicaid agencies.
- **Wedge hints:** working-capital advance against Medicaid receivables for agencies; FI/payroll for consumer-directed programs in states that will RFP statewide FIs after NY; EVV-to-claims reconciliation; caregiver workforce pipeline (visa-independent) for agencies.
- **Insider roles:** home care agency owners/administrators, state Medicaid long-term-care program managers, FI operations VPs, EVV compliance leads, HCBS waiver case managers.

# POOL: Clinical laboratory testing (independent, hospital-outreach and reference labs; Medicare CLFS cuts)  [hidden giant: yes]
(raw notes saved 2026-10-14)
- **Money flow:** Payers (Medicare Part B, MA, commercial, Medicaid) pay labs per test; labs fight for payment/coverage; Medicare rate set by PAMA private-payer-rate survey.
- **Size:** US clinical lab services ~ $99.7B revenue in 2024, projected $122.5B by 2029 (Hyde Park Capital, secondary; https://www.hydeparkcapital.com/insights/industry-reports/laboratory-and-diagnostic-services-market-insights-summer-2025/ ); Medicare Part B lab spend $8.4B in 2024 per HHS OIG as cited by MarkNtel (secondary), genetic tests 43% of spend. Narrow-definition market-research numbers ($10B) are NOT comparable.
- **Shock:** CAA 2026 (signed 2026-02-03) delayed CLFS cuts again (7th delay), moved data-collection period to 2025; CMS preliminary 2027 CLFS would cut ~15% on ~775 tests (ACLA) / ~1,200 tests with deeper cuts 2028-29; RESULTS Act (third-party claims database to set rates) pending, House in recess until after Nov 2026 midterms. https://www.medtechdive.com/news/lab-test-providers-want-medicare-payment-reform-will-congress-pass-it/831384/ ; ACLA https://www.acla.com/acla-welcomes-pama-relief-in-funding-package-urges-congress-to-advance-results-act-in-2026/ 
- **Incumbents:** Quest, Labcorp (consolidating hospital outreach labs), plus thousands of regional independent and specialty/genetic labs; billing: XIFIN, Sunquest, Orchard, and offshore RCM shops.
- **Fragility:** independent labs have thin margins and are exposed to payer underpayment/coverage (MolDX LCDs) and the PAMA cliff; hospital systems selling outreach labs.
- **Funded startups:** not verified (no lab-RCM startup list obtained in my searches). Crowdedness: UNVERIFIED, likely OPEN-to-CONTESTED on lab-specific payer-underpayment recovery.
- **Wedge hints:** lab-specific claims underpayment/denial recovery on contingency; payer-rate benchmarking for labs (PAMA 2025 data submission); outreach-lab carve-out/transition services for health systems.
- **Insider roles:** independent-lab CFOs/billing managers, hospital outreach-lab directors, MolDX/coverage consultants, lab sales-rep managers.

# POOL: Rural Health Transformation Program (state grant money, $10B/yr)  [hidden giant: semi]
(raw notes saved 2026-10-14)
- **Money flow:** CMS -> 50 states (formula: half equal, half CMS-scored) -> state agencies -> providers, vendors, workforce programs; funded by H.R.1 Sec. 71401, $10B/yr FY2026-FY2030.
- **Size:** $10B per year, $50B total. FY2026 awards: $147M (NJ) to $281M (TX), avg ~$200M (CMS announcement 2025-12-29). https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/cms-announces-50-billion-in-awards-to-strengthen-rural-health-in-all-50-states.html 
- **Shock/timing:** States must obligate year-one funds within ~10 months and spend within one more year or risk claw-back and redistribution (Manatt 2026-02-09 https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/health-highlights/rural-health-transformation-program-what-s-next-considerations-as-states-begin-implementation ); workplans finalized Mar-Jun 2026, RFPs posting Jul 2026-Q1 2027, contracts through Q2 2027 (Mindbowser, vendor source, low reliability). Offsets Medicaid cuts (KFF framing).
- **Incumbents:** state agencies, big consultancies (Deloitte, Guidehouse, Accenture), EHR vendors (Epic, Oracle), telehealth vendors, rural hospital associations.
- **Fragility:** states "spend fast" under claw-back pressure, creating many isolated pilots; no established vendor for "RHT-compliant" outcome tracking; rural hospitals lack grant-writing/IT capacity.
- **Funded startups:** none found specifically dedicated to RHT (no direct sources); adjacent: rural telehealth/hospital-at-home vendors. Crowdedness: OPEN.
- **Wedge hints:** grant-management/compliance and outcome reporting platform sold to state RHT offices (50 buyers, $200M each); rural-hospital subgrant application services; shared-services (IT/cyber/RCM) for critical-access hospitals paid out of RHT funds.
- **Honest caveat:** 5-year grant, then cliff; the addressable vendor share of $10B/yr is unknown (admin cap and provider-payment caps exist in statute but I did not verify the percentages).

Senate HSGAC letters to Humana/UHC/CVS 2026-07-14. CMS interoperability/prior-auth API rule deadlines Jan 2027. MA denials = ~7% net revenue reduction for SNFs (Skilled Nursing News). 
- **Incumbents:** R1 RCM, Waystar, Optum, Ensemble, Cotiviti; payers' AI-driven UM (naviHealth nH Predict).
- **Funded startups (2025-26):** Amperos Health ($16M Series A, Apr 2026, Bessemer; $4.2M seed Jun 2025), Candid Health ($120M Series D, Sixth Street), RapidClaims ($11M, Accel, Apr 2025), Basalt Health ($20M Series A, NEA, SNF/post-acute admissions & PA), ExaCare AI ($30M Series A Insight, SNF referral/admissions), Willow (YC, LTC RCM, Feb 2026), Clinware ($4.25M seed), Cascala ($8.6M seed 2025), Enzo Health ($20M Series A May 2026); R1 acquired Humata Health (prior auth). Crowdedness: CROWDED for hospital denials; CONTESTED-to-OPEN for SNF/post-acute-specific MA denials.
- **Wedge hints:** SNF/home-health-specific MA denial appeals on contingency (95% overturn rate means high win rate); payer-contract compliance auditing for long-term care operators.
- **Insider roles:** hospital denial-management directors, SNF administrators/business office managers, MA appeals nurses, payer medical directors.

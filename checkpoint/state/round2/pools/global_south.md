# Big money pools: high-growth markets outside the US and Europe (round 2, mid-October 2026)

Slice: India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Brazil, Mexico, Gulf. Nine pools mapped, five of them hidden giants. Research date 2026-10-14.

**Evidence labels.** (F) = I opened the page and read it. (S) = figure came from a search-result summary only; verify before relying on it. Exchange rates are approximate: ~Rp15,900/$ (the source's own conversion), ~INR 92/$ (implied by a Jan 2026 RBI conversion), ~R$5.5/$.

**Honest summary.** The two cleanest pools (Indonesia free meals, India gold loans) are real, huge and disturbed by dated shocks. In both, a state actor (BGN's own marketplace, RBI's own-branch rules) limits what a startup can do. Brazil's farm-input credit and Mexico's cross-border freight are big, boring and fragmented, but have funded players. The remittance pool is crowded and the Gulf construction pool is shrinking and has a state-owned platform competitor. Treat those two as "know about", not "build".

## Pool 1. Indonesia: the free-meals programme (MBG) money flowing through ~27,000 community kitchens  [HIDDEN GIANT]

- **Money flow.** Indonesian Treasury -> National Nutrition Agency (BGN) -> per-meal payments to ~27,000 privately financed kitchens (SPPG) run by "partners" (foundations, police-linked groups, businessmen, Kadin) -> ~70% of each kitchen's operating budget buys ingredients (eggs, chicken, rice, vegetables, oil, milk) from farmers, traders, cooperatives and village firms (BUMDes) -> meals to schoolchildren, pregnant women and toddlers. BGN says it has made suppliers' purchases flow through cooperatives and BUMDes (rule reported Dec 2025).
- **Size.** 2027 draft budget Rp240 trillion = $15.09B for 60.6M beneficiaries, announced by the finance minister on 14 Aug 2026 (F, investortrust.id). 2026 allocation Rp335T (~$21B), with Rp153.2T disbursed by end-Sept 2026 and 57M beneficiaries served via 27,486 kitchens (S, search summary of ANTARA/Wikipedia-type coverage). Derived: Rp240T / 27,000 kitchens = Rp8.9B (~$560k) per kitchen per year; ~70% on ingredients (S, Bisnis.com-reported BGN figure) = ~$390k per kitchen, **~$10B a year of ingredient purchasing nationally**. Private capital already sunk in kitchen buildings: Rp54T (S, BGN via Bisnis.com, Apr 2026).
- **Shock (2026-08 and after).**
  - Poisoning cases: independent monitor JPPI counted 5,360 cases by Sept 2025 (S); BGN had suspended 56 kitchens and then shut 1,276 kitchens lacking a hygiene certificate (SLHS) (S).
  - 13 Aug 2026 directive and Berastagi (N. Sumatra) school poisoning: BGN considering rapid chemical/bacterial test kits for every kitchen, teachers must reject unfit meals, negligent civil-servant kitchen heads face dismissal (F).
  - 12 Sept 2026: BGN announced its own raw-materials marketplace (beef/buffalo, chicken, eggs, rice, cooking oil, milk), to launch late Sept / early Oct 2026 with association-vetted "verified sellers" (F, Veritask).
  - July 2026: BGN paused new kitchen construction; a partner association says up to Rp8.7T of private investment is at risk and ~13,000 kitchens could stall (S).
  - A 2026 IPB study in South Tangerang found only 44% of kitchens sampled held the SLHS hygiene certificate (S).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** There is no incumbent buyer; the "incumbents" are the kitchen partners, local wet-market traders, cooperatives and BUMDes. BGN is a young agency running manual inspections and paper-heavy reporting, with disbursement lags (only ~46% of the 2026 budget spent by end-Sept). Kitchen owners carry bank loans (e.g., BSI financed Rp181B for 127 kitchens, ~Rp1.4B each (S)) while policy swings. The agency cannot easily inspect 27,000 sites, so it is adding rules faster than it can monitor them.
- **Crowdedness: OPEN for the kitchen-operations / food-safety / working-capital layers, but with a state-platform risk.** Searched English and Indonesian: found no venture-funded startup specifically serving SPPG kitchens (S). BGN is building its own commodity marketplace, which could squeeze a private procurement marketplace to the margins. Adjacent Indonesian food-supply-chain startups exist (Sayurbox, Eden Farm, Chickin and others from my background knowledge, not verified this run).
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Kitchen operating system: menu costing to BGN's cost cap, inventory, daily reporting in BGN's format, SLHS certification tracking. (2) Food-safety testing and certification service for kitchens (inspection, lab testing, test kits, audit logs). (3) Working-capital and invoice finance for kitchens waiting on BGN disbursements, underwritten on the BGN payment stream. (4) Cold-chain and last-mile ingredient logistics to rural kitchens. (5) Supplier-side tooling for cooperatives/BUMDes that BGN now forces kitchens to buy from.
- **Bottom-up check (my arithmetic).** $10B ingredient flow x 1% = $100M but needs essentially every kitchen. SaaS at $150/month x 27,000 = $49M at 100% share. Testing at $1,500 per kitchen-year = $40M at 100% share. Reaching $100M realistically needs 2-3 stacked layers or a balance sheet. Flag: the programme itself could be cut or re-scoped (the 2026 spend is running well below the allocation, and the 2027 draft is lower than 2026's allocation).
- **Insider roles.** SPPG kitchen heads and nutritionists (ahli gizi), BGN regional coordinators, school principals and UKS officers, village cooperative managers, egg and chicken traders, Puskesmas sanitarians (environmental health officers), BPOM/Dinkes inspectors, bank (BSI/BRI) kitchen-loan officers.
- **Sources.** https://investortrust.id/national/113274/food-poisoning-scare-rattles-indonesias-15-billion-free-meal-program-as-agency-tightens-kitchen-oversight (F); https://veritask.ai/en/artikel/mbg-marketplace-to-be-launched-soon-who-can-become-a-supplier (F); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Nutritious_Meals (S); https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20260416/12/1966996/bgn-investasi-dapur-mbg-tembus-rp54-triliun-total-27000-unit-sppg (S); https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20251208/12/1935119/bgn-sebut-dapur-mbg-wajib-beli-bahan-pangan-dari-koperasi (S); https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/consumer-services/statistics/68d108f2386ba/over-5360-cases-of-mbg-poisoning-reported-by-september-2025 (S).

## Pool 2. India: interest and fees households and small businesses pay on gold-backed loans  [HIDDEN GIANT]

- **Money flow.** Borrowers (farmers, shopkeepers, small traders, households, pledging family jewellery) -> pay interest and fees to banks and NBFCs (Muthoot, Manappuram, IIFL, SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Federal, South Indian) and informal pawnbrokers. Lenders store the gold in branch vaults, value it by in-house appraisers and auction it on default.
- **Size.** Outstanding gold loans ~INR18.6 lakh crore in March 2026 per bureau data (Experian/CRIF), growing 50% year on year; ~INR20 lakh crore by Sept 2026; RBI governor says no overheating (S). That is ~$200-215B. At a blended yield of ~10% (NBFC rates are far higher than bank rates) the interest pool is **~$20B a year**, ~$26B at a 13% blend. Caveats: RBI's own bank-only series showed just ~INR4 trillion in Jan 2026 ($43B), so "total" depends on definitions (banks-only vs. NBFC vs. agri-gold loans reclassified), and the sources conflict (S).
- **Shock (2026-04).** RBI's "Lending against Gold and Silver Collateral Directions, 2025" were notified 6 June 2025 and had to be adopted by 1 April 2026 (F, Vinod Kothari). Key terms: LTV caps of 85% (up to INR2.5 lakh), 80% (to INR5 lakh), 75% above; bullet consumption loans capped at 12 months; gold must be handled at the lender's own branches by its own employees, and branches without a secured facility cannot lend; mandatory purity/assaying standards; formal auction SOP with empanelled auctioneers; INR5,000 per day compensation for late release of collateral. Plus a gold price run-up (one outlet puts the spot peak near $5,595/oz on 29 Jan 2026, then a ~27% fall; single commentator, unverified (S)). NBFCs' share of retail gold loans rose from 28% to 40% in a year (S).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Muthoot (market cap passed INR1 trillion in June 2025), Manappuram (Bain Capital approved to take up to ~41.7% (S)) and banks make money on branch networks, cheap local trust and high NBFC yields. They are strong, not fragile. What is fragile is the manual process underneath: appraisal by staff, physical vaults, paper auctions, appraiser fraud (fake or under-carat gold), and a thin price buffer if gold falls (one commentator estimates 7-8% (S)). The own-branch rule also blocks the "doorstep gold loan" outsourcing model and makes third-party sourcing harder.
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED.** Rupeek (Peak XV-backed; >$164M raised in total; 2024 down round at ~INR1,785 crore valuation; profit after tax INR15 crore in H1 of the current FY; reportedly seeking $50M in 2026; works through Federal Bank, South Indian Bank, Axis; co-lending with Indian Bank) (S). IndiaGold: could not verify. The strongest fact for an entrant: even the best-funded gold-loan startup is a profitable but modest business, because the lenders own the customer and the vault.
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Auction and NPA-disposal marketplace to the new SOP standard. (2) Purity and appraisal tech (XRF/handheld assaying, appraiser certification, photo/weight/evidence capture), which the Directions make mandatory. (3) Compliance tooling for banks' rural branches to add gold lending safely (secure-facility certification, LTV monitoring across the loan's life, since LTV must hold through the tenor). (4) Loan-takeover / balance-transfer rails as borrowers move from NBFCs to cheaper banks. (5) Fraud and fake-gold intelligence shared across lenders.
- **Bottom-up check.** Service-layer revenue pools are a small slice of the $20B interest pool; e.g., if annual disbursals are ~2x the stock (~INR40 lakh crore, ~$430B, my assumption on tenor), a 0.25% service take is ~$1B and a 10% share is ~$100M. Assumption-heavy; do not treat as validated.
- **Insider roles.** Branch gold appraisers (assayers) at Muthoot / Manappuram, NBFC auction and collections heads, rural bank branch managers, small-town jewellers and pawnbrokers, BIS hallmarking-centre operators, lender internal-audit and compliance heads, gold refiners.
- **Sources.** https://vinodkothari.com/2025/06/a-pledge-of-prudence-new-gold-lending-directions-keep-it-balanced-non-discriminatory-and-flexible/ (F); https://www.kotakneo.com/news/sectors/gold-loans-reach-20-lakh-crore-but-rbi-sees-no-signs-of-overheating/ (S); https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/muthoot-finance-rallies-10-in-2-days-mcap-crosses-1-trillion-here-s-why-125060900184_1.html (S); https://inc42.com/?p=457087 (S, Rupeek down round).

## Pool 3. Brazil: what companies spend each year to comply with taxes, now being rebuilt around a dual VAT  [HIDDEN GIANT]

- **Money flow.** ~Millions of Brazilian firms -> pay accountants (contadores), ERP and fiscal-document software vendors (TOTVS, SAP, Senior, Sankhya, Omie, Conta Azul), tax consultancies and in-house tax teams, to compute, invoice and file taxes; from 2026 they must also re-engineer systems for the new CBS (federal) and IBS (state/municipal) VAT that replaces PIS, Cofins, IPI, ICMS and ISS by 2033.
- **Size (soft).** Published estimates conflict: FEA-USP "over R$30B a year"; IPEA-hosted study 0.75% of GDP, "about R$50B" (~$9B) on older GDP (S); at today's GDP 0.75% would be higher, maybe $15B+; a Deloitte figure of R$181B is an outlier I could not verify (S). FIESP says industry spends 1.2% of revenue on tax computation and payment (S). I use **~$9B** (R$50B) as a floor-ish figure. Needs a primary source before any pitch.
- **Shock (2026-01).** LC 214/2025 set the IBS/CBS rules; 2026 is a test year (symbolic rates: CBS 0.9%, IBS 0.1%, shown on invoices and offset against PIS/Cofins); invoices (NF-e / NFS-e) must carry the new fields; full CBS charging from 2027; the old and new regimes run in parallel until 2033, so every ERP must support both (S, vendor and law-firm blogs, so commercial bias). One ERP vendor says invoices without the fields are rejected from 3 Aug 2026 (single vendor claim, unverified).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** ERP vendors and accounting offices bill for change. Parallel regimes, still-moving regulations (the Comitê Gestor of the IBS and Receita are issuing rules), and credit-tracking under the new non-cumulative VAT (a buyer's credit depends on the supplier having paid) create work the vendors' legacy customisations handle poorly. They can respond, so this is not a "cannot respond" pool; the opening is speed and the mid-market that has no in-house tax team.
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED to CROWDED for SME invoicing and accounting** (Spedy grew 33% in Q1 2026 on reform-driven demand (S); Omie, Conta Azul, Contabilizei, Arquivei, Dootax, Avalara and Sovos operate; some from background knowledge). OPEN for reform-specific tooling (split-payment reconciliation, supplier-compliance monitoring, credit tracking), but incumbents will ship those quickly.
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Tax-rules engine as an API for mid-market ERPs lacking one. (2) Credit and supplier-compliance monitoring under IBS/CBS. (3) Transition audits and credit recovery on contingency fees. (4) Distributors and retailers with heavy ICMS-ST complexity. (5) Simples Nacional / hybrid-regime optimisation for small firms.
- **Bottom-up check.** If ~1M mid-market firms (my assumption) paid $1,000-2,000 a year for rule-engine/credit-tracking software, the pool is $1-2B and $100M needs 5-10% share. Plausible but the product is exposed to ERP vendors bundling it free.
- **Insider roles.** Tax controllers at mid-market distributors, accountants (contadores) at SME accounting firms, ERP implementation consultants (TOTVS / SAP partners), auditores fiscais at SEFAZ, tax attorneys (tributaristas), Comitê Gestor / Receita working-group members.
- **Sources.** https://www.senior.com.br/blog/reforma-tributaria-em-2026 (S); https://www.conjur.com.br/2024-dez-27/custo-de-conformidade-da-arrecadacao-tributaria-no-brasil//?print=1 (S, cites IPEA 0.75% and R$50B); https://mundorh.com.br/como-reduzir-custos-da-conformidade-fiscal (S, FEA-USP R$30B); https://startupi.com.br/pedy-refaz-planos-para-2026/ (S, Spedy).

## Pool 4. Brazil: farmers' annual purchases of crop inputs, 72% on credit extended by input retailers, manufacturers and cooperatives  [HIDDEN GIANT]

- **Money flow.** Farmers -> buy seeds, fertiliser and crop protection from retailers (revendas), direct from manufacturers and from cooperatives. About 72% is sold on credit; farmers repay at harvest in cash or in grain (barter / CPR). Retailers fund themselves from manufacturer terms, banks and capital markets (FIDCs, CRAs).
- **Size.** Input-industry revenue R$112.7B in 2024 (~$20B), R$80.8B of it (72%) involving credit; resale channel carries R$44.3B of that credit, direct sales R$22.7B, cooperatives R$13.8B; barter R$13B (S, CropLife Brasil / Markestrat mapping, July 2025). Distributor-side data (Andav, 2025): R$171B total revenue, R$105B in inputs, +2.4% (S); different scope, do not add. Outstanding CPR stock R$565B (S, year uncertain).
- **Shock (2026-03).** Record agribusiness judicial-recovery (bankruptcy-protection) filings: 1,990 in 2025, +56.4% (Serasa Experian), and 474 in Q1 2026, +21.9% (S). Retailers' own filings rose 32.4% in a year (RGF) and three major chains (Agrogalaxy, Lavoro, Belagricola) are restructuring, with ~R$5B of creditor losses (S). Drivers: high interest rates, dollar-linked input costs and weaker soy/corn prices. Private Plano Safra resources ~20% below last year at the same point (S). Courts: physical CPRs are protected from recovery proceedings (Law 14.112/2020); a CNJ provision in 2026 reportedly reinforces that for barter structures (commentary, unverified).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Retail chains and manufacturers (Syngenta, Bayer, BASF, Nutrien, Yara, Mosaic, Corteva) are lending without bank-grade credit data, with thin visibility into a farmer's total debt, collateral and planted area. Retailers are now both victims and the transmission channel of the stress, and they cannot easily fund more risk.
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED.** Traive (about $48M raised; $20M round with Banco do Brasil's CVC and Astella; ~$84M post-money; sells credit analytics and registry to input supply chains), Agrolend ($21M; works through resellers, applied for a central bank licence), plus others I did not verify (S). Rounds found are from 2023-24; I found no 2025-26 raise.
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Farm-level credit and collateral monitoring (satellite plus registry cross-checks) sold to retailers and distributors as a risk-management tool. (2) Physical-CPR / barter origination and registry compliance so retailers can move to legally safer structures. (3) Recovery and workout services for the R$ of defaulted farm debt. (4) Receivables purchase / FIDC infrastructure for mid-size retailers shut out of bank credit.
- **Bottom-up check.** R$80.8B of credit sales; a 1% fee/spread-equivalent on it is R$0.8B (~$150M). A $100M revenue line needs a risk-product or finance spread on roughly R$30-40B of volume, i.e., a large share. Risk: lending on the balance sheet into a defaulting sector; only data/software sales avoid that, and these are small. Be sceptical.
- **Insider roles.** Input-retailer credit managers (analistas de crédito), cooperative agronomists, agribusiness bankruptcy lawyers and judicial administrators, FIDC managers, rural-credit officers at Banco do Brasil / Sicredi, grain-trading originators.
- **Sources.** https://cooperativismodecredito.coop.br/2026/03/recuperacoes-judiciais-no-agronegocio-atingem-recorde-em-2025/ (S); https://www.canalrural.com.br/economia/recuperacao-judicial-no-agro-dispara-e-bate-recorde-em-2025-aponta-serasa-experian/ (S); https://www.theagribiz.com/credito-rural/crise-das-revendas-agricolas-ja-destruiu-r-5-bi-dos-credores/ (S); https://croplifebrasil.org/cropdata/arquivos/boletim.pdf (S); https://agfeed.com.br/grande-slam-do-agro/andav/setor-de-distribuicao-de-insumos-fatura-mais-mas-ritmo-desacelera-mostra-levantamento (S); https://agfundernews.com/agrolend-aims-to-connect-more-farmers-in-brazil-with-much-needed-credit-in-latest-21m-raise (S); https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/noticias-sobre-fintechs/traive-capta-uss-17-milhoes-com-lideranca-de-sp-ventures-e-astella (S).

## Pool 5. Mexico: freight, customs-brokerage and compliance spend on US-Mexico truck trade  [HIDDEN GIANT]

- **Money flow.** Importers/exporters (auto parts, electronics, appliances, ag) -> pay truckers, drayage carriers, freight forwarders (C.H. Robinson, Kuehne+Nagel, DHL, Expeditors, Uber Freight / Transplace) and licensed customs brokers (agentes aduanales, who hold a government "patente") for line-haul, border crossing, customs clearance, bonds, insurance.
- **Size (derived, assumption-heavy).** US-Mexico trade was $872.8B in 2025, +3.9% (S, AJOT summary of BTS annual report). Trucks carry the majority: BTS shows ~$46.1B of US-Mexico truck freight in Sept 2025 alone, which annualises to ~$550B (S). Door-to-door cross-border logistics plus brokerage typically runs 3-5% of goods value (**my assumption, not sourced**), giving **~$16-28B; I use $20B**. Needs a real freight-revenue source before it is used in a pitch.
- **Shock (2026-01).** Mexico put tariffs of up to 50% on Chinese and other Asian imports from 1 Jan 2026 (S, C.H. Robinson / FreightWaves), a customs-law overhaul that adds duties and heavy liability to licensed brokers (S, Benesch via FreightWaves), the US 25% tariff on goods that do not qualify under USMCA, and the USMCA joint review (July 2026; no outcome found in my search). Rules-of-origin and "substantial transformation" scrutiny is rising. Cross-border carriers reported near-zero growth in 2025 and rates were expected flat in early 2026 (S).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Brokers (patente-holders) are a regulated, fragmented guild (hundreds of agencies, mostly paper and phone workflows); thousands of small carriers; a few big 3PLs. The new liability and origin regime raises the compliance load on exactly the segment least able to automate. The patente is a legal moat: a startup must partner with or acquire a broker rather than replace one.
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED.** Nuvocargo (digital forwarder + customs broker; $5.3M seed, $12M Series A, $20.5M Tiger-led round Dec 2021, $36.5M Series B in 2023 at >$250M valuation; reported unverified $10M convertible June 2025; acquired Mentum Nov 2025; launched "Nuvo AI" March 2026) is the funded leader (S). Flexport and the big 3PLs sell cross-border services. No evidence of anyone owning the mid-market exporter's origin-compliance problem.
- **Wedge hints.** (1) USMCA origin-qualification and certificate management for mid-sized exporters and IMMEX plants. (2) Broker-liability and audit tooling for agencies (post-reform record keeping, classification). (3) Drayage and border-capacity marketplace for small Mexican carriers. (4) Duty and tariff-engineering analytics for importers. (5) Financing for duties, bonds and freight (trade finance at the border).
- **Bottom-up check.** $20B pool; $100M is 0.5% of spend if revenue is gross freight margin, or a larger share if it is software/fees only. Needs a precise mapping of take rates before relying on it.
- **Insider roles.** Agentes aduanales and their pedimento clerks, IMMEX trade-compliance managers at maquilas, Laredo/Nuevo Laredo freight forwarders, drayage dispatchers, customs attorneys, plant logistics managers, bonded-warehouse operators.
- **Sources.** https://www.freightwaves.com/news/borderlands-mexico-customs-overhaul-may-reshape-cross-border-manufacturing-expert-says (S); https://www.chrobinson.com/en-au/resources/insights-and-advisories/north-america-freight-insights/jan-2026-freight-market-update/cross-border/ (S); https://www.ajot.com/news/transborder-freight-data-annual-report-2025 (S); https://www.bts.gov/newsroom/north-american-transborder-freight-increased-12-december-2025-december-2024 (S); https://latamlist.com/nuvocargo-secures-36-5m-in-series-b-funding-round/ (S); https://www.freightwaves.com/news/nuvocargo-targets-mexico-growth-with-new-hires (S).

## Pool 6. Nigeria: what households and businesses spend on petrol and diesel generators  [not a hidden giant; energy founders already watch it]

- **Money flow.** Households, shops, hospitals, factories, bank branches -> buy petrol/diesel and gensets (Mikano, Sumec Firman, Perkins distributors, local dealers) because the grid (DisCos) is unreliable.
- **Size.** Most-cited: **$14B a year on generator fuel** (attributed to Akinwumi Adesina / IMF in Nigerian press); $10B a year (SEforALL, possibly Lagos-only) (S). Naira terms: ~N16 trillion in 2023 on self-generation fuel (S). Excludes generator purchases, maintenance and downtime. I use **$12B** (mid-range). The numbers come from press quoting older estimates; verify at source.
- **Shock (2026-06).** NERC published Net Billing Regulations on 3 June 2026 letting prosumers sell surplus solar to DisCos (S, BusinessDay). Under the Electricity Act 2023 states can run their own markets. Band A customers pay ~N220-225/kWh for a promised 20 hours (S), with a Feb-Mar 2026 compensation directive for shortfalls. Capital arrives: $300M Distributed Renewable Energy Fund (NSIA and Africa50) launched 21 Sept 2026, with $25M from the World Bank (S). A solar-panel import ban was proposed and is unsettled (S). Fuel is dollar-linked and has been volatile.
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Fuel retailers, depots, genset dealers, DisCos. DisCos are financially weak and cannot deliver reliability; genset economics only held while solar plus batteries were expensive.
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED, and capital is flowing in.** Arnergy ($18M Series B incl. $15M extension, 2025), Starsight ($15M BII mezzanine, March 2026; 500+ sites, 36MW), Daystar, Rensource, Westa Solar, Sun King and CrossBoundary (not verified in this run) (S). Nigeria installed ~803 MW of solar in 2025, second in Africa (S).
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Small-business (10-50 kW) solar+battery lease for pharmacies, cold rooms, print shops, clinics, underwritten on prior diesel spend. (2) Post-installation O&M and monitoring across heterogeneous installs. (3) Embedded financing sold through banks and genset dealers. (4) Net-billing metering/settlement software for DisCos and prosumers.
- **Bottom-up check.** 1% of $12B = $120M; 2-3% share gets to $250M+, so the ceiling is high, but capital intensity (asset finance in naira risk) is the real constraint, not the market size.
- **Insider roles.** Facility managers at banks and hospitals, genset technicians, solar EPC installers, DisCo metering engineers, NERC tariff analysts, estate managers in Lagos and Abuja.
- **Sources.** https://businessday.ng/energy/article/new-law-allows-nigerians-sell-surplus-solar-power-to-discos-in-historic-shift/ (S); https://businessday.ng/energy/power/article/running-on-empty-inside-nigerias-multibillion-dollar-generator-economy/ (S); https://21stcenturychronicle.com/nigeria-spends-14bn-on-generators-fuel-yearly-akinwumi/ (S); https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/africa-press-releases/nigeria-opens-a-300-million-fund-for-distributed-solar-and-mini-grids-1509944 (S); https://nairametrics.com/2026/03/16/starsight-energy-africa-group-starsight-partners-with-british-international-investment-bii-to-advance-clean-energy-growth-in-west-africa-through-us15-million-m/ (S); https://nextbillion.net/news/nigerias-arnergy-raises-18-million-to-scale-solar-lease-plans-as-fuel-and-grid-costs-soar (S).

## Pool 7. Emerging markets: fees and FX margin that migrants pay to send money home (US to Mexico; Gulf to South Asia, Philippines, Egypt)  [CROWDED]

- **Money flow.** Migrant senders -> pay a fee plus an FX spread to remitters (Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, Remitly, Wise), exchange houses (Gulf), banks, and card/wallet rails. Recipients get the net.
- **Size.** Remittances to low- and middle-income countries ~$685B in 2024 (S, World Bank via HumanProgress). World Bank average cost 6.49% (Q1 2025; $200 benchmark, probably) (S). 6.49% x $685B = ~$44B at face; large transfers are cheaper and digital players charge less, so I use **~$30B** for the fee-plus-FX pool (global, above the $15B threshold). Mexico alone received $61.79B in 2025, down from $64.75B in 2024: the first annual fall since 2013 (S, LatinNews/El Pais).
- **Shock (2026-01).** US 1% federal excise tax on remittances funded with cash, money orders or cashier's cheques (IRC section 4475; transfers from bank accounts and US-issued cards exempt), effective 1 Jan 2026; proposed regs April 2026, comment close 12 June 2026, no final rule found (S, CBH). Real effect on Mexico looks small: 98.6% of Mexican remittances were electronic in Jan 2026, so only ~1.2% were cash or in-kind (S, Banxico via El Imparcial); the bigger drivers are US immigration enforcement and labour-market effects. Mexico flows fell 3.6% in Aug 2026 (S).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Agent-network incumbents earn on cash-in and FX spread and are being undercut by app-based players. Not fragile in aggregate.
- **Crowdedness: CROWDED.** Remitly, Wise, LemFi, Felix Pago, Nala, Wave and many others (from background knowledge; not re-verified in this run). Skip unless there is an unusual angle.
- **Wedge hints.** Weak. Possible: cash-to-digital conversion in stores serving cash-heavy corridors (the tax targets cash); payout rails and compliance for Gulf-to-South-Asia worker wage-to-wallet flows; B2B payout for platforms paying migrant workers.
- **Insider roles.** Exchange-house branch managers (UAE, Saudi), MSB compliance officers, corner-store remittance agents in Mexico, correspondent-bank relationship managers, labour-recruitment agents in the Gulf.
- **Sources.** https://www.cbh.com/insights/articles/excise-tax-on-remittance-transfers-irc-section-4475-explained/ (S); https://humanprogress.org/remittances-to-poor-countries-reached-685-billion-in-2024/ (S); https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/rpw_main_report_and_annex_q125_1_0.pdf (S); https://www.elimparcial.com/dinero/2026/03/02/remesas-a-mexico-arrancan-2026-con-caida-envios-desde-estados-unidos-bajan-14-en-enero-reporta-banxico/ (S).

## Pool 8. India: health-insurance claims that insurers pay to hospitals

- **Money flow.** Policyholders -> premiums -> general and standalone health insurers (Star Health, Niva Bupa, Care, HDFC Ergo, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard) -> claims paid to hospitals (cashless, ~66% of claim value) or to patients (reimbursement ~29%) -> via third-party administrators (TPAs handled ~69% of claims by number).
- **Size.** IRDAI annual report, FY2024-25: 32.6 million claims settled; **INR94,248 crore paid (~$10-11B)**, up from INR83,493 crore; 87% settled, 8% repudiated, 5% pending; average payout INR28,910 (F, IndiaMedToday citing IRDAI annual report). FY2023-24: government said INR26,037 crore of claims were rejected (S).
- **Shock (2025-09).** In Aug 2025 AHPI (private hospitals' body) told North India hospitals to suspend cashless treatment for Bajaj Allianz from 1 Sept over unrevised tariffs, unilateral deductions and delayed settlements; a truce followed in September with the cost dispute unresolved (S). IRDAI is pushing toward 100% cashless with pre-authorisation within one hour and discharge approval within three hours (S). Complaints on the Bima Bharosa portal rose from 47,658 (FY24) to 64,365 (FY25) and 73,729 in FY26 through Feb 2026 (S). GST was also removed on individual health-insurance premiums on 22 Sept 2025 (my background knowledge; not verified in this run).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Insurers and TPAs run adjudication on PDFs, call centres and hospital-by-hospital rate cards. The public National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX) is a standardised-data public rail that could commoditise the plumbing (I did not find its current status; do not skip checking it).
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED.** Medi Assist (listed TPA), Plum, Loop, Onsurity, Even (from background knowledge; not verified this run). Hospital-side claims and RCM automation startups exist but I did not name or size them.
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Hospital-side claims desk for 10-100-bed hospitals with no revenue-cycle team: pre-auth packaging, discharge documentation, dispute appeals. (2) Contingency-fee recovery of disallowed or short-paid claims. (3) Rate-card and deduction analytics sold to hospital chains and associations negotiating with insurers.
- **Bottom-up check.** Disallowed + repudiated claims run ~INR26,000 crore (~$3B) a year (S). Recovering 20% on a 15% contingency is ~$90M a year at 100% penetration, which is thin; $100M needs the broader RCM budget, not just recoveries. Mark this pool as medium-quality.
- **Insider roles.** Hospital billing and insurance-desk managers, TPA claim adjudicators and medical officers, insurer claims heads, hospital CFOs, NHA / state PM-JAY agency officers, IRDAI grievance staff.
- **Sources.** https://indiamedtoday.com/record-health-insurance-claims-settled-in-fy25-average-payout-per-claim-declines/ (F); https://www.outlookmoney.com/insurance/bajaj-allianz-cashless-claim-ahpi-ends-stand-off-with-bajaj-allianz-cashless-cover-back (S); https://moneylife.in/article/health-insurance-claims-worth-rs2603765-crore-rejected-by-insurers-in-fy2324-govt/76282.html (S); https://cafemutual.com/news/insurance/38460-insurance-industry-disposed-off-287-lakh-complaints-in-fy26-through-bima-bharosa (S).

## Pool 9. Saudi Arabia: construction contracts issued by the state and PIF to contractors, now collapsing  [shrinking; treat as a distress and working-capital pool]

- **Money flow.** Project owners (PIF and its giga-projects such as NEOM, ministries, Aramco, private developers) -> main contractors (Saudi and international: Webuild, Hyundai, Saudi Binladin, Nesma) -> subcontractors and materials suppliers (steel, cement, MEP) -> labour camps and workers (mostly migrant).
- **Size.** Contracts issued: $71B in 2024 falling to under $30B in 2025 (Saudi Contractors Authority); PIF share fell from ~38% (~$27B) to 14% (S, AGBI Feb 2026). A different database (MEED Projects) shows $164B in 2024 falling to $84.5B in 2025, so the level depends on the source, the direction does not (S). Total construction, transport, power and industrial output was $148B in 2024 per Knight Frank (S). I use **$30B** (the 2025 contract-awards ceiling) as the conservative live flow. The pool is shrinking, not growing.
- **Shock (2026-02).** PIF cut spending across its portfolio from early 2025 and giga-project schedules slipped; NEOM terminated large contracts (a roughly $1B tunnel contract in March 2026; Hyundai and Webuild rail terminations). One report says NEOM's 2026-30 budget includes SAR60B ($16B) for contractor termination payouts versus SAR40B for new infrastructure (single source, unverified (S)). Saudi bank lending growth reportedly slowed by more than half in Jan-May 2026 as PIF cutbacks bit (S, AGBI, July 2026). One report links Gulf contract disruption to an Iran war (S, AGBI, Aug 2026; I did not verify details).
- **Incumbents and fragility.** Contractors and banks hold the exposure; subcontractors are paid only after main contractors collect. History: in the 2016-17 arrears episode late government payments exceeded SR100B (~30% of the government projects' volume) and contractors cut staff (S). The structure repeats when the owner is the state.
- **Crowdedness: CONTESTED / CROWDED for B2B finance.** SILQ (Sary + ShopUp merger; $20M structured debt from Gemcorp, July 2026; 50,000 businesses), BRKZ ($31M Series B, $18M debt line), Comfi ($65M pre-Series A, April 2026, expanding to Saudi), Project Suppliers (SAR3.75M pre-seed, Oct 2026, targeting MEP suppliers' delayed payments), Lamaa; and PIF launched its own supply-chain finance platform, Tawrid, while SAMA put supply-chain-finance rules out for consultation on 26 Aug 2026 (S). A state-owned platform in the same space is a structural risk.
- **Wedge hints.** (1) Claims and termination-settlement preparation for contractors (delay analysis, quantum, evidence). (2) Redeployment marketplace for idle equipment, modular camps and labour. (3) Receivables financing for tier-2/3 subcontractors on state-owned obligors (after the SAMA rules settle). (4) Procurement for contractors who have switched to cheaper materials.
- **Bottom-up check.** The addressable money is mostly one-off settlements and working capital, not recurring spend. I could not find a path to $100M of recurring revenue without taking balance-sheet risk on state counterparties. Probably "know about, do not build".
- **Insider roles.** Contractor claims managers and quantity surveyors, project controls and planning engineers, construction lawyers (arbitration), subcontractor owners, MEP suppliers' finance heads, bank project-finance officers.
- **Sources.** https://www.agbi.com/construction/2026/02/saudi-pif-construction-contracts-plummet-amid-scaleback/ (S); https://www.agbi.com/analysis/banking-finance/2026/07/saudi-banks-slow-lending-as-pif-cutbacks-bite/ (S; page returned 403 to my fetch); https://www.agbi.com/giga-projects/2026/03/neom-terminates-1bn-tunnel-contract-at-heart-of-the-line/ (S); https://enterpriseam.com/menaplus/2026/06/10/saudi-may-end-up-with-an-outsized-cancelation-bill-in-neom/ (S; 403 to my fetch); https://www.wamda.com/2026/07/silq-raises-20-million-scale-embedded-finance-saudi-smes (S); https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2026/10/07/project-suppliers-raises-sar-3-75-mn-in-a-pre-seed-round-from-undisclosed-angel-investors/ (S); Knight Frank output via https://www.arabnews.com/node/2607215/business-economy (S).

# Gate report: P032 (US power and distribution transformers), 2026-10-14

Pool: US purchases of power and distribution transformers, about $14B a year (broad definition). 8 ideas gated. Outcome: **0 strong, 3 kill, 5 weak. No picks.**

Scale used (same as P079): kill = realistic 5-7 yr ceiling well under $25M, already done, or false premise. weak = a plausible $25-100M outcome but with an unresolved dealbreaker. strong = credible $100M+ that survives the competitor, payer, legal and why-now checks.

What the pool taught: the shortage is real but narrower than the ideas assume. Distribution-class lead times have fallen a lot; the pain that remains is large power, GSU and three-phase pad-mount units. That is also where funded startups (Ayr, FluxCo, GridCARE), a PE-backed roll-up (Sunbelt Solomon, sold to TJC in July 2026), co-op pools (RESCO, UUS) and the OEMs already sit. Intermediary margins are thin, and the only party that creates supply is a manufacturer.

## Corrections to the generator's inputs (these change several verdicts)

- **"Lead times over 2 years" is the 2023-24 peak for distribution units, not today's number.**
  - Wood Mackenzie's survey average for distribution transformers is about 30 weeks, down from above 100 weeks in 2023 (second-hand via Giga Energy): https://www.gigaenergy.com/blog/lead-time-delays-for-data-centers
  - ERMCO quotes 10-14 weeks to RESCO for single-phase pole-mount and pad-mount; 16-24 weeks for three-phase: https://www.cooperative.com/news/Pages/RESCO-Investments-Pay-Off-as-Inventory-Hits-Record-High.aspx
  - Hawaiian Electric says supply issues "have primarily been resolved" and lists pole mounts at 75 kVA and below at 6 months: https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/electrical-services/upgrade-modify-or-install-service/newsletter-updates
  - Still long: three-phase pad-mount at 40-65 weeks (contractor, 2026): https://terrapincg.com/news/switchgear-transformer-generator-lead-times-2026
  - Still very long: power transformers at 128 weeks and GSUs at 144 weeks (Wood Mackenzie Q2 2025, via Power Magazine): https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/
- **EO number and timing.** Sources disagree on 14420 vs 14421. DOE's request for information closed **October 9, 2026**, so "file comments before Dec 24" (idea 7) is stale: https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/doe-rfi-bulk-power-system-eo.html
- **The 2026 shortage is partly an artifact of procurement rules, per one middleman.** A supplier claims a standard substation unit ships in 12-14 months after engineering approval. The article says this is hard to validate: https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/

## Evidence by idea

### 1. Swap, Don't Buy: kill
- Competitors and substitutes:
  - Xylem/Sensus Transformer Utilization (virtual meter per transformer, flags oversized units; catalog page now labels it discontinued): https://www.xylem.com/en-ie/Catalog/products--services/software/transformer-utilization-application/
  - Wake Electric case study (13,152 of 17,000 transformers not used to capacity; no dollars shown in what I could read): https://v2.iotone.com/case-study/electric-utility-uses-data-driven-solutions-to-meet-goals/c762
  - Itron transformer load monitoring and sizing: https://na.itron.com/what-we-offer/grid-operations
  - Siemens EnergyIP asset topology mapping (their own 5-20% bad-record estimate): https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:e9193aad-139d-47f7-b60c-365b6ee78408/emdg-b10132-00-4aus-onepager-eipas-144.pdf
  - Oracle Utilities data intelligence (transformer overload flags): https://www.oracle.com/a/ocom/docs/industries/utilities/util-data-intelligence-grid-operations-sb.pdf
  - Utilities' own planners and shops (free); APPA's voluntary equipment-locator service: https://www.publicpower.org/node/20902
- Revenue math:
  - The $5k average avoided unit is about 2x too high. A 2024 North Little Rock bid for 25 kVA pole-mount units came in at $2,005-$3,440: https://nlr.ar.gov/download/724/2024/11582/24-3857-2025-pole-mount-transformers-for-the-nlred.pdf
  - Kentucky Power told its regulator the cost differential of replacing a 15 kVA unit with a 25 kVA unit is **$196**. Oversizing one step is cheap, which is why planners do it: https://psc.ky.gov/pscecf/2020-00174/lmscott@aep.com/04142021031041/KPCO_R_KPSC_04142021.pdf
  - A swap means taking down one unit and hanging another at each site, so about two crew visits to save one $2-3k purchase. Net savings per swap are near zero to small. (The crew-cost point is my inference; BGE reported a pole-plus-transformer replacement averaging $7,100 in 2011.)
  - Corrected: 105k swaps x about $2.7k = about $280M gross avoided, and the net after labor is a fraction of that. A 30% fee on a net pool of under $100M, at 10% participation, is single-digit millions. The $90M of subscriptions would have to displace Itron, Siemens, Oracle and Xylem.
- Why-now is weaker than claimed: single-phase pole-mount supply has largely recovered (see corrections).
- Legal/premise: no legal issue. Premise issue is that oversizing is a cheap, rational planner choice and IOUs lose earnings by avoiding capex (the idea admits this).

### 2. Cores for Coils: weak
- Competitors:
  - Maddox Industrial Transformer: five remanufacturing shops, "thousands" of stocked units, buys retired units at up to 5x scrap, 1-4 week delivery claimed: https://www.powermag.com/partner-content/beating-the-transformer-bottleneck-remanufacturing-build-to-stock-and-smart-procurement/ (sponsored content)
  - RESA Power: remanufactured units, 2-3 week ship, "large inventory of tested and warrantied transformers": https://resapower.com/services/transformer-remanufacturing/
  - Sunbelt Solomon: new, remanufactured and rental; 42 locations, 1,600+ employees; being bought by TJC from Trilantic (July 2026): https://www.pehub.com/tjc-to-acquire-electrical-power-firm-sunbelt-solomon-from-trilantic-north-america/ and https://www.cahill.com/news/firm-news/2026-07-24-cahill-private-credit-group-represents-financing-sources-in-tjc-acquisition-of-sunbelt-solomon
  - Utility in-house shops and regional rewinders.
- DOE's own view of this market (primary source, 2024 final rule, section IV.G.3): the rebuilding/rewinding market "has historically been relatively small", rebuilding takes extra labor so new is the preferred option, and the recent uptick is "primarily a function of long lead times" and "likely temporary". Idaho Power said refurbished and new costs are "somewhat close": https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-04-22/html/2024-07480.htm
- Price premise is inconsistent: RESA wording is ambiguous ("60-70% cheaper" vs "60-70% of cost"); the Power Magazine piece cites 10-40% below new.
- Revenue math: 40-60k units x $2-4k needs 3-5% of all unit demand and several times the throughput of any named rebuilder. NREL's replacement stream is 1.44-2.56M units a year (DOE deck: https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/Distribution%20Transformer%20Shortage%20Mitigation.pdf), but the rebuildable share is unmeasured. The idea's own 90-day test (200 pulled units) is the right test and has not been run.
- Legal premise: not confirmed. DOE treated rewind/repair as "additive" services in 2024, and the 2029 standard applies to units manufactured or imported on or after April 23, 2029, but I could not retrieve DOE's holding on rebuilt units, and the June 2026 RFI could change it. NRECA's "DOE counsel said" claim is unverified.
- Why the ceiling is not zero: automated coil winding and exclusive scrap-stream contracts are a real wedge. The large-power rewind line ($30-90M) competes with OEM service arms.

### 3. Gavi for Transformers: kill
- Competitors and substitutes (they already exist for the segment that is easiest to organize):
  - RESCO: 272 co-ops and public power districts, about $60M record inventory, ERMCO partnership: https://www.cooperative.com/news/Pages/RESCO-Investments-Pay-Off-as-Inventory-Hits-Record-High.aspx
  - United Utility Supply (co-op owned since 1948, ERMCO distributor): https://kyelectric.coop/services/united-utility-supply/
  - ERMCO is co-op owned and has committed much of its output to co-ops (same RESCO source).
  - DOE's Distribution Transformer Working Group (24 utility-association members, 18 manufacturers) is building a common-configuration matrix (roughly 15-40% fewer of the 79,421 types) and an interchangeability matrix, free: https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/Distribution%20Transformer%20Shortage%20Mitigation.pdf
  - Distributors (Graybar, Wesco, Border States and similar), APPA's locator service.
  - Possible federal credit enhancer: DPA Title III determinations (signed April 20, 2026) allow DOE purchase commitments, loans and loan guarantees for transformers and electrical core steel; no awards named yet: https://www.enr.com/articles/62887-trump-taps-defense-production-act-to-address-grid-equipment-energy-project-bottlenecks
- Revenue math: principal sales of $100M are reachable (about 7-10% of a muni/small-IOU slice), but at the idea's own 6-9% margin that is $6-9M of gross profit, and the idea concedes it becomes a business only with a financing spread and manufacturer fees. Those belong to CFC/CoBank and the co-op wholesalers who already have the relationships. Net revenue ceiling about $15M; sales up to about $60M.
- Payer: member capex budgets exist, but members can already buy through a co-op channel at lower cost.
- Why-now: distribution lead times have eased for standard units; the pooling pain is concentrated in three-phase pad-mount.

### 4. TSMC for Transformer Cores: weak
- Competitors:
  - Cogent Power (Burlington, Ontario; JFE Shoji bought it from Tata Steel in 2019; wound, mitre and amorphous cores; new one-meter cutting line for large power units): https://magneticsmag.com/?p=14201
  - Tempel Steel (Worthington): Tempel Canada opened an $85-120M Burlington expansion on June 24, 2026 meant to double transformer core and lamination output (reports disagree on cost): https://transformer-technology.com/tempel-canada-opens-new-120m-transformer-component-plant-in-burlington/ and https://megaproject.com/news/factory/tempel-canada-opens-us-85m-burlington-ont-expansion
  - Captive OEM core lines (Virginia Transformer runs its own cutting machines; Hitachi, Prolec, ERMCO and others), Mexican lamination houses.
  - Cleveland-Cliffs: sole US GOES mill, building its own $150M three-phase transformer plant in Weirton (a competitor to any customer): https://www.clevelandcliffs.com/news/news-releases/detail/644/cleveland-cliffs-announces-its-new-state-of-the-art
- Supply constraint: Butler Works GOES output rises up to 25% only by early 2028 (DOE page says 2029): https://gmk.center/en/news/cleveland-cliffs-is-investing-200-million-in-the-expansion-of-the-butler-works-plant-in-the-us/ . A sole-source DLA contract takes up to 53,000 net tons through 2030: https://www.aist.org/the-defense-logistics-agency-makes-us$400-million-supply-agreement-with-cleveland-cliffs . A neutral foundry would need 30-40 kt a year from a mill that competes with its customers, or imported GOES at 50% duty (I did not verify whether GOES itself is in the 50% steel tier).
- Revenue math: Commerce (BIS 2021) put GOES at about 25% of transformer production cost and foreign sourcing above 75%: https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/redacted-goes-report-updated-10-26-21.pdf . A $100M core plant is mostly pass-through steel; the idea's 10-15% core-share figure is lower than Commerce's survey. Ceiling about $60M sales, thin value-add. Needs signed volume before the machine order, which the idea itself says is the gating step.
- No US startup foundry found; that is the gap, but Canada-based neutral suppliers already serve USMCA buyers and are expanding.

### 5. Time-to-Power Fleet: weak (best of the pool)
- Competitors:
  - Sunbelt Solomon: sells, rents and remanufactures; rental mobile substations up to 230 kV and 50,000 kVA; markets to data-center projects: https://sunbeltsolomon.com/about-us/ (page text did not load; scale from the PE Hub/Cahill items above)
  - Maddox: stocked new and remanufactured units "in days": https://www.powermag.com/partner-content/beating-the-transformer-bottleneck-remanufacturing-build-to-stock-and-smart-procurement/
  - FluxCo: procurement, "Transformer as a Service" lease and finance, servicing and warranty; $26M seed (Congruent, 8VC): https://www.fluxco.com and https://fundraiseinsider.com/blog/fluxco-raises-26m-seed-for-ai-transformer-sourcing-platform/
  - Ayr Energy: Indian-built transformers to its specs, orders "north of $500M", power transformers 40-72 weeks advertised, designs modular so customers can change orders late, $25M raised: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/this-energy-startups-bet-on-100-year-old-grid-tech-is-paying-off/ and https://www.ayr.energy
  - Grid Assurance (transmission-owner spares, 23 states, cost-based): https://www.eei.org/-/media/Project/EEI/Documents/Issues-and-Policy/Reliability-and-Emergency-Response/Spare-Equipment-and-Grid-Resilience-Programs.pdf
  - Hyperscaler direct contracts and developer deposits (secondary source, low authority): https://www.datacentres.com/news/36-month-lead-times-turn-transformer-shortage-into-data-centre-site-selection-ki-slot3-2026-08-07
- Why it is not strong yet:
  - **The fleet needs the scarce input.** It must buy OEM production slots, and OEMs allocate on forecast certainty. A new buyer without frame agreements is behind Ayr, hyperscalers and incumbents. The idea does not show where the slots come from.
  - **Utility spec acceptance is the premise and is unverified.** DOE found 79,421 distribution types and utilities tailor specs; "80% of substation units are the same few designs" has no source. Industry standardization work is slow and covers distribution units: https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/Distribution%20Transformer%20Shortage%20Mitigation.pdf
  - **Demand quality.** Wood Mackenzie: of 1,066 GW of requested data-center load, about 12% is firm and 28% likely: https://capacityglobal.com/news/wood-mackenzie-us-grid-operators-set-to-serve-28pc-of-data/ and https://www.utilitydive.com/news/efforts-to-curb-data-center-speculation-gain-ground-across-the-us/830495/
  - **Asset-price risk.** New capacity: Hitachi's $457M Virginia plant (2028), Siemens Charlotte (early 2027), Eaton South Carolina three-phase (2027): https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/ . A $0.85B fleet leased at 12% leaves little margin over cost of capital and depreciation if prices fall.
  - Utilities cannot hold speculative units, so the realistic model is inventory-for-sale with floorplan financing, which is a dealer business with a thin spread.
- Revenue math: selling 35 units at $3M gives about $105M of sales but gross profit near 10%. Lease-only needs the $0.85B balance sheet. Roll-up route (Sunbelt Solomon scale) is real but is a decade-long, PE-style path. Ceiling about $70M.
- Why-now has real support: EO 14420 could remove a source (WoodMac says Chinese high-power imports rose from under 1,500 in 2022 to over 8,000 in 2025 per a Bloomberg-based report, and $22B of bulk-power imports are exposed): https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/us-executive-order-banning-chinese-bulk-power-equipment-set-to-worsen-critical-transformer-shortage/

### 6. Time-to-Restore Cover: kill
- Competitors and substitutes:
  - Grid Assurance and EEI STEP (utility spares): links above and https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/transmission/utilities-subscribe-to-grid-assurance-transmission-spare-joint-venture/
  - Swiss Re parametric BI deductible buy-down for IPPs: https://corporatesolutions.swissre.com/dam/jcr:4304809c-65a6-4438-a188-a50a3c83e3d0/deductible-buy-down-protection-for-business-interruption.2026-01-26-12-31-17.pdf
  - Descartes parametric suite for data centers (up to $140M per policy): https://www.reinsurancene.ws/descartes-launches-parametric-product-suite-for-data-centres/
  - Marsh SLA parametric for data centers: https://www.marsh.com/content/marsh2/en_gb/services/cyber-risk/insights/service-level-agreement-parametric-insurance-for-data-centres.html
  - Zurich already tells clients to set 24-month indemnity periods for transformers above 450 MVA: https://www.zurich.co.uk/news-and-insight/how-indemnity-periods-could-help-fight-against-underinsurance
  - Sunbelt Solomon rentals, FluxCo warranty, OEM service contracts.
- Revenue math: the $240-360M "price" is gross premium on a pool ($24B) that the idea marks as an assumption. An MGA keeps only part of premium, so company revenue is a fraction (the exact take is not benchmarked in public sources I found). The idea itself calls $100M 28-40% of the pool. Failure base rate for 69 kV+ units is 0.5-0.94% a year (CIGRE, via INMR): https://www.inmr.com/testing-to-reduce-transformer-failure-risk . Typical solar and storage main transformers are likely well below $2M (my estimate; EIA counts were not pulled by the idea or me). Ceiling about $15M.
- Premise: Marsh flagged lead times exceeding indemnity periods back in 2008 (https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/international/2008/05/09/89871.htm); the gap is known, and the market answer so far is longer indemnity periods, not a new product.
- Capital: a real time-bound restoration promise needs owned spares, which brings back the balance-sheet problem of idea 5.

### 7. Achilles for the Grid: weak
- Mandate is real: EO 14420 was signed August 26, 2026 under IEEPA. It covers equipment tied to covered foreign entities for 69 kV+ bulk-power equipment, reaches installed equipment, lets DOE set pre-qualified vendor lists, and requires DOE rules within 120 days (Dec 24, 2026): https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/powerandpipes/2026/08/bulk-power-system-executive-order-adds-a-new-grid-supply-chain-compliance-layer and https://www.dwt.com/blogs/energy--environmental-law-blog/2026/08/eo-14420-bulk-power-system-equipment
- What is not there: no third-party attestation or accreditation role in the commentary I read; criteria for the vendor list are an "open issue"; only China is generally understood to be a major supplier of the gear (Morgan Lewis). DOE's RFI asks how deep sub-tier tracing should go, but comments closed October 9: https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/doe-rfi-bulk-power-system-eo.html
- Competitors:
  - Fortress Information Security: A2V network built with AEP, $125M from Goldman (2022), claims to secure 40% of the US grid; current offering is software and assessment-library based, with hardware BOM named only as an adjacency: https://www.powermag.com/goldman-sachs-stake-in-fortress-underscores-supply-chain-cybersecurity-priority/
  - Exiger (FOCI and sub-tier controls, published on the earlier grid order): https://exiger.com/perspectives/preparing-the-us-bulk-power-supply-for-the-next-generation-of-threats
  - Interos, Everstream, Resilinc, test and inspection majors, and DOE itself (a free list).
- Precedent: the 2020 predecessor order (EO 13920) was suspended in January 2021 and never became a market: https://spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/latest-news-headlines/biden-suspends-trump-order-on-bulk-power-system-security-pending-90-day-review-62296724
- Revenue math: 15,000 covered purchases x $20k is unsourced and high for inverters and relays. The installed-base pool ($0.4-1.2B once) rests on a "weak" import count and on DOE choosing to order removals. $100M needs 25-30% of the recurring pool, which the idea calls heroic. Ceiling about $40M, conditional on DOE using accredited assessors.
- The gap that is genuine: Fortress is not a factory-floor or firmware lab, and a Korean unit with a non-compliant sub-tier part is a real exposure. Whether anyone pays for that depends on rules not yet written.

### 8. Virtual Transformers: weak
- Competitors and substitutes:
  - Hitachi Energy cooling-system upgrade service ("increase the loading capacity"): https://www.hitachienergy.com/products-and-solutions/transformers/transformer-service/maintenance/cooling-system-overhaul (OEMs also sell life extension; one report puts its service-contract rate under 1% of a 500,000-unit installed base, which shows the market is underserved, not that it pays: https://toyokeizai.net/articles/print-tbt/937543)
  - Doble Engineering (ESCO; 70+ transformer consulting engineers; fleet and condition assessments): https://www.doble.com/services/consulting-testing-services/consulting-services/expert-consulting-services/
  - Dynamic Ratings (loading 10-20% above static ratings, 2003 claim): https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/kuhlman-electric-corp-partners-with-dynamic-ratings-inc/ ; DTR1tech (transformer dynamic-rating SaaS, Stockholm): https://solarimpulse.com/companies/dtr1tech-ab
  - Same budget, different tool: GridCARE ($64M Series A, May 2026, finds "hidden" grid capacity for data centers): https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/15/gridcare-raises-64m-speed-ai-data-center-projects/ ; Emerald AI (flexible data-center load): https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidia-backed-emerald-ai-raises-245m-to-turn-data-centers-into-grid-assets/ ; bridge power from on-site generation.
  - Consultants and utilities' own planners.
- Nobody found guaranteeing MVA, which is the true gap. But uprating is bounded by each unit's cooling stage, condition and N-1 headroom, and utilities already use emergency ratings.
- Revenue math: the $50k per MVA and 100 GVA a year figures are unsourced (I found no GVA-denominated public data). $100M at $10-12k per MVA needs 8-10 GVA unlocked a year, or thousands of retrofit jobs. A guarantee backed by an insurer adds the same parametric-capacity problem as idea 6. Services-heavy with OEM warranty gatekeeping. Ceiling about $35M.
- Why-now: lead times for large units are the strongest in the pool (128-144 weeks), and a customer-funded, utility-owned structure gives a payer.

## Picks

**None.** Nothing clears the bar.

- Idea 5 is the closest. It would become pickable only if (a) a utility engineering group signs off on one multi-ratio design, (b) an OEM commits slots, and (c) an infrastructure lender prices the fleet, all before any capital is raised. Those are the three items in its own first-90-days plan, and none has happened.
- Idea 7 becomes interesting only if DOE's December rules create an accredited-assessor role.
- Narrowing any of these to a boutique (one co-op region, one voltage class) would put it back under $25M, which the rules forbid as a rescue.

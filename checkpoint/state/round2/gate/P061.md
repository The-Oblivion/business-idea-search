# Gate report: pool P061, Medicaid home- and community-based services (personal care, home care agencies, fiscal intermediaries)

Gate date: 2026-10-15. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with roughly 3-5 searches each (about 40 searches and 12 page reads in total). Result: 4 kill, 2 weak, 2 strong. Two picks: the neutral LTSS manager and the fiscal-intermediary consolidator.

## Headline findings

- **The pool is real, but the money mostly passes through.** Pool size is $129.4B of HCBS spend, and the personal-care slice is about $46.4B. Vendors can only claim a thin fee on that. The recurring error across the ideas is pricing a vendor fee at 3-5% of managed spend when observed fees are about 0.5-2%.
  - New York's statewide fiscal intermediary was bid at $68.50 per member per month (DOJ complaint, via https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2026/06/justice-department-sues-ppl-new-york-medicaid-fraud/). Against about $10B for about 250k people, that is roughly 2% of spend.
  - North Carolina's statewide LTSS assessment contract is $56.9M over 3 years, about $19M a year (https://acentra.com/news/north-carolina-comprehensive-independent-assessment-entity-contract-ncliftss). One idea's source text had this as $569M. It is not.
- **The data layer is already owned.** HHAeXchange (HHAX) reports $38B of payments managed, 485M visits a year, 180+ MCOs and 30 state programs (https://www.hhaexchange.com). It bought Sandata in 2024 (23 state EVV contracts, https://homecaremag.com/news/hhaexchange-acquires-sandata-technologies) and launched CarePay payroll on EVV hours on 2026-08-19 (https://www.hhaexchange.com/press-releases/hhaexchange-launches-carepay-a-homecare-payroll-solution). Any idea that says "only someone with visit plus payroll data can do X" has HHAX as the first competitor.
- **The best live opportunities are the two with real state or plan budgets and a fresh shock.**
  - New York's single-FI experiment is under federal attack. On 2026-06-16 DOJ sued PPL and two state health officials, calling the procurement a sham (https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2026/06/justice-department-sues-ppl-new-york-medicaid-fraud/). PPL agreed to a $162M settlement of an aide underpayment class action. PPL's largest owners are DW Healthcare Partners and Linden Capital (https://pestakeholder.org/news/private-equity-backed-ppl-settles-wage-theft-case-faces-doj-lawsuit/).
  - Elevance closed its roughly $2.7B CareBridge purchase on 2024-12-10 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603925000136/elv-20250930.htm). CareBridge had been funded in 2022 by Anthem, Optum, CVS and Centene (https://bhbusiness.com/2022/06/16/how-carebridge-won-over-investors-mcos-in-140m-funding-round/). Its rivals' preferred outsourced HCBS manager is now a competitor's subsidiary.
- **Premise corrections.**
  - The staffing idea's $30 per hour assumption is wrong. KFF's 2023 state survey puts the median Medicaid personal-care rate at $19 an hour, with agency rates ranging from $10 to $36 (https://www.kff.org/medicaid/payment-rates-for-medicaid-home-and-community-based-services-states-responses-to-workforce-challenges/).
  - The 71% fill rate is for home health aide services, from a low-response member survey. 64% of respondents named lack of caregivers as the reason for unfilled hours (https://community.allianceforcareathome.org/discussion/medicaid-fill-rate-survey-results-3).
  - Ohio's DODD statewide FMS RFP closed on 2026-01-14 (https://samsearch.co/gov-explore/sled/ddd-statewide-financial-management-services-for-the-department-of-developmental/SRC0000028354). Kentucky's FMS RFP (listed as due 2026-11-06, with a conflicting 2027 date in the same listing) is the only live target found (https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16859242).

## Per-idea notes

### 1. Clock-Out Capital (weak)

- **Competitors.**
  - Alleon Healthcare Capital: 85% advance on a $2.5M facility to a NY home care provider, funding within 24 hours (https://magazine.factoring.org/news/alleon-healthcare-capital-provides-25mm-medical-a/r-facility-to-home-care-provider).
  - Crestmont ($10k-$5M) and GetABusinessLoan ($25k-$10M), per the newswire link above.
  - eCapital and bank ABL lines (not re-verified).
  - HHAX CarePay plus partner earned-wage access (DailyPay case study, https://info.hhaexchange.com/hhaexchange-ecosystem-caregiver-retention-guide). It has no balance sheet, so the idea's "HHAX can't lend" claim holds.
  - No venture-funded home-care lender found.
- **Revenue math.**
  - If every personal-care dollar were agency-billed with a 50-day turn, receivables outstanding would be $46.4B x 50/365, about $6.4B. The idea's $667M average balance is therefore about 10% of all of it.
  - Much of the pool is not financeable this way: self-directed spend goes through FIs, and MCOs pay faster.
  - If 15-25% of agency-billed receivables are externally financed (my assumption, unverified), that is roughly $0.5-1B, so $667M is most of it.
  - The pitched 12-16% price is below the unverified 18-36% reported for factoring, so revenue per dollar of balance falls.
- **Legal.** 42 CFR 447.10(h) bars Medicaid payment to or through a factor. The listed exceptions (government reassignment, court order, business agents paid non-percentage fees, practitioner employers) do not mention lender lockboxes. Financing therefore has to be a loan secured by receivables with payment to an account in the provider's name under a control agreement, checked state by state. This is workable (Alleon does it) but it is not "buy the visit".
- **Edge that survives.** Recoupment is a clawback against future payments, so a lender's real exposure is the reserve, not the invoice. Pricing on that is a good insight, but it is an underwriting edge, not a moat.
- **Upside.** About a 25% share of externally financed balances ($350-450M) at 14% plus payroll fees is about $55M. $100M needs most of the market.

### 2. Exception-free fiscal intermediary (strong)

- **Competitors.** PPL (PE-owned, NY sole FI, bid $68.50 PMPM), GT Independence (60k+ participants, H.I.G. growth investment 2025-11), Acumen, Palco (took West Virginia from PPL on 2024-04-01), Tempus, and HHAX as the EVV layer. No free or in-house substitute: FMS is mandatory in self-directed waivers.
- **Pool.** The 2023 national inventory counts 1,520,267 self-directing people (includes veterans and non-FMS programs; https://www.acumenfiscalagent.com/key-findings-of-the-2023-self-direction-national-inventory/). California IHSS runs state payroll, so the FMS-vendor pool is probably 0.7-1.0M people, or roughly $0.8-1.2B a year at $70-117 PMPM (my estimate).
- **Prices.** Louisiana pays $105.88 PMPM, with an RFP maximum of $116.94 tiering to $99.75. NY is $68.50. The idea's "$75-115" band is right; "30% under the incumbent" only works outside NY.
- **NY upside.** 250k x $68.50 x 12 = about $205M a year in one contract, and the DOJ complaint says PPL also earned money from hourly billing outside that structure. That is the only single contract in the pool that is a $100M+ revenue event. A rebid is speculative: no rebid or receiver ruling found as of the July search window.
- **Wounds in the pitch.** Ohio DODD closed in January 2026. Kentucky is three weeks out and has no stated past-performance weights in the public listing. States will weigh past performance heavily after NY's day-one payroll problems. "30-40% of timesheets need a human" is unsourced.
- **Median** $20M: one acquired FI plus one or two mid-size wins. **Upside** $110M: about 85k participants at about $100 blended PMPM, or a large share of a NY-class rebid.

### 3. Proof-of-Visit (weak)

- **Competitors.** Sandata and HHAX hold the visit data and now payroll. Vendor material describes duplicate-visit and wrong-location flags (not re-verified as a shipping product). FEI CareVisit advertises GPS, BLE and NFC. Optum, Gainwell, Conduent and Cotiviti sell payment integrity (not re-verified). States already cross-check claims to EVV; 21 of 48 responding states use or plan AI.
- **Premise.** "Only a party holding payroll data can build it" is false because HHAX now has both. The NY $14.5B figure is the Comptroller's count of claims without matching EVV over Jan 2021-Mar 2023 (27 months), and DOH says about $2.7B should be excluded (https://www.osc.ny.gov/press/releases/2024/11/dinapoli-state-paid-145-billion-medicaid-home-care-services-lacked-verification).
- **Price.** EVV is free to agencies in several states and is state-paid. No per-visit fee was found. $0.40 x 250M visits is 30-40% of national visits.
- **Contingency risk.** Delaware's Medicaid RAC RFP drew no bids. Savings-share needs a state to approve paying 20% of avoided cost.
- **Where it survives.** As a scoring feed inside a lender or a plan product, not as a standalone $100M rail.

### 4. Personal-care administrator for FFS states (kill)

- **Competitors.** Maximus (Kansas HCBS assessment organization), Acentra (NC, WV), PCG, Conduent, Gainwell, HHAX. "Nobody does it whole" is true but each can bundle.
- **Fee premise.** Observed state admin fees: NC about $19M a year for statewide LTSS assessment; NY FI about 2% of spend. Hitting $100M at 2% needs $5B of managed spend, 11% of the national personal-care pool, in 18-36 month sales cycles.
- **Structure.** Many states route personal care through MCOs, so the FFS carve-out shrinks as managed care grows. KFF counts prior authorization done by MCOs in 12 states and by UM vendors in only 7.
- **NEMT analogy.** NEMT brokers earn capitation and take risk (ModivCare 2024 revenue $2.79B, Chapter 11 in 2025, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251212297501/en/). A no-risk admin fee does not make that business.

### 5. Neutral LTSS manager (strong)

- **Competitors.** CareBridge (Carelon/Elevance, 17 states plus DC, about 115k patients in 2023, about $2.6B reported 2023 revenue), plans' own LTSS teams, HHAX payer network and analytics tools (no risk-taking), Optum in-house capacity. No independent risk-bearing HCBS manager found in four searches.
- **Demand evidence.** In 2022 Anthem, Optum Ventures, CVS Ventures and Centene (via HLM) all co-invested in CareBridge, and the article says MCOs investing oversee about 60% of HCBS members. After the sale those plans' preferred vendor belongs to a rival. Elevance says it will keep serving all customers; that is the thing rivals have to decide whether to trust.
- **Open risks (the idea's own kill test stands).**
  - Whether Centene, UHC, CVS/Aetna or Molina will move volume to a neutral vendor rather than build or keep CareBridge.
  - Savings: more than half of LTSS users are dual-eligible, and acute savings accrue to Medicare, not Medicaid plans, unless the same parent runs the D-SNP (https://www.chcs.org/early-state-efforts-to-advance-value-in-medicaid-managed-long-term-services-and-supports-programs/).
  - Evidence that care management cuts spend is mixed (same source).
  - Capital for at-risk contracts.
- **Math check.** CareBridge sold at about 1x reported revenue, so its net economics are thin. Using about $1,100-1,500 net per member-year (5% of about $30k, consistent with the exit multiple, my inference), $100M needs about 70-90k members, 60-80% of CareBridge's 2023 base. That is top-decile, not median.

### 6. Risk and Mobility MGA (kill)

- **Premium pool.** At 1.8M agency aides x $28k payroll = about $50B, workers' comp at $1.2-3.9 per $100 is $0.6-2.0B, not $2.5B. Other lines are unsourced. A 15-18% MGA take gives $225-540M at 100% share, so $100M needs 20-45%.
- **Competitors.** One80 Manchester Specialty (national home care and hospice workers' comp program, 20+ carriers, sold through brokers), PMC Insurance Group, state funds, PEOs.
- **Legal.** Using client health data for underwriting is a HIPAA question; de-identified scheduling data avoids it, but then loses the "visit mix" edge.

### 7. Continuity Bench (kill)

- **Competitors.** ShiftMed/HomeCare.com (W-2, $200M raise in 2023, $10M HomeCare.com round led by Blue Heron), Clipboard Health (expanding into home health), agencies' per-diem pools.
- **Price versus value.** Median agency rate $19 an hour; a $3.5-4.5 spread is 18-24% of the bill rate. Overtime avoidance is the only place it pays.
- **Labor supply.** The shortage is of caregivers and pay, not pooling. A pool cannot supply workers who lost work authorization. The TPS shock is real: about 13,000 nursing assistants and 8,000 caregivers among Haitian TPS holders (Axios link above), but one-time and shrinking.
- **Legal.** Whether Medicaid agencies can bill for subcontracted staff varies by state (unverified); NY has a licence moratorium per the idea itself.
- **Revenue.** The idea's $100M is net spread on about $600M gross billings. Even on gross billings the business is a commodity with a funded leader.

### 8. Partner-not-buy network (kill)

- **Competitors.** IHCA (2021 BPO pledge, no visible traction), CSI Network Services (450+ providers, commercial/MA only), HHAX network tools, AxisCare/AlayaCare/WellSky/Axxess software, chains that buy.
- **Economics.** A 5% fee on revenue is most of a typical independent's margin (Addus runs about 6.5% net margin per an investor narrative, a weak source). Medicaid rates are set by states, so a joint payer contract has little uplift to share. Physician-practice MSOs work because of commercial contracting leverage, which is absent here.
- **Legal.** 447.10(f) allows a billing agent paid by non-percentage compensation. The fee has to be flat or cost-based if the manager touches payments; a percentage fee works only if Medicaid payment goes solely to the agency.
- **Pool.** The count of Medicaid-heavy independents at $10M+ (1,500-2,500) is unverified.

## Picks

### Pick 1: The neutral LTSS manager (from idea 5)

**Sharpened version.** A plan-owned-by-nobody HCBS operating system for D-SNP-aligned Medicaid plans. It combines real-time visit and authorization data across all EVV aggregators, same-day missed-shift response, continuity-based network steering, fraud triage, and a risk-adjusted shared-savings contract. The buyer is the VP of LTSS or Medicaid CMO at plans whose Medicare and Medicaid sides sit under one parent (FIDE-SNP and HIDE-SNP), so acute savings are capturable. Capital comes from two or three non-Elevance plans co-investing in exchange for volume commitments, the 2022 CareBridge template. A written data firewall and a governance seat for each plan answer the neutrality question. Revenue scales through members (target 70-90k at-risk members by year 6) and through modules sold on the same data.

**Payer and signer.** Plan SVP/VP of LTSS or Medicaid chief medical officer, paid from medical-cost management budgets and out of savings.

**Why it keeps the $100M path.** It does not shrink to a 5,000-member boutique; the pilot is only the first contract. Reaching $100M is about 60-80% of CareBridge's 2023 base, so the claim is top-decile and stated that way.

### Pick 2: Fiscal-intermediary consolidator (from idea 2)

**Sharpened version.** Buy a small or mid FI with state contracts and a clean record (past performance is the entry ticket after NY), re-platform it so EVV-approved hours flow straight into payroll, and compete in the rolling wave of state rebids (Kentucky now, Ohio and others on 5-year cycles, New York if it reopens). Drop the "30% under" headline. NY already sits at $68.50 PMPM and other states pay $100-117, so the pitch is an SLA: zero missed first payroll, onboarding measured in days, and a funded payroll backstop if the state pays late. Add MCO-delegated FMS as a second channel. Cross-sell working capital later.

**Payer and signer.** State Medicaid director or developmental-disabilities director through competitive procurement, from the FMS administrative line with federal match.

**Why it keeps the $100M path.** About 85k participants at about $100 blended PMPM, or half of a NY-class contract. It stays a state-contract services business at 10-15% margins, so the claim is revenue, not profit.

## Pool note

The pool's real money is state or plan budgets for administration (FI, plan-side management), not fees skimmed off aide wages. Ideas that assumed 3-5% of spend, a $30 hour, or "only we hold the data" failed on checking. The two survivors have a named signer, a precedent contract or exit, and a fresh shock.

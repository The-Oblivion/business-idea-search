# Gate report: pool P015, US building-permit plan review and inspection

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 3-6 searches each, plus a read of the enrolled Florida HB 803 text. Result: 4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. **No picks.**

## What changed the picture

1. **The leading idea is already built.** Inspected (Plantation, FL) is a tech-enabled private provider doing remote video inspections for contractors. It says it has done 100,000+ Florida inspections since 1 Jan 2024 (https://drj.com/industry_news/inspected-celebrates-broward-countys-approval-of-virtual-inspections-for-building-permits/). It closed a $2.2M Series A (https://constructionreviewonline.com/inspected-com-announces-closing-of-2-2m-series-a-financing/). Its press release calls it a "fastest-growing company under $10 million", so about $4-6M a year at 60k inspections a year. Broward lifted its remote-inspection ban in Aug 2025.
2. **Private-provider work is already normal, not new.** Private providers did 25% (FY21) and 30% (FY22) of Pasco County inspections, about 198,000 inspections, and about 35% of passed inspections in one Jacksonville month (https://abcactionnews.com/news/local-news/i-team-investigates/florida-homebuyers-want-more-oversight-of-private-inspectors). Large incumbents already serve this: Universal Engineering Sciences (about 4,000 staff, https://jobs.boaf.net/profile/universal-engineering-sciences/706995/), Bureau Veritas/CAP (https://group.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/acquisition-majority-stake-cap-government-inc), Milrose (https://www.milrose.com/press-release/littlejohn-co-acquires-milrose-consultants/), SAFEbuilt (about 1,700 staff, 1,900 customers, Riverside-owned, 19 bolt-ons, https://pulse2.com/safebuilt-acquires-northeast-inspection-consultants-to-expand-pennsylvania-operations/).
3. **The statute helps and hurts.** Enrolled HB 803 (https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/803/BillText/er/PDF), as I read it:
   - Private-provider inspections "may be performed in person or virtually" (s. 553.791(9)). This confirms the tele-inspection premise but also means anyone can do it, so there is no regulatory moat.
   - A "duly authorized representative" must be an employee (chapter 443 reemployment-assistance test) of the private provider. This hurts any "independent contractors under a platform" model.
   - The mandatory 25%/50% fee cut applies to **commercial** projects only. Residential gets the narrower rule that a locality may not charge plan-review or inspection fees when a private provider does the work. Localities may still charge a "reasonable administrative fee" (Pasco charges $600 per single-family home).
4. **State laws elsewhere are thinner than the ideas assume.**
   - California AB 253 is plan review only, 1-10 units, applicant-paid (https://cayimby.org/legislation/ab-253/).
   - Texas HB 14 is a fallback only after a city misses a deadline by 15 days (https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/analysis/html/HB00014H.htm). SB 1450's enactment could not be confirmed (https://www.texaspolicyresearch.com/bills/89th-legislature-sb-1450).
   - Washington HB 1403 is an express-warranty exemption for ADUs and 12-or-fewer-unit condos, not an inspection mandate (https://www.hcmp.com/blog/hcmp-client-alert-new-law-changes-condominium-construction-warranties-in-washington).

## Per-idea notes

### 1. Tele-Inspection Factory (kill)
- **Competitors:** Inspected (above); Inspektr ($99 a job, third-party dossier only, link to Tew & Taylor unconfirmed); Freedom Code Compliance (47 counties, 150+ registrations, 10k+ projects, self-reported); municipal programs (Tampa/VuSpex, Broward BORA guidelines).
- **Substitutes:** the city's own free virtual inspections; in-person private providers who already sell same-day service.
- **Math:** 1.7M inspections at $60. Inspected, the leader, runs about 60k a year (100k in 20 months). The reachable Florida base is the idea's own "1-1.5M permitted trade finals" across all private-provider states, and Florida is the only one with a virtual-inspection statute. A new entrant at $45-75 against a $99 price point faces compression.
- **Legal:** the premise is correct. "In person or virtually" is in the enrolled HB 803 (s. 553.791(9)). Virtual inspection is barred for structural inspections of threshold buildings (per the HB 667 guide). Florida's 2021 law lets building departments inspect virtually as well.

### 2. Inspection-as-a-Service for Top Homebuilders (weak)
- **Competitors:** UES, BV/CAP, SAFEbuilt and Milrose for code inspection; 2-10 HBW (Frontdoor, $585M, risk-management side); builder in-house QA; ACI Inspections and forensic QA firms for third-party quality certification (https://aciinspections.com/home-inspection-services/new-construction-builder-quality-certified-consultations/); SiteAware ($15M Series B Jan 2022, commercial GCs, https://www.robotics247.com/article/siteaware_raises_15m_series_b_digital_construction_verification_technology). No funded US homebuilder-QA inspector was found.
- **Substitutes:** the private provider the builder already uses; the city; in-house QA.
- **Math:** see table. The funded price ($900-1,500) comes from the city fee the builder avoids, but the locality can keep an admin fee ($600 per single-family home in Pasco), and only Florida, Georgia, Tennessee and Utah are reachable. Texas's trigger is a missed deadline.
- **Risk not tested:** builders may not want a discoverable third-party defect record (my inference, not verified).
- **Legal:** the s. 553.791(2)(b) fee prohibition holds for residential. The 25%/50% cut is commercial-only, so the idea's "statutory fee reduction" funding is overstated.

### 3. NHBC for the U.S. (kill)
- **Colorado MCIP:** opt-in, effective 1 Jan 2026, run by DOLA (https://dre.colorado.gov/hb25-1272-summary). No participation data was published. ABC Rocky Mountain called the inspection mandate possibly not viable.
- **Washington:** the 10-year structural warranty and building-enclosure inspection regimes already exist (RCW 64.35, RCW 64.55), and HB 1403 loosens them for small projects.
- **Competitors:** 2-10 HBW/Frontdoor; local envelope consultants in Washington; FHA dropped its 10-year warranty rule in 2019 (the idea's own claim).
- **Math:** the UK analog works because lenders require a warranty. No US lender requirement was found.

### 4. Platform Behind Independent Private Providers (kill)
- **Competitors:** employed-pro firms (UES, BV/CAP, Milrose, Tew & Taylor), Inspected (Permit Hub), Freedom Code Compliance. No dedicated provider-platform was found, but no evidence of demand for one either.
- **Legal:** HB 803 requires a duly authorized representative to be an employee. HB 803 also reportedly requires every municipality to run a mandatory registration system (per a Miami Beach page, unverified), which helps a registration product but also shows registration is solved by process, not a platform.

### 5. Fixed-Price Special Inspection and Closeout (weak)
- **Competitors:** Terracon, ECS, Intertek-PSI (#24 on the 2025 ENR Top 500 Design Firms, https://www.intertek.com/building/enr), Kleinfelder, Braun Intertec, S.W. Cole (https://swcole.com/construction-inspection-and-observation/), roll-ups such as FST Technical Services; software: InspectMind AI, Scope, Giatec SmartRock for data-center and tilt-up concrete (https://info.giatecscientific.com/data-center).
- **Substitutes:** the existing T&M agency; sensors that cut test counts.
- **Why weak and not dead:** no digital-native fixed-fee firm was found, and the pain (late reports holding the certificate of occupancy) is plausible. But incumbents can offer lump sums, the technician is still on site, and the pool figure is unsourced.

### 6. Permit-Clean (kill)
- **Competitors:** PermitVista (appears only in sponsored content, https://southfloridaagentmagazine.com/2026/08/10/open-permits-what-south-florida-real-estate-agents-need-to-know-before-closing/), PropertyKey IMAPP, Shovels, Cherre (reseller), municipal lien search vendors, Collier County's desk.
- **Substitutes:** the title agent's municipal lien search, the contract's permit clause (seller must cure up to a cap), the city's own reinstatement process.
- **Premise:** "title insurance excludes open permits" is plausible per Florida attorney sources but not verified against a policy form. The seller-cure share and price inputs are unsourced.

### 7. The Shot-Clock Concession (weak)
- **Competitors:** SAFEbuilt, BV/CAP, Interwest and Willdan (often paid by applicant deposits, Placer County memos, e.g. https://www.placer.ca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/52468/24A), Milrose, software vendors.
- **Premise check:** AB 253 is plan review only and applicant-paid, so it does not drive city concessions. HB 803 does let cities use private-provider firms for their own public works (new s. 553.791(24)), a small adjacent opening.
- **Why weak and not dead:** SAFEbuilt proves a $100M+ outsourcing business exists (about 1,700 staff), so a pool path exists, but it took two decades and 19 bolt-ons. Council procurement is slow and politically fragile.

### 8. Energize-on-Schedule (weak)
- **Competitors:** Salute Mission Critical (acquired Iconicx, OBMG), NV5 (Herman Cx), Cupertino Electric (CDCDG), BV, Terracon, Milrose, owner's reps, hyperscalers' own teams (not verified).
- **Premise check:** the TCEQ pause on 21 Sep 2026 is real (https://www.fox7austin.com/news/texas-pausing-permits-all-data-centers). Commissioning benchmarks of 0.75-2% of construction cost are consistent with the idea's math. Loudoun's third-party inspection program is optional and lists footing, fill and steel, not final C of O (https://www.loudoun.gov/2647/Third-Party-Building-Inspection-Program).

## Picks

**None.** Nothing in this pool is both defensible and able to reach $100M without 20%+ share of its reachable pool.

- Closest call: idea 2 (builders). It would need to become a multi-state, builder-paid inspection-plus-QA network that wins three or four national builders by 2029, and the evidence says the extra value above code inspection is only a few hundred dollars a home. A 5-builder, 2-community Florida pilot would test the premium cheaply, but it is not a $100M thesis yet.
- Second: idea 5 (special inspection). The share math works on paper, but the product is a services roll-up against $1B incumbents, not a new category.

# Gate report: P104 (commercial aviation MRO and aftermarket, ~$100B pool)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: adversarial. Default assumption was that someone already does each idea and the market is smaller than claimed.

**Result: 0 picks.** Five kills, three weak. The best of the pool (Engine Lifeline) is a real fee pool with a clean payer, but it is a late-cycle, capital-race business with no verified edge, and its honest upside is about $75M a year, not $100M.

Sizing convention: figures are the business's own net revenue (fees, spreads, or gross margin on materials and staffing), not pass-through of parts, wages or financed rent. The pool file uses the same convention ("a USM/PMA business at 10-20% gross margin needs roughly $0.5-1B of throughput"). Gross-billing figures are given where they differ. Median = 5-7 years, competent execution. Upside = 5-7 years, top-decile execution.

## Idea-by-idea evidence

### 1. Slot Book (kill)

**Competitors and substitutes**
- Engine-management firms already sell "get me a better shop visit": AJW negotiates contract terms and workscope with a "preferred supplier network" and claims purchasing power above many airlines ([AJW](https://ajw-group.com/engines/key-services/engine-repairs), [Donavia deal](https://avitrader.com/2015/09/08/jsc-donavia-deploys-ajw-engines-to-manage-engine-shop-visits/)). Acumen is similar (per pitch, not re-verified).
- Lessors buy capacity directly through framework agreements (StandardAero-AviLease), and big airlines bundle it in PBH contracts, which IATA says also limits independent access ([IATA](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-24-urgent-action-needed-to-ease-engine-mro-bottlenecks/), [afm.aero](https://afm.aero/engine-mro-bottlenecks-disrupting-airline-operations-with-shop-visits-set-to-rise-sevenfold-by-2040-iata-study-finds)).
- I searched three ways for a neutral slot aggregator or exchange and found none. The gap exists, but the reasons below suggest it exists because the economics are poor.

**Premise problems**
- The pitch's why-now says capacity reservation "just became the currency" via StandardAero-AviLease. The release describes a general terms agreement that "paves the way" for MRO services, not reserved capacity. StandardAero's published terms say it "has no obligation to perform Services and may reject any Order." A take-or-pay block with a guaranteed induction window is a contract shops do not appear to sign today (the pitch itself marks shop behavior as an insider hypothesis).
- Bain's primary cause of slow turnaround is parts (OEM parts demand outpaces supply by 10-20%), with slot waits of 2-3 months a secondary effect. A guaranteed induction date does not shorten a 180-day turn when the engine waits for blades.
- New-generation engines (LEAP, GTF) sit largely in OEM closed networks, so the wedge is legacy CFM56/V2500, where shop visits are at their peak now and the next surge goes to closed-network engines (Bain).

**Revenue math check**
- The pitch assumes ~4,700 narrowbody visits a year. An IBA-sourced figure I found puts visits at ~3,800 a year through 2027 (scope unclear) and above 4,000 in 2028, so the base is plausibly 15-20% high.
- At 3,900 visits x 40% mid-tier x $4.5M = $7.0B of mid-tier spend; a 4.5% take is $316M at 100% share. $100M needs ~32% share. The reservation fee (10-15%) is a deposit, not revenue.
- The take-or-pay exposure sits on the broker's balance sheet. If scarcity eases (IATA/Bain: shops are adding lines), the book is stranded. Shops can also sell direct and keep the premium.

**Sizing.** Median $4M: a few dozen visits a year at ~4%. Upside $35M: ~175 visits a year (about 12% of mid-tier visits) with the take intact. That needs shops to sign binding capacity, regulators and OEM licenses to allow resale, and scarcity to last. None of those is plausible at top-decile odds.

### 2. The Repair Library (weak)

**Competitors**
- Chromalloy: private, database estimates of $1.0-1.4B revenue and ~2,300-3,800 staff; its release states 50+ DER repairs including 25 advanced repairs on CFM56-5B/7B, plus an FAA-approved PMA HPT blade ([Chromalloy](https://www.chromalloy.com/chromalloy-secures-faa-approval-of-cfm56-high-pressure-turbine-blade-pma/)).
- HEICO Flight Support Group: FY2025 net sales $3.12B, operating income $750M ([HEICO 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/46619/000004661925000076/a10312025ex991earningsrele.htm)).
- FTAI spent seven years and more than $100M to bring one FAA-approved blade alternative to market with Chromalloy ([FTAI call](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ftai-aviation-q2-earnings-call-highlights)). GKN and OEM programs (Pratt's additive GTF repair) also compete.

**Premise check.** The mechanism is real: major repair data needs FAA approval; DERs approve multiple-use repair data through repair specifications ([FAA N 8110.111](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Notice/N%208110.111.pdf), [AC 33-6](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_33-6.pdf)). IATA says CFM committed in Jan 2026 that CFM manuals can be used with non-CFM parts and repairs ([IATA](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/2026-01-20-01/)), and other reports say CFM repair licensees may perform licensed repairs. That helps adoption but also means shops have several routes to a repair that do not go through a new library.

**Revenue math check**
- The pitch's $5.6B repair pool and $2.2B independent-addressable slice are the pitch's own multiplications (engine MRO $50.7B x 55% x 20% x 40%), not sourced.
- Royalty of 17.5% on $200M licensed value is high, and licensed shops with their own schemes (Chromalloy-type) are the least likely licensees. Licensing alone would need $570M of repaired value, so the plan leans on owned cells.
- Scale check: Pratt's entire GTF additive program projects ~$100M of recovered parts over five years, about $20M a year. The model needs several programs of that size.
- The $300k per scheme R&D figure fits simple repairs, not the hot-section parts where the money is (FTAI's $100M+ blade).

**Sizing.** Median $10M: ~20-30 schemes, two small hubs, tolling plus modest royalties. Upside $80M, requiring: (1) three or four hot-section schemes with DER precedent that shops adopt (moderately plausible), (2) $60-100M of capital for cells (plausible but venture has not funded this niche, per the pool file), (3) a lessor workscope clause naming approved schemes (speculative; one lessor so far is only a 90-day goal), (4) the field-evidence data moat actually forming (shops are unlikely to share CT data with a neutral party). Each is individually possible; together they are below top-decile odds, and the upside stays under $100M even then.

### 3. Fleet Buying Group (kill)

**Competitors**
- AAR Airvoyant: launched Apr 2026 with Delta and Air Canada input and launch partners Air Europa, Allegiant, Atlas, JetBlue, Thai, Virgin Atlantic; integrated with Aeroxchange's ~5,000 suppliers; demand-consolidation, vendor-optimization and negotiation agents are scheduled for later in 2026 ([DOM](https://www.dommagazine.com/node/343238), [Placera](https://www.placera.se/telegram/aar-introduces-ai-powered-procurement-platform-for-airlines-mros-20260421)). That is the pitch's year-2 and year-3 product, built by a $2B-plus supplier that also owns inventory.
- SkySelect: $9M raised Mar 2026, 3,000+ suppliers, $6B cumulative and $1.3B in 2025 processed, customers include JetBlue, Sun Country, Vueling ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/03/12/skyselect-raises-9m-to-modernise-aircraft-parts-procurement-with-ai/)).
- IATP pooling (6,600 parts, 350 stations) and IATA's MRO SmartHub, agreed 2026 ([travelextra](https://www.travelextra.ie/iata-and-iatp-to-cooperate-on-airline-supply-chain-resilience/)).
- The one dedicated aviation GPO I found, AVIAA (business aviation, nearly 500 aircraft at its peak, 70+ suppliers), is listed as dead with $3.9M raised ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/aviaa), [AIN](https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/business-aviation/2018-04-05/aviaa-brings-group-purchasing-business-aviation)).

**Premise check.** FTC/DOJ withdrew the 2000 collaboration guidelines on 2024-12-11 with no replacement ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/12/ftc-and-doj-withdraw-guidelines-for-collaboration-among-competitors)). Buyer pools are judged case by case; there is no aviation equivalent of the healthcare GPO fee safe harbor. Not fatal, but it adds counsel cost and vendor-paid-fee conflict risk.

**Revenue math check.** The $40B+ annual parts spend and $50B excess inventory are SkySelect's own marketing claims. The pitch's mid-tier share (40-50%) is unverified, and the biggest slice of parts spend is sole-source OEM parts priced by contract, where pooled volume buys less leverage than in healthcare supplies. $4B of managed spend at 2.5% is 20%+ of the mid-tier pool, which the pitch itself calls heroic.

**Sizing.** Median $3M (a benchmark business with a few thousand dollars per member plus small rebates). Upside $35M: 6-8% of mid-tier spend under management, requiring OEMs and distributors to pay rebates to a new buyer collective. That is the wrong direction to bet while AAR and SkySelect are shipping pooling features.

### 4. Graded Parts (kill)

**Competitors**
- DUST Identity: MIT spinout, ~25 staff, $53M+ raised (Kleiner Perkins, Airbus, Lockheed Martin); launched Theseus in Apr 2026, a physical diamond-dust marking plus AI document verification for MROs; pilots with Airbus and others (company claims) ([AIN](https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/aerospace/2026-04-15/dust-identity-targets-counterfeit-aircraft-parts)).
- Aeroxchange eARC and Boeing: first digital FAA 8130-3 shipment, Oct 2025, rolling out to Boeing's nine repair centers pending FAA authorization ([Boeing](https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2025-10-13-Boeing,-Airline-Partners-Set-New-Standard-for-Parts-Authentication)).
- Aviation Supply Chain Integrity Coalition (Airbus, American, Boeing, Delta, GE, Safran, StandardAero, United), whose report pushes digital ARCs ([GE](https://www.geaerospace.com/news/press-releases/aerospace-industry-leaders-form-coalition-strengthen-aviation-supply-chain-integrity)). Shops' own incoming inspection and back-to-birth paperwork became standard after AOG Technics ([AOG Technics coverage](https://aviacionline.com/?p=214889)).

**Premise check.** The indemnity layer is the unproven part. I found no aviation-parts indemnity or counterfeit-coverage product and no evidence an insurer will price consequential AOG loss. AOG Technics involved roughly 70-86 documents and 50 part numbers, which shows rare but severe events; that is hard to price into a 1.0% premium without actuarial data nobody has. Escrow status and MGA licensing are unresolved.

**Revenue math check.** $4B of graded flow is 65% of the pool file's $6.2B used-serviceable-material figure. The "$20B non-OEM-direct" base halves an unverified $40B claim. At a still-generous 5% of a $20B base, the 2.5% fee yields $25M.

**Sizing.** Median $3M. Upside $25M (needs lenders and insurers to mandate the grade, which the pitch itself says is the only path, and the free competitors are already being funded by the OEMs that would have to adopt it).

### 5. Kit-First (weak)

**Competitors and substitutes**
- FTAI Aviation: Aerospace Products revenue $1.6B in 2025 (vs $1.08B in 2024), Q4 EBITDA margin 35%; refurbished 757 CFM56 modules in 2025, raised 2026 target to 1,200; physical capacity 3,000 modules a year across six sites; directing a growing share to third-party customers; management says CFM56 is "supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained" ([FTAI Q2](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ftai-aviation-q2-earnings-call-highlights), [FY25 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1590364/000162828026011685/ftai123125earningsrelease.htm)).
- AAR: CFM56-5B surplus material for Cebu Pacific across 30 planned shop visits, sourced through its FTAI partnership ([AviationPros](https://www.aviationpros.com/aircraft-maintenance-technology/engines-parts/press-release/53099561/aar-corp-aar-cebu-pacific-sign-cfm56-5b-surplus-material-agreement)).
- CFM Materials LP (GE and Safran JV): shop-visit support with used material, material services agreements, and customer-owned-material set management ([listing](https://theflyingengineer.com/listing/cfm-materials-lp/)). AerSale: Q1 2026 USM sales fell because it consumes the material in its own engine builds ([call](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/aersale-corporation-nasdaqasle-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript-1757114/)). AJW offers a "full engine material solution" per shop visit.

**Premise check.** Customer-furnished material is normal practice, so the legal side is fine. The shortfall is that the scarce item is the part, not the forecast. Teardown feedstock is shrinking (a third-party view in the FTAI coverage has retirements tracking 1.5-2% against an earlier 3-4% forecast), and the holders of that feedstock are the competitors.

**Revenue math check.** The pitch's own arithmetic is right: 300 visits x $2.5M = $750M revenue, $90-112M gross profit at 12-15%. But 300 visits is 6-8% of narrowbody visits and needs $120-150M of inventory capital. FTAI earns ~35% EBITDA on a similar activity, so a 12-15% margin entrant is either underpricing or buying at worse cost than the incumbent.

**Sizing.** Median: ~14 kitted visits a year, revenue ~$35M, gross profit ~$5M. Upside: revenue $150M, gross profit ~$22M, needing ~$100M+ of inventory financing, privileged teardown supply, and shops accepting induction gating (which the pitch concedes cuts against their utilization metric). Revenue passes the bar; the business does not.

### 6. Shop-Floor Ledger (kill)

**Competitors**
- EmpowerMX (EMX Vision, aircraft records): about $11M raised, acquired by IFS July 2024 ([IFS](https://www.interempresas.net/Aeronautica/569753-IFS-adquiere-EmpowerMX.html)).
- Soji AI (Hamburg/Singapore, backed by Airborne Capital; records partnerships with Active Partners, May 2026) ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251215242441/en/SOJI-AI-and-Airborne-Capital-Limited-Announce-AI-Partnership)); Amygda (Boeing-backed) ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/amygda)).
- Lufthansa Technik's AVIATAR has absorbed flydocs and AMOS; Ramco, Trax, Veryon, Rusada are the incumbent MRO systems.

**Revenue math check.** Ramco Systems, a listed MRO-software incumbent with a broader product line, reports ~$80M total FY26 revenue ([filing summary](https://filingreader.com/news-wire/mumbai/2026-07-25/ramco-systems-returns-to-profit-with-13-revenue-growth)). The pitch needs 55,000 paid seats, 7% of 800,000 technicians (the technician total is the pitch's estimate), for a capture product that sits next to the system of record. The aviation MRO-software market is quoted at ~$7B, but those analyst figures lump in ERP and are not a budget line for a capture add-on ([GMI](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/aviation-mro-software-market)). Value-per-seat ($7-9k saved vs $1.8k price) is plausible; seat count and adoption speed are not.

**Sizing.** Median $4M (a few thousand seats at lessor-sensitive shops). Upside $20M (30,000 seats at a lower effective price, with an ERP vendor not bundling a native version). Fits the round-1 cliche list (AI copilot for a vertical).

### 7. Engine Lifeline (weak; best of the pool)

**Competitors and substitutes**
- Short-term spare engines already exist: AerCap Engines provides short-term leases for unplanned maintenance on CFM56-7B and LEAP-1A ([AerCap](https://aercap.com/our-business/engines)); Shannon Engine Support (AerCap/Safran 50:50) claims 500+ TRUEngine-qualified spares for short-term cover ([SES](https://ses.aero/products-services/)); ELFC holds 200 CFM spares and bought 50 LEAP spares ([CFM](https://www.cfmaeroengines.com/press-articles/engine-lease-finance-corporation-finalizes-cfm-leap-spare-engine-agreement)); CFM runs its own lease pool tied to TRUEngine.
- Multi-owner pools are not new: Willis ran a shared CFM56-7B pool for American, Southwest, WestJet, Alaska, later Delta ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/aircraft-propulsion/aa-southwest-westjet-enter-737ng-engine-pool)). The pitch's claim that no one aggregates other owners' engines is wrong for the capital side.
- Third-party engine capital is already organized: Willis Lease total AUM $4.4B, Willis Aviation Capital platform $1.4B (up ~80% y/y), $1.3B discretionary dry powder, Q2 2026 lease rent $77.1M ([Willis Q2](https://finviz.com/news/376492/willis-lease-finance-corporation-reports-solid-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)); Willis and Blackstone targeting $1B+ over two years ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018164/000101816426000003/wlfc-20260105.htm)); Bridgepoint, AIP, GFH platform at $1B+, 11 more LEAP-1B on order ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/mro/aircraft-propulsion/gfh-joins-bridgepoint-aip-engine-leasing-venture)); a newly announced Aero Engine Leasing ([AviTrader](https://avitrader.com/tag/aero-engine-leasing/)); SMBC Aero Engine Lease and FTAI.

**Why-now partly used up.** The "institutional capital just arrived" signal that makes the idea attractive is also the crowding signal. A search for risk commentary found lessor executives warning that rents will collapse if high LEAP spare ratios reflect temporary problems (SMBC Aero Engine Lease CEO, Feb 2026; the source URL was not captured in my search results) and that premature part-outs will soften spare demand later (ELFC executive, [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/mro/aircraft-propulsion/lease-rates-challenged-premature-part-outs)). IBA says current-gen engine values have levelled off while rates stay elevated ([IBA via AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/its-a-lessors-market-says-iba-as-engine-lease-rates-and-market-values-escalate)). A vehicle launched in 2027 buys near the top.

**Revenue math check**
- Pitch: 450 engines, ~$3.9B, total rent ~$630M (16% of asset value), fee 12% of rent = $75M plus 1,170 swaps x $40k = $47M.
- Willis's rent is ~$77M a quarter on ~$3B of owned assets, about 10% a year. ISTAT quotes long-term LEAP rent of $130-150k a month (13-15% of a $12M engine) and 2025 short-term quotes of $6-7k a day ([ISTAT](https://www.istat.org/ISTAT-Online/ISTAT-Online/Aircraft/ArtMID/1205/ArticleID/1639/Appraisal-CFM-LEAP-1B)). A pool-wide 16% yield at 85% utilization at short-term rates assumes nearly every engine earns the premium day rate. At 10-14% yield the fee line is $47-66M.
- The $47M swap line is the weakest number: the pitch says a swap costs $150-300k in work, so a $40k fee is either pass-through or loss-making.
- $4B of third-party engine AUM is about three times the size of Willis's whole third-party platform. AIP reached ~$4B in about two years, but on the back of a sponsor (777 Partners).

**Sizing.** Median $12M: ~$0.8-1.0B under management by year 6 at ~1.3-1.9% blended (fee on rent, remarketing and technical fees), with the usual stall rate for emerging managers. Upside $75M: ~$3-3.5B AUM and swap-fee revenue that is net of cost. Assumptions: an anchor LP of $300-500M (plausible: GFH just did this for Bridgepoint); engine supply by secondary purchase because OEM spare allocations go to CFM's own leasing arm and SES (plausible but expensive); rents stay high to 2030 (contested); the standardized swap really cuts the $150-300k cost (unverified insider hypothesis); no TRUEngine/warranty friction when airlines install third-party engines (unverified). On a principal-lessor basis revenue would pass $100M with ~$0.9B of owned engines, but that is a financing business with no edge over Willis or Blackstone-backed rivals, so I do not count it.

### 8. Certifier Capacity (weak)

**Competitors**
- LAUNCH Technical Workforce Solutions: self-described largest aviation workforce firm, 500,000-candidate database, PlaneTechs acquisition, eLAUNCHNow technician-matching platform ([LAUNCH](https://launchtws.com/launch-aviation/)); third-party revenue estimates range from $69M to $460M, so scale is unverified.
- Aerotek posts A&P contract roles at $35-48 an hour ([posting](https://careersataerotek.dejobs.org/mesa-az/ap-mechanic/663F417092C9424692657A8C6BA776CC/job/)); Job Aire Group filed for an $8M IPO ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/105844/aviation-staffing-company-job-aire-group-files-and-sets-terms-for-an-8)); VP Aviation Technical Services; Jet Professionals (corporate aviation).

**Premise check.** 14 CFR 145.157 requires each person approving an article for return to service at a US station to be an appropriately certificated mechanic or repairman, and 145.151 keeps the station responsible for qualifying them ([eCFR text via Cornell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/145.157)). Authorization is per station and per manual, so a "portable authorization" is a faster onboarding checklist, not a legal status that hosts must accept. I did not retrieve 145.217 (contract maintenance); the pitch's 1099/W-2 and visa limits remain unverified.

**Revenue math check.** The pitch's $22 an hour spread (bill $65, pay $43) is a 34% gross margin. Typical staffing guidance puts net margin at 5-15% of bill rate. $100M of gross profit needs ~2,400 FTE and ~$300M of billings; $100M of revenue needs ~800 FTE. The pitch's own moat line calls this "the least defensible idea."

**Sizing.** Median: ~200 placed FTE, revenue $25M, gross profit $5M. Upside: ~1,000 FTE, revenue ~$120M, gross profit ~$25-30M, if a tech-enabled roster wins two or three large MRO accounts. Revenue clears the bar, but at staffing margins and with an incumbent that already has a matching platform, this is not a best-new-business candidate.

## Picks

**None.** The three ideas whose gross revenue could exceed $100M (Kit-First, Engine Lifeline, Certifier Capacity) fail on net economics, incumbents or both. Engine Lifeline came closest and would need one of the following to re-enter:

- Evidence that a standardized swap protocol cuts per-swap cost by more than half and an operator is willing to pay for it (one live swap with measured cost).
- A signed anchor commitment of $300M+ for a multi-owner pool that explicitly includes airline-owned spares.
- A reason why a new manager gets engine supply that SES, ELFC, Willis and the Bridgepoint/AIP platform do not.

## What this pool taught

Aftermarket aviation is a genuine $100B pool, but the scarcity profits sit in physical supply (parts, repairs, engines), held by balance-sheet incumbents (FTAI, HEICO, Chromalloy, Willis, AerCap). All eight ideas are intermediaries or information layers on top of that bottleneck. Information-layer ideas top out at roughly $20-35M; supply-creating ideas need hundreds of millions of capital and face scaled competitors.

# Gate report: pool P060, 340B covered-entity purchases (the spread and the rebate transition)

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with about 35 searches plus fetches of the AHA bill comparison (read via pdftotext), Drug Channels' June 2026 contract-pharmacy analysis and AJMC's report on the CVS suits. Result: 4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. **No picks.**

## What the pool research changed (applies to every idea)

1. **The hospital-side 340B wallet is small and already crowded.** Craneware (owner of Sentry) just reported FY26 revenue of $206.0M, flat on $205.7M, with ARR flat at $185M and net revenue retention down to 100%. It guided FY27 to about $185M and blamed the stalled 340B program (https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/craneware-says-full-year-revenue-below-expectations-prudent-outlook, https://quartr.com/events/craneware-plc-crw-h2-2026_FL5iwYaZ). Pillr bought CaptureRx in May 2026 and says it supports 500+ organizations (https://pulse2.com/pillr-health-capturerx-acquisition-expands-pharmacy-solutions-to-more-than-500-health-care-organizations/). Plenful has raised $76M and sells 340B audit plus MFP tools (https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/plenful-scores-50m-and-more-funding-news, https://www.plenful.com/use-cases/340b-audit-optimization, https://www.plenful.com/use-cases/mfp-intelligence). R1 sells a contingency "340B Recovery" service (https://www.r1rcm.com/what-we-do/regulatory-navigation/340b-recovery). McKesson/Macro Helix sells "340B Impact" analytics (https://www.mckesson.com/health-systems/business-services-solutions/professional-advisory-services/340b-impact/). A 2023 study counted 48 TPA firms (https://www.healthcare-economist.com/2023/12/11/who-are-340b-third-party-administrators/). A new hospital-side vendor needs roughly 10% of the whole category to reach $100M.
2. **The manufacturer side just consolidated.** Model N bought Kalderos (Truzo) on 2026-06-17, so the best-known 340B platform now sits inside a public gross-to-net vendor that sells to the same manufacturers (https://www.modeln.com/company/news/press-center/model-n-acquires-kalderos-to-expand-its-340b-capabilities-and-gross-to-net-revenue-management/). Second Sight's Beacon was the platform for all eight manufacturers approved in the 2025 pilot (https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/hrsa-approves-8-drug-manufacturers-340b-rebate-models).
3. **The rebate "why now" is narrower and shakier than the pool file suggests.** HRSA's revised pilot covers only selected drugs for 2026 and 2027, under 5.5% of 340B sales, and the manufacturer bears all platform costs (https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-lifesciences-hltc-340b-drug-rebate-pilot-program-revised-by-hrsa). Becker's reports 10 approved manufacturers on Oct 1 (https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/hrsa-approves-10-drugmakers-for-340b-rebate-model-pilot-program/). The AHA sued again on Oct 9 (https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-10-09-aha-others-file-suit-stop-costly-340b-rebate-program-harming-hospitals-and-patients). The Senate SUSTAIN bill would end the pilot, and SECURE would require upfront discounts for four years. Rebate-dependent revenue has to be haircut for that.
4. **The Medicare cut is real but its size is a little off in the ideas.** CMS proposes ASP minus 33.4% for 340B-acquired separately payable drugs from 2027-01-01, with JG/TB/XX modifiers. CMS's own estimate is $4.55B off Original Medicare spending (about $5.7B including beneficiary cost-sharing), not the $4.85B used in idea 2 (https://www.huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/cms-proposes-to-reduce-340b-drug-reimbursement-under-2027-opps-rule, https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1814610/medicare-payment-cuts-for-340b-drugs-take-two-cms-proposes-37-cut-to-340b-hospitals). Children's, PPS-exempt cancer and rural sole-community hospitals are exempt. The final rule was not out when I checked. The 2018 ASP-22.5% cut was struck down in 2022 for lack of a survey (https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/health-care-counsel-blog/2027-opps-and-asc-proposed-rule-cms-proposes-deep-340b).
5. **Size anchors that hold up.** Covered entities bought $100B in CY2025 (HRSA via AHA, in the pool file). Contract-pharmacy locations are 31,593, relationships 239,905, and the five chains hold 77% of relationships (https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/06/the-340b-contract-pharmacy-market-in.html). Nephron put the five operators' 340B gross profit near $3.4B in 2024 (same source). The money is real. The part that is contestable by a new software or services layer is small.

| 1 | Retro-Flag | weak | ~$25M | (a) The $6.5B is a PhRMA-funded BRG estimate of Medicaid rebates not collected, and by law no rebate is owed on a 340B-priced unit, so it is not money a data-correction loop recovers. It is recovered only by moving units back into the rebate program, which is a state-versus-hospital payment fight (https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/finance/340b-pulled-65b-drug-rebates-medicaid-2024-phrma-backed-report-finds). Indiana's version is a state-estimated $63M a year, FQHCs were carved out in April, and hospitals still oppose it (https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/05/01/health-clinics-exempt-from-drug-discount-rule-change/). (e) NACHC counts 37 states with laws protecting community health center 340B savings (https://www.nachc.org/a-year-in-review-state-legislative-trends-in-340b-in-2025/). $100M needs about 15 states to adopt Indiana-style policy. |
| 2 | Route Desk | weak | ~$30M | The premise is real and the idea's own break-even math checks out: at ASP-33.4% versus non-340B at ASP+2.7% bought and ASP+6% paid, the break-even discount is about 37% (https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1814610/medicare-payment-cuts-for-340b-drugs-take-two-cms-proposes-37-cut-to-340b-hospitals). Realistic: routing is worth roughly $0.5-0.8B of preserved margin a year on a $12B base ($4.55B / 0.37), and 15-25% share of a 15-20% fee is $20-40M. The "Day-One map" built from public ASP and ceiling files is a spreadsheet that consultants (McKesson 340B Impact, QuicksortRx) will give away (https://www.mckesson.com/health-systems/business-services-solutions/professional-advisory-services/340b-impact/, https://quicksortrx.com/340b-rebate-pilot). Account splitting between 340B, GPO and WAC already ships in Wellpartner's product (https://www.wellpartner.com). Everything hangs on one final rule due around Nov 1 plus litigation. I found no 2027 routing product from Pillr, Sentry, Vizient or Premier, so the gap is open, but it is a feature gap. |
| 3 | Cass for 340B | kill | ~$20M | Float cannot carry it (idea's own: $100M at Cass's yield needs half of all 340B purchases), and the three streams need 70%+ share and a 1%+ leak rate that nobody has published. Those vendor numbers are claim-capture, not invoice errors, so the leakage rate stays unmeasured. The counterparty on invoices is three wholesalers, not thousands of carriers, so the Cass analogy (many payees, high error rate) does not carry over. The one real nugget is remittance auditing: Michigan, Kansas and Mount Sinai allege about $250M of diverted 340B savings 2020-2025 because the administrator re-tags claims weeks later and the PBM repays the pharmacy a lower rate (https://www.ajmc.com/view/cvs-health-sued-over-alleged-scheme-to-siphon-340b-drug-program-savings). ## Evidence notes per idea

**1. Retro-Flag.** Competitors and substitutes: Gainwell (point of sale and rebate administration in many states), MCO PBMs, HRSA's Medicaid Exclusion File, Kalderos (now Model N), which asks states for claim-level data on behalf of manufacturers and so sits on the other side (https://www.muckrock.com/foi/new-york-16/medicaid-drug-rebate-program-claims-level-data-requests-57530/, https://help.kalderos.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048532071-How-do-I-avoid-duplicate-discounts-using-Discount-Hub). I found no vendor selling the state side and no Conduent or Myers & Stauffer 340B product. That is a genuine gap, but the buyer population is states with hospital lobbies against them. Legal premise: the duplicate-discount ban is statutory; a retroactive reprice of a paid claim is a contract fight with the pharmacy and covered entity. The earned secret (the administrator decides 340B status days later) is confirmed by the CVS lawsuits' own description of the mechanism (AJMC link above). The idea's MCO-side logic fails because the three largest PBMs own both the MCO PBM and the 340B pharmacy, as the idea notes.

**2. Route Desk.** The legal premise (340B is elective per unit) holds, but DSH, children's and cancer hospitals are barred from GPO purchasing of covered outpatient drugs, so their non-340B alternative is WAC, not GPO, which moves the break-even down a little. I could not verify the idea's D.D.C. 2026-03-31 replenishment ruling. The 340B route versus WAC route comparison depends on a final rate (CMS also solicited ASP minus 28%).

**3. Cass for 340B.** Cass's 2025 revenue of $190.8M is consistent with the quarterly figures I found (46.41 + 44.40 + 50.06, implying about 49.9 in Q4), but I did not open the release (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260122966914/en). The "about $94B of annual freight payments" yield was not verified. Wellpartner's published fee schedule and the idea's 10-14% spread figures were not found in public sources.

**4. Switzerland.** Legal: the House SECURE Act (H.R. 9599, introduced 2026-07-06) and Senate SUSTAIN Act (S. 5244, 2026-08-05) both create an independent clearinghouse and restrict manufacturer or PBM use of its data; Cassidy's draft offers a Secretary-run claims repository (AHA comparison above). All three differ on rebates, so the only constant is a federal contract, not a market. Whether HRSA has approved any vendor for the 2027 pilot other than Beacon or Truzo is unconfirmed.

**5. Open TPA.** Wellpartner's reported $382M of 2023 TPA fees and the Walgreens 8-20% fee range could not be confirmed from sources I could open (the Senate HELP letter returned 403). The Drug Channels page I opened does not give claim counts or TPA fees, so the idea's 100M-claim base is unsupported.

**6. JG-Day for Payers.** I could not find how any MA plan will treat JG, TB or XX in 2027. The 54% MA share and the $15B MA 340B base are the idea's estimates.

**7. Specialty Door.** I found no commercial ownership ban for specialty pharmacies, consistent with the idea. The "over 70% frozen out" figure is a rounding up of the 68.8% in a 2021 survey.

**8. Pigeonhole.** The statutory lower-of rule and CMS's refusal to police nonduplication are confirmed. Manufacturer counts and the $2.2B duplicate estimate are the idea's own. Health Affairs' July 31 piece gives no dollar figure for duplicates (could not open; read via search summary).

## Picks

**None.** Reasons, tied to the lessons file:

- **No bottom-up path to $100M survives.** The best ceilings are Pigeonhole at about $35M and Route Desk and Switzerland at about $30M. The two ideas with real Medicare-sized money (Route Desk, Pigeonhole) each need 40% of a pool or 4-5 million dollars from every possible buyer, and the idea files flag this themselves.
- **Hidden incumbents again.** Shields (80+ systems), Model N/Kalderos, Beacon, Plenful, R1, Pillr/CaptureRx, McKesson/Macro Helix, QuicksortRx, Wellpartner and Gainwell each cover a slice of the eight ideas.
- **Wrong payer or no budget line.** Retro-Flag sells to states that are in a fight with their own hospitals; Switzerland's money is a federal fee that does not reach the operator; JG-Day sells to plans that edit claims themselves.
- **Why-now partly used up or still unripe.** The Medicare rule is proposed, the rebate pilot is in its second lawsuit, the Senate bill would end it, and PBM-reform dates fall in 2028-2029.
- **Shrinking to survive.** The defensible versions are a Day-One map for 100 hospitals, a Louisiana-only reconciliation, or a manufacturer-side bound for one drug. Each is under $30M, so I call them dead rather than narrowing them.

What would reopen the pool: (1) CMS finalizes ASP-33.4% with no exemption widening and the court lets it stand, which makes Route Desk a 2027-2029 compliance spend for roughly 1,000 hospitals (still tops out near $40M unless MFP-on-Part-B in 2028 adds a second routing layer); (2) Congress enacts SECURE or SUSTAIN and the clearinghouse contract goes to a small independent bidder; (3) a state-side vendor proves a measured, not modeled, recovery of tens of millions in one state without an FQHC carve-out.

## Gaps in this check

- Direct fetches of Health Affairs, the BRG PDF, Becker's, Fierce Healthcare and the Senate HELP letter returned 403; figures from them come from search summaries and from sources I could open.
- Final OPPS rule not yet issued when I checked, and I did not read the Federal Register text of the proposal.
- No public data on national contract-pharmacy claim counts, TPA revenue, or Wellpartner and Walgreens fee schedules; Craneware does not break out Sentry.
- No evidence on how MA plans or Medicaid MCOs will treat the new modifiers.
- Vendor claims (R1, Plenful, Kalderos) are company-reported.

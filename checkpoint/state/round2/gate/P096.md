# Gate report: pool P096, captives, group captives and fronting

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 2-5 searches each, plus page reads of the FreightWaves broker-insurance series, the McDermott reciprocal-jurisdiction note, Captive International's M&A piece, Captive Review's MSL piece, and Great American's open-program list. Result: 3 kill, 5 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The pool is real ($79.1B of Marsh-managed captive premium) but the money a new company could actually collect is a thin, concentrated fee layer, and every idea either needed double-digit share of it or a regulatory loophole that does not hold.

- The group-captive market is one incumbent's market. Captive Resources says it advises 50+ groups with 7,500 members and over $5.5B of annual premium, while Travelers' figure for all US group-captive premium is $6.5B. That is roughly 85% of the market in one consultancy (https://www.captive.com/captive-services/captive-resources ; https://captivereview.com/news/group-captives-top-6-5bn-in-us-premium-with-further-growth-expected/). Its CEO projects $10B by 2030 and 97-98% member retention.
- The "nobody consolidates this" claim is false. Strategic Risk Solutions took PE money from Integrum in 2023 and bought group-captive administrator Garnet in 2024 (https://www.captive.com/news/strategic-risk-solutions-partners-with-investment-firm-integrum ; https://www.captive.com/news/srs-acquires-garnet-captive-insurance-services). Spencer Capital agreed to buy USA Risk (https://www.businessinsurance.com/Captive-insurance-manager-USA-Risk-to-be-acquired-by-Spencer-Capital/). Holmes Murphy bought Global Captive Management, Howden bought ARM Group, Davies bought Citadel Risk, and Risk Strategies has bought three captive managers. CB Insights lists Lee Equity Partners as a 2012 leveraged-buyout investor in Captive Resources itself (https://www.cbinsights.com/company/captive-resources/financials).
- The zero-collateral regulatory route does not cover captives. NAIC reciprocal-jurisdiction status for Bermuda applies only to commercial Class 3A, 3B and 4 reinsurers and long-term Classes C, D and E (https://www.bermudareinsurancemagazine.com/news/naic-grants-bermuda-reciprocal-jurisdiction-status-5311).
- The one real shock in the pool (Montgomery v. Caribe Transport, decided 2026-05-14, unanimous: negligent-selection claims against brokers are not preempted) is confirmed (https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/bad-match-big-consequences-supreme-court-holds-freight-brokers-accountable-for-negligent-carrier-selection), but the broker premium it moves is far smaller than idea 7 assumed.

## Revenue-math corrections (where the $100M paths break)

- Idea 1: the $20B premium pool treats all 13,400 PE-backed companies as $150M-revenue businesses paying $1.5M, and counts every line as captive-eligible. PE-backed companies skew small. If 30% (my assumption) have $1M of captive-eligible premium, eligible premium is about $4B and a 6.5% fee stack is about $260M, so $100M is about 38% share. The fee stack includes a reinsurance override the sponsor's broker already earns.
- Idea 2: $2.2B of premium is not "3% of prospects"; it is about a third of current group-captive premium and 22% of the 2030 projection, against an incumbent holding about 85%. The wedge ($40-50M premium) yields about $2M of fees at 4-5%.
- Idea 3: $2B of premium at 5% only works if the zero-collateral rule applies, and AM Best-style leverage would likely require capital well above $250M for $2B of long-tail premium (my reasoning, not sourced). Share is 20% of 2030 group premium.
- Idea 4: $100M of revenue from 8-12 acquisitions at $8-15M each is arithmetically possible ($64-180M) but depends on deals that competing funded buyers are also bidding for, and on a fee pool the pitch assumed at 7-9% with no source.
- Idea 5: 5.6% effective take on $1.8B of top-slice claims is about 15% of the entire captive casualty top slice by the idea's own count, in a market where savings-share fees are barred in at least North Carolina.
- Idea 6: $125M needs 40 reinsurers paying $0.3-1.5M for anonymised benchmarks plus 2,500 captive-side buyers at $15-40K. No source shows reinsurers buy such a product, and the largest managers already sell benchmarks to the same clients.
- Idea 7: the pitch assumes $0.9M of liability premium per $300M-gross-revenue member, built on TIA's 1-3% of revenue for all insurance (FreightWaves, attributed to TIA's Chris Burroughs; I could not find the original). Albrecht's $10K to $30-40K at $30-40M gross revenue scales to roughly $0.3-0.4M at $300M, and contingent auto is only one of several lines. Corrected: 110 members gives about $35-45M of premium, so $100M needs 250-330 members, 25-35% of the assumed 900-broker tier. Year-one premium from 12-15 founding brokers is more like $5-8M than $20-30M. Fee-only revenue at 12-15% is $4-7M. Premium counted as revenue is not profit: the owners carry nuclear-verdict tail on $15-25M of capital.
- Idea 8: 80 TPAs x 40 groups x $470K is $1.5B of premium. A 2019 Milliman survey found captive coverage under 10% of stop-loss premium (https://us.milliman.com/-/media/milliman/importedfiles/ektron/observations-employer-stop-loss-2019-survey.ashx); even at 15% of $45B (about $6.8B, my assumption) the idea needs about 22% of all MSL captive premium through a channel with no precedent. Mark Farrah notes stop-loss pricing still trails claims (https://markfarrah.com/mfa-briefs/mark-farrah-associates-data-confirms-stop-loss-rate-hike-trend/), so first-layer captive results are exposed.

## Picks

None. No idea has a bottom-up path to $100M that survives the data, an uncrowded field, and an intact legal premise. The closest are ideas 7 and 8, and both fail for the round-1 reason: shrinking them to what the evidence supports (a $5M-fee broker facility, or a TPA referral channel into someone else's captive) leaves a boutique.

What would reopen the pool:

- A published count of US broker contingent-auto premium showing at least $1B a year after repricing, plus a rated-paper route (Marsh-style panel or an A- fronted RRG that shippers accept). That would turn idea 7 into a fee business on a pool large enough to carry $100M.
- Bermuda counsel and the BMA confirming that a segregated-account reinsurer with a related-party book can be treated as a 3A/3B reciprocal-jurisdiction reinsurer, plus a front willing to waive collateral in writing. That would be the only route to idea 3.
- Evidence that a funded TPA (Yuzu-type) wants to take a sponsor share in an MSL captive and has a reinsurer committed above $1M. That would change idea 8 from a channel hope into a deal.

Closest-to-viable follow-up (not a pick): a freight-broker carrier-vetting evidence file sold as software to brokers and their underwriters, priced against the Montgomery discovery question rather than against insurance premium. It avoids the RRG capital and shipper-contract problems, but carrier-vetting software (Highway, MyCarrierPackets, Carrier Assure) is already established, and nothing here sizes it.

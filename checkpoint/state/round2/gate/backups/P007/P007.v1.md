# Gate report: P007 - Japan shippers' spend on contracted road freight (multi-tier trucking subcontract chain)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Pool size: about JPY 16.6T (about $110B). Ideas gated: 8. Result: 0 kill-proof picks. 5 kills, 3 weak, 0 strong. No idea clears BOTH tests (median >= $20M and a believable $100M+ upside).

Basis note on "revenue": every figure is on the pitch's own basis. Labour, roll-up and pallet businesses book gross revenue; brokers and exchanges book the fee. Gross profit is stated next to it because in this pool (carrier operating margin 1.0%, ordinary 2.5%) pass-through revenue is cheap to show and expensive to earn.

## What this pool taught (read first)

1. The shock is real (Toriteki Act 1 Jan 2026; registers and written terms 1 Apr 2026; CLO plans due end Oct 2026; permit renewal and cost floor by June 2028), but the pain is being attacked at three levels already: the government (METI-funded traevo, MLIT relay-hub certification, MLIT pallet roadmap), owner-backed incumbents (Hacobell with Seino; Fukuyama, Senko and Logisteed relays; UPR and JPR tagging pallets) and free help (prefectural trucking associations, subsidised SME consultants).
2. Four of the eight ideas open with "I found nobody who does this". In four cases a 30-second search found them (traevo, UPR Smart Pallet, Funai Soken Logi, multi-party relays in Hamamatsu).
3. The pool's economics: carriers earn 1.0% operating margin (JTA FY2024, n=2,631). Anything that passes carrier cost through shows large revenue and small gross profit. The best-funded domestic example of the broker model (Hacobell) took about 6 years to reach JPY 2.9B gross revenue and was still loss-making.
4. The "why now" for the dock idea is partly spent: waiting plus loading plus ancillary time fell from 3h18 to 2h02 in ONE year (MLIT FY2025 survey) with no crew business involved.

## Per-idea findings

### 1. Dock Crews with a Turn-Time Guarantee - WEAK

- Competitors and substitutes
  - Timee: JPY 21.0B revenue in the six months to Apr 2026; 43% of businesses using its spot workers are logistics (Oct 2025 survey). Dock labour is already bought by the hour. https://ecnomikata.com/ecnews/eclogistics/48626/
  - Warehouse staffing agencies, 3PL in-house labour (Logisteed, NX, Sagawa Global Logistics), shipper DC staff. Japanese 3PL contracts are routinely priced per unit handled, so "per truck-turn" is a variation on a normal price list, not a new category.
  - Equipment instead of crews: MLIT lists tailgate-lifter subsidies and cage-cart loading at about one-third of the time (search summary, not primary).
  - I did not find a seller of a per-truck-turn SLA. That is a thin signal, not proof of demand.
- Numbers checked
  - MLIT FY2025 survey (published 2026-07-10, 1,032 runs): binding time 10h13 (-1h33); waiting plus loading plus ancillary 2h02 (-1h16): waiting 1h00 (was 1h28), loading 48 min (was 1h34), ancillary 14 min (was 16). So the loading minutes the crews would replace HALVED in one year. https://www.rodo.co.jp/news/222908/
  - Standard fare (effective 2024-06-01): medium truck loading or unloading JPY 2,180 per 30 min machine, JPY 2,100 manual, so about JPY 4,200-4,360 an hour is confirmed. +50% surcharge when waiting plus loading exceeds 2h. Filing rate 65.2% (May 2026); only 53% of carriers say they collect roughly standard-fare levels. The standard fare is guidance until the cost floor (June 2028 at the latest). https://wwwtb.mlit.go.jp/kinki/content/000339671.pdf
  - Idea's loading split (41 + 21 min) differs from the press (48 + 14 min); the 62-minute total is the same.
- Revenue math: JPY 15B / JPY 2,500 per crew-hour = 6M crew-hours = about 3,100 FTE is arithmetically fine. It is gross labour revenue; gross profit at a 15% margin is about $15M at $100M. A top-decile case needs about 600-750 DC sites at about JPY 20-25M each, with a Japanese operator co-founder.
- Legal: ukeoi versus dispatch is the live risk. Crews directed by DC supervisors with the shipper's equipment look like disguised dispatch (both sides exposed). The port-dock dispatch ban only covers port transport. I did not find a primary source; treat as unverified and needs a sharoushi.
- Upside assumptions
  1. Shippers buy per-turn SLAs instead of hourly labour (plausible, unproven).
  2. Ukeoi structure survives scrutiny at scale (moderate).
  3. About 3,100 FTE can be recruited in a market where labour shortage is the root cause (hard).
  4. The pain does not evaporate (it fell 38% in a year; weak).
  5. A data or standard asset (turn-time data, ASN standards) emerges so it is not just staffing (unproven).
- Verdict reason: genuine payer and price signal, but the why-now is partly spent, substitutes are plentiful and the margin is staffing margin.

### 2. Open-Book Tier-1: Floor Plus Ten Percent - KILL

- Competitors
  - Hacobell: ex-Raksul, JV with Seino since 2022; FY Jul-2019 revenue JPY 1.54B; FY Jul-2021 revenue JPY 2.94B (+34.5%) with a JPY 114M segment loss; 40,000+ partner vehicles (2023); Jan 2026 round (Regrowth, Kiyo, Shigin, Seino) raised for M&A; bought Truckers Manager from Azoop (Dec 2025). Already marketed as the fix for multi-tier subcontracting. https://www2.jpx.co.jp/disc/43840/140120220610576617.pdf ; https://thebridge.jp/en/2026/02/hacobell-expanding-logistics-dx-platform-conducts-third-party-allotment-to-accelerate-ma
  - Trabox (Visional, since 2020): 20,000+ carrier members, 700k matched contracts (2023). CBcloud PickGo (JPY 6B Series C in 2021). LIGO's Hakopuro (direct-contract matching so small carriers can be tier-1). https://thebridge.jp/2020/02/visional-buys-logistics-platform-trabox
  - US analogue: Convoy raised $400M+ and shut in 2023; digital brokerage margins are thin everywhere.
- Legal and premise: a Type-1 forwarder registration needs about JPY 3M net assets, an office and about 3 months, so thousands hold one (no licence moat). Registered forwarders owe the actual-carrier register and written terms from 1 Apr 2026, which also burdens you. I found no statutory "10% fee"; it is the standard fare's allowed subcontract-fee idea, not a rule. https://www.shigyo.co.jp/search_post/logistics/riyou-unsou/riyounso-about/ ; https://www.logi-today.com/932130
- Revenue math: the pitch counts the 8% fee as revenue. $100M of fee revenue needs $1.25B of freight. Hacobell's best disclosed year was about $20M gross freight (so about $1.6M of fee at 8%). The upside needs about 15x Hacobell's 2021 volume in 6-7 years.
- Upside assumptions: shippers collapse tiers voluntarily (the 2-tier rule is only effort-based until a hard rule, though JTA is asking for a hard one); posted fee is not undercut by Hacobell/Seino (they own carrier margin, so the conflict argument is real but also means they can match by burning cash); the 3-day-pay float is financeable (about 0.3% cost fine at scale).
- Verdict reason: the owner-backed leader did this and is small; the open-book fee is a pricing tactic anyone can copy; economics are brokerage economics.

### 3. The Anchor Roll-Up - WEAK

- Numbers verified: JTA FY2024 (n=2,631): average revenue JPY 285.2M (+8.0%), operating margin 1.0%, ordinary margin 2.5%, 52% operating-profitable, 63% ordinary-profitable. TDB: transport succession-absent rate is high (overall 52.1% in 2024; transport president average age 60.3). https://www.logi-today.com/943949
- Legal premise confirmed: amended Trucking Business Act promulgated 2025-06-11; permit renewal and the appropriate-cost duty start by ordinance within three years, so by June 2028. Panel met 2026-07-17 and 2026-08-26; recommendation by year-end; notification expected 2027 with a one-year grace. https://www.rodo.co.jp/news/223331/
- Competitors: strategic buyers SBS, Hamakyorex, Trancom, Seino, Fukuyama; brokers Batonz, Mastory, Nihon M&A Center; Hacobell raised specifically for M&A. I found no PE trucking roll-up in Japan (LOGI-TODAY's 2026-09-18 headline concerns funds chasing logistics firms' hidden real-estate gains, which is a different play). US analogue risk: FAST Group post-merger meltdown with the PE backer freezing a fund. https://www.logi-today.com/tag/ma
- Hole in the thesis: the permit is not a scarce asset. New one-truck entrants can still apply for a permit, and renewal screens weak operators out rather than raising the price of a permit. The cost floor is a floor for everyone, so it lifts margins for the buyer and the seller alike; the buyer who paid net assets plus 0.9x EBITDA gets no excess return from it.
- Revenue math: 57 average carriers (JPY 264M each) is about JPY 15B revenue, purchase at about 0.3x revenue is about $30M plus working capital. That part is fine. Profit: 1.0% margin gives about $1M on $100M; the upside needs EBIT at about 5% ($5M), and no source shows that tier-collapse, backhaul and purchasing levers do that. Median (20 deals, about $35M revenue) earns about $0.4M.
- Verdict reason: revenue is purchasable, value creation is unproven and small; do not pick.

### 4. The Unit-Load Loop - KILL

- Premise ("incumbents cannot see where a pallet is") is weak:
  - UPR launched the Smart Pallet information system in 2015; Sept 2026: UPR, Keio SFC and Japan Radio announced an active plus passive RFID tag prototype. https://www.kri.sfc.keio.ac.jp/ja/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/61cf7e5697e692d5f8cda0201fb59670.pdf
  - JPR runs the Logiarx individual-container tracking system (June 2026) and the Tag Reading Fork. https://www.logi-today.com/957360
  - UPR sells X-Order and X-DD for ordering, returns and ledgers. https://www.logi-today.com/986045
  - MLIT roadmap for FY2030: T11 share of production 26% to 50%+, rental fleet 26.5M to 50M+ pallets, shared collection sites 42 to 400+, and a joint platform among the rental operators. The government is pushing the incumbents to interoperate, not inviting a third pool. https://www.logi-today.com/625760
- Scale check: UPR (TSE 7065) FY Aug-2025 revenue JPY 15.35B, operating profit JPY 277M (1.8%), about 5.28M pallets, so about JPY 2,900 revenue per pallet-year. UPR is already the "$100M" business; the idea needs a third pool of about that size ($160M capex at JPY 4,000 a pallet) for a roughly 2% operating margin. https://finance.matsui.co.jp/stock/7065/settlement/index
- Verdict reason: done, capital-heavy, poor returns even for the winners.

### 5. The Carrier's Advocate - KILL

- Substitutes: Funai Soken Logi's shipper-negotiation consulting (cost accounting through to drafting the proposal); Rikko Jitsugyo under an Ibaraki trucking-association subsidy; prefectural free SME-diagnostician dispatch (Ibaraki, Saitama case studies); Kagoshima Trucking Association free sessions (Feb 2026); JTA's free standard-fare calculator. MLIT FY2024: 74% of carriers negotiated, 39% got the full ask, 75% got some understanding. I found no pure contingency-fee provider, but the free layer makes paid demand thin. https://www.logi-today.com/1009453
- Legal: Attorneys Act art. 72 bars compensated handling of dispute-natured matters by non-lawyers, including negotiation; a 1963 Supreme Court ruling voids such contracts under Civil Code art. 90. The pitch's "carrier sends it himself" design leaves you as a template-and-evidence vendor, which is the free layer's product. (General legal background; no trucking-specific primary source found.)
- Revenue math is correct but fatal: central case needs clients with JPY 1.5T of freight revenue (about 9% of the pool, 1,500-2,500 carriers of JPY 0.5-1B revenue each) paying 20% of a one-off uplift for 24 months. The 2028 appropriate-cost formula (the panel is already studying waiting time and loading fees) will mechanise most of the uplift.
- Verdict reason: small, free-substituted, legally narrow and one-shot.

### 6. The Neutral Clean Room - KILL

- Premise is false. traevo Platform plus traevo noWa (joint-transport matching with anonymity, run with the TDBC consortium) were selected as a METI "Ouranos Ecosystem" lead project on 2026-01-15; as of 2026-02-01: 80 participating firms, 12,000+ registered routes, 650,000+ vehicles; free trial through end-2025; claims 30-40% load-efficiency gains in trials. Matched-deal counts are not published. https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/mono_info_service/digital_architecture/ouranos/ouranos_project/20260107_traevo_applicationform.pdf
- Evidence the match step is hard: the Hokkaido METI pilot recorded 1,743 registered routes (44 firms) in FY2024 and only 82 routes (10 firms) asking for a match, 4.7%. https://www.hkd.meti.go.jp/hoksc/20250826/leaflet.pdf
- Antitrust: JFTC maintains consultation cases on joint logistics, so the legal route exists; this is not the blocker.
- Verdict reason: government-funded, free and already in market; settlement-on-savings at 30% would have to beat a free incumbent.

### 7. The Capacity Reservation Exchange - WEAK

- Pain evidence (supports the problem): WebKIT spot-fare index 143 in Sept 2026 (+5 points y/y); open load requests 164,129 (+14.2% y/y) while contracts fell 10.0% to 22,237, so the fill rate dropped to 13.5% (-3.6 points). https://www.logi-today.com/172057 . NX Research Institute: 14.2% capacity shortfall (FY2024) and 34.1% by FY2030 if nothing is done (a no-action projection, not an observed refusal rate). JILS 2025: carriage became impossible or hard from Oct 2024, worst in Dec 2024. METI ran a shipper survey on year-end shortage in Feb 2026; I could not find results.
- Competition and substitutes: no Japanese exchange-style product found (three searches). Real substitutes: annual dedicated or contract carriage (standard in the US as dedicated contract carriage), 3PL bundled capacity (NX, Yamato, Sagawa, Seino, Fukuyama), and Tokio Marine's trucking insurance with substitute-transport arrangement (since May 2024), which shows insurers see the same gap and puts the Insurance Business Act line close.
- Why it is only weak: (1) WTP is untested; the closest evidence is a 2024 article that shippers could not secure year-end trucks. (2) The product sits between spot (cheap, unreliable) and long-term contracts (reliable, direct); if the shortage is mild shippers will not pay retainers, and if it is severe carriers prefer direct long-term contracts with big shippers and skip the exchange. (3) The substitution guarantee puts capacity risk on your balance sheet. (4) The data advantage belongs first to Hacobell and Trabox, which already see tender acceptance.
- Revenue math: $100M net at a 4% take needs JPY 375B of reserved fare (2.3% of the pool, about 114M per designated shipper across 3,300 firms). Top-decile with the shortage materialising could be about a quarter of that.
- Upside assumptions: the shortage worsens as forecast (moderate, but contested by an easing business-cycle risk); CLOs book capacity assurance as a line (unproven); carriers accept retainers rather than direct deals (plausible at 0.6-1% margins); you out-run a 3PL copy (weak).
- Verdict reason: best unmet pain and largest numeric upside of the eight, but too many untested links and a median below $20M.

### 8. The Relay Exchange - KILL

- Incumbents already do it, multi-party: Logisteed plus Fukuyama trailer and tractor relay at Fukuyama's Hamamatsu branch (trial Dec 2024, full operation Apr 2025); Senko plus Fukuyama joint relay at Hamamatsu (Jun 2025); a regional relay service launched Aug 2026; MLIT is asking prefectural trucking cooperatives to run relay matching; Hokkaido Development Bureau ran the Logisk matching model in 2023. https://www.logisteed.com/jp/news/img/20250319.pdf ; https://www.logi-today.com/789585 ; https://www.rodo.co.jp/news/154070/
- The state is building the nodes: revised Logistics Efficiency Act enacted 2026-05-13; relay-hub certification with five years of property-tax relief, JRTT equity and loans, 20 hubs targeted by FY2030, ordinance effective 2026-11-01. This caps physical nodes and turns the "standard" into a public one. https://www.sakigake.jp/news/article/20260513CO0062/
- Demand signal is mixed: relay implementation was only 16% in MLIT's 2021 survey; Univearth's 2025 survey says 38% of shippers tried relay but only 13% saw an effect (from the pitch, not independently checked).
- Revenue math: 24M long-haul runs is the pitch's own guess; JPY 15B at JPY 6,000 a relay is about 10% of them, a heroic share.
- Verdict reason: no national relay-settlement startup found, but nothing proves a standalone paid layer; the largest carriers have bilateral and trilateral deals and the state is setting the standard.

## Picks

None.

Reasoning in one place:
- No idea reaches a median of $20M of revenue AND a credible $100M upside AND real profit. Idea 3 clears the revenue test on paper only ($35M median, $100M upside) but earns about $0.4M and $5M at best, and the permit-scarcity premise is wrong.
- Idea 7 has the largest real upside but a $4M median and untested willingness to pay. Idea 1 has a real price signal but a $13M median on staffing margins and a why-now that is partly spent.
- Round 2's rule is not to shrink an idea to make it survive. The only way to keep either alive is to narrow it to a boutique, so I am calling them weak and not picking them.

Cheapest tests if anyone wants to revisit:
- Idea 7: 20 CLO interviews on "what did the failed December tender cost you, and what would you pay to remove it" (this is idea 7's own week 1-4 plan; run it before building anything). Kill if fewer than 5 of 20 name a number above 1% of freight spend.
- Idea 1: ask 10 DC managers whether they now staff the dock themselves since drivers stopped loading, and who they buy those hours from. Kill if the answer is Timee for under JPY 1,800 an hour with no pain.

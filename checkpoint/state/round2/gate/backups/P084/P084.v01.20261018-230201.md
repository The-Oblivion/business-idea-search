# Gate report: pool P084, US customs-duty payments on industrial inputs and the duty-mitigation services market

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas, 34 searches (standard mode) plus 5 page fetches (19 USC 1313 via Cornell, GingerControl, Foley, a Freightfigures page, a Mondaq B2B-refund article). Result: **4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. No picks.**

Evidence caveat: search results came back as synthesized summaries, not raw pages. Only the statute (19 USC 1313) was read as primary text. CBP ruling HQ H350722, the April 2 2026 Section 232 proclamation (and its Annex IV), CSMS 68253075 and CSMS 69326983 were NOT fetched; claims about them rest on law-firm and trade-advisory summaries (Diaz Trade Law, Barnes Richardson, Foley, White & Case, Polsinelli, Alliance, GingerControl). GingerControl is an interested vendor in several of these areas. Quantities supported by one weak source are marked "(unverified)".

## Headline finding

The pool is real ($214B of trailing-twelve-month customs duties, secondary tracker) and the legal premises mostly hold. But the eight ideas fail on three of round 1's seven failure modes:

1. **"I found no vendor" was wrong or beside the point in 5 of 8.** Optravis (msg group) already sells operational transfer-pricing SaaS with customs and anti-dumping rules inside the engine and SAP connectors (Idea 1). Contingent-fee duty recovery is crowded: Tarifflo, Duty Discovery (FRS + ProfitTrust), Leyton, Baker Tilly, Yamato America (Idea 5). CBIZ, Baker Tilly and several law firms already sell recovery to indirect tariff payers (Idea 8). A PE roll-up (Alba Wheels Up, Southfield) and three AI-stack entrants (Amari, Alchemize, Altana+Cervo) already occupy both halves of Idea 4. Altana sells provenance to CBP and to the 8 of the top 10 logistics providers (Idea 3).
2. **The fee pool is a small slice of the duty pool, and the "defensible savings rate" that carries each $100M path is unsupported.** Related-party savings of 2% of customs value (Idea 1), 0.6% overpayment (Idea 5), 12% imported-input content (Idea 2) and 40,000 importers with $250k+ of duty (Idea 6) are all the pitch's own assumptions. Each one, corrected, lands the path at 20-45% of the entire addressable fee pool, not a plausible share.
3. **Two why-nows are mostly used up or narrower than claimed.** About $126B of the ~$166B of IEEPA refunds had already been sent to Treasury by 2 October 2026 (Idea 8). The Section 232 15% weight line only covers goods outside HTS chapters 72, 73, 74 and 76, and only a thin band of SKUs sits near it (Idea 7).

Where the pitches were right: the legal premises. Drawback transfers "directly or indirectly" (19 USC 1313(j)(2)) and the importer-liability cap at the amount it authorized ((k)(2)) are exactly as stated. HQ W548314's pre-existing-formula rule and the 21-month reconciliation window are real. H350722 does treat OCR of entry data and Form 5106 filing as customs business. The SEC contingent-fee bar on audit clients (Rule 2-01(c)(5)) is real. The Perfectus $549.5M FCA settlement (12 May 2026) is real, though it was an AD/CVD "pallets" case on 2011-14 conduct, not a Section 301 origin case.

## Per-idea notes

### 1. Intercompany Reconciler (weak; median $12M, upside $70M)
- **Premise:** confirmed. CBP HQ W548314 (2012) accepts post-importation TP adjustments if the written policy predates the imports and is used for US tax; reconciliation (21 months) is the "strongly encouraged" vehicle. The KPMG survey (100+ companies) found only 54.5% made a customs valuation adjustment after a TP adjustment, so the gap exists.
- **Competitors the pitch missed:** Optravis (msg group; founded 2014, acquired 2021; SAP R/3 and S/4 connectors; customs and anti-dumping rules in the calculation engine; Henkel). Missing from Optravis is the ACE entry-filing arm, which is the pitch's real differentiator. Also GingerControl (PO-to-entry reconciliation service), and Big Four, Forvis Mazars, FTI and Alston playbooks.
- **Math:** Census 2025 related-party imports are $1,663.1B of $3,391.2B (49.0%, verified). That counts US firms importing from their own foreign subsidiaries, which are not all multinational groups with a TP policy to reconcile. The 6.3% rate is an all-import average (it includes since-refunded IEEPA duty), not a related-party rate. At the pitch's 2% reduction on $360B of customs value, savings are $504M and a 20% fee is $101M. At a 0.5% reduction (timing and true-up fixes, which is what the legal premise actually supports) the fee is about $25M. A lower import price also moves profit into the US (more US tax) and invites the parent-country authority to push back, and an after-the-fact downward adjustment fails W548314.
- **Upside:** top-decile is an enterprise SaaS-plus-managed-filing product at about $150k a year for ~500 large groups = ~$75M. Needs a licensed-broker partner, a clean answer to the 1059A and 482 conflict, and beating Optravis and an eventual ONESOURCE module to the ACE link. Plausibility: moderate to low.
- **Fee-bar premise:** Rule 2-01(c)(5) is real but narrow (audit clients of SEC-registrant auditors). Non-Big-Four firms (Sandler & Travis, BDO-tier, trade boutiques) already take contingency.

### 2. Rights Ledger (weak; median $6M, upside $60M)
- **Premise:** confirmed from statute. 19 USC 1313(j)(2): claimant may be a party that received the goods "directly or indirectly" from the importer, provable by ordinary business records. (k)(2): the importer is liable for the lesser of duties claimed or duties it **authorized**, jointly and severally with the claimant. Because the importer controls the authorization, the "insurer-backed indemnity" is less needed than the pitch says. 19 CFR 190.33(b): further transfers need records; no multiple substitutions.
- **Competitors:** no cross-company ledger or marketplace found. Single-claimant vendors already do multi-party drawback with the broker as "firewall": Alliance Customs Brokers, C.H. Robinson, Flexport, Consyn, Lightsource, GingerControl, Camtom. Scale not disclosed.
- **Math flaw:** (j)(2) substitution needs exported goods of the same 8-digit HTS as the imported goods. The pitch's base (12% imported-input content of manufactured exports) is manufacturing-drawback logic. Paid drawback is about $1B a year (GAO-20-182, 2019); a vendor blog says claims reached ~$3.9B in 2023 (unverified); "80% unclaimed / $15B" is vendor marketing with no CBP primary. $667M of claims a year would be 15-65% of everything paid at those figures.
- **Upside:** the new Section 301 duty (10-12.5% on 60 economies, effective 24 July 2026) is described as drawback-eligible by Alliance and GingerControl (the CSMS itself is reported silent). If it persists, export drawback becomes a VAT-refund-like margin lever and exporters that do not import need the ledger. That is a real upside, but it depends on 301 surviving a pending court challenge and on distributors joining for a revenue share.

### 3. Origin Rail (weak; median $5M, upside $45M)
- **Demand is mandated:** Perfectus $549.5M (12 May 2026) and Ceratizit $54.4M (Dec 2025) FCA settlements; CF-28 volume reportedly up since September 2025; a CBP ANPRM on supply-chain visibility could require foreign export documents. Origin Rail does sell into that.
- **Why it still fails:** a $20 per-shipment attestation backed only by factory self-attestation is not evidence CBP will credit; real evidence needs audits ($1-5k a visit), so the price and the cost collide. Altana ($1B valuation, ~$322M raised, CBP Global Business Identifier program, passport ID on entries, 8 of top 10 logistics providers) owns the provenance rail and bought Cervo AI in July 2026. SGS, Intertek and Bureau Veritas own factory-paid verification at scale. A shared "vault of bad facts" has the privilege problem the pitch itself names.
- **Math:** 25,000 factories x $2k plus 2.5M shipments x $20 needs ~25% of evidence-sensitive entries. The pitch calls that heroic; I agree.

### 4. Licence Bank (weak; median $18M, upside $90M)
- **What exists:** Alba Wheels Up (Southfield Capital platform 2021; add-ons KSI, V.T. Mancusi, John A. Steer, Perishable Specialist), Livingston (Platinum Equity), Flexport Customs, Amari ($4.5M seed, 30+ brokerage customers), Alchemize (YC S26, two founders, ~$500k pre-seed), Altana+Cervo ("end-to-end agentic customs broker workflow"). H350722 is confirmed by commentary but the ruling text was not read.
- **The scarcity premise is overstated:** a corporate broker licence needs a licensed officer plus responsible supervision, and national permits let Flexport scale. The real bottleneck is entry-writer labour, which AI removes for every broker, including the ones buying Amari. So the AI cost edge is not cornered by the acquirer.
- **Math:** Mordor $5.48B US brokerage market (paid summary); full-service fees $100-250 per entry; KGH sold at 16.3x EBITDA with an 18% margin (strategic premium, not a small-firm comp); small-firm listings run ~1.5x revenue. $100M revenue is ~35 firms at $2.9M each; capital $50-100M. It is on round 1's cliche list ("AI roll-up").
- **Upside:** capital-backed team buys 30-40 firms in 6-7 years, retains customers, AI lifts margins, and chassis fees from vendors add. Closest to $100M of the eight, but it is a financing story and the cost edge is replicable.

### 5. Second Filer (kill; median $7M, upside $45M)
- Tarifflo (YC S26, licensed-broker ex-KPMG founder, "fee only on refund", claims 1 in 8 of 1M+ entries overpaid, $1.3M recovered for one importer); Duty Discovery (FRS + ProfitTrust, paid after refund; press release claims $5.8B a year overpaid); Leyton; Baker Tilly; Yamato America; KlearNow post-entry audits. The pitch's "licensed, independent, contingent, counsel-supervised entrant has no conflict" describes the incumbents.
- Windows: post-summary correction ~300 days, protest 180 days after liquidation, 1520(d) one year. Only drawback reaches five years. The pitch's "five years of ACE data" is mostly unfixable.
- The 0.6% overpay rate is the pitch's 5% assumption multiplied by a vendor's self-reported 1-in-8. Even if true, the whole market's fee pool at 30% is ~$385M, so $100M is ~26% of it.

### 6. Peer Ledger (kill; median $3M, upside $30M)
- No product pooling declared entry data for peer-outlier scoring was found (an arXiv paper benchmarks classification tools only). That gap is real, and there are reasons for it: information exchange among competing importers on values is an antitrust risk; a shared "reasonable care" file is discoverable; importers will not pool adverse facts into a vendor that also sells to CBP-adjacent buyers; cold start.
- Institutional buyers (customs sureties; $400-600 premium on a standard $50k bond) number in the tens. $150k x 300 institutions is not credible. 11,000 importers at $5k is 27% of the pitch's own unsourced 40,000. I could not find a Census size class by duty paid; my unverified guess is 8-12k importers above $250k of duty.

### 7. Weight Line (kill; median $4M, upside $30M)
- **Premise confirmed on weight** (Foley, White & Case, AFS, Polsinelli, Mallory), against one blog (Freightfigures) saying value. Duty is on full customs value; below 15% aggregate covered-metal weight a product is outside Annex I-B/III duty, but only for goods **outside HTS chapters 72, 73, 74 and 76**. Fittings, fasteners and cookware (the most metal-intensive derivatives) are excluded. The boundary wording at exactly 15% differs by source.
- The older derivative lists covered ~$150B of 2024 imports (Global Trade Alert, CRS); the share outside chapters 72-76 and near the line is much smaller. Hitting $1B a year of avoided duty is ~4-6% of all relevant 232 duty. Redesign is one-time per SKU. Altana's duty calculator took 232 melt/smelt inputs in November 2025. 232 rules have changed repeatedly (Express Fasteners v. US pending).
- A compliance-evidence SaaS for exemption claimants is slightly larger than the redesign business, but I found no claimant count.

### 8. Pass-Through (kill; median $8M, upside $35M)
- CAPE had accepted ~$134.7B by 15 September; ~$126B of ~$166B had been sent to Treasury by 2 October; Phase 3 (6 October) covers ~$11B with narrow eligibility. No general duty to pass refunds downstream; claims turn on invoice and contract language, strongest where the surcharge was itemised.
- Already served: CBIZ, Baker Tilly, Tariff Recovery Group, Jones Walker, AGG, Kelley Drye, Foley. UPS and FedEx are passing refunds to shippers voluntarily; consumer class actions (Lululemon, ~$240M) push importers to settle. Non-lawyer demand letters carry an unauthorized-practice risk. I found nothing on "One Eagle Advisory".
- The author's own standing-audit pool (~$190M total fee pool) puts $100M at half of it.

## Picks

**None.** No idea clears both bars (upside of at least $100M on assumptions I believe, and median of at least $20M).

Closest: **Licence Bank** (median $18M, upside $90M), and the two assumptions it needs are the weak ones: that an acquirer can raise $50-100M of capital for brokerages while Alba and the AI-stack vendors already compete, and that the AI cost edge stays with the owner instead of being competed away. **Rights Ledger** (upside $60M) has the best real asymmetry (a standard no one has built, a confirmed legal premise, and a 301 duty that may make drawback mainstream), but its median is $6M and it hinges on 301 surviving a court challenge.

## Pool note

A $214B duty pool does not mean a big fee pool. Every real gap here (TP-customs alignment, indirect drawback rights) is a $50-70M niche, and the "no vendor found" claims mostly dissolved on a second search. The one $100M mechanism was a financed roll-up, which is a capital story, not a product edge.

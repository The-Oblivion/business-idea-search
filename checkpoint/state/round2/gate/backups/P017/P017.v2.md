# Gate report: pool P017, US manufactured-home sales and chattel (personal-property) finance

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 2-5 searches each, plus primary-source reads (Ginnie Mae April 2026 remaining-principal-balance report, America's Credit Unions' FHFA letter, the text of H.R. 7792 and the CRS summary of P.L. 119-101, 24 CFR Part 201 via secondary summaries, Zippy's community page). Result: 4 kill, 3 weak, 1 strong. One pick: Covenant Parks.

## Headline finding

The pool is real: about $13.6B of new homes a year, a $5-11B chattel origination market, and roughly 4.3M land-lease homesites paying lot rent. But this batch was written as if the finance and insurance wedges were empty. They are not, and four ideas die on facts the pitches did not check.

- **Fresh capital has just arrived in every lending wedge.** Zippy closed a $20M Series C on 14 Oct 2026 and already runs a community channel for park operators. Pretium agreed to buy Cascade Financial Services on 10 Sep 2026. Apollo closed $850M of a manufactured-housing fund. The park-lender integration in Lot-Lender is a smaller step from these players than the pitch admits, because 21st Mortgage already runs a park-approval program.
- **The Title I premise is false on the primary data.** Ginnie Mae's manufactured-housing MBS had only $92M of principal outstanding in April 2026, down every month from $131M in April 2024, so nothing is being issued. Title I insurance is capped at 10% of a lender's portfolio under 24 CFR Part 201, so it does not turn a chattel loan into a Treasury-linked asset.
- **MSI (Baldwin Group) launched park-lease embedded insurance for manufactured-housing communities nationwide on 30 Sep 2026.** That is 18 days before this gate. Park Shield's "nobody can sign the lease addendum" claim is stale.
- **Grant-funded infrastructure is the wrong payer.** HUD's PRICE program got $0 of new money in FY2026 and the President's FY2027 budget proposes eliminating it.
- **The only idea whose gap survived is the one that is not a financial product.** Covenant Parks is an owner-operator positioned against REIT rent growth. Its revenue math needed correcting (small-park lot rent is $6-8k a site, not $9.0-9.4k), which raises the sites and equity it needs by about a third, but the $100M path survives.

## Idea notes

### 1. Lot-Lender (weak; median $25M, upside $100M)

**Who does this today.**
- Zippy: digital chattel lender, 27 states, more than $600M of capital commitments, Forum Markets up to $150M of loan purchases, $20M Series C from Saluda Grade on 14 Oct 2026 to launch products "across its community, retail, and wholesale" channels. Its communities page pitches park owners to convert renters, lease-to-own customers and rent-credit holders into owners, with a dedicated partner, and handles tag and title. It does not mention lot-rent data or a three-party agreement. The founders are former park owners.
- 21st Mortgage and Vanderbilt (Clayton, Berkshire): Berkshire's FY2025 10-K data tags show $31.3B of manufactured-housing loans before allowances (tagged-data extract, not cross-checked against the table). A BiggerPockets guide describes 21st Mortgage's park program: the park and owner are approved, homes are placed for $0 out of pocket, twelve months before any payment, and 21st Mortgage "creates and services the note" for approved buyers. That is a lender-park contract today.
- Cascade, bought by Pretium on 10 Sep 2026 from Centerbridge; Triad (dealers, brokers, community managers, credit unions); credit unions; UMH holds $100.0M of its own installment notes at about 7.0% and about six years.

**The "no incumbent has a contract with the park" claim is false in part.** Lender advice already says to get a written lot-rent agreement with the park before lending in a land-lease community.

**Math.** $1.0B book at about 10% gross yield is about $100M of interest revenue, but it needs about $150M of equity and $850M of warehouse debt, and Zippy five years in shows funding is the binding constraint. The median case is a $250-350M book by year 6-7. The upside needs a proven loss edge, exclusive agreements covering 100+ communities, and funding at roughly Clayton's cost, which the pitch itself says it cannot match.

**Confirmed.** FHFA's proposal (91 FR 37848): 65.6% chattel denial vs 8.8% for site-built, 9.24% average rate vs 6.63%, chattel 70-80% of new homes, final rule expected effective by 1 Jan 2028, comments closed 24 July 2026. UMH's letter says the GSEs have not bought a single chattel loan in 18 years.

**Still alive because** the central hypothesis (60-day lot-rent history predicts default better than FICO) is genuinely unknown and testable in 90 days. That is a research bet, not a gate pass.

### 2. Open Lot (weak; median $60M, upside $250M, all revenue)

- The consolidators are the manufacturers. Cavco closed American Homestar (19 stores, 2 plants) for $190M in Sep 2025. Champion agreed in May 2026 to buy 11 Homes Direct stores, described as the largest independent dealer in the West.
- Dealer counts conflict: about 1,200 companies at $11B (VerticaliQ), 2,438 businesses (IBISWorld), 4,077 retailers and distributors receiving factory shipments (MHI, Q1 2026). I could not find any source for dealer gross margin, so profit per $100M of revenue is unverified; my working assumption is low double-digit gross margin and mid-single-digit EBITDA.
- 21st Mortgage says it serves almost all independent retailers, so independents already shop lenders. The neutrality edge is thinner than pitched.
- Revenue math checks ($9M average dealer, 11 dealers is $100M), but revenue is not the question. Upside needs 25+ dealers at 0.3x sales or less in a -7.3% shipment year, multi-brand wholesale terms against plants favoring their own stores, and retaining F&I reserve under state law. It is a distribution roll-up with no moat.

### 3. Park Shield (kill; median $8M, upside $65M)

- MSI, a Baldwin Group MGA, launched embedded insurance plus LeaseTrack compliance tracking for manufactured-housing communities nationwide on 30 Sep 2026, extending a renters program that reached over a million residents.
- Triad has sold a community insurance program billed with rent since at least 2017 ($156 a year, community-owned rentals). Complete Markets markets a Mobile Home Parks Program. CoverTree has raised $23M with Markel, and sells Maple, resident-insurance software for property managers.
- Math: as an MGA at 15-20% commission, $100M needs $500-650M of premium, 13-17% of a $3.9B pool that rests on unverified counts and consumer-site premiums ($700-1,600 a year). As a risk-bearing carrier it needs $50M+ of surplus. The upside case is both large REITs plus regionals, roughly 60,000 policies at $1,100 = $65M of premium.
- Legal premise unchecked: licensing and referral-fee limits for a park that collects premium, and opt-out enrollment rules, vary by state.

### 4. Park Utility Co (kill; median $12M, upside $50M)

- Demand is real: AP analysis of EPA data found nearly 70% of mobile home parks running their own water systems had violations over five years, against 48% for cities.
- The money is not. Minnesota's 2024 cycle drew $13.3M of requests and funded 9 proposals for $2.7M (peak: $16M appropriated in 2023). Vermont's $13.3M Healthy Homes awards had a September 2026 completion deadline. HUD's PRICE: $225M first appropriated for FY2023, zero new money for FY2026, elimination proposed for FY2027 (authorized to 2033 by Sec. 304 of P.L. 119-101 but not appropriated). Free technical assistance exists: CDI's program reports $41.7M of grants won by 64 communities since March 2023.
- Route 1 (design-build): $100M needs 40 parks a year at $2.5M. Capturing a third of every visible grant dollar gives about $50M. Route 2 (utility) needs a $2.3B rate base; American Water paid $315M for 47,000 connections and Nexus is selling non-core systems, so consolidators want scale, not 90-home parks.
- Wrong payer: a utility charge is a rent increase by another name, and residents are already fighting rent increases. Minnesota grants carry a 25-year covenant limiting rent growth to 5%.

### 5. Covenant Parks (strong; median $25M, upside $100M)

See the pick below.

### 6. Deposit Bridge (kill; median $10M, upside $45M)

- America's Credit Unions' 24 Jul 2026 letter (PDF read) says chattel is only 0.50% of credit-union loan originations and asks the GSEs for pilot guidelines. The base for that percentage is unclear, so I could not convert it to dollars.
- A large credit union already is a top chattel lender: the NCLC 2022 comment names San Antonio FCU ($1B) among five lenders with over 52% of the estimated market. Triad runs a content and program push to credit unions. PremierOne launched a program in Aug 2025.
- I found no CUSO dedicated to manufactured housing, but the $45-65B "stock" the $6B managed book is 10% of is unsourced. The pitch's own math needs 11-24% of annual originations. A realistic upside of a $3B managed book gives about $55M. I discount that to $45M for the sales cycle through ALCO and board approvals at hundreds of credit unions.

### 7. Title I Reborn (kill; median $2M, upside $15M)

- **Primary data.** Ginnie Mae's April 2026 remaining-principal-balance report shows $92M of manufactured-housing MBS, down from $94M in March and $131M in April 2024. A pool type with that balance and a falling line has no active issuance.
- **Primary text.** 24 CFR Part 201: insurance coverage is capped at 10% of the lender's insured Title I portfolio through a reserve account, pays at most 90% of loss and only up to the reserve, and the claim is reduced by the appraised value; FHA can contest a claim for two years. The pitch's "up to 90% of loss on each loan" is the ceiling, not the cover. This is secondary summaries of the CFR, so confirm on ecfr.gov.
- **Stale premise.** The Title I modernization is law: P.L. 119-101 (ROAD to Housing Act), enacted 11 Jul 2026, Sec. 303 raises limits and sets a 30-year maximum term. H.R. 7792 as introduced (read) does not touch the insurance reserve, the 90% coverage, premiums or claims, and I did not read the enrolled text.
- $2.0B a year of issuance against $92M outstanding is not a niche to revive; it is a program the market abandoned.

### 8. Keyring (weak; median $15M, upside $80M)

- UMH shows the model works inside an owner's own parks: about 11,400 rental homes at 95% occupancy (1 Oct 2026), built over many years on a REIT balance sheet. I found no one doing it at scale in third-party parks, which may mean poor economics, not an open gap.
- The "owner lacks capital for the home" premise is partly solved: 21st Mortgage's park program places homes for the park owner at $0 out of pocket. Zippy converts rent-credit and lease-to-own residents into owners.
- Law: New York's 2019 rent-to-own law requires a written contract and refund of rent-to-own payments if the park ends the tenancy. North Carolina Chapter 47H treats disguised installment arrangements on manufactured homes as contracts for deed, and a 2022 class action (Rodriguez v. Riverstone) challenged a park operator's used-home installment contracts. I did not find the outcome.
- Economics (my estimate): $12,000 rent on $95k is 12.6% gross; after vacancy, repairs, insurance, tax, servicing and credit losses (assumed 35-40%) it is a 7.5-8% net yield, close to the cost of 70-75% debt, before depreciation of a personal-property asset. A resident paying $1,000 plus lot rent needs roughly $64k of income at one-third of income, which is above the typical profile of someone denied a chattel loan (assumption).
- The park-keeps-lot-rent, remits-home-rent design makes the park a collection agent for the fleet, adding counterparty risk.

## Picks

### Pick 1: Covenant Parks

**Sharpened pitch.** The business is the first scaled for-profit owner-operator of small and mid-size land-lease communities that records a rent covenant at closing. Rent growth is capped at the lesser of CPI or 4% a year for 10 years, with documented capital-cost pass-through reviewed by the residents' association. Delaware's CPI-linked cap produced large increases in the 2022 inflation spike, so the covenant needs a fixed ceiling. Residents get a pre-agreed formula price and a 90-day path to buy, as an option that recycles our capital, not an obligation. It is sold to three audiences. Sellers aged 65+ in right-of-first-refusal states (New York since Oct 2023, Maine since Sep 2025) get a clean, litigation-proof closing. Agency lenders get tenant-protection compliance built in (Fannie financed 19,808 pads with tenant site lease protections in 2024). Pension and insurer capital gets stable lot-rent cash flow with no rent-gouging label, and a hedge against rent regulation (a Pennsylvania bill, HB 1250, would cap increases at 4% tied to CPI; the federal Manufactured Housing Tenant's Bill of Rights Act, H.R. 2461, is only introduced). The $100M path is kept, not shrunk: about 14,000 sites. The mission is the sourcing and capital moat, not a limit on scale.

**Payer and signer.** Residents pay lot rent every month, an existing budget line. Signers: the selling owner (or the broker in a right-of-first-refusal state); the investment committee of a pension, insurer or impact fund; a Fannie or Freddie MHC lender for debt.

**Path to $100M (corrected).** Lot rent: Sun's same-property base rent is $766 a site a month ($9.2k a year, June 2026) and Equity LifeStyle's is $956 ($11.5k); independent mid-tier parks run $350-500 a month and institutional comparables in the same counties $550-750 (vendor claim). For a pool of small parks I use $7,000 a site a year. $100M / $7,000 = about 14,300 sites = about 143 communities of 100 sites = about 0.33% of 4.3M homesites. At Northmarq's Q1 2026 median of $58,400 a space, that is about $835M of assets, $290M of equity at 65% LTV, and about 22 parks a year (about $125M of purchases a year) over 6-7 years. The original pitch ($9.0-9.4k a site, 10,700-11,100 sites, $230-270M of equity) understated sites by about a third. Median case: $75-80M of equity and about 3,500 sites, about $25M of revenue.

**Competitors and edge.**
- ROC USA: nonprofit, converts parks only when residents organize, funded by grant and CDFI capital ($47M acquisition pool; $38.1M from HUD PRICE).
- New York's MHAP: low-interest loans to mission-driven buyers with a 3% rent cap.
- Minnesota's Manufactured Home Community Redevelopment grants: awardees record a 25-year covenant limiting rent increases to 5% without state permission, so covenant-plus-capital has precedent but only for grant recipients, not as an acquisition platform.
- Public REITs (ELS, Sun, UMH): underwrite 5-6% rent growth, so they cannot offer a cap.
- Private equity and funds (Havenpark, Yes!, Apollo's $850M fund, OZ Impact Funds' 21 communities for $100M): no covenant.
- Fannie's tenant-protection pricing partly overlaps but includes no rent cap.
- No for-profit covenant owner at scale found.

**Why now.** Right-of-first-refusal laws in New York and Maine (first Maine use in Jay, 2026), a Maine law imposing a per-lot fee on some large buyers (April 2026, per a stakeholder summary I did not verify against the text), pending caps in Pennsylvania and a federal bill, 2025 transaction volume up 47% (Berkadia; its 460-community count looks low), and REIT rent growth of 5.2-5.6% fueling backlash. The shock is intensifying, not used up.

**Moat.** Counter-positioning (REITs cannot copy without cutting guided rent growth), seller trust in right-of-first-refusal states, and pre-compliance if states cap rents. Thin until sellers are shown to accept a covenant bid.

**Biggest open risk.** Price-to-return. A buyer capped near 4% bids against REITs and funds underwriting 5-6% growth at a roughly 6% cap rate, and impact capital that accepts below-market returns already goes to ROC USA at near 1%. Whether a middle pool of pension and insurer capital accepts roughly 7-8% levered returns at $290M scale is unproven.

**Cheapest next test (under 30 days).** Underwrite five live listings in New York and Maine with the cap and without it, and compute how far below market the covenant bid sits. Get written responses from a Fannie/Freddie MHC lender on whether a covenant earns pricing beyond tenant protections, and from three pension or insurer allocators on a 7-8% target at $50M+ tickets. Ask 20 sellers aged 65+ through brokers whether a bid within 5% of market with a covenant and a clean closing wins. Success: three LOIs and one allocator term sheet.

### Pick 2: none

Lot-Lender would be next, but the park-lender wedge and the community channel exist (Zippy, 21st Mortgage) and the loss-edge claim is unproven. Open Lot has the surest revenue but is a weak, moatless roll-up. Keyring fails on thin spreads and third-party-park legal exposure. Picking none is better than picking a weak idea.

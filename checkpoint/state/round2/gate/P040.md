# Gate report: pool P040, US banks' and credit unions' spend on core, digital-banking and processing vendors (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry)

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 5-8 searches each (about 50 in total), plus primary-source fetches (Fiserv Investor Day transcript filed with the SEC, CU*Answers financial reviews, NCUA Q1 2026 data, Fed FedNow statistics, CSI and Candescent deal releases). Result: 2 kill, 6 weak, 0 strong. Zero picks. Evidence log with every URL: P040.notes.working.md; numbered snapshots in backups/P040/.

## Headline findings

- The pool is real ($19.5B a year) but the money is locked inside vendor bundles, and the layers around it top out at $30-70M, not $100M. Every service layer the ideas propose (conversion assurance, contract benchmarking, retention, shared back office, mirror ledger) has a funded or decades-old incumbent, or sits on a conversion/renewal budget that is only 10-20% of what a bank spends on the event.
- The strongest single fact in the pool kills the "why now" of Sunset and weakens Parity. At its 14 May 2026 Investor Day (SEC filing DEFA14A), Fiserv publicly committed to no forced core migrations, said clients no longer need a large, expensive conversion to modernize, and attributed a doubling of core attrition (75bp to 150bp of Financial Solutions revenue) to its own consolidation of cores "from 16 to 5". The vendor tried the sunset the idea sells, lost clients, and reversed. FIS and Jack Henry have announced no sunset program either (Jack Henry's line is "at your own pace").
- Several pitched "gaps" are populated. Thought Machine plus AWS Transform launched an AI migration pipeline on 29 Sep 2026 that extracts business intent into specifications and tests (target: Thought Machine only). Tweezr partnered with 10x in May 2026 to recover hidden business rules from source code. Mechanical Orchard has raised over $84M for behavioural-equivalence mainframe rewrites. Fiserv announced OpenAI agents aimed at core conversions (secondary source). Finzly BankOS, Volante ($116M raised), Episode Six ParallelLedger, Percipient (bought by Accenture, Jan 2025) and DCI/Nymbus sidecars occupy the "ledger beside the batch core" space. Pinwheel (about $77M raised, wired into Q2, Candescent, Narmi, Bankjoy) sells deposit and bill switching. Velera (PSCU plus Co-op Solutions, about $1.3B revenue, about 16B transactions) and SHAZAM are already member-owned processing cooperatives.
- Tenancy's claim that no shared-services CUSO has passed a few dozen owners is false. CU*Answers has 210+ credit unions in 34 states, 310+ staff, and FY2024 sales of $77.9M after roughly 50 years. That is both the best proof of demand in the pool and the ceiling a new entrant has to beat in six years.
- Two dollar assumptions in the pitches were off. CSI sold for about $1.6B on $291M of revenue (about 5.5x revenue, not the 2-3x Bureau assumes). Typical all-in community-bank conversions in the filings I found cost $0.5M to $3.7M, so the $350k Parity takes per conversion is 10% of a big conversion and over half of a small one.

## Notes by idea

**1. Parity.** Competitors named: Thought Machine+AWS Transform (Sep 2026), Tweezr (with 10x), Mechanical Orchard, FIntegrate (archives, claims $40-250k typical data conversion cost), QualityKiosk/Katalon/ContextQA (parallel-run regression testing), Cornerstone/Plante Moran/Wipfli (PMO), vendors' own conversion teams. Substitutes: two to three mock conversions by the vendor with the bank's staff sign-off, which is what banks do today. Premise check: the FDIC proposal of 17 Sep 2026 is real (90-day standard review, 5-day de minimis). Jack Henry deconversion fees were $10.9M (FY23) and $23.0M (FY22), mostly from acquired clients, so the exit-fee pool is small. Upside needs: (a) boards and examiners start asking for independent parity proof (not required by any rule I found; interagency guidance asks for exit plans; low-moderate); (b) vendors give data-dictionary access despite lock-in incentives (moderate); (c) a $250-350k price holds against a 10% share of conversion cost (moderate above $1B in assets, low below); (d) about 30% of ~300 sizable events a year (low-moderate); (e) challenger cores license it per live bank rather than building (Thought Machine and 10x already partner or build; low); (f) an insurer-funded warranty actually gets agreed (unproven). Upside sums to about $60M, and the idea itself says it only clears $100M with Sunset, which is dead.

**2. Tenancy.** The best-structured idea and the one with proof of demand, which is also why it is not a gap. CU count fell from 4,411 to 4,250 (Q1 2025 to Q1 2026); 748 CUs are over $500M; 639 CUs have negative earnings, overwhelmingly under $100M. NCUA approved 157 mergers in 2025 (162 in 2024; peak 254 in 2019) and most reason codes are expanded services, with a median target near $11M in assets, so the CUs actually dying are far below the $50-500M segment that would pay $500k. Pricing check: CU*Answers lists core member processing at $0.505-0.56 per member per month (7-year term), and median technology spend is about 0.35% of assets (Cornerstone), so a $3-4 all-in price is only credible if it displaces about six salaries as well as the tech. Legal check: 12 CFR 712 permits service CUSOs; the board must designate the BSA officer (12 CFR 748.2(c)(3)) and I could not confirm an outside firm may be that officer. Upside needs: (a) 100-120 CUs at ~$550k by year 7 (low-moderate; CU*Answers took decades for 210); (b) AI workflows hold a 40-50% cost edge over in-house staff (moderate); (c) examiners accept a shared BSA seat (uncertain); (d) boards decide in under 18 months (low); (e) $30-60M for a multi-tenant stack from anchor CUs or venture (moderate). Could not verify the pitch's Fiserv figures (1,155 CUs, 25.9% share); the 2022 consolidation backlash at Fiserv is verified.

**3. Quorum.** The data source (bank invoices) is the interesting part; the payer is the weak part. Vendors pay for leads per qualified shortlist, not 6% of contract value, and B2B bank-tech lead generation is already sold by CBANC, Bank Director, Datos and others. Consultants take a percentage of savings (Wipfli) and keep the database. The 2014 BPI study (10.2-41.4% over fair market) used Paladin data and is a decade old. Bank Bribery Act risk is manageable if fees go to the platform only, but it adds counsel cost.

**4. Sunset.** The earned secret in the pitch is correct (customers leave when forced to move) and the vendor has already learned it the hard way. Fiserv Q2 2026: organic revenue -5%, Financial Solutions -8%, guide -1% to flat, new CEO since June, portfolio review of non-best-in-class products with debit networks in scope, Project Elevate savings of at least $500M. That is divestiture energy, not migration energy. A paid migration partner could still emerge for Finxact onboarding or acquired-bank deconversions, but that is a services contract inside Fiserv's cost of sales with an integrator's margin.

**5. Books of Record.** Technically the most interesting, commercially the most contested. Open questions that decide it: which cores expose journals or events, who owns the right to them under existing contracts, and whether a bank will trust a startup as ledger of record. Real-time OFAC/AML inside FedNow's 5-second window is a screening problem a ledger copy does not solve. The expansion step to $1-3M ledger-of-record pricing is a core-replacement business, competing with Nymbus, Finxact, Thought Machine and now FIS's OpenCoreOS.

**6. Day One.** Best treated as a module of a larger conversion offer. CFPB 1033 is enjoined (Forcht Bank v. CFPB) and under reconsideration, so the pitch's legal reading (no right to pull data; runs on bank authority plus consent) is right and means every connection needs consent flows that the digital-banking vendor already owns.

**7. Switchboard.** Cleanest payer and budget line in the pool, and the free debit P&L audit is a credible wedge, but the audit is also sold by consultants. Reg II check: 2022 final rule extends two-unaffiliated-networks to card-not-present from 1 Jul 2023; the 2023 lower-cap proposal is not final; the district court in Corner Post ruled against Reg II but stayed vacatur pending an Eighth Circuit appeal. A coordinated decision by FIs to disable a network is an antitrust problem, as the author notes. The $0.035 margin is an assumption; I could not source issuer-processing margins.

**8. Bureau.** A financing play, not a venture: the product is the balance sheet. KC Fed 2022 shares match the pitch (CSI 7%, COCC 3%, Finastra 3% of banks). COCC is bank-owned. The target list is small and the best assets are already in sponsor hands.

## Picks

None. Closest was Tenancy (median 12, upside 65): it fails the $100M-upside bar because the only comparable (CU*Answers) reached $78M after decades, and shrinking it to a back-office boutique would be the round-1 mistake. Next closest were Switchboard and Parity. Nothing here keeps a credible $100M upside once the incumbents and the conversion-budget share are counted.

## What would change this verdict

- A vendor (Fiserv, FIS or Jack Henry) announcing a dated sunset of a platform with at least several hundred clients, plus a willingness to outsource it (revives Sunset and the Parity engine).
- A Fed/NCUA/OCC statement that independent conversion or parity testing is an expected control for core conversions (turns Parity from optional to mandated).
- A signed STAR or Accel sale to megabanks, with community-FI customers looking for an alternative network and routing layer (revives Switchboard's cooperative leg).

## Pool note
Pool is contested: incumbents and funded startups occupy every layer, and the dollars sit in vendor bundles. Conversion, benchmarking and back-office services top out at $30-70M. Fiserv publicly renounced forced migrations. The only outsized economics are in cores and cooperatives (CU*Answers, Velera), which took decades.

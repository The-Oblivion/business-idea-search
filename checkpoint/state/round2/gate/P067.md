## Evidence by idea

### 1. Approved Buyer (weak as written)
- **Trigger status.** FTC Second Request 2026-06-11; compliance certified 2026-10-02; closing barred before 2026-12-11; Cintas expects close before end of 2026. No divestiture or remedy reported anywhere. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000072325426000047/ctas-20260831.htm
- **Precedent.** Cintas/G&K (2017) cleared after an 8-month FTC and Canadian review with no divestitures. https://www.jonesday.com/en/practices/experience/2017/03/cintas-secures-antitrust-clearance-for-22-billion-purchase-of-gk-services
- **Deal protection.** Cintas agreed a $350M reverse termination fee if the deal is blocked on antitrust grounds, and the 424B3 says the agencies could sue, require divestitures or limit Cintas after closing. So a block, a clean clearance and a remedy are all live. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000114036126020376/ny20069194x3_424b3.htm
- **Vestis.** Advent/Apollo/CD&R interest was 2024-25 and Elis walked; I found no 2026 sale or strategic review. FY26 adjusted EBITDA guide $310-315M on $2.68-2.73B revenue (about 11.5% margin). https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001967649/000162828026055485/vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm
- **Existing roll-ups (the pool file's "open" is wrong).**
  - Alsco: Advanced Linen (Mar 2026), Audc&l Holdings (9 Mar 2026), Churchill Linen (2024), a Toronto operator (2025). https://alsco.com/news/alsco-uniforms-acquires-advanced-linen-service
  - Surge PE: White Plains Linen, over $50M, closed 2025-08-01, its 15th platform, targets $4-35M EBITDA. https://www.jw.com/news/result-surgepe-whiteplainslinen-investment/
  - Falcon Affiliates: Century Linen platform (10 facilities, 2,000+ customer locations) plus Angelica New England plants. https://peprofessional.com/2022/07/falcon-buys-linen-platform-quickly-adds-new-facilities/
  - Quintex: Mat Master (closed 2026-07-21). https://www.mltaikins.com/?p=32791
- **Sellers and prices.** About two-thirds of independents have revenue of $15M or less and all but three are family-owned (TRSA). Broker (CT Acquisitions, unverified) ranges: 5-9x EBITDA for uniform rental, 4-6x for single-plant laundries. No 2025-26 closed revenue multiples disclosed anywhere I could find. https://www.trsa.org/magazine-article/trsa-independently-owned-and-operated/ ; https://www.ctacquisitions.com/how-to-sell-a-uniform-rental-business/
- **Math.** Ten independents at $10M is $100M of bought revenue. At 0.8-1.2x revenue (5-8x EBITDA at 10-15% margin) that is $80-120M of EV, $50-70M of equity at 50% leverage. This is about 3% of the roughly 300 sub-$15M independents and 0.4% of the pool. Not heroic.
- **Flaw in the why-now.** Cintas paying about 2.3x revenue (idea's arithmetic) raises seller expectations, which is a headwind to a buyer, not a tailwind.
- **Substitutes.** Sellers' real alternatives are Alsco, PE platforms and family succession; none is free, so there is no free-substitute problem.

### 2. Rental-grade RFID mill (kill)
- Tag vendors already sell the "at birth" step: Fujitsu WT-A533G heat-seal garment tag aimed at uniform rental, NXP UCODE 9 laundry tags rated 200-500 washes, integrators such as RFID4U and CPCON. https://www.fujitsu.com/jp/group/frontech/en/solutions/business-technology/intelligent-society/rfid/a533g ; https://rfid4u.com/scrub-linen-rfid/
- Incumbent suppliers: Superior Group (Fashion Seal Healthcare sells to healthcare laundries), Standard Textile (Cincinnati, 29 facilities in 16 countries, Industrial Laundry and Workwear divisions), Carrington Textiles (joined TRSA Sept 2025 to expand in the US). https://builtin.com/company/standard-textile ; https://www.carrington.co.uk/en/media/newsroom/carrington-textiles-joins-trsa-to-expand-in-us-market/
- The idea's own math: $100M of sales is 4.5% share, 15-20% gross margin, $40-60M plant. That is a low-return apparel factory with the buyers' captive-factory rivals (Cintas, UniFirst) out of the market. No sourced reason the buyers would switch from distributors.

### 3. Laundry Foundry (weak)
- No neutral toll processor found. Customer-owned-goods (COG) laundering is an established category (hospitals, hotels send their own linen to commercial laundries), but I found no documented competitor-to-competitor toll arrangement. https://www.trsa.org/magazine-article/customer-owned-goods-what-does-your-contract-say/ ; https://umbrex.com/resources/industry-primers/business-services-industry-primers/uniform-rental-linen-laundry-services-industry-primer/
- Forcing function is missing: no federal PFAS effluent guideline covers laundries (CRS, 2026-09-30), so the "PFAS capex wall" is a risk, not a mandate. https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-30_IF12148_7411d6bc05ccbbc70ee4e9ed6f8ffb1569d3d172.html
- Big players are consolidating plants and shrinking volume (Vestis pounds -4.5%), so surplus capacity, not scarcity, sits on the other side.
- Robotic folding is real but small: Sewts VELUM at Greif (Germany) since Nov 2022, Series A EUR7M; no US rental-plant deployment found. https://www.therobotreport.com/sewts-brings-in-7-6m-to-automate-the-textile-industry/
- Capital: about $200M for 4 hubs at $100M revenue is about 2x capital per revenue dollar, against about 20% EBITDA at best. Neutrality is also a worse offer to an owner than selling to a consolidator (idea 1).
- Folded into the pick: the hub-and-spoke plant consolidation becomes the post-acquisition operating model, not a separate business.

### 4. Route Desk (kill)
- Per-location uniform spend is about $8k a year (P3 audited a 17-location dealership group spending about $140k). An 8% take is about $660 per location, so $100M needs about 150,000 managed locations. Widening to unsized adjacent services does not fix this. https://www.costanalysts.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/White-Paper-Uniform-and-Linen-.pdf
- Substitutes already live: Cost Analysts/P3 (claims 30-40% savings), Fine Tune, Schooley Mitchell (20% cut by linking two accounts), ASN (about 20 independents, national accounts), CSCNetwork (180+ independent laundries, multi-location account support), Rentokil multi-site centralized invoicing. https://www.costanalysts.com/categories/uniform-linen/ ; https://networkcsc.com/about/ ; https://www.trsa.org/news/sitex-joins-apparel-services-network ; https://www.foodlogistics.com/home/article/22340535/fine-tune-top-supply-chain-projects-fine-tune-saves-client-100000-after-auditing-deep-dive
- The idea concedes its own $100M is "heroic".

### 5. Garment Pool (weak)
- No third-party lessor to launderers found. Closest are Prudential's "lease" (customer launders, sold to end customers) and MEWA's barcode-tracked shared textile pool, which sits inside the integrated operator. https://www.prudentialuniforms.com/rent-lease-or-purchase/uniform-lease-service ; https://www.drkservice.de/fileadmin/DRKService.de/_StrategischerEinkauf/Dokumente/MEWA_Broschuere_Berufskleidung_Ergocare.pdf
- Math fails the buyer: lease fee about 18% of billing vs about 13% to own plus financing at bank rates, so independents pay about 5 points more to move stranded-garment risk that termination fees and loss charges already cover. Re-issue across plants means shuttling garments between competitors' plants, which CHEP-style pooling does not require.
- The insider claim that the 5-year term is a financing device is untested (idea says no contract text was available).

### 6. White-label rental for dealers (kill)
- No white-label rental platform found, but dealer channels exist: Cintas dealer program in Canada, Cintas-Touchstone partnership with a promo distributor, PPAI "Uniform Stores" (voucher model). More than half of about 450 independents already sell direct. https://printandpromomarketing.com/?p=5097 ; https://www.ppai.org/solution/uniform-stores/ ; https://www.madetomeasuremag.com/it-takes-an-open-mind-to-grow-a-business-merging-trends-in-rental-direct-sales-and-promotional-products
- Needs ideas 3 and 5 to exist first, 5,000 dealers at 20 accounts each, and the smallest accounts ($4,000 a year) carry the highest route cost per stop.

### 7. Route Brokerage (kill)
- Only the platform's 30-35% share is true revenue; $100M of gross commissions is about $30M net. Needs 15% of all non-Big-2 new bookings (the idea says heroic).
- Non-compete premise is weaker than it looks: Cintas lost a non-compete case (Cintas v. Perry, 7th Cir. 2008, Illinois law, covenant overbroad, Cintas paid fees), but outcomes vary by state and non-solicit clauses are separate. https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/06-1958/31/0.pdf
- Independents can hire the same released reps directly; no moat in a recruiting engine alone.

### 8. Clean Effluent PFAS (kill)
- No federal PFAS pretreatment standard for laundries: EPA has finalized no PFAS ELG and the 2026 agenda targets OCPSF and metal finishing. Limits are case-by-case via local POTW permits. https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-30_IF12148_7411d6bc05ccbbc70ee4e9ed6f8ffb1569d3d172.html
- TRSA itself says consumer sources are far larger; Michigan EGLE puts laundries at about 5% of PFAS waste. https://www.trsa.org/news/pfas-regulatory-update-risks-and-opportunities/
- Cost premise is soft: $1.9-5M plus $100-500k a year comes from one consultant webinar quote that appears to describe carbon-type systems and is not stated per plant; TRSA otherwise says "high six figures". No installed laundry ion-exchange case found.
- Incumbent water-treatment vendors (Evoqua/Xylem) already market PFAS removal for industrial laundries. https://www.xylem.com/fi-fi/resources/videos/solutions-for-industrial-laundry/
- NJ and NY apparel bans cut the PFAS entering plants, shrinking the treatable stream. Heroic share (38% of plants) is the idea's own number.

## Picks

### Pick 1: Approved Buyer, sharpened (from "Approved Buyer: a pre-cleared consolidator ...")

**Sharpened pitch.** An operator-led, sponsor-backed consolidator of independent route-based uniform and linen laundries that wins on seller preference and post-deal retention, with FTC-remedy readiness kept as a free option instead of the thesis. Targets are about 300 family-owned independents under $15M of revenue, where succession gaps, the PFAS and automation capex question and the Cintas/UniFirst repricing push owners toward a sale. Edge: three ex-regional presidents or plant GMs signed before the raise, an equity pool for route service reps, and a written 24-month price lock plus month-to-month option for acquired customers, aimed at first-year leakage. After deal 3, close duplicate small plants into one hub-and-spoke wet-processing plant per metro (the Foundry idea becomes internal consolidation, which also concentrates any PFAS pretreatment in one place). Math: ten independents at about $10M is $100M of bought revenue at 0.8-1.2x revenue, or $80-120M of EV and $50-70M of equity at 50% leverage, with $10M for integration; exit at 1.5-2.0x revenue to Elis-type strategics or PE, since Cintas cannot buy. First close capped at $60-80M of equity so the platform exists before any remedy appears.

- **Median $45M, upside $200M.** Median: four to five deals in 5-7 years. Upside: $100-150M from independents alone plus an option on a remedy package or Vestis route book.
- **Payer and signer.** Sponsor investment committee and unitranche lender fund; NewCo CEO signs each purchase; the signer on the sell side is the independent's family owner.
- **Open risk.** Seller price expectations after Cintas paid about 2.3x revenue, with Alsco, Surge, Falcon, Platte River and Quintex bidding for the same targets; plus whether a first-time operator team can raise $60-80M.
- **Cheapest next test.** Call 20 independents under $15M with owners aged 60+ (TRSA directory), ask what multiple and what terms would make them sell to an operator-led buyer with route-rep equity, and get three IOIs or NDAs in 60 days; ask two M&A advisers for closed 2025-26 multiples.

Everything else: no pick. Foundry and Garment Pool are the closest runners-up, and both are better as features of the consolidator than as standalone businesses.

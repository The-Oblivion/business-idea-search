# Gate report: P079 (US metal castings, defense-critical), 2026-10-14

Pool: US OEM and prime purchases of castings, about $53.7B (AFS, July 2026). 8 ideas gated. Outcome: **0 strong, 4 kill, 4 weak. No picks.**

Scale used: kill = realistic 5-7 yr ceiling well under $25M, already done, or false premise. weak = a plausible $25-100M outcome but with an unresolved dealbreaker. strong = credible $100M+ that survives the competitor, payer, legal and why-now checks.

## Verdict table

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tooling Bank (pattern custody + twin) | **kill** | 12 | Event-limited TAM (about 45 net foundry closures a year), foundry stores tooling free, OEM-tooling platforms already exist, printed sand removes the pattern. |

## Evidence by idea

### 1. Tooling Bank: kill
- Competitors and substitutes:
  - COAST Systems (cloud tooling management for OEM supplier networks): https://www.moldmakingtechnology.com/products/get-to-know-coast-systems
  - Matix (tool location and documentation for molds, dies, die-casting tools): https://www.matix.cloud/use-case/streamlining-tooling-asset-management
  - DLA Aviation forging and casting engineering, with a tooling database that located a supplier for a backordered part and saved 100-150 days (free, DLA NSNs only): https://www.dla.mil/Aviation/Offers/Engineering/Forging-and-Casting
  - Foundry-provided storage with sprinklers, and OEM spreadsheets. The Casting Source columnist's advice is simply "inventory your tooling and hold proof of ownership": https://castingsource.com/node/1462
- Revenue math: the pool file's foundry count (about 1,956, 2018) is stale. AFS data in 2022 testimony: 1,725 facilities, down from 2,620 in 2003 (about 47 net closures a year): https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2022-05/James_Brown_Testimony.pdf. Custody at $250 a set on 300k sets (8-9% of all OEM-owned sets) is heroic. Tooling in active use stays at the foundry that needs it, so only dormant or at-risk tooling is vaultable. A closure-triggered rescue market of about 45 events a year at, say, 1,000-3,000 tools each, with 10% scanned, is roughly $10-25M a year.
- Sand printing cuts against the thesis: DLA/CTC/ExOne exist precisely to cast obsolete parts without physical patterns ($15M potential): https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220602006029/en/Desktop-Metal-and-Concurrent-Technologies-Corporation-to-Advance-3D-Printed-Sand-Casting-Mold-Technology-through-DLA-Contract-Worth-a-Potential-15-Million
- Legal: Illinois Tool and Die Lien Act gives a processor a possessory lien on customer patterns for unpaid balances, subordinate only to perfected Article 9 interests: https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/077001050K1.htm. A custodian pulling patterns from an insolvent, owed-money foundry walks into that fight.

### 2. Second-Source Factory: weak
- Who does it: prime in-house supplier quality teams (Lockheed posting to lead PAC-3 second-source qualification: https://lockheed.jobs.hr.cloud.sap/job/Supplier-Quality-Eng-Sr-E3/10828-en_US/; Electric Boat guides non-traditional suppliers through welding, NDT, SUBSAFE: https://careers-gdeb.icims.com/jobs/19646/program-representative-non-traditional-supplier/job); DLA MetaL FACT and AFCAT (free); American Metalcasting Consortium and its DLA-funded EMCS program: https://amc.ati.org/resources/; DLA slide deck: https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2024/dla/Tue_Breakout_Cast_Forge.pdf.
- Premise: DLA's "3% of NSNs, 20% of the 100 oldest backorders" is confirmed (same deck). But the owner of the part approves a new source, usually the prime, and qualification takes years: https://www.bwcoconsulting.com/blog/missile-multiyear-second-source. A GMU paper says defense moved away from second sourcing because qualification and requalification costs deter it: https://webdocs.gmu.edu/wp-content/uploads/olivia-letts-jerry-mcginn-richard-beutel-back-to-the-future-second-sourcing-in-defense-acquisitions.pdf
- Money: the $605M FY24 supplemental supplier-development line is real, but a Navy spokesman said only $250-300M goes through BlueForge (https://breakingdefense.com/2024/06/navy-investment-in-blueforge-alliance-up-to-500-million-and-growing/), and BlueForge's sole-source contract (up to $980.7M) covers workforce, supply-chain diversification and more (https://breakingdefense.com/2024/09/navy-awards-blueforge-alliance-new-950m-contract-to-boost-sub-industrial-base/). None of it is shown to buy third-party qualification packages.
- Math: the $75k per approved part number is an assumption with no benchmark. 800 approvals a year is 150 outsourcing buyers each running 5-6 qualifications. No evidence primes outsource. Its own kill test (no prime accepts a third-party package) is the whole business.
- Could not retrieve the DoW ManTech FY2026-2031 plan itself; the "qualification delays" claim rests on a secondary report.

### 3. Tier-Three Credit: weak
- Who does it: SAP Taulia sells sub-tier supply chain finance for aerospace and defense (https://taulia.com/industries/aerospace-and-defense/); C2FO; PrimeRevenue; Citi's defense working-capital programs (https://www.citigroup.com/global/insights/defense-industry-working-capital-insights); PO and AR lenders such as Viva Capital ($6M defense manufacturer deal, June 2026: https://www.sfnet.com/home/chapters/canada/news-detail/2026/06/03/viva-capital-provides--6-million-financing-solution-to-support-defense-manufacturer-transition); Twinco (EUR 165M raised for PO finance: https://pulse2.com/twinco-capital-raises-e165-million-to-scale-purchase-order-finance-platform/). The gap is documented (https://warontherocks.com/2026/01/the-primes-arent-the-real-bottleneck) but not empty.
- Public money: OSC direct loans are up to $150M, capex-style (https://defensescoop.com/2026/04/06/pentagon-office-strategic-capital-loan-program-budget-increase/). The $20.2B FY27 request is mostly mandatory funding the House is not matching ($2.16B loan authority in the House defense bill). The $500M-$1B National Security Fund Finance facilities are for critical minerals only (https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2236/2026-08-31-office-strategic-capital-opens-application-window), so no cheap-capital tailwind for this lender.
- Math check: $100M revenue at 12% yield needs about $830M outstanding and a $1B+ facility. At net spread (about half of revenue), profit needs twice that. A startup lender reaching $250M of book in 6-7 years is generous, so $30M revenue.
- Legal: UCC 9-406 generally overrides anti-assignment clauses for accounts; state lender licensing and true-sale treatment of PO advances still need counsel. The dealbreaker is behavioral: primes rarely sign payment direction for sub-tier suppliers.

### 4. Foundry BOT: kill
- Who does it: turnkey foundry engineering and project firms (GEMCO, EC&S, J-Tec: https://castingssa.com/experienced-foundry-colleagues-form-partnership-to-offer-turnkey-and-project-management-solutions); Inductotherm turnkey melt systems (https://foundry-planet.com/profiles/suppliers/show/inductotherm-corp); in-house builders: SpaceX Bastrop (https://www.foundry-planet.com/d/spacex-plans-its-own-turbine-blade-foundry-in-texas-ai-boom-meets-casting-bottleneck), Pratt Asheville ($650M: https://www.rtx.com/prattwhitney/newsroom/news/2020/10/22/pw-announces-plans-for-at-least-650m-investment-through-2027-in-turbine-airfoil-production-facility), Howmet (five plants).
- New foundry cost is only publicly anchored at "more than $100M": https://www.foundry-planet.com/d/usa-new-foundry-estimated-to-cost-more-than-100-million/. The idea's own math says gross design-build revenue of $100M a year is 17% of a $3B capex pool (an assumed 25 projects) and net fees are 10-25% of that. Net ceiling about $20M. The scaling assets it names (permitted-site bank, equipment slots, co-ownership) are a different, capital-heavy business.

### 5. The Non-GE Foundry: weak
- Verified: GE Aerospace agreed on 2026-09-08 to buy CPP for $11.75B (about 18x 2027 EBITDA with synergies, 26x without; close 2H 2027; CPP about 6,600 staff, 20+ facilities): https://www.aviationpros.com/aircraft-maintenance-technology/engines-parts/news/55403433/ge-aerospace-acquires-consolidated-precision-products-for-1175b. Howmet fell 8-11%: https://www.tikr.com/blog/howmet-aerospace-stock-dropped-nearly-11-after-ge-bought-a-rival-casting-supplier-heres-whether-the-selloff-is-an-overreaction
- Premise problem: CPP supplies only about a quarter of GE's needs and GE said it keeps Howmet and ATI; Howmet is the largest independent caster and holds over 50% of industrial gas turbine blades; so "non-captive buyers lost their neutral supplier" is overstated. Neutral capacity already exists at Howmet, PCC, Doncasters, Chromalloy.
- Incumbents are spending: Howmet capex over $500M in 2026 and rising, five plants being built or expanded, new commitments producing capacity around Aug 2028 (https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/howmet-aerospace-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-08/). Doncasters IPO raised about $919M with a $930M backlog (https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/120063/aerospace-and-defense-engine-manufacturer-doncasters-group-prices-upsized) and has a $1B+ Siemens Energy long-term agreement (https://www.doncasters.com/news/contract-worth-over-1-billion-usd-signed-with-siemens-energy). SpaceX is building its own (Turbomachinery, 2026-09-11: https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/ge-aerospace-cpp-acquisition-spacex-turbine-blade-foundry). Fabri has $13.5M and is aero-focused (https://www.tectonicdefense.com/fabri-announces-its-raised-13-5m-for-new-age-casting/).
- Math: one CPP-average plant is about $100M, so share is not the problem; capital ($150-300M), single-crystal talent (SpaceX and GE are bidding for the same people) and 3-5 year OEM qualification are. A plant started in 2027 ramps after Howmet's 2028 capacity, and Howmet's CEO says he will avoid investing around a single-year peak. No evidence any OEM will prepay a new entrant (searched, nothing found).
- Verdict: the one pool idea with a genuine $100M+ prize, but it is an infrastructure or PE roll-up bet, not an insight a founder can test cheaply.

### 6. US-Pour: kill
- The 10% rate exists but the rule keeps moving. 2026-04-02 proclamation (effective 04-06) set tiers and a 10% rate for US-melted-and-poured derivatives (https://phillipslytle.com/administration-restructures-section-232-tariffs-on-metal-and-derivative-products/; https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/4/updates-for-importers-on-ieepa-refunds-and-section-232-metals). The 2026-06-01 proclamation (effective 06-08) cut the "entirely US" threshold from 95% to 85%, added Annex I-C, and sources disagree on which annex holds the 10% rate (https://www.troutman.com/insights/president-trump-further-adjusts-section-232-tariffs-on-aluminum-steel-and-copper/). Annex III reduced rates expire 2027-12-31.
- Already built: CBP has required country-of-melt-and-pour reporting at entry since 2024-11-21 (https://www.ajot.com/news/cbp-adds-new-information-requirements-when-filing-section-232-import-entries), and Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE has a melt-and-pour feature feeding the entry (https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/import-management/steel-melt-and-pour.html).
- Math: the $150B derivative list (Global Trade Alert, 2024 imports: https://globaltradealert.org/reports/US-tariffs-aluminium-steel-derivative-products) is two-thirds aluminum. The share made from 85%+ US-melted steel is unmeasured and likely small (mostly USMCA assemblers, which already have a US-content deduction). 6.7% of the list through the rail is heroic. Policy can vanish; false-claim liability sits with the importer.

### 7. Cleaning Room as a Service: kill
- Free substitute: Eagle Alloy (steel job shop, Muskegon) built its own robotic grinding cells with refurbished ABB robots at $15-20k each, first cell about $25k of hardware, cutting man-hours per net ton from 55-60 to 40-48 (about 20-25%): https://www.moderncasting.com/articles/2022/09/07/robotic-grinding-steel-job-shop. Benton Foundry bought Vulcan Foxall cells: https://www.foundry-planet.com/d/benton-foundry-continues-to-streamline-cleaning-operations/. Pivot Robotics (YC W24, well under $5M raised across conflicting sources) runs vision-guided grinding at a cast iron foundry on a hardware-plus-subscription model: https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/pivot-robotics
- Price exceeds value: $250k per cell-year against a 20-25% labor-hour saving, and cells that cost tens of thousands to build, is not a deal a foundry takes.
- Math: the 20-35% cleaning-cost share is from a 2005 Foundry Magazine article (about 20% for a 10-kg ductile casting): https://www.foundrymag.com/issues-and-ideas/article/21926824/same-old-grind. Applying 20% to all $53.7B includes aluminum die and high-volume auto castings that barely need grinding. Realistic addressable high-mix ferrous work is a fraction of the idea's $2.9B, and unionized subcontracting clauses cut it more.

### 8. Supplier of record + CT screen: weak
- Who does it: small casting sourcing and turnkey houses (CPM Industries: https://www.thomasnet.com/company/cpm-industries-431669/profile; Associated MetalCast; Castech: https://www.mining-technology.com/sponsored/quality-castings-forgings-and-fabrications-for-the-mining-industry-meet-castech-solutions/; Bral Corp at about $4M revenue per a data aggregator: https://www.unisco.com/importers/bral-corp); Xometry (Q1 2026 revenue $205M, marketplace $191M at 34.7% gross margin, mostly prototype and low volume: https://investors.xometry.com/node/12506/html); Hadrian (CNC plus in-house casting, $260M Series C reported: https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/hadrian-secures-260m-for-expansion).
- Premise problem 1: Lumafield Saturn's 225 kV source penetrates up to 25 mm of steel or iron (100 mm of light metal): https://www.lumafield.com/article/introducing-saturn-large-format-ct-for-the-parts-you-couldnt-scan-before. Heavy valve, pump and rail castings are thicker; they need megavolt systems from service bureaus. Premise problem 2: a steel-founders white paper says no inspection available before machining reliably finds the porosity that machining reveals, and customers can already shift the risk by ordering castings in the machined condition: https://cdn.sfsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/WhitePaper-Machining-SteelCastings.pdf
- Math: $100M revenue is only 0.19% of the pool, but that is pass-through. At 18-25% gross margin it is $18-25M of gross profit before scrap risk, product-liability insurance and CT capex. A 5-12% premium is the same price band as the loss it covers, so price-versus-value (lesson 5) is untested. Existing sourcing houses are tiny, a sign of a thin, relationship-driven market.

## Picks

**None.** No idea cleared "strong".

Why not a sharpened version of any weak idea:
- 2, 3 and 8 only reach $100M by assuming a behavior (primes outsource qualification, primes sign payment direction, OEMs pay a risk premium for a guarantee that is available by contract) that nothing found supports. The cheapest tests are cheap, but a surviving version of each is a $20-40M services or lending business, which is the "shrink to survive" outcome round 1 died of.
- 5 is the only one where the prize is clearly above $100M, but it needs $150-300M and elite single-crystal talent against Howmet, GE, SpaceX and Doncasters, with first parts arriving after the incumbents' 2028 capacity. That is a capital-allocation bet, not a gate-passing idea.

What would change the call:
- A signed take-or-pay or prepay from a turbine OEM for a new entrant (none found), which would move idea 5 toward strong.
- Evidence that a prime or DLA has paid a third party for a complete casting second-source approval package, which would reopen idea 2.

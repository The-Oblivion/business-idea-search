# Gate report: pool P004, German Mittelstand ownership succession

Gate date: 2026-10-18. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with 3-5 searches each, plus a full read of the KfW Nachfolge-Monitoring 2025 PDF (Fokus 526, Jan 2026). Result: 3 kill, 4 weak, 1 strong. 1 pick (Meisterwerk, sharpened and renamed).

## Headline findings

- The "EUR 54B pool" is sellers' hope, not money that moves. KfW's 109,000 succession wishes a year and 114,000 planned closures come from a 3.87M-firm universe that explicitly includes Kleinstunternehmen and Soloselbstaendige. The KfW average planned price is EUR 499k, but the median is EUR 375k, and 5% of planners value the firm at EUR 50k or less. Source (read as PDF): https://www.kfw.de/PDF/Download-Center/Konzernthemen/Research/PDF-Dokumente-Fokus-Volkswirtschaft/Fokus-2026/Fokus-Nr.-526-Januar-2026-Nachfolge-Monitoring.pdf
- The "37,200 actual handovers a year" (IfM, 186,000 over 2026-2030) is smaller once split by route. IfM's meta-analysis: just over half go to family, about 17% to employees, 29% to outsiders. So outside sales are about 10,800 a year and employee takeovers about 6,300. The IfM count covers only firms it judges worth taking over. Sources: https://www.ihk.de/dresden/servicenavigation/presse-publikationen/meldungen/unternehmensnachfolgen-in-deutschland-2026-bis-2030-6936092 and https://www.ihk.de/stuttgart/fuer-unternehmen/nachfolge/unternehmensnachfolgen-in-deutschland-678884 . Every fee or service idea that divides by 37,200 understates its required share by 2-3x.
- Seller price expectations are 1.5-2x what the ideas assume buyers pay. KfW: the median planner wants 60% of one year's revenue. DHZ on KfW Handwerk data: average expected price EUR 453k (median EUR 375k), about 87% of annual revenue, against a FINANCE buyer range of 0.25-0.50x. Source: https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/?p=374071
- Consolidation of Handwerk and engineering by PE is already happening at scale. Konzmann: 21 firms, EUR 180M revenue 2024, sold to KLAR Partners in autumn 2025. HWP Handwerkspartner: 30-40 units, about 1,250 staff, sold to GRIP in 2023. Builtech: 51 firms, 2,000+ staff, merging with VDK into about EUR 2B. XPartners (Axcel): about 90 engineering firms, EUR 450M, entered Germany in Aug 2026. So the revenue path is proven, and so is the competition.

## Per-idea detail

### 1. Werkhof (weak)

- Competitors found: StoneVest and Madison (EUR 10-50M tickets, two Bavarian assets so far); Investec Advisory (sale-and-leaseback from about EUR 10M); DAL and Sparkassen Immobilien-Leasing (DAL volume above EUR 1.6B a year, no minimum found); WDP (logistics); Nord Leasing (machinery). Nobody specialises below EUR 5M, but that looks like a cost problem more than an oversight.
- Substitute: the owner keeps the building and rents it to the successor. After a share sale the Besitzunternehmen is treated as discontinued and hidden reserves are taxed, unless the owner elects Betriebsverpachtung (the lessor's election). So the tax-cheapest path is to keep the building, and selling to Werkhof realises the reserves. The idea's "owner picks the cheapest tax path" is not a Werkhof product.
- Deal flow, rebuilt from the idea's own inputs: 37,200 handovers x 46% non-family x 25% owner-held building (idea's assumption) = about 4,300 a year. With the E&Y filter the idea cites (11.6% could imagine selling real estate; not verified by me) that falls to about 500 a year. The idea needs 225 a year: 5% of the first figure, 45% of the second.
- Frictions not in the idea (from memory, to verify): real-estate transfer tax of 3.5-6.5% by state, notary and diligence on a EUR 1.2M asset earning about EUR 84k of rent, and thin lender appetite for single-tenant small assets.
- Scale reality: EUR 1.24B of buildings is balance-sheet "revenue". A sponsor's own income on that AUM would be nearer EUR 12-20M.
- Upside case: widen from succession to all Mittelstand sale-and-leaseback (about EUR 2.1T of corporate property, 35% light industrial, about EUR 100B institutional, per the Refire/StoneVest report), land an institutional sponsor for EUR 400M equity, and get a pooled Pfandbrief facility. Plausibility: market size high, sponsor and lender appetite low to moderate, yield of 7% unproven (no source found).

### 2. Verkaufsreif (weak)

- Price check: 9.5% of the KfW median of EUR 375k is about EUR 36k, of a realised (lower) price less. 186,000 is IfM's five-year 2026-2030 total (37,200 a year); KfW's 186,000 is the short-term count to end-2026, so the idea blurs two figures.
- Economics: it funds 12-24 months of work and needs a 45-55% close rate. Two attempts per closing at even EUR 15k a try nearly erase a EUR 36-45k fee. Disintermediation after free prep work is the unsolved risk.
- Upside case: 1,000 closings a year (about 9% of outside sales) at EUR 40k = about $46M, plus small data revenue. Reaching $100M needs 2,000+ closings, which is not plausible.
- Legal: Sec. 9 StBerG bars fee-splitting for referrals and Sec. 9a bars success fees except a narrow single-case rule (both confirmed). So the Steuerberater channel can refer for free only.

### 3. Meisterwerk (strong, see picks)

- Revenue math corrected: 60 firms x EUR 1.5M = EUR 90M (about $103M). Purchase consideration is more likely EUR 45-80M at 0.5-0.87x revenue (half deferred) than EUR 22-45M, because sellers expect a median 60% of revenue and Handwerk about 87%.
- Share is not the limit: KfW Handwerk data shows about 49,000 hand-overs and 54,000 closures expected over two years, and 33% of firms plan a handover within five years (HWK Dortmund: https://www.hwk-do.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Sonderumfrage-Betriebsnachfolge-im-Handwerk-Fruehjahr-2026.pdf ). Twelve deals a year is a fraction of a percent of reachable A-trade sellers.
- Profit reality: 6-9% operating margin means $100M of revenue is only $6-9M of EBIT.
- Evidence of flips: Adiuva sold HWP (2023) and Konzmann (2025) within about five years each, so a hold-forever promise is a real selling point.

### 4. Netzplan (weak)

- BDEW pathway of about EUR 10B now to about EUR 15B in five years is consistent with the idea. The unverified part is how much planning is bought outside. If 30-40% is outsourced and planning plus supervision is 8-10% of capex, the external pool is EUR 0.4-0.6B and EUR 87M is 15-22% of it.
- Purchase prices of 0.56-1.03x revenue are being bid up by XPartners and formicon. The Aug 2026 survey that Sondervermoegen barely reaches engineering offices is in the idea's own kill risks.
- Possible legal limits, from memory and unverified: Ingenieurgesellschaft ownership rules in some Laender and the status of oeffentlich bestellte Vermessungsingenieure. Stadtwerke are sector contracting entities, so unit-price framework deals need tendering.

### 5. Teamkauf (kill)

- Even the upside is small: 5% of employee takeovers (about 315 a year) for six years = about 1,500 firms x EUR 21.6k = EUR 32M of fees plus about EUR 3M of origination, about $41M gross before churn, and $38M after.
- The 1.2% back-office fee competes with a part-time bookkeeper, Steuerberater and DATEV. Personal liability on 077 is the real reason employees decline, and the packager cannot remove it.
- The GmbH mit gebundenem Vermoegen has key points only, no draft bill.

### 6. Aufloesungsboerse (kill)

- Fee payers are scarce: an owner whose firm is worth under EUR 100k must pay EUR 2.5k to wind it down. Steuerberater already do the Schlussbilanz. A GmbH liquidation service sells at a fixed EUR 10,000 (https://www.business-on.de/geschaeftsaufloesung-berlin-wubb.html ), so the wind-down layer is priced and taken.
- The 45%-with-a-bundle assumption has no source. I found no headcount data for closers.
- Upside: about 3,000 clients a year x EUR 8.6k = EUR 26M, about $30M, before the labour-law risk.

### 7. Zulieferwerk (weak)

- Arithmetic works: 15 shops x EUR 6M = EUR 90M. The buy price of EUR 35-50M is 0.39-0.55x revenue, low for shops with a defence story that rivals will also chase.
- The "qualification takes 18-36 months and a large budget" claim is unverified. Rheinmetall's own supplier guides list ISO 9001 plus AQAP 2105/2110/2131 (https://www.rheinmetall.com/Rheinmetall%20Group/Unternehmen/Tochtergesellschaften/rheinmetall-landsysteme/RLS-Lieferantenleitfaden.pdf ); PwC says EN 9100 can take "many months, if not years". If it is months, owners can do it themselves and the buyer adds little.
- Demand backdrop is real: defence budget about EUR 108B in 2026 rising to EUR 152B by 2029; Rheinmetall backlog EUR 63.8B; BDSV membership 243 to 440 since Nov 2024. Whether it reaches Mittelstand machinists is the open question.
- Upside needs: a prime or tier-1 capacity commitment before closing, EUR 50-65M of equity, shops bought at 0.55x or less, clearances for the new owner.

### 8. Anschluss (kill)

- Premise partly right: the connection relationship passes on property sale (Sec. 2(4) NAV, confirmed), and the new Reifegradverfahren (transmission level, since 1 Apr 2026) scores land security and permit status, not MW. The draft Netzpaket would extend such prioritising to distribution grids.
- Value problem: the right to a 400 kVA-2 MVA connection is worth a few hundred thousand euro at most per site; the real prizes are 10-100+ MW connections that developers and utilities already chase.
- The 3-5% of closers with at least 400 kVA is an assumption; most closers are micro firms. $100M needs about EUR 1.08B of property, which is a fund, not a connection business.

## Picks

### Pick 1: Meisterwerk (sharpened, to be renamed)

One-line: permanent-capital buyer of owner-led Handwerk and technical-service firms below PE's size floor, with an owned origination funnel and a Meister-equity ladder.

Why it survives: the money is operating revenue from customers with statutory inspection duties; the legal premise holds (a GmbH can be registered through a qualified Betriebsleiter); Konzmann shows EUR 180M revenue across 21 firms, and HWP (about 1,250 staff) and Builtech (2,000+ staff) are of similar or larger scale; and seller supply (tens of thousands of Handwerk exits a year) far exceeds buyers.

Sharpened version, answering what the gate found without shrinking:

1. Buy the contract book, not revenue. Target firms with at least 50% recurring statutory inspection or service revenue (electrical testing, fire protection, heating and water service, lift maintenance) and price against that book.
2. Hub-and-spoke. Six to eight hubs of 15-60 staff (EUR 2-4M revenue) in Bavaria and NRW, then tuck in micro firms and the customer books of closing firms around each hub. This avoids the unit-economics trap of 60 standalone micro deals.
3. Own origination. Chamber and Steuerberater referral is shared with HWP, Builtech and Konzmann. Add a pre-sale programme for owners two to three years out with a right of first offer, plus a catch net for the roughly 27,000 Handwerk closures a year (get labour counsel on Sec. 613a for tuck-ins).
4. Price the gap honestly: 0.4-0.6x revenue with half deferred against contract retention, the old Meister kept as Betriebsleiter for two to three years, and a Geselle-to-Meister equity ladder.
5. Capital: EUR 15M permanent seed, EUR 45-80M total consideration deployed over five years using seller notes and Buergschaftsbank cover where eligible.
6. Rename: "Meisterwerk" belongs to a funded Berlin software startup.

Cheapest next test (6-8 weeks): through HWK Nachfolgemoderatoren get anonymised customer ledgers from 6-8 target firms and measure recurring share and three-year retention; sign three non-binding term sheets at 0.6x or less with half deferred; hold five conversations with Versorgungswerk or family-office CIOs. Stop if recurring revenue is under 40% or sellers insist on over 0.8x in cash.

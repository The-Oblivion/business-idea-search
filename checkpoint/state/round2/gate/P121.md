# Gate report: P121 (US farmers' spend on equipment repair, maintenance and parts)

Gate run: 2026-10-14. Outcome: **0 strong, 5 weak, 3 kill. No picks.**

## Bottom line

The pool is real and the shock is real, but the eight ideas are thin slices of it, and in every case the OEMs, dealers or a PE-backed roll-up are already doing the slice or are about to. None reaches $100M of annual revenue at credible share. The three that come closest (Any-Shop Plan, Core Bank, Standard Shop) each fail on their own revenue math.

## Pool and premise check

- **The Deere settlement is real, but not final.** The FTC and five states (AZ, IL, MI, MN, WI) settled on 2026-07-08. Deere must give farmers and independents the same repair resources it gives dealers: fault-code read/clear/reset, reprogramming, parts pairing, emissions-shutdown restarts and manuals. It runs 10 years, with deadlines of Aug 1 and Dec 31, 2026. It needs Judge Johnston's approval, and I found no report of approval as of mid-October.
  - https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/07/08/ag-nessel-secures-settlement-with-deere-company-advancing-farmers-right-to-repair
  - https://www.ksl.com/article/news/business/john-deere-owners-will-get-the-right-to-repair-their-own-equipment-under-a-new-ftc-settlement/51595661
- **Part of the why-now was already spent.** Deere's Operations Center PRO Service has allowed owner-permissioned reprogramming since 2025. The owner price is $195 per machine per year and the independent-shop business license is reported at $5,995 per year. The AFBF memoranda with Deere, CNH, AGCO, Kubota and CLAAS already cover about three-quarters of the market. EPA's Feb 2026 guidance says the Clean Air Act does not bar owners and shops from repairing DEF/emissions systems.
  - https://www.croplife.com/?p=106930
  - https://texasfarmbureau.org/?p=47059
  - https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/19030-afbf-inks-more-right-to-repair-agreements
  - https://www.cfbf.com/ag-advocacy/advocacy-updates/newsletter-content/2-20-2026/epa-issues-updated-guidance-on-right-to-repair
- **Pool size is softer than the brief says.** NASS's $24.0B (2024) is "farm supplies and repairs", not equipment repair alone. The $18B repair figure came from a Senate deck and I could not verify it. Equipment repair and parts is probably $12-18B, and dealers capture most of it. $100M is under 1% of the pool, so the pool is not the constraint. The business model is.
  - https://data.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Highlights/2025/2025_FarmExpenditures_Highlights.pdf
- **The OEM is closing the gap from its side.** Deere says it cut prices on almost 190,000 parts and will sell a lower-priced alternative tier "through other places customers buy today". "Essentials by John Deere" already exists. Deere's Parts OnSite puts dealer-managed parts cabinets on farms. NAEDA says dealers already sell parts to about 9 independent shops each, and 56% of dealer parts leave over the counter.
  - https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/equipment/article/2026/03/10/deeres-worldwide-ag-president-deanna
  - https://www.deere.com/en/parts-and-service/parts/parts-onsite/
  - https://rilegislature.gov/Special/comdoc/House%20Innovation%20Internet%20and%20technology%202024/01-25-2024--H7095--Eric%20Wareham--NAEDA.pdf
- **The market is in a trough.** 2025 US combine sales were 3,579 (-35.6%) and tractor sales 195,857. Tractor Zoom's Q2 2026 playbook has 60% of dealers calling the bottom at end-2026 or 2027.
  - https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/?p=41512
  - https://info.tractorzoompro.com/hubfs/B2B/Events/Webinars/Q2%20Dealer%20Webinar%20-%202026/Q2-2026-DealerPlaybook.pdf

## Per-idea notes

### 1. Harvest Pit Crew (kill)

- **Already done in part.** Deere runs two vans up the harvest run, from Altus, OK to Minot, ND and from Munday, TX to Conrad, MT, plus a semi trailer of harvest-specific parts parked at dealers. JD Protect gives a 24-hour uptime assurance on new combines with dealer maintenance. Gleaner's GleanerCare gives a free loaner after 24 hours. RDO Ready+ and RDO's "Ready To Harvest" inspection program are dealer-run. The brand-agnostic gap is real but small.
  - https://www.farmershotline.com/story/gleaner-introduces-gleanercare-s8-super-series-combines
  - https://www.rdoequipment.com/resources/blogs/rdo-s-ready-plus-program-extended-warranty-for-farm-equipment-uptime
- **Substitute.** Crews fix their own machines and carry their own parts trailers. The idea's own "earned secret" concedes this.
- **Price versus value (round 1 lesson 5).** The $12-18k fee per machine-season is about what a crew combine's whole annual repair bill likely is (my estimate; I could not retrieve the Nebraska figures). The customer would pay roughly a full repair budget for a response-time promise.
- **Revenue math.** 6,000 x $16.5k = $99M. The idea's own custom-crew pool is about 4,000 combines. The remainder has to come from farms that have dealers and their own shops.
- **Other.** Seasonal labor utilization is poor. The credit is a service contract, and 43 states regulate those.

### 2. Shared Shop (weak)

- **Math is arithmetic, not economics.** 500 x $200k = $100M, but most of that is pass-through parts and shop payroll at cost, with the idea's 15-20% margin. Gross profit at the ceiling is $15-20M, and a realistic ramp gets to a fraction of that.
- **Buyer pool is smaller than claimed.** USDA shows about 16k farms at $5M+ across all commodities, including livestock, poultry and nursery. The idea assumes 20-25k.
- **Substitute.** In-house shops are cheaper than $140+/hr outside labor, and large farms keep shops for control during the five-day planting window. Dealers sell on-site periodic maintenance contracts.
- **No evidence of demand.** The only precedent is a UK 10,000-acre farm. I found no US equivalent in two searches.

### 3. Machine Passport (weak)

- **"No TractorFax / lenders mark blind" is wrong.** Tractor Zoom Pro sells lender valuations to Farm Credit, IronAppraiser values go to banks, Machinery Scope has inspected and warrantied since 2013, and RB Global did 600k+ inspections in 2020 (across all equipment).
  - https://rbauction.com/news-releases/20210406-ritchie-bros-inspection-services-completes-600-000-plus-equipment-and-truck-inspections-in-2020
- **Price points are unsupported.** The model assumes $1,100 per inspection against a market of $100-300. It also assumes $75 per loan per year for monitoring with no buyer evidence.
- **Scale evidence.** The best-funded ag used-equipment data company raised about $27M and is estimated at about $2M of revenue. That is third-party data and unverified, but it points the same way as the rest.
- **Data access.** The unverified "earned secret" (which fields the tools expose) is the central unknown. It is a feeder dataset for a warranty or lender product, not a $100M business on its own.

### 4. Tech Bench (kill)

- **Bill rate is wrong.** 650 FTE x 1,700 h x $90 = $99M. A Trackside-style traveling field tech is posted at $26-30/hr, and BLS's mean is about $52k. A 25% margin at $90 implies $65-70/hr pay, about 2.5x the market. At a realistic $50-60 bill rate the same bench is $55-66M, and a bench of that size is not credible in 5-7 years.
- **Competitors.** Ag-specific recruiters and temp firms (Red Seal, Labor Finders, Hansen), dealers' own road techs, and OEM field-support rosters.
- **Cyclicality.** The travel-nurse analogy cuts the other way: AMN and Cross Country travel revenue fell 37-41% once the shortage eased.
- **Moat.** The idea's own text calls it the weakest moat in the set. Workforce Pell is a roughly $1.3-4.3k subsidy per trainee with 70% completion and placement tests, not revenue.

### 5. Any-Shop Plan (weak; structurally the most scalable)

- **The price is a combine price.** PowerGard on a new combine runs $3.5-5.4k/yr comprehensive, and limited powertrain tiers run $550-1,100/yr. At a blended $2k, $100M of premium needs about 50,000 contracts, which is about 11% of the idea's own unsourced 450k-machine base. As an MGA keeping about 25%, revenue is about $25M.
- **Incumbents.** Machinery Scope/Ag Guard (up to 15-year-old combines and tractors, CNA-backed), AmTrust (heavy-equipment and ag extended warranty), Assurant with CNH Capital and with EPG, HSB (implements and electronics), RDO Ready+, Deere PowerGard.
  - https://www.farm-equipment.com/articles/20194-15-years-on-combines-and-tractors-machinery-scope
  - https://amtrustfinancial.com/specialty-risk/products/heavy-equipment
  - https://equipmentfa.com/news/36140/assurant-cnh-industrial-capital-partner-to-deliver-equipment-protection-products
  - https://www.precisionfarmingdealer.com/articles/1416-hsbs-new-insurance-follows-farmers-into-the-field
- **Legal.** This is solvable but slow. State service-contract statutes follow the NAIC model (https://content.naic.org:443/sites/default/files/model-law-685.pdf), and NCOIL says 43 states regulate service contracts, often requiring reimbursement insurance. The idea's "NCOIL treats it as insurance" is imprecise; the operative text is the NAIC model plus state law.
- **Economics risk.** Adverse selection on high-hour used machines, and the "pay independents at $90 instead of $140" saving is a claim-cost argument, not demand. Take-rate evidence for ag extended warranties is thin ("pretty low" per an F&I consultant).

### 6. Standard Shop (weak)

- **Economics.** Dues of $3.6M plus a 22-28% margin on parts means the real business is parts distribution. 500 shops at $200k each is a very high wallet share for a shop that also buys from its dealer.
- **Competitors.** Dealers (56% of their parts go out over the counter), PartsASAP, A&I, Messick's, FleetPride/TruckPro and NAPA for the commodity layer.
- **The OEM response is price.** Deere's price cuts, second-tier parts and Parts OnSite attack the same margin a shop network would live on. Dealers call aftermarket the bright spot in their outlook, so they will defend it: https://www.farm-equipment.com/articles/24878-aftermarket-business-continues-to-be-bright-spot-in-dealer-outlook
- **Insight that survives.** Trust and parts credit, not price, are what hold farmers at the dealer. That is a real point and I could not disprove it. It does not make a $100M revenue line.

### 7. Core Bank (weak; the best of the set)

- **The event math contradicts itself.** The idea says a 3-5% event rate across about 1M machines makes the pool "far above 35,000". 1M x 3-5% = 30-50k events a year, so 35,000 exchanges would be 70-115% of all events. Even counting several reman-able components per machine, the share is well over the "under 10%" claimed.
- **Competitors are big and funded.** Deere Reman (500+ people, five Springfield-area sites, core center expanding to 400k sq ft in 2026) sells through dealers only. CNH reman runs through SRC Holdings in Springfield. PartsASAP is a Kinderhook roll-up (14+ add-ons, 42 locations in 2022) that calls itself the largest buyer of salvage ag equipment and runs rebuild facilities. That is the cornered resource the idea claims. Independent rebuilders (Abilene Machine since 1980, Texas Final Drive) sell reman at about 50% under new. Core brokers already exist.
  - https://www.kinderhook.com/press-release/kinderhook-industries-recapitalizes-all-states-ag-parts
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRC_Holdings_Corp.
- **Settlement angle.** The parts-pairing and reprogramming terms help electronic-module reman, but tool access is per machine, needs owner permission, and the order is not yet approved. EPA's Feb 2026 guidance still requires equipment to be returned to certified configuration.
- **What is real.** Cores are the moat in reman, and a $100M independent ag-parts business plausibly exists (PartsASAP). That is evidence the pool can hold a large independent, not that a new entrant wins one.

### 8. Elevator Depots (kill)

- **Gross-sales illusion.** $100M GMV with a 10-12% retailer commission and 10-15% contribution is a $10-15M business. That is the exact "revenue is not value" failure from round 1.
- **First-party supply is not exclusive.** Deere says its alternative tier will be sold wherever customers buy parts today, so a retailer can buy from Deere directly.
- **Substitutes.** Dealer counters, Parts OnSite cabinets, mail order with 35k+ SKUs, farm stores, and OEM-owned dealers that offer two-hour delivery.
- **Budget line and willingness.** I found no US co-op parts program, in two searches. That is either an opening or a sign that co-ops have decided against it, and I found no evidence for the first.

## Picks

**None.** No idea survived as strong.

**Closest to viable, not recommended as-is.** A lender-embedded service-contract program for used late-model machines (the Any-Shop Plan wedge) is the only structure here that can scale on a balance sheet instead of labor. It would need all four of these:
1. A quoted attach rate from a lender or auction partner above 10%, against today's "pretty low".
2. A fronting carrier that will write the form.
3. A blended price near $2k that still pays a 25% margin.
4. A data-driven loss ratio that beats Machinery Scope's.

Testing that costs a few lender calls, not a build. I would not spend more on this pool unless those four come back yes.

## Cross-cutting lessons for the pool

- Every slice here competes with an OEM move or a dealer program that is cheaper to extend than to displace. The Deere settlement opens tool access; it does not remove the dealer's parts-and-service margin, and Deere is cutting prices on that margin itself.
- Three of the eight ideas hit $100M only by counting gross sales or pass-through costs as revenue.
- Unverified inputs I could not check: Titan's 61.5% service margin, the 65% dealer-poll figure, Deere's $1.2B tariff expense, and the idea-supplied counts of 70-100k newer combines and 450k outside-warranty machines. None of the verdicts depends on them.

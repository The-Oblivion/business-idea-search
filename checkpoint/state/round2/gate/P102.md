# Gate report: pool P102, US heavy-duty (Class 6-8) truck repair, maintenance and parts aftermarket

Gate date: 2026-10-15. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with roughly 3-5 searches each, plus primary-page reads of the WarrantyWeek 2025 and 2024 claims tables, the Samsara June 2026 product post, California Insurance Code 12800, the Fullbay 2026 report coverage, the ATRI 2025 cost coverage and the First Student "First Services" release. Result: 4 kill, 4 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The pool is real (ATRI: repair and maintenance 22 cents a mile plus tires 5 cents, at 85,991 miles per truck in 2025, so about $23k per truck-year) and the shock is real (Section 232 tariffs, technician shortage, small-carrier bankruptcies). But on every one of the 8 ideas, the "nobody does this" premise was wrong or partly wrong, and the three biggest revenue claims break on one check each.

- The warranty pool is 5x too big for this pool. WarrantyWeek's $3.94B of 2025 claims is about 67% farm, construction and mining equipment (Deere $1,330M, Caterpillar $836M, AGCO $415M). On-highway truck and bus makers in the table total about $792M, of which PACCAR is $772M, and that is a global figure. Daimler, Volvo and International are not in the table. Source: https://warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20260521.html (2024 table: https://www.warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20250501.html).
- cbCharge already does Shop Ledger's core product. It is Corporate Billing, a SouthState Bank unit (factoring and receivables for transportation and auto-service providers), in business since 1995. It pays the shop fast, takes the fleet's credit risk, is preferred vendor for NAPA Truck Service, HDA Truck Pride, PTP and Jasper, and sits behind Fleetio's Maintenance Billing Account. It also already holds the cross-shop invoice ledger the idea calls its moat. Sources: https://www.corpbill.com/our-solutions/cbcharge/ , https://www.jasperengines.com/blog/cbcharge/ , https://help.fleetio.com/en_US/fleetio-maintenance-billing-account-overview , https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/764038/000155837024002302/R9.htm
- The labor-services ideas are labor-limited and the incumbents are already consolidating. Ryder bought Pit Stop Fleet Service (about $24M of revenue after 27 years), then Truck Service Depot, and says Torque by Ryder now has about 200 technicians in 140 markets (27 states per one trade report). Garnett Station launched True North Fleet Services as a PE roll-up. First Student already sells maintenance-only contracts to school districts with 1,250 technicians and 45,000 vehicles. $100M of field labor needs about 3x Ryder's entire mobile technician base.
- Several "why now" items are already absorbed by the telematics vendors. Samsara (June 24, 2026) now drafts OEM warranty claims when a work order closes; Motive (Aug 18, 2026) launched an AI maintenance platform with invoice scanning and warranty tracking; Fullbay bought Pitstop (telematics fault data) in 2026.

## Revenue-math corrections (where the $100M paths break)

- Idea 1: $3.3B of financed invoices at a 3% take is $99M, and arithmetic holds, but the sizing does not. Fullbay's 3,400+ shops billed a claimed $5.04B, about $1.5M a shop. If 25-50% of that is credit-terms invoices to small fleets, $3.3B needs roughly 4,400-8,900 shops, which is 1.3x to 2.6x Fullbay's entire installed base, all joining one network. The "6.6% of outsourced spend" is therefore share of invoices that are on credit terms, not share of spend, which is nearer 25-50%. A credit book also needs a balance sheet through a freight recession (small-carrier bankruptcies are climbing: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/small-fleets-brokers-drive-new-wave-of-trucking-bankruptcies).
- Idea 2: $345k a crew-year is 2 techs x 1,500 billable hours x $115. $100M is about 290 crews and about 580 technicians. Pit Stop took 27 years to reach about $24M; Torque by Ryder has about 200 technicians after two acquisitions. Fullbay's 2026 report coverage gives a median shop labor rate of $149 an hour (up from $135) and median technician pay of $32 (https://forum.freightwaves.com/t/fullbay-s-2026-report-heavy-duty-shops-face-structural-technician-shortage/9024 ; 7.4% rate growth also at https://www.motor.com/2026/03/fullbay-releases-sixth-state-of-heavy-duty-repair-report/), so $115 undercuts shops by about a quarter, which Ryder or Love's can match.
- Idea 3: $715M of originations is 21% of a $3.4B pool that the idea itself built from unsourced inputs (1.2M trucks x 0.35 repairs over $2,500 x $8,000). The fleet-size premise is OK (about 91% of fleets run six or fewer trucks and 97% run fewer than 20, per ATA/FMCSA 2019 data reported by FleetOwner: https://www.fleetowner.com/truck-stats/fleet-owner-500/document/21154698/2021-fleetowner-500-top-forhire-fleets), but net yield is about zero.
- Idea 4: $1.0B of pass-through is 25% of $3.94B, but the real on-highway claims pool is a fraction of that (about $0.8B in the table plus unlisted Daimler, Volvo and International, my estimate $2-3B in total, unverified). Even then 25% means dealers give up a quarter of their warranty labor. At a 10% net take, $100M needs about $1B moved, which no single OEM program has done.
- Idea 5: $12,750 a truck-year (17 cents x 75,000 miles) x 7,800 trucks is $99.5M gross; at a 22% keep, $100M net needs 35,600 trucks, 81% of Ryder's whole SelectCare customer-vehicle base. For comparison ATRI average spend is about 27 cents (22 repair and maintenance plus 5 tires) on 85,991 miles, about $23k a truck, so the plan's price is about 55% of the average bill and the covered-repair add-on carries real adverse-selection risk on 6-15-year-old trucks.
- Idea 6: $100M is 18,200 buses at $5,500, but only about 31% of that spend is labor, and First Services already maintains 45,000 vehicles. At 240 buses a contract (about $1.3M), $100M is about 75 separate RFP wins.
- Idea 7: $100M is 83,000 units at $1,200 and 5,500 donor trucks. The existing reman market is about $3.5B, so share is fine; the constraint is cores and testing capacity, as the idea admits, and the product is a commodity with OEM and distributor channels.
- Idea 8: $2.0B of DPF-related spend is built on a 5.3M-truck assumption and treats every cleaning as swappable; $100M is 5% of all of it, against OEM exchange, dealer same-day cleaning and in-house cleaning that already take most of it.

## Picks

None. No idea clears the bar (median at least $20M and a believable upside of at least $100M with a named, plausible chain of assumptions). The closest, ideas 2 and 6 (the same Level-1 yard-crew model sold to private and public fleets), reach about $18M and $15M medians and about $90M and $80M upsides because the business is capped by technician hiring, the scope is narrowed by 49 CFR 396.19 and 396.25, and Ryder, Love's, Penske and First Student can copy a labor model. Shrinking either into a regional boutique is the failure mode round 1 documented.

What would have to be true to reopen the pool:

- A pilot showing fleets will pay $115 an hour for hourly Level-1 crews at 70%+ utilization in their own yards, with a compliance structure for inspections (a documented Level-3 inspector of record), would reopen ideas 2 and 6 as one combined "fleet-yard maintenance workforce" company.
- A published cbCharge fee schedule and loss history, or a refusal by cbCharge and Fullbay to use ELD data, would reopen idea 1 as a data-led credit network; today the incumbent already holds the invoice ledger.
- An OEM willing to pay a multi-brand intermediary for campaign completion speed, with a signed pilot, would reopen idea 4; none exists beyond Love's.

Closest-to-viable follow-up (not a pick): idea 5 sold through a distribution partner that already holds small-fleet cash flow (an ELD vendor, a freight factor or Progressive's commercial book) as an MGA with insurer paper. Its blocker is distribution and adverse selection, not demand.

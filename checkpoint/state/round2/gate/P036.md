# Gate report: pool P036, gas-turbine fleet services and aftermarket

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas checked with roughly 4-8 searches each. Result: 3 kill, 4 weak, 1 strong (conditional). One pick.

## Corrections to claims in the ideas

- Idea 3: Hallador's $760/kW excludes roughly $100M of transport, refurbishment and insurance, so delivered cost is about $980/kW (https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/hallador-energy-bypasses-oem-backlog-secures-unfired-siemens-turbine-in-350-million-deal).
- Idea 5: "roughly 1,100 US baseload F-class units" looks high. GE's entire global F-class fleet was reported at 1,100+ units years ago (https://www.power-eng.com/gas/turbines/ges-f-technology-gas-turbines-achieve-71-million-fired-hours/). Plan on 600-800 addressable US F-class units until a roll-off list is built from EIA-860 and OEM install data.
- Idea 5: "only OEMs offer F-class LTSAs" is outdated. Third-party F-class term agreements date from 2007 (NYPA, Emery, AECI Dell) and 2015 (MEAG).
- Idea 1: a 2017 trade source put hot-gas-path spare sets for 150-200 MW machines at $1.5-2.5M (https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/the-oem-gas-turbine-overhaul-war-on-efficiency-price-intellectual-property); the $5-10M figure only holds if the rotor is in the set. Revenue of $6M per outage means the pool captures 50-100% of the whole event spend, which is head-on competition with ISPs.
- Idea 1: DEWA's rotor swap and 64 extra days are Siemens/DEWA press-release figures (https://www.powermag.com/press-releases/siemens-completes-extensive-service-in-record-time-at-power-plant-in-dubai/). DEWA owns eight units on site, which is the fleet-owner case, not the single-unit case.
- Idea 7: the "6 GW" is a Siemens Energy vendor estimate for its own eligible US F-class units.
- Idea 8: Cleanview itself says many projects are phantom and 2027 operating capacity ranges from ~5 to ~13 GW.
- Ownership: Allied Power Group appears to be owned by Warren Equity Partners, not AEA (https://www.rwbaird.com/transactions/investment-banking/dealcard/6793/). Hanwha Power now includes PSM (March 2026).
- Why-now caveat across the pool: casting scarcity (GE Aerospace paying $11.75B for CPP, https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/2026/09/08/ge-aerospace-to-buy-castings-maker-cpp-for-nearly-us12-billion/) hurts independents that buy castings at least as much as it helps them. PJM capacity at the cap has a cliff after the 2029/30 delivery year unless the collar is extended again.

## What the pool taught

The money is real (OEMs hold about 62-80% of a $10-30B service pool) but it flows through LTSAs, parts and shop work that are already contested by PE- or conglomerate-backed ISPs. Most "gaps" were either existing ISP programs (rotor/component exchange, lease pools for aeroderivatives) or thin staffing and brokerage layers. Only the pricing-and-risk-wrapper layer (idea 5) is both large and unproductised.

## Pick

### Pick 1: Progressive for Turbines, as a hardware-pooled, insurer-wrapped, condition-priced maintenance contract

Why it is the only pick: it is the one idea whose bottom-up arithmetic reaches $100M at modest share (25-35 units is about 3-5% of ~600-800 addressable F-class units at $3-4M per unit-year), it has an existing budget line (LTSA and transactional maintenance fees), and its core mechanism (price on measured wear, wrap with reinsurance, hold pooled spares) was not found offered by anyone.

Honest ceiling: ~$70M in 5-7 years, range $40-110M. It is credit- and capital-heavy, so the first fixed-price contract needs a risk-capital facility behind it. The OEM can answer by matching price on the best units. Standard property insurance excludes wear and tear, so reinsurer appetite is unproven.

Sharpened pitch: see the structured output. Cheapest test: five free LTSA forensic audits for co-ops and municipal agencies, one broker-led reinsurer term-sheet request, and three lender independent-engineer interviews. Kill threshold: median measured saving below ~8% versus the equivalent-hours price, or no reinsurer willing to quote a parts-cost excess-of-loss.

Not picked: idea 1 (folded into the pick as the pooled-hardware layer) and idea 2 (folded in as the serial-level data feed). Everything else stays dead or weak.

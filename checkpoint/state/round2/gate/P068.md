# Gate report: pool P068, US building owners' fire and life-safety ITM and monitoring (POTS copper cutover)

Gate date: 2026-10-14. Stance: skeptical by default. 8 ideas, about 40 searches, plus page reads of the FCC order draft, the Broadband Breakfast piece on AT&T's approvals, MarketSpark's Series B release, Everon's white paper and Telgian's 40-year profile. Result: 2 kill, 6 weak, 0 strong. No picks.

## Headline finding

The shock is real and the pool is big, but every layer an idea tries to occupy already has a funded owner, and the first copper waves are much smaller than the ideas assume.

- **The owner-side POTS-exit program already exists.** MarketSpark sells managed POTS replacement to enterprises (site surveys, install, 24/7 management, "no upfront" per third-party profile). It raised a $7M Series B in April 2021 and says it supports 35 Fortune 500 companies (https://www.marketspark.com/?p=8603). Its retail case study audited about 360 locations and installed 350 kits (https://marketspark.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/MarketSpark-USE-CASE-for-Department-Stores.pdf). Ooma AirDial has 41 reseller partners including T-Mobile for Business and AireSpring (https://www.barchart.com/story/news/34076969/airespring-signs-distribution-agreement-with-ooma-to-offer-nationwide-pots-replacement-solution). Alarm.com launched a UL 864 dual-path commercial fire communicator on 2026-07-16 (https://s24.q4cdn.com/652723797/files/doc_news/Alarm-com-Expands-into-Commercial-Fire-with-New-Fire-Communicator-2026.pdf). DataRemote, TPx, EPIC iO and 5Gstore (rental at $69.99 a month, https://5gstore.com/blog/2025/02/04/5gstore-pots-line-replacement-solution/) all sell the same box.
- **The category leader's own goal is the size of idea 1's whole plan.** Ooma's management says its long-term goal is 300,000 AirDial lines and $100M of AirDial ARR, about $333 a line a year (FQ3 2026 call per https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/ooma-inc-nyseooma-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript-1657178/, secondary summary). It has not reached it. It also admits resellers' POTS cost can exceed what they bill. Total Ooma revenue guidance is only $332-333.5M for FY27 (https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260826510873/en/Ooma-Reports-Fiscal-Second-Quarter-2027-Financial-Results).
- **The first wave is small and rural.** AT&T's Dec 2025 application (the "additional 25% of wire centers") covers about 90,000 customers across 18 states, with service ending from 2026-11-15 (read from https://broadbandbreakfast.com/at-t-approved-to-discontinue-service-at-more-than-30-of-copper-footprint-this-year/). Ooma's management said it does not yet see Verizon active. The FCC counts 15.0M switched lines in June 2025, down from 27.2M in mid-2022 (https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-421558A1.pdf), so the pool is a shrinking, one-time conversion wave, not a growing market.
- **The FCC source in the pool file is the circulated draft, not the adopted text.** DOC-419252A1 is FCC-CIRC 2603-03 (March 5, 2026). Secondary sources confirm adoption of FCC 26-19 at the March 26 meeting (https://broadbandbreakfast.com/fcc-adopts-copper-retirement-call-center-onshoring-orders/). The draft text never mentions fire alarms or elevators; it names "home security alarms and medical monitoring devices" (para 29) and notes the alarm industry asked whether replacement services will interoperate (n.96). The fire-alarm link is from vendors, as the pool file said.
- **Nobody sells a $100M-per-year incumbent-free niche here.** The independent owner-side fire/life-safety program manager (Telgian) is a 40-year-old firm estimated at $80-111M of revenue and about 360 employees (third-party estimates, https://growjo.com/company/Telgian). That is the ceiling of the "neutral program" model after four decades.

## Notes per idea

**1. Zero-Down Copper Exit (weak).**
- Competitors and substitutes: MarketSpark (enterprise, $7M raised, 35 Fortune 500 customers, "tens of thousands of lines" per its grocery case study), Ooma AirDial, Alarm.com Fire Communicator, DataRemote POTS IN A BOX, TPx Digital POTS (https://www.tpx.com/resources/product-literature/digital-pots/), EPIC iO ($0 CapEx options), Telguard and NAPCO StarLink through dealers, Everon/ADT and Johnson Controls for their own bases. Buyer guides put a cellular fire communicator at $15-40 a month versus $50-120 a POTS line, two per panel (https://usmadesupply.com/resources/guides/fire-alarm-communicators-guide), so the $75-a-site ARPU leaves almost no savings to share with the owner and sits well above what competitors quote per line.
- Revenue math: 110k sites x $900 = $99M checks out arithmetically. The share does not. It needs more than the leader's long-term goal while financing $110-175M of deployments, and the early cutover demand is small. Alarm lenders do exist (Alarm Financial Services, a lender focused on recurring-revenue loans), and practitioners put commercial fire RMR near 40x (https://www.securityinfowatch.com/alarms-monitoring/central-station-alarm-monitoring/article/10781324/recurring-monthly-revenue-and-contracts), but I found no evidence they lend against a communicator-only data contract that is not UL central-station monitoring RMR.
- Legal: NFPA 72 permits a single supervised cellular path if the device is UL 864 listed and the AHJ accepts it, edition by edition (https://www.adt.com/commercial/resources/fire-alarm-communication-pathways). That is workable but is AHJ-by-AHJ friction for everyone. The 4G LTE sunset around 2030 (Everon) means a second replacement cycle.

**2. Wire-Center Sweeps (kill).**
- Door-to-door sales to small owners for a $1,200 compliance swap, with a 15% close-rate requirement, competes against the owner's existing alarm dealer. The only retail "customers" without a dealer are the unmonitored, who have no code obligation to convert.
- The idea's "AT&T's first ~500 wire centers" were reported as approved in 2025 with effect in 2026 (https://www.lightreading.com/broadband/at-t-s-copper-retirement-plan-plows-ahead), and the next batch is 90k customers, so a 100-door metro pilot has no calendar to anchor on.
- Originated contracts sold to roll-ups at about 1x revenue ($540 a year) cannot repay a $1,200 swap plus CAC.

**3. Second-Opinion Desk (weak).**
- Cintas Fire gross margin 50.8% in Q4 FY26 is confirmed (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000072325426000025/ex992026-5x31.htm). That supports the repair-margin conflict. It does not make a neutral third party big: Telgian has run that exact model since 1985, and its own brochure lists repair among its services.
- $100M at 20-25% margin is $20-25M of gross profit on a pass-through principal model; facilities outsourcers (JLL, CBRE) and ServiceTrade-class software sit on both sides.

**4. Panel Liberation (weak).**
- Forum accounts say Notifier issues a code to owners on a waiver and Siemens partners can take over any Siemens panel after a short transition (https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/simplex-fire-alarm-device-compatibility.110335/), so the locked-panel population is mostly Simplex. A SUNY Stony Brook award cited that only Johnson Controls was qualified on Simplex.
- No fire-alarm lease-purchase, performance-contract or zero-down program turned up. Public owners usually cannot sign 5-year pay-from-savings deals without procurement and appropriation risk.

**5. Full-Service Fire Protection (weak).**
- A real single-panel price: JCI's 2026 proposal to a school district priced a semi-annual test and inspect plus system labor and panel parts at $3,566 for the main panel. Pasco County's SimplexGrinnell "Full Service Contract" is a 2008 precedent that still left some repairs billable.
- Realistic spend per square foot is probably $0.08-0.15 (the pool file's $8-12B of recurring ITM and repair over ~96B sf), so a 50k sf building is $4-7.5k, not $15k. At $0.10 per sf, $100M is about 20k buildings (3-5% of the assumed 400-600k), still not impossible, but the gross margin must hold at 30% under adverse selection with no loss data yet.

**6. Impairment Desk (kill).**
- The payer split is the problem: the insurer's desk is free to the insured, the dealer or central station already phones the owner, and a guard firm is paid per hour. A $15-40 monthly membership for coordination competes with all three. The global fire-watch market is quoted at $2.8B by a vendor (https://marketintelo.com/report/fire-watch-services-market), most of which is guard hours the idea says it would cannibalize.
- Trigger thresholds (NFPA 25 at more than 10 hours in 24, many AHJs at 4 hours) come from secondary summaries; I did not verify them against the standards.

**7. Hyperscale Life-Safety Crew (weak).**
- Sources disagree on the fire share: the idea says 2-4% of construction cost; vendor guides put "fire suppression and controls" at $0.5-1M per MW against about $11.3M per MW (4-9%) (https://www.datacentres.com/guides/data-centre-costs-explained). Either way the pool is $1-4.5B, so share is not the constraint. Customer access, bonding and labor are.
- The 29%-retiring workforce figure is a trade-site summary of an NFPA survey (https://fireprotectionfinder.com/news/fire-protection-workforce-shortage-nicet-2026 returned 404 on fetch; treat as unverified).

**8. Hood-to-Alarm Program (weak).**
- No bundled hood plus suppression plus extinguishers plus alarm program was found, which is a true gap. But the unit economics are a sub-contracted coordination fee on a $1.2-4.5k ticket, franchisee-paid, and franchisors rarely mandate fire vendors. Market size sources conflict ($2B US kitchen-exhaust cleaning from a broker listing against $437M global from IndustryARC).

## Picks

None. The strongest idea (1) fails the "do not shrink" test: at a realistic price and share it is a $20-30M business, and the $100M version requires beating a public company's stated long-term target from a standing start.

## What this pool taught us

A mandated shock (copper exit) and a capex boom (data centers) did not leave a venture-scale gap. Each layer already has a funded owner: MarketSpark, Ooma and Alarm.com for the box, Telgian for the neutral program, Brycer for AHJ data, APi and Comfort for data-center labor. Early copper waves are tiny, and services margins cap revenue.

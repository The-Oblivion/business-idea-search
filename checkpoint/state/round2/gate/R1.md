# Round 2 gate, batch R1: adversarial due diligence

Date: 2026-10-18. Two ideas re-aimed at bigger pools. Posture: assume someone already does it and the market is smaller than claimed.

**Result: both fail the bar. Idea 1 is a kill, Idea 2 is weak. Zero picks.**

| Idea | Verdict | Median 5-7 yr revenue | Upside 5-7 yr revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large-Load Queue Bureau | kill | ~$5M | ~$45M |
| SNAP/Medicaid payment-accuracy ledger | weak | ~$8M | ~$60M |

---

## 1. Large-Load Queue Bureau (reality scores that price the $50,000-per-MW deposit)

### What the searches showed

**Premise facts that checked out**
- ERCOT is tracking about 474 GW of large-load requests, about 90% data centers. Abbott's Aug 3 directive ordered a verification and audit of 250-300 Batch Zero projects (about 200 GW). Source: Global Data Center Hub; Pillsbury; Utility Dive.
- ERCOT's Sept 14-15 board deck gives 362 conditionally classified Batch Zero projects: 204 base (66.4 GW) plus 158 studied (127.9 GW), about 194 GW.
- FERC issued six show-cause orders to RTOs/ISOs on June 18, 2026.
- Wood Mackenzie puts "phantom" load at about 768 GW of 1,066 GW requested.

**Premise facts that failed**
1. **The 80%-forfeited-at-six-months rule is not the adopted rule.**
   - The PUCT adopted 16 TAC 25.194 (Project 58481) on Sept 18, effective Oct 8.
   - The 80% forfeiture and six-month trigger were in the March draft.
   - The final rule gives 24 months, measured against the energization schedule as a whole.
   - At that point the utility draws on the security for amounts actually owed, keeps 20% tied to the unused capacity, and returns the rest.
   - Zero allocation means the security comes back after amounts owed are collected.
   - Security is posted within 60 days after ERCOT's study allocates capacity.
   - Sources: Utility Dive and colocationscout. White & Case describes a staged 20%-steps release. I did not read the PUCT order itself.
   - Consequence: loss-given-default on the deposit is far smaller than the pitch assumes. The credit layer's "$80-145M pool per cycle" collapses.
2. **The audit is ERCOT's own, and it is one-time.**
   - ERCOT sends RFIs through the transmission and distribution providers, has statutory consequences (ineligibility for false or missing responses), and files its Eligibility Verification Report by Dec 10, 2026.
   - No outside verifier role is described anywhere.
   - ERCOT's own deck calls Batch Zero a "one-time transitional study".
   - The wedge ("sell evidence files to funders, developers and intervenors, pilot by day 90") lands after the audit ends.
3. **ERCOT already filtered the queue without a bureau.** The same deck shows 373 projects (302 GW) excluded from Batch Zero. The deposit and eligibility criteria did the work.
4. **Utilities are screening with money.**
   - AEP Ohio's pipeline fell from more than 30 GW to 13 GW after its data-center tariff.
   - Exelon's pipeline fell from 43 GW to 25 GW, with about $1B in collateral posted.
   - A posted deposit is a better reality test than any score. The mechanism that creates the why-now also removes the need.
5. **The cross-queue duplicate registry runs into the statute.**
   - Per the House committee analysis of SB 6 (I did not read the enrolled text), applicants must disclose duplicate requests, and the PUC must bar utilities from selling, sharing or disclosing that information.
   - Inside Texas, ERCOT is a single queue anyway, so intra-Texas duplicates are visible to ERCOT.

### Competitors, in-house teams, substitutes
- **GridUnity**: interconnection-lifecycle software with utilities as customers (Entergy, PG&E, SCE, Southern, Xcel). DOE selected it for a $49.5M grant (total project $99M). It is mostly generation-side. Its LinkedIn mentions large loads, and Latitude Media says it is pushing financial and commercial readiness screening for load queues.
- **Enverus**: published a 12-signal Batch Zero readiness model (55 projects/21.7 GW expected in Batch Zero, 62 projects/37 GW in Batch 1+). It is also reported to have bought Pearl Street (unverified).
- **Cleanview**: tracks unannounced projects from permits and satellite imagery. Bootstrapped and profitable.
- **LandGate**: sells load-queue analysis to developers.
- **Free/public**: interconnection.fyi, DC Hub's queue tracker, Epoch AI hub, Wood Mackenzie public analysis.
- **GridCARE**: $64M Series A in May 2026, $77.5M total, with National Grid participating. It sits adjacent (finding capacity), not a verifier.
- **Deposit funding**:
  - The Dynamix/Staubach/Soda Springs $95M SSSC Batch Zero Fund is the only dedicated vehicle found.
  - Banks write letters of credit (Halcyon tracks 67 of 109 collateral filings citing LCs).
  - Surety is a cheaper substitute. NYISO and ERCOT's credit rules allow surety bonds, and brokers quote 1-3% of the bond amount. Whether Batch Zero accepts surety was not confirmed.
  - The Batch Zero deposit window (July 10-24) has already closed.
- **In-house**: ERCOT, PUCT, utility planning staff, and utilities' own tariffs and financial-commitment screens.

### Revenue math check (their numbers vs reachable counts)
- "100 utilities with 1 GW+ queues x $400K": I could not find a count. Six named utilities clearly qualify. My estimate is 30-50. At 45 x $400K the ceiling is $18M, not $40M.
- "7 ISOs x $2M": ISOs buy workflow software (GridUnity) or build in-house. No evidence any ISO has bought a third-party registry. Realistic is 2-3 buyers.
- "2,000 applications x $15-20K": the applicant is the party being scored and will pay only if an ISO or utility makes the attestation mandatory. No sign of that.
- The credit layer pool is overstated for two reasons. The forfeiture premise is gone. And financing refundable security costs about 1-3% per year (surety range) and sits with banks, sureties and funds, which are balance-sheet businesses.

Median case: about $5M (hundreds of evidence files plus a few utility subscriptions, competing with Enverus, Wood Mackenzie, Cleanview and LandGate).

Upside case: about $45M. Assumptions: FERC or two or more ISOs adopt a standard cross-queue attestation, and the statute permits furnishing. Then roughly $27M from 3 ISOs at $2M, 20 utilities at $400K, 500 applications at $16K, and 45 lender seats at $100K. Another $10-18M comes from arranger fees on third-party deposit funding at 15% share of a $20-90M annual fee pool. It does not reach $100M under any assumption set I can defend.

### Legal premise
Wrong on forfeiture (above). Unverified and likely adverse on data furnishing (SB 6 sharing bar). Lending licensing for the credit layer is still open.

### Deciding reason
Posting $50,000 per MW is itself the reality test, ERCOT already ran the audit in-house on a one-time basis ending Dec 10, and the adopted rule cut the forfeiture that made the credit layer big. What is left is a diligence-data shop competing with Enverus, Wood Mackenzie and Cleanview. **Kill.**

Cheapest test if someone insists: before building anything, ask 5 ISO/utility planning leads whether they would sign a data-furnishing agreement and a $400K subscription, and whether the SB 6 sharing bar blocks it. One "no" ends it.

## 2. Independent payment-accuracy ledger for state SNAP and Medicaid agencies

### What the searches showed

**Premise facts that checked out**
- Cost-share tiers: 0% below 6%, 5% at 6-7.99%, 10% at 8-9.99%, 15% at 10% or more. Starts FY2028 (Oct 1, 2027). Sources: CRS (IN12723), CBPP, FRAC.
- FY2028 uses the lower of the FY2025 or FY2026 rate. FY2029 and later use the rate from 3 years earlier.
- FY2025 national rate is 10.62% (about $10.1B in incorrect payments). 41 states plus DC are above 6%. FFIS estimates a $9.4B annual shift to states.
- Florida: $4M appropriated for FY2026-27, a vendor required by Sept 1, FY2025 rate 12.97%, about $1B exposure. No award found as of Oct 18.
- The North Carolina rent-deduction prompt is real: closed 20,000+ times (Route Fifty). It was built by state staff, with no vendor named.

**Premise facts that failed or weakened**
1. **The time window.**
   - FY2026 ended Sept 30, 2026. The FY2028 and FY2029 cost-share years are therefore already locked to FY2025/FY2026 rates.
   - Anything sold now first moves money for FY2030 (the FY2027 rate, published about mid-2028).
2. **Policy risk.**
   - The Senate Agriculture Committee draft (Aug 6, not advanced) would delay cost-share to FY2029, base FY2029 on the lower of FY2026 or FY2027, and raise the top tier to 20% from FY2031.
   - The House bill does not include it.
   - States above 13.33% already have a delayed start.
3. **Gainshare is the legally poisoned lever.**
   - Error-rate-linked consultant pay is the Wisconsin/Osnes pattern (payment contingent on a USDA bonus). DOJ pursued the consultant (a $751,571 False Claims Act settlement over alleged QC data falsification).
   - USDA OIG flagged the conflict between accurate QC reporting and mitigating errors to earn money. FNS found 42 of 53 agencies using "biased practices".
   - FNS QC Policy Memo 16-02 bars treating consultant activity that departs from federal policy as an allowable expense, requires 30-day advance notice to FNS plus the contract and deliverables, and says individual-case reviews by consultants happen after results go to FNS.
   - A fee tied to the tier avoided would sit on exactly the incentive that history punished, and 2 CFR 200 limits percentage-of-cost contracting. I did not retrieve 2 CFR 200.324 or find a rule that expressly bans gainshare, but nobody should assume it is allowable.
4. **Layer 2 is already sold by large incumbents.**
   - **SAS** launched Payment Integrity for Food Assistance on Nov 17, 2025. It analyzes every active SNAP case and recomputes the error rate. SAS says it has met with dozens of states, and Nevada chose it in June 2026 (exposure about $52M).
   - **Maximus** Accuracy Assistant, launched Jan 2026.
   - **PCG** SNAP accuracy improvement (root-cause analysis; Rhode Island pre/post-authorization review programs; Michigan training).
   - **KPMG**: Virginia, $1.7M for six months. **CNC Consulting**: NYC, $409K over 36 months.
   - **Nava** (Pennsylvania, open-source). **U.S. Digital Response** (Arizona, North Carolina, Maryland, free playbook). **Code for America**. **Digital Government Hub** (public QC data modeling).
5. **Re-adjudication of a sample of closed cases is what state QC already does.** Every state runs a QC unit, and federal re-review follows. The ledger's layer 2 duplicates it and, under Memo 16-02, must wait until after results go to FNS.
6. **Layer 1 (conformance packs) overlaps with mandated independent oversight.** Federal rules (45 CFR 95.626, Medicaid E&E) trigger IV&V on systems projects that meet risk criteria, and IV&V vendors already exist. SNAP-side applicability was not confirmed. Integrators also test their own releases.
7. **Medicaid is harder, not easier.**
   - H.R.1 sec. 71106 starts the 3% erroneous-payment reduction in FY2030, with counting rules changing in 2029. That is a real mandate.
   - But ten vendors (Accenture, Acentra, Conduent, GDIT, Deloitte, Gainwell, Maximus, Curam, Optum, RedMane) pledged $600M+ in no-cost or discounted work-requirement implementation services. CMS is giving $200M in FY2026 grants. Incumbents are giving the work away.

### Revenue math check
- The idea's "41 states x $2.5-4M = $105-165M" is extrapolated from one anchor (Florida's $4M).
- Observed anchors: Florida $4M, Virginia about $3.4M annualized plus a $2.4M in-house QA team, NYC about $0.14M a year, Nevada undisclosed.
- My estimate of total external SNAP-accuracy spend at peak: about $90M a year. Assumed split: 10 large states at about $5M, 15 mid-size at about $2M, 17 small at about $0.6M. This is mine, not sourced.
- SAS, Maximus, PCG and KPMG compete for it. An independent ledger gets perhaps 8% in the median case.
- The gainshare math ($1.3B/yr avoided across three states x 5%) assumes a 2-4 point error reduction, state attribution to the vendor (NC fixed theirs in-house), and legal permission. The legal permission is the weakest assumption.

Median case: about $8M. About $6M from the share of external accuracy spend, plus conformance packs (about $2M, 15 states x 3 rule changes x about $50K).

Upside case: about $60M. Assumptions: gainshare or outcome pricing is cleared by FNS and state procurement; a 25% share of the fixed-fee pool (about $22M); conformance packs adopted as an acceptance test across 30 states (about $10M); a Medicaid extension at about 10 states x $2M ($20M); and a small gainshare line of about $10M. Heroic share (over 20% of a pool) and a legal unknown. It does not reach $100M.

### Deciding reason
There is a real mandated budget (Florida's $4M, Virginia, Nevada), and independence from the integrator is a real differentiator. But the only lever that scales to $100M (a share of the avoided cost-share) sits on a practice FNS, OIG and DOJ have already punished. Without it this is a $10-30M niche in a market where SAS, Maximus, PCG and KPMG already sell the adjacent product. **Weak.**

Cheapest test: send the QC-risk memo to 3 state SNAP directors and ask whether FNS would approve an outcome-linked contract (they would need to ask FNS). Also ask Florida DCF who won the $4M and on what terms. If no state will put a paid pilot of $40K+ on the table by day 90, stop.

## Pool note

Zero picks. The two headline pools (the $9.7B deposit ceiling; the $9.4B a year SNAP cost shift) are real, but the people who sign for the tool are not the people who bear the penalty, and the money that moves flows to balance sheets, incumbents, or in-house staff. In both cases the mandate that created the why-now also created the free or incumbent substitute.

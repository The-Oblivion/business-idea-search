# C04 Dossier: Time-to-Power Fleet (P032 idea 5)

Date: 2026-10-18. Idea: hold a financed fleet of pre-built, multi-ratio 30-100 MVA substation transformers and sell, lease or option them for 8-16 week delivery. About 45 searches and fetches. Vendor, sponsored and aggregator sources are flagged. Inferences are marked.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: weak, near-dead as framed. Big-and-defensible score 3/10.** The gate's "weak, ceiling $70M" is right on sales and generous on profit.

1. The "nobody holds stock" gap is false at the dealer layer. JCL, Giga, Maddox and Sunbelt Solomon already sell or rent stocked or fast units. What is genuinely empty is a multi-ratio 30-100 MVA fleet carrying stranded-project risk. The evidence suggests it is empty for physical and economic reasons.
2. $100M of revenue is reachable only as pass-through sales (gross profit about $11-14M) or with a $0.6-0.85B lease balance sheet that earns roughly its cost of capital only at near-full utilization (section 5).
3. The timing is bad. A fleet bought now takes delivery in 2028-29, when new OEM capacity lands and Rystad says prices and lead times have stopped rising.
4. Transformer delay is often not the binding constraint. Grid connection is.

## 3. Bottom-up market and the payer

**Sourced anchors.**
- In 2019 US demand for transformers above 60 MVA was about 750 units, 82% imported, and DOE expected about 900 a year by 2027: https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-10/EXEC-2022-001242%20-%20Large%20Power%20Transformer%20Resilience%20Report%207-10-24.pdf
- Wood Mackenzie says substation power transformer demand is up 116% since 2019 (Reuters): https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots
- Vendor estimates put large US units at $2.1B in 2025: https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/united-states-large-power-transformer-market. Mordor Intelligence puts all US power transformers at $4.49B (seen only in a search summary; URL not verified).
- Price benchmark: recent European awards for 50-100 MVA run $30-35k per MVA, so about $1.5-1.75M at 50 MVA, $2.5-2.9M at 84 MVA, $3.0-3.5M at 100 MVA: https://powerpeakdigest.com/global-transformer-supply-constraints-ease-rystad/
- Dominion keeps filing 84 MVA units for Loudoun substations: https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/teac/2026/20260106/20260106-item-07---dom-supplemental-projects.pdf. This shows convergence inside one utility, not across utilities.

**Inference.** The 30-100 MVA slice is perhaps $1.5-3B a year, about 800-1,600 units. No source splits it. $100M of sales is 3-7% of it. Average unit price is nearer $2M than the idea's $3M, so $100M needs about 50 units, not 35.

**Who signs.** A developer VP Power or CFO, or a utility chief engineer or supply-chain VP once an electric service agreement is signed. The fleet's marginal customer is the one who cannot get a direct OEM slot, which means mid-size developers and neoclouds. Only about 12% of requested data-center load is firm and 17% likely (via Bloomberg; the shares sum to 29% against a stated 28%): https://capacityglobal.com/news/wood-mackenzie-us-grid-operators-set-to-serve-28pc-of-data/. Inference: buyers asking for an unsigned-project reservation skew toward the speculative 72%, and the credit-worthy 12% already hold OEM frames.

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

**For:**
- Developers "pay significant premiums or buy production slots well in advance, sometimes before a specific project exists" (Burns & McDonnell in Reuters): https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots
- Roseville moved from buying one year ahead to three, and now buys for projects five years out. Some buyers prepay for earlier slots: https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-07-09/us-power-companies-scramble-secure-equipment-surging-data-center
- A Utah city's order is "two years out" on 30/30/40 payment milestones (order, drawing approval, shipment): https://sanpetemessenger.com/mt-pleasant-approves-major-transformer-purchase-delays-a-fire-billing-ordinance/
- A Michigan city chose Virginia Transformer ($1.32M, up to 1.5 years) over Niagara ($1.28M, 3.5-5 years), paying 3.4% more to save 2-3.5 years: https://transformer-technology.com/harbor-springs-advances-6-million-electrical-substation-project-with-transformer-purchase-transformer-technology-news/

**Against:**
- No RFP, tender or contract for a leased or stocked 30-100 MVA fleet turned up. The nearest are the co-op pool above and sub-50 MVA rentals.
- The only sourced markup for speed is Bolt's 12-15% over factory cost for 12-14 months, not 8-16 weeks.
- Wesco's CEO calls power availability the main constraint (https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/03/wesco-wcc-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/). Its slide shows "3-5 Years Time to Power" against a 1-2 year build: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000929008/000092900826000012/wcc-1q2026slides.htm. A stocked transformer does not shorten a 3-5 year interconnection.
- Rules now penalize speculation. AEP Ohio requires 85% of subscribed demand for up to 12 years plus exit fees: https://www.powermag.com/regulator-approves-aep-ohios-landmark-data-center-tariff/. Virginia moves to 14-year contracts and collateral in January 2027: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/715957/000119312526115192/dei-ars-12312025.pdf. Texas SB 6 requires site control and duplicate-request disclosure: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/efforts-to-curb-data-center-speculation-gain-ground-across-the-us/830495/. Inference: this removes the weakest demand and leaves buyers who can order direct.

## 5. Unit economics and capital

**Sale route.** 35-50 units for about $100M at a 12-15% markup is about $11-14M gross profit (my arithmetic on Bolt's markup). That is a dealer's spread, before carrying cost.

**Lease route.** Willis Lease reports a lease-rate factor of about 1.04% a month, roughly 12.5% a year, on aircraft engines: https://allmind.ai/earnings/event/bt_WLFC_2025_Q3. That matches the idea's 12-15%, but engines have deep lessee pools and global resale. No transformer lease comp exists; FluxCo and Sunbelt Solomon do not publish rates.

My illustration (assumptions: 280 units at $2.5M = $700M; 70% debt at 7.5%; 2.5% depreciation on DOE's 40-year life; 2% storage, insurance and logistics):

| Utilization | Revenue at 12.5% | Costs | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70% | $61M | $68M | negative |
| 100% | $88M | $68M | $20M |

The $20M roughly equals a 10% return on the $210M of equity. Break-even is about 78% utilization, and earning a 10% equity return needs about 100%.

**Capital.** Startups in this niche have raised tens of millions (Ayr $25M, FluxCo $26M), against $0.6-0.85B for the fleet. OEMs allocate slots on forecast certainty, and the DOE report says plant build-out takes 1-3 years, so a new buyer without frames queues behind hyperscalers. Buyers normally fund the OEM in stages (the 30/30/40 terms above); a fleet must front all of it. The workable form is a floorplan-financed dealer or lessor, which is what JCL, Maddox and Sunbelt Solomon already are. Sunbelt Solomon (a 2019 merger plus add-ons, now a private-credit-financed PE sale) shows the route exists but is a multi-year roll-up: https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/zqSlxv

## 6. Legal, regulatory and liability

- **EO 14420 (Aug 26, 2026).** Reaches foreign-produced bulk-power equipment of 69 kV and up from "covered foreign entities" (ITAR 126.1-type countries; China is the main supplier). DOE rules are due in 120 days (about Dec 24), and installed equipment can be isolated or removed: https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/powerandpipes/2026/08/bulk-power-system-executive-order-adds-a-new-grid-supply-chain-compliance-layer. Korea, India and Mexico are not named, so allied-origin sourcing is lawful. The risk is sub-tier provenance on speculative stock (Chinese bushings or controllers in a Korean unit) if DOE tightens definitions. DOE's comment window closed Oct 9 (per the gate: https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/doe-rfi-bulk-power-system-eo.html). Whether "bulk power" reaches data-center loads is unclear: https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/us-executive-order-banning-chinese-bulk-power-equipment-set-to-worsen-critical-transformer-shortage/. The 2020 predecessor order was suspended and never reinstated.
- **Tariffs.** A 50% duty on steel and aluminum content of transformers since Aug 2025 (https://tedmag.com/trump-administration-increases-tariffs-on-certain-transformers/). I could not confirm the April 2026 restructuring. Inventory is exposed to repricing in either direction.
- **Efficiency standards.** DOE's 2024 rule covers distribution transformers and is under reconsideration: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-04-22/html/2024-07480.htm. I found no federal efficiency standard for the 30-100 MVA class; verify the scope boundary.
- **Spec acceptance (the real legal-technical risk).** DOE: impedance is "a particularly difficult parameter to standardize," and large units are "very site-specific." Flexible designs cost more and one ran about 12% heavier. ABB's modular approach is "relatively high-cost": the DOE report above. DHS/EPRI's flexible spare replaces over 90% of 345/138 kV units, but voltage and MVA must match, impedance must be close, and some transmission owners vetoed a common design: https://dhs.gov/sites/default/files/publications/RecX%20-%20Emergency%20Spare%20Transformer%20Strategy-508.pdf. One utility's flexible spare is expected to last 5-10 years, not 40. Each (high side, low side, MVA, impedance, vector group) combination is a separate SKU, so "multi-ratio" does not collapse the fleet to a few designs.
- **Procurement law.** Public utilities bid competitively (the two Michigan bids above), so selling to them from a fleet needs a qualifying bid.
- **Liability.** The fleet seller inherits warranty and early-failure risk (HSB: design and manufacturing issues are the second-leading failure cause, often at energization, per the Maddox article). Idle oil-filled units need storage maintenance per DOE. Whether a stored spare is "operational equipment" under EPA SPCC rules is unconfirmed: https://www.epa.gov/node/222171. Hoarding optics: the DPA determination calls capacity "dangerously limited"; I found no 2026 use of DPA anti-hoarding or priority-rating authority on grid equipment: https://www.enr.com/articles/62887-trump-taps-defense-production-act-to-address-grid-equipment-energy-project-bottlenecks

## 7. Is the why-now real?

**Real:**
- Power-transformer lead times of 128 weeks and GSUs of 144 weeks (Wood Mackenzie Q2 2025): https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/. GSU lead times exceeded 160 weeks by Q1 2026 (Reuters link above).
- 2025 deficits of 30% (power) and 10% (distribution): https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/08/15/us-transformer-supply-may-fall-sharply-in-2025-says-wood-mackenzie/
- In 2025 Mexico, China, South Korea and Europe supplied 76% of US imports of 10-100 MVA units, so a China cut-off bites in this class (Rystad link above). Goldman data say China supplies only about 4% of US demand, which conflicts with Wood Mackenzie's "virtually all bulk-power imports": https://greentechlead.com/power/us-ban-on-chinese-power-equipment-threatens-22-billion-import-market-as-transformer-shortage-deepens-55085

**Used up or fading:**
- Wood Mackenzie's 2026 shortage estimate is 15% for power transformers (30% in 2025) and 8% for substations (woodmac link above).
- Rystad (June 2026): growth in prices and lead times has "largely stalled," peak lead times could ease within 12 months, and nearly 200 GVA of capacity arrives in 2026, with the US taking 35-40% of additions through 2028 (powerpeakdigest link).
- New US plants: Siemens Charlotte early 2027, Eaton 2027, Hitachi Virginia 2028 (Power link above).
- Correlated downside: in 2025 Microsoft reportedly walked away from about 2 GW of projects (TD Cowen): https://w.media/microsoft-cancels-2gw-data-center-projects-in-us-and-europe/. The idea's "portfolio effect" fails when every lessee's demand falls together.

Inference: the window for a fleet is roughly 2027-29, and slots ordered today deliver at its end.

## 8. Upside-case assumptions tested

| Assumption | Finding |
|---|---|
| 35 units at $3M = $105M | $3M is a 100 MVA price. The class midpoint is nearer $2M, so about 50 units. Gross profit about $11-14M. |
| Lease at 12-15% of cost | Plausible by aircraft analogy (12.5%). No transformer comp. Needs about 100% utilization to earn its equity cost. |
| "About 80% of substation units are the same few designs" | Unsourced. DOE says site-specific with impedance the hardest parameter. Only intra-utility convergence (Dominion 84 MVA). |
| 280 data-center units a year | Rests on 24 to 110 GW by 2030. If only the 28% firm/likely share applies, it is about 80 (inference). Hyperscale buys direct, and at least one hyperscaler uses 120-150 MVA units. |
| 8-16 week delivery | Beyond any sourced made-to-order claim (Giga 26 weeks; Ayr 40-72 weeks). Equals stock sales. |
| Fleet turnover of 100-140 units a year is 15-25% of flow | No sourced denominator. On my 800-1,600 estimate it is 6-18% of flow (inference), so share is less heroic than claimed, but only if that sizing holds. |
| EO makes allied-origin stock scarce | Real but modest. Allied supply is expanding fast. |

## 9. Verdict and what would change it

The only version that clears $100M revenue is a balance-sheet dealer/lessor roll-up. The lessons file accepts a balance sheet as a scalable asset, but the spread here is thin and cyclical (about 10-13% gross on resale; roughly cost of capital on lease). Narrowing to one voltage class would drop it under $25M, which the rules forbid as a rescue. **Call it dead as a startup, weak as a financing play.**

It revives only if all hold: (a) three utilities sign off one multi-ratio spec in writing; (b) an OEM commits slots at a fixed price with a buy-back or resale floor; (c) a lender prices the fleet above 70% loan-to-value; (d) DOE's December rules or a federal purchase commitment make stocked allied-origin units a mandated category.

**Gaps.** No sourced US count of 10-100 MVA units. No transformer lease-rate comp. No Sunbelt Solomon revenue. Wood Mackenzie primary reports were unavailable (press and secondary only). Dominion's per-MW collateral is unconfirmed.

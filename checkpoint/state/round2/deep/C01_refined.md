# C01 refined: "Capped Outage" (P036, idea 5, restructured after three fatal critiques)

Date: 2026-10-18. Tags: [S] sourced, [V] verified by me this pass, [D] derived arithmetic, [A] assumption, [C] carried from the critics or dossier, not re-verified.

## 1. Verdict

**Alive, but only just: roughly a 3% chance of reaching $100M revenue, and it should be killed at the first gate if the demand test fails.** The original (a 10-year condition-priced independent LTSA with a reinsurer parts-cost cap) is dead. All three critiques land on it: Hanwha can copy a pricing clause, a no-reference entrant cannot qualify for scored RFPs or 100% bonds, the contestable roll-off flow is tiny, and the parts-cost wrap is probably uninsurable.

What survives is a different product aimed at a different buyer: a neutral prime that sells non-LTSA owners a **fixed-price, growth-banded, date-guaranteed, surety-bonded outage**, one event at a time, priced on measured condition and performed by third-party shops. It keeps a path to $100M of revenue (about 20 outage packages a year at about $5M, roughly 7-10% of the wedge) without shrinking to a boutique. Two things stay uncomfortable and are stated plainly: revenue is mostly pass-through (gross margin 10-14%, so $100M of revenue is about $10-14M of gross profit and perhaps $4-7M EBITDA), and the moat only arrives in year 4-6.

## 2. What the business is now

- **One-liner.** A neutral prime that sells owners with no OEM LTSA a fixed price near the incumbent's base-scope quote, with as-found scope growth up to a set band included, a hard return-to-service date with liquidated damages, and a bonded performance guarantee. Price is set from borescope, NDE, hours, starts and trips; units that open better than forecast get a rebate.
- **Customer (wedge).** Owners of 1-6 older-design F-class (7FA.03, 7FA+e, 501F) or 7EA units with an outage in fall 2027-2028 who buy OEM-transactionally today: co-ops and other buyers with negotiating room, non-platform IPPs and fund-owned plants, industrial cogens. Excluded in phase 1: the scale fleets (NRG, Vistra, Constellation, Talen, FPL, Southern) and statutory scored-RFP buyers.
- **Signer.** VP Generation or plant manager for the event; CFO or treasurer co-signs because a fixed-price counterparty needs a credit check; co-op boards above their thresholds. Budget line: major-outage O&M or maintenance reserve. The earlier claim that the lender's independent engineer is the real buyer had no source and is dropped.
- **Performance.** Named qualified shops (repair houses and ISPs) under frame agreements with reserved slots. No owned hardware at first.
- **Door.** Not a free audit (a commodity, and a procurement conflict if it shapes a spec). The door is a free written fixed-price bid in 5 business days from the owner's last outage report and OEM quote. A bid is a bid, not advice to the owner.
- **Later.** Convert the best accounts to 3-5 year programs ($/EOH with annual true-up), bid scored RFPs as a teamed prime once references exist, and add Canada, Mexico and the Gulf.

## 4. Market, bottom-up

- US F-class units: 900-1,100 [A, dossier].
- Less scale and in-house fleets (40-50%): 450-660.
- Less units under long OEM LTSAs (26%, GE rate applied to all OEMs [S, A]): 330-490.
- Less units under 10 years old or mid-term on a 7+ year ISP deal (about 25% [A]): 250-370.
- Less statutory scored-RFP buyers (about 20% [A]): **200-300 units, central 250.**
- Outage wallet per unit-year $3-4M (NYPA 2007 independent deal implies about $2.75M nominal per unit-year; EIA cross-check about $3.8M [C]); I use $3M for transactional owners. **US wedge wallet about $600-900M a year, central $750M.**
- Add Canada, Mexico, UK, Gulf (about +30%) and teamed public bodies in phase 2 (about +20%) [A]: about $1.1B.
- $100M is about 13% of the US-only wedge, about 9% of the full $1.1B. In package terms, about 20 packages a year against a wedge flow of roughly 200 packages a year. That is an average-sized competitor's share (about eight competitors). It is not heroic, but it is not small.

## 5. Unit economics (one $5M package, OEM-transactional owner)

- Owner's OEM base-scope quote R; the OEM would add about 12% growth by change order [A, to be measured].
- Our price about 1.00R-1.02R with growth up to 20% of base scope included. The owner's expected saving is about 10-12% versus the expected OEM total, plus the days.
- Cost: shops at about 0.77R on base (non-OEM parts about 30% below list [C]), growth reserve about 0.09R, bond, tail and stop-loss about 0.04R. Total about 0.90R. **Gross margin about 10-12% at par pricing.**
- If the owner's alternative is an ISP at 0.87R, margin is about 2-5%. We do not chase those.
- At $100M: gross profit $10-14M, opex $8-10M, EBITDA $4-7M. The profit prize is far smaller than the revenue prize.

## 6. Median and upside

**Median, if the gates pass (not counting the likely outcome of dying at a gate):** about $30M revenue in year 7: roughly 6 packages a year, break-even to a small loss, a subscale company most likely sold to an ISP at $20-40M.

**Upside, about $110M in year 8-9.** Assumptions:
1. First package in year 2; packages per year 2, 5, 9, 13, 16, 19, 22 over years 2-8.
2. Mix: US events about $63M, US 3-5 year programs about $26M, teamed public-body awards about $7M, Canada, Mexico and other about $12M.
3. A sponsor closes $15-25M by month 18, growing to $40-60M, plus a surety line of about $60M in force.
4. Frame agreements keep rotor and casting slots through the squeeze; a $20-30M owned spare-set pool is added in phase 2.
5. Realized growth within band averages 9% of price or less; gross margin holds at 12%.
6. Hanwha and Allied take at least three years to match the cap, or match it at lower scale.
7. Win rate about 1 in 4 on 80+ bids a year by year 6.
8. 40% of accounts convert to programs by year 5.

**Probability of reaching the upside: about 3%** (about 40% to pass the demand gate, then about 40% to clear capital, surety and supply, then about 20% to scale).

## 7. Moat, honestly

- **Reachable.** Scale economies (risk-loading per dollar falls from 5 to 25 packages; frame-agreement pricing improves with committed volume), reached at about 15-20 packages a year. Cornered resource (a forecast-versus-as-found dataset across shops and OEMs that no single ISP sees), valuable after about 50 packages, so year 5-6.
- **Early, fragile.** Counter-positioning against time-and-materials incumbents who earn margin on change orders. This is logic, not evidence.
- **Not available.** Switching costs on single events are low. Programs raise them modestly.
- Years 1-4 have no moat. The company wins on speed, neutrality and a balance sheet.

## 8. Competitors and edge

- **Hanwha Power (PSM + Hanwha Power Systems):** LTA/LTMA/Frame tiers, monitoring, Hanwha Group balance sheet. The most dangerous copier. Probably the incumbent at Austin Sand Hill Unit 5: the 2011 agenda shows Power Systems Mfg held that contract, cap $22.9M, later raised about $9M [V, search summary of the agenda]. So the 2026 win is probably a renewal.
- **Allied (Warren), MD&A, Ethos (OEP), Wood, Sulzer, Chromalloy:** fixed-scope turnkey outages with schedule claims already exist (MD&A 19-day 7FA hot gas path, sponsored content [V]). Their gap is scope-growth risk on thin PE balance sheets.
- **OEMs:** transactional pricing up, no cap or date guarantee outside an LTSA.
- **Advisers (Troutman, IEM, Mercer Thompson):** advise, take no risk. Possible referral channel.
- **Parametric forced-outage cover (Aon):** owners can buy availability cover directly. It is not an outage-cost cap.
- **O&M primes:** NAES (ITOCHU, about 200 plants, cost-plus-fee, 3-20 year terms [V, search summary]) holds owner relationships, references and bonding but takes no maintenance price risk. A channel hypothesis, untested.

## 9. Risk capital and insurance design

- **Insured:** resulting damage and delay after damage on our work (standard contractor and equipment-breakdown lines).
- **Bonded:** performance at 100% of the package, back-to-back with the shop's bond and a sponsor indemnity. The NY DFS opinion expressly allows an authorized surety to guarantee performance of a maintenance agreement [V].
- **Retained:** as-found growth within band, diversified over the book. At 20 packages a year, book growth SD is about $3.4M [A]; 3-sigma plus a correlated parts-inflation shock is about $15M; with margin, retained-risk capital is $20-25M.
- **Optional upside:** book-level stop-loss or service-contract reimbursement cover. The NY opinion contrasts maintenance agreements with service-contract reimbursement insurance, which may indemnify a provider [V], but whether commercial equipment events fit the state statutes is unverified.
- **Total capital at upside:** retained risk $20-25M, bond collateral $6-12M, parts deposits and working capital about $12M, roughly $40-60M, plus $20-30M if a spare-set pool is financed.

## 10. Sequencing and gates

- **G1 (weeks 1-2): demand.** The test in section 11.
- **G2 (month 3): wedge list.** EIA-860 and OEM install data confirm at least 200 qualifying units.
- **G3 (months 3-9): supply.** Frame agreements with at least 2 non-prime shops, with 2028 slot reservations.
- **G4 (months 3-9): insurance.** Written indications from at least 2 markets on the insured slice, plus a counsel memo on classification. Stop-loss is a bonus.
- **G5 (months 6-12): credit.** A sponsor or guarantor commits at least $15M, and a surety indicates $10M per event.
- **G6 (months 9-18): references.** Two packages awarded for fall 2027 or spring 2028. Sponsor close is conditioned on them.

First revenue realistically spring 2028, not earlier.

## 11. Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test

**Riskiest assumption:** owners at non-LTSA F-class units bear material, volatile as-found cost growth and schedule slip today, which a fixed-price contractor is not already absorbing. They would pay roughly half of it to cap it, and would award to a newcomer teamed with a named, bonded shop.

**Test (14 days, about $0):**
1. 25 calls, at least 15 with non-LTSA, non-scale-fleet F-class owners. Sourcing: 7F Users Group attendee lists, APPA and NRECA generation contacts, plant managers on LinkedIn, and referrals from two LTSA consultants.
2. For each owner's last 2-3 outages: awarded price versus final cost, days late, contract form, who bore the growth, and who earned margin on it.
3. Show a one-page term sheet (price near the OEM base quote, 20% growth band, LDs, bond, named shop). Ask: would you take a bid; what premium for a hard cap; would you send your last two outage reports and an OEM quote for a written fixed-price bid in 5 days; can you export hours, starts and trips.
4. Desk pull of at least 15 council or board change-order and amendment records for F-class maintenance and outage work, to measure award-to-final variance.
5. Stretch: obtain one real RFQ and an indicative price from one non-prime shop.

**Pass:** median award-to-final growth at least 10% on at least 60% of sampled outages; at least 8 of 25 would take a bid from a teamed newcomer; at least 5 would share records for a bid.
**Kill:** median growth below 6%, or 70% or more say their ISP already absorbs growth under fixed price, or fewer than 3 would consider a newcomer.

Stated-preference calls are weak evidence. Only a signed event is strong evidence. The test only decides whether to spend the next six months.

## 12. Pre-mortem (it is 2029 and this is dead)

1. **The product already exists.** ISPs absorb growth in fixed-price deals, or Hanwha matches the cap, and margin falls below 8%. Fixable now: the calls reveal this.
2. **Adverse selection and a bad early loss.** Caps attract the worst units. Casting inflation or a rotor failure on package 3 wipes retained capital. Partly fixable: pre-quote borescope, bands, deductible, retained capital sized before event 1.
3. **Allocation in scarcity.** In a casting squeeze, ISPs serve their own contract customers first and subcontracting primes last. Partly fixable: slot deposits, non-prime repair houses, a financed spare pool. This may be the hardest risk to remove.
4. **No sponsor.** The business has no standalone path to bonds. Not fixable by the founder alone.

# C07 dossier: "Day 21" (P108, idea 7), multi-state operator of full-service forensic restoration beds

Date: 2026-10-18. About 55 searches and fetches, including the 117-page Ward v. Muth opinion, a live HHSC bed contract, and Colorado's April 2026 budget memo. Tags: [S] sourced, [D] my arithmetic on sourced inputs, [A] assumption or judgment. Where a page would not load and I used a search-result summary, I say so.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: wounded, close to dead on the $100M test. Overall 4/10, upside 4/10.** This is the best-evidenced finalist in the pool. A federal injunction exists, a named agency owns the budget line, and a signed HHSC contract for a close variant of this product (16 geriatric forensic restoration beds) is public. But the idea fails lessons.md rule "big AND defensible" on four counts:

1. **The $100M path needs about 410 beds at the real Texas price, not 600 at $450.** That is roughly the size of the largest incumbent's entire Florida forensic footprint (446 beds, built since 2005-07). Realistic uncontested Texas demand is 150-400 beds, and Texas is already building or granting most of the rest.
2. **The order does not require Texas to buy anything.** HHSC says it is not ordered to fund a project or contract, the state is appealing, the waitlist has already fallen 40% from its peak, and HHSC did not ask for new bed funding in the 2026-27 session.
3. **The idea's price and speed assumptions are wrong in both directions.** $450 a day matches neither the jail-based product ($261-421) nor the hospital product ($697-1,050). "6-12 months, modular" collides with a hospital-licence-plus-Joint-Commission requirement.
4. **Incumbents are deep and the state is financing county-built shells that incumbents then manage.** Wellpath Recovery Solutions runs the Montgomery County model (county builds with a state grant, operator manages as "junior partner"). The thin moat the gate rated is thinner.

## 3. Bottom-up market

- **Texas stock and trend.** Waitlist 1,628 at end of June 2026; Sept-June monthly average 1,669, down 19% from 2,058 a year earlier ([San Antonio Report](https://sanantonioreport.org/texas-court-order-competency-wait-21-days-bexar-county/)). The peak was 2,711 in December 2022 ([State Auditor data](https://sao.texas.gov/Reports/DataSupplement/25-007Interactive.html)), so the list is down 40% [D]. HHSC reports 2,110 operational forensic beds against 2,299 funded; projects since 2017 add more than 680 state-owned beds; grants may add up to 332 more ([TPR](https://www.tpr.org/criminal-justice/2026-08-23/bexar-county-defendants-wait-months-in-jail-for-competency-treatment-judge-says-texas-is-violating-their-rights)).
- **Texas flow.** Demand grew 100-300 commitments a year recently, implying 1,000-4,000 more orders over four years than the last four ([opinion](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382.266.0.pdf), fn. 29). My read: the recurring contractable gap is the 315-bed shortfall plus growth, perhaps 150-400 beds by 2030 [A].
- **National.** 32 of 40 reporting states have adult waitlists, but the mean is 137 people and the median 48 across 24 states; 14 of 39 states have been sued ([NRI, June 2026](https://nri-inc.org/media/df0dzlmu/nri-2024-2025-state-profiles-report-competency-restoration_june2026.pdf)). The big stocks are Texas (1,628), Georgia (697 jailed awaiting restoration, 187-day average) and Colorado. Georgia is building its own: $409M for a state hospital plus $20.7M for a 40-bed jail unit ([DBHDD](https://dbhdd.georgia.gov/press-release/2026-04-06/statement-agreement-between-dbhdd-and-gaoschr-regarding-forensic-0)). California spent $2B since 2021 and cut its list from 2,000 to under 300 ([Colorado memo](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/HUM%20BA8%20Memo%2004-22-26.pdf)). Oklahoma's Forensic Center wait fell from 214 to 79 days between June and September 2025 ([Public Radio Tulsa](https://www.publicradiotulsa.org/local-regional/2025-11-20/oklahoma-agency-fined-over-competency-restoration-settlement)).
- **Colorado is the cleanest price-and-volume read.** Contract-bed spending rose from $3.4M (FY23) to $31.9M (FY27), +836%, while inpatient restoration orders rose 126% (750 to 1,696). Beds cut the list, then orders refilled it. The Special Master's report says neither jail-based nor contracted beds "should ever be the primary strategy"; Colorado's own staff recommended funding fewer contract beds than requested (16 of 27) ([memo](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/HUM%20BA8%20Memo%2004-22-26.pdf)).
- **What $100M takes.** At 95% occupancy: about 640 beds at $450, 414 at $697, 275 at $1,050 [D]. A 414-bed operator is about the size of Wellpath's Florida forensic estate (238 + 208 = 446 beds, [TAC](https://www.tac.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Florida-State-Hospital-System-2023.pdf)). Wellpath Recovery Solutions' whole business is $425M of revenue and about 3,000 patients after 20+ years ([BHB](https://bhbusiness.com/2024/12/06/wellpaths-425m-behavioral-health-division-on-the-market-following-bankruptcy-announcement/)). Reaching $100M in 6-7 years means roughly a quarter of the incumbent's scale. Under lessons.md that is "heroic share".

- **Cost side.** A Texas private psychiatric operator put full cost at $700-900 a day against a $529 Medicaid rate and said "we're 90% percent full and we're losing money" ([Texas Tribune, 2024](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/09/19/texas-medicaid-psychiatric-hospitals)). A $697 forensic contract therefore sits at the low end of cost, so margin is thin unless the operator is lean. I assume 8-12% EBITDA at 95% occupancy [A]. $100M of revenue would be $8-12M of EBITDA.
- **Valuation signal.** Wellpath's $425M-revenue behavioral division drew about 70 NDAs, one lower second-round bid and no qualifying outside bids. Lenders took it by $395M credit bid, 0.93x revenue [D] ([BHB](https://bhbusiness.com/2025/01/09/wellpath-gets-court-approval-to-spin-off-behavioral-health-assets/)). Markets have already passed twice on scaled forensic operators.
- **Product mismatch.** At $450 the idea is priced like a jail unit but specified like a hospital. Jail units are cheap (county supplies the bed) but restore poorly: 10 of 19 Texas programs restored half or fewer, three under 20% ([opinion](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382.266.0.pdf), ¶123).

## 6. Demand evidence, payer and signer

- **Budget line and signer: real.** Beds are funded by HHSC; the ramp-up plan must be approved by the HHSC Associate Commissioner for State Hospitals ([contract](https://contracts.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/HHS001336800001-contract.pdf), s. 3.1.1); Deputy Executive Commissioner Schalchlin testified on capacity. Colorado's line is General Fund only.
- **Pro-contracting evidence.** An expert testified that "contracting for beds is typically faster than building new capacity"; HHSC's state construction often runs four to seven years; state hospitals lose beds to staffing while contracted ones do not ([opinion](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382.266.0.pdf), fn. 28, ¶95, ¶134).
- **Anti-contracting evidence.** HHSC "retains discretion" and is not ordered to fund a specific contract ([TPR](https://www.tpr.org/criminal-justice/2026-08-23/bexar-county-defendants-wait-months-in-jail-for-competency-treatment-judge-says-texas-is-violating-their-rights)). HHSC leadership did not request added inpatient funding before the 2026-27 session (¶116). I found no open HHSC solicitation for inpatient restoration beds. Colorado's decree "encourages a limited role" for private beds.
- **No RFP, no spend history beyond small contracts.** The only posted private forensic contract I could read is 16 beds.

## 7. Capital and speed

- State builds: $573M/250 beds, $452M/200, $159M/75, $121M/50 max-security, i.e. $2.1-2.4M per bed [D]; blended $3.7M ($2.5B/680) [D]. Colorado estimates $95M for 50 beds ($1.9M/bed) ([search summary](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/HB%2026-1301%20Analysis.pdf)); Georgia's jail unit is $518k/bed [D].
- Grant-financed private beds carry $500-890k/bed of state money [D]. Owning 414 beds would need $0.2-1.0B. A start-up must be asset-light, which means being a manager on someone else's building: exactly what Wellpath already is in Montgomery.
- Staffing is the real constraint: Texas direct-care turnover 33% and 292 of 2,770 funded beds offline ([opinion](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382.266.0.pdf)).

## 8. Regulatory and liability

- Texas permits inpatient restoration only at HHSC-operated or HHSC-contracted facilities. The contract requires a licensed general or special hospital with Joint Commission or CMS-deemed accreditation (s. 3.1.3). Modular "license-ready" jail units cannot sell this product.
- SB 528 (Health & Safety Code ch. 580, in force since 9/2025) requires MOUs with the county, municipality and local mental health authority, and a cost comparison to state hospitals, first due Aug 1, 2027 per the enrolled text ([statute](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/HS/htm/HS.580.htm), search summary). That first report is the public price-discovery event; it also hands HHSC a benchmark to squeeze rates. SB 1677's rural-center contracting authority names nonprofits and governmental entities only ([bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/analysis/html/SB01677F.htm)).
- Liability: forensic patients are the highest-risk cohort. A December 2025 wrongful-death suit against a Florida forensic contractor (two murders after placement) shows tail risk ([BHB](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/02/23/what-a-wrongful-death-lawsuit-reveals-about-gaps-in-forensic-patient-care/)). Wellpath's custody-health collapse is the cautionary precedent. I did not check certificate-of-need rules or Medicaid IMD treatment of forensic beds.

## 9. Why now

The ruling is real (permanent injunction July 7, 2026; 21 days; four years; benchmarks within 45 days, schedule within 60) and the PLRA exhaustion defense failed ([opinion](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382/gov.uscourts.txwd.830382.266.0.pdf), [PLN](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2026/oct/1/federal-court-orders-texas-to-admit-incompetent-jailed-defendants-to-mental-health-facilities-within-21-days/)). But the shock is a four-year glide path on a shrinking list, under appeal. The natural catalyst is the 2027 Legislature setting 2028-29 funding, which I could not preview.

# C11 refined: Outreach Partner Labs, rebuilt after three fatal critiques

Written 2026-10-18. Tags: (S) sourced, (A) my arithmetic or assumption, (U) unverified. Inputs: P062 idea, gate report, C11 dossier, three critiques, lessons.md, strategy-frameworks.md. I re-checked three facts myself (marked "re-checked").

## 1. Verdict

**Alive, low conviction. Roughly a 5% chance (range 2-8%) of reaching $100M+ revenue (A), about 45% chance of failing to sign a second client.**

The original design is dead. Operator-controlled 51% LLC, percent-of-revenue fee, operator-run commission sales, selling to hospitals that want cash, and $120M raised up front all fail on legal, capital or buyer-alternative grounds. The critics are right about all of them. I am not defending any of those.

What survives is a different business: an **open-book operating company for health systems that want to keep their outreach lab**. It is capital-light at the start, keeps the hospital as legal owner of everything that bills, and reaches $100M only through three stacked layers (managed operations, an opt-in hub, one sponsor-funded performing-lab JV). The honest cost of the fixes: revenue is mostly pass-through service revenue at roughly 8% EBITDA, not equity-consolidated test revenue at a venture multiple. At $110M revenue the company is worth roughly $80-130M (A), not a unicorn. It clears this round's revenue bar and nothing more.

This is not a boutique. The $2-6M "advisory" outcome is the bear case, not the plan. But the upside needs about 9% of reachable systems to sign and renew with a startup, and I found no US precedent of that (a non-national operator renewing past year 3). Say that plainly to anyone who funds this.

## 2. What changed (one line each)

| Piece | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Any system, including sellers | **Stayers only**: systems with $15-50M outreach that are not selling. Sellers take cash; I stop competing with cash. |
| Legal owner of the billing entity | Operator holds 50-51% | **Hospital holds 100%** (Frisbie template). Operator is a vendor. |
| Pay | 6-10% of revenue | **Open-book cost plus 10-12%, plus a flat platform fee**, per-claim flat billing fee, no percent of anything |
| Sales | Operator sales force paid on revenue | **Hospital-employed, salaried, no volume pay.** Operator sells enablement (CRM, territory data) at a flat fee. |
| First product | Free contribution model, then a JV pitch | **Network and Margin Audit, then Entity Stand-Up** (a mandate-driven project), then managed operations |
| Capital | $50-120M up front | Staged: seed/A for phases 0-1, then project money per phase (section 6) |
| Revenue basis | Consolidated JV revenue | Service revenue (gross of pass-through), then hub revenue, then one JV |
| Moat | Control pitch | Counter-positioning on neutrality, route and billing density per metro, cross-system data |

## 3. Answers to the critiques

### Critique 1: incumbents already sell it, and Sonic, Elevance, OIG, regional rivals

- **Quest and Labcorp offer "keep control" and can outbid.** Agreed for sellers, so I drop sellers. The thing they cannot offer is neutrality. Their management deals exist to move volume into their hubs (Kootenai got a 5-year noncompete after PAML was sold; UMass volume reportedly fell 10-20% after Quest's announcement, secondhand (U)). The contract I would sign gives the opposite: hospital owns its TIN, payer contracts and data; no noncompete beyond the term; operator earns nothing from where a test is run; reference lab chosen by the hospital through open RFP (ARUP, Mayo, Quest, Labcorp all allowed). This is counter-positioning (7 Powers). It is real only for the share of stayers who care more about neutrality than about the nationals' scale. I put that at 10-20% (A).
- **Epic Aura weakens the connectivity wedge.** Agreed. Connectivity is not the wedge any more.
- **Sonic's US returns.** I cannot disprove that Sonic's weak US returns come from hospital JVs; I can only avoid the part that hurts. Sonic carried payer concentration and performing-lab capital. My phases 0-1 hold neither.
- **Elevance site-of-care.** Re-checked: Healthcare Dive confirms the policy covers lab tests, commercial/MA/Medicaid, "throughout 2026 and 2027", but does not say how on-campus hospital-lab tests are treated (S). So I do not claim the policy forces conversions. It is one of three pressures (with UHC's preferred tier and PAMA) and week one of the test is to read Elevance's own notice and drop it from the pitch if on-campus outreach is unaffected.
- **UHC.** Re-checked via the UHC FAQ in search results: hospital labs are not excluded from UHC's network, only absent from the Preferred Lab Network tier (effective July 1, 2026). To get in, a hospital lab must contract as a freestanding lab and meet proprietary criteria including more than CLIA certification, online capability and data security (S, search summary). That is a concrete job: build a freestanding-lab entity that meets those criteria. I have not seen the criteria.
- **OIG contractual-JV bulletin.** The bulletin's red flags are an owner who neither operates nor commits substantial resources, pay tied to referrals, and a would-be competitor. Phase 1 has no operator equity, so there is no contractual JV. Phase 3 (a JV) is allowed only where the operator runs the lab and commits real capital, which is the opposite of the flagged pattern. Both still need a written counsel opinion before the first CFO meeting.
- **Regional substitutes (Orion/FMOL, ARUP + ELLKAY, Mayo Outreach Insights, Chi/Accumen, TriCore, Atalan).** Accepted: each metro has one. Mayo, ARUP and Chi advise; the hospital still runs it. Orion and TriCore operate. My edge is therefore narrower than "nobody does this": I win only where there is no strong regional operator, on SLAs and neutrality. I do not enter Louisiana.
- **Physician base moving to employed groups (82%, S secondary).** Turn it round: employed-physician volume is captive to the system. The job for a stayer is stopping leakage of its own physicians' orders to Quest/Labcorp through payer steering, which is a routing, payer-status and logistics problem, not a selling problem. That also removes most of the EKRA exposure.

### Critique 2: no workable price, cash alternative, EKRA, funnel, no moat

- **No price works for both.** Agreed for 6-10% of net revenue. New price: the operator's actual cost to deliver a defined scope, audited and open-book, plus 10-12%, plus a flat platform fee of $250-400k a year, plus a flat per-claim fee for billing. The hospital's side (A): commercial-function cost today of 15-18% of outreach revenue (billing 5-8% from a vendor blog (S, weak); couriers, PSCs, client services, IT are my estimates; **no published benchmark exists**, re-checked by search). On a $25M program that is $3.75-4.5M. Operator price is at most 92% of the baseline cost per requisition at month 18, or the markup is reduced to cover the gap. The hospital keeps 100% of collection gains, recaptured leakage and avoided network losses. A 1.5-point gain in net collections on $25M pays back the whole fee (A).
- **PAML/Kootenai.** The failed pattern was a 50/50 revenue split with an operator that was later bought and re-papered with a noncompete. Mine has no revenue share, a hospital-owned TIN, no noncompete and portability of data and payer contracts. The risk that remains is that a successful operator gets bought by a national; I would resist this by contract (change-of-control clause that lets the hospital exit).
- **Cash is the alternative; the shock does not move sellers.** Agreed. Evidence: PAMA cuts of about 10% a year in 2018-20 did not raise deal volume. I no longer sell to sellers.
- **EKRA, 9/25/2026.** Re-checked via the Mintz summary: DOJ denied ACLA's petition and treats volume-linked pay as inherently suspect, even for employees. The article does not say whether flat salary or non-volume bonuses are safe, so I treat that as counsel-dependent, not settled. Design response: nobody in my structure is paid per test, per dollar or per referral. Reps are hospital-employed on salary; operator fees are cost plus flat. The growth lever is employed-physician routing and network status, not commission selling. The cost is that I cannot promise 30%+ growth, and I do not.
- **Funnel and capital.** At 9% of $13M the original needed 87 systems. Gross service revenue per system (billing, couriers, PSCs, client services, data) is about $4M (A), so $60M needs about 15 systems. Phase 0-1 needs working capital, not an equity purchase.
- **Payer enrollment takes 90-180 days.** Start the stand-up during the audit and keep the hospital on its own TIN until the new entity is live. No revenue gap.
- **No moat.** Only one early moat is real (neutrality). Scale economies in routes and billing need at least three systems in a metro. Data (denial and payer-rate patterns across systems) builds with every contract. Switching costs come from multi-year terms and payer enrollments, though the hospital owns the TIN, so I cannot count those as mine. Honest rating: weak until 8-10 contracts.

### Critique 3: Medicare billing rule, EKRA, margin cut from three sides, state approvals, durability

- **Medicare referral-lab rule (CMS manual ch. 16 §40.1; SSA 1833(h)(5)(A)).** The critic's reading of the original is right: a 51% operator LLC is not "wholly owned" by anyone and cannot bill for tests the hospital performs. Where I disagree is "the only escapes destroy the pitch". The statute's second exception covers an entity that is wholly owned by the performing entity, or wholly owns it, or both are wholly owned by a third entity. A **100% hospital-owned outreach LLC** with its own CLIA, NPI and TIN, billing for tests run in the hospital core lab, is the Frisbie template. The cost of that fix is that the operator holds no equity in the billing entity and therefore does not consolidate its test revenue. I accept that cost. It is the reason revenue is now service revenue. Counsel must confirm; I rely on the statute text as quoted by two critics and the dossier, and I did not retrieve the CMS PDF myself.
- **42 CFR 424.73 (agent pay).** An agent that bills for the hospital cannot be paid by anything "related in any way" to dollars billed or collected. A flat per-claim fee and cost plus are the standard way to stay clear; payment goes into a hospital-controlled account. Counsel must confirm that an incentive on clean-claim rate is also unrelated to dollars (U).
- **PAMA, Elevance and UHC cut the premium.** Agreed. The cost-plus price does not depend on the hospital rate premium, and the pitch to the CFO changes from "grow outreach" to "keep payer access and stop paying more than you have to for the commercial half". My arithmetic on the cut: the critic's 53% number applies to a percent fee on shrinking revenue. Mine is a cost-based price, so a cut makes it a bigger share of revenue but does not change the dollars. It does make some systems sell, and I lose those.
- **State transaction laws and tax-exempt control.** Phase 1 involves no change in ownership of any hospital asset and no private-equity owner of a provider organisation. That probably keeps it outside Illinois-style review, but Maine's "operational control" wording and any management-contract rules need checking (U). The hospital remains in full control of its LLC, so the ancillary-JV control problem (Rev. Rul. 98-15) does not arise until Phase 3.
- **Durability.** CompuNet (2017) and Froedtert/Dynacare (2015) unwinds, and Mrak's 2018 interviews (17 chairs, qualitative), say JV benefits erode. I accept that, which is why equity JVs come last and only where the sponsor carries the capital.

## 4. The business

### Job to be done
A health-system CFO with a $15-50M outreach program and no plan to sell says: "Payers are narrowing my lab status, my outreach P&L is shrinking, and I do not trust Quest or Labcorp to run it. I want a partner who runs the commercial half, will not take my business, and costs less than I spend today." Non-consumption (do nothing, keep the in-house team) is the main competitor, followed by ARUP/Mayo/Chi advice and a national's management offer.

### Sequence (each phase has a stop condition)

| Phase | Months | What | Revenue to us | Capital | Stop if |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Audit | 0-6 | Network and Margin Audit for 5-8 systems in 2 metros: payer status (UHC tier, Elevance exposure, Anthem), commercial-function cost per requisition, leakage of employed-physician orders, denial profile. $25k, credited to phase 1. | $0.3-1.0M (A) | Seed, $1-2M | Fewer than 3 systems will share cost data |
| 1. Stand-up | 3-12 | 100% hospital-owned lab LLC with CLIA/NPI/TIN, payer enrollments, UHC Preferred Lab Network application, billing migration. $250-500k fixed. | $1-3M | Same | No payer letter or enrollment after 2 systems |
| 2. Managed ops | 9-48 | 5-year cost-plus contract: billing, payer contracting, couriers and PSC logistics, client services, data, plus a flat enablement fee. | $4M a system at maturity (A) | Series A $8-12M for platform, team, working capital | Cost 8% below baseline not shown at month 18 |
| 3. Hub | 30-60 | Buy a distressed independent lab (0.4-0.8x revenue, A) to be a performing lab for opt-in send-outs and overflow; firewall it from the managed-ops entity | $1.3M a system (A, weakest number) | $15-25M, partly debt on lab assets | Hospitals will not send volume to a lab we own |
| 4. Performing-lab JV | 48-96 | With 6+ references, a sponsor-funded JV where we run the lab and commit capital, hospital holds 49%, and counsel has cleared §40.1, OIG and EKRA | $30M a JV | $50-60M sponsor co-invest | Counsel opinion fails or sponsor will not commit |

Tension to state openly: a hub we own conflicts with the neutrality claim. Mitigation is structural: separate legal entity, hospital chooses the reference lab by RFP, operator's managed-ops pay does not depend on hub volume. If hospitals do not believe the firewall, the hub dies and the upside drops to about $75M. I have no way to test that cheaply.

### Pricing summary
- Audit $25k, credited. Stand-up $250-500k one time.
- Managed ops: open-book cost plus 10-12%, flat platform fee $250-400k, flat per-claim billing fee, five-year term, change-of-control exit for the hospital, no percentage of revenue or collections anywhere.
- Guarantee: 8% below baseline all-in cost per requisition by month 18, otherwise the markup is reduced.
- Hub: published wholesale price list, no minimum.

The lessons file asks me to flag heroic share: the system-count share and the win rate are the heroic parts.

## 6. Cases (year 6-8, revenue; all (A))

**Median: $12M revenue, about breakeven EBITDA.** 4 managed systems at about $3.3M, no hub, a handful of audits. This is the unconditional median including the early failures. Conditional on getting past the section 8 test and two paying clients, it is about $20M.

**Upside: $110M revenue, about $8-9M EBITDA after $5-6M of overhead.**

| Layer | Math | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Managed operations | 15 systems x $4.0M | $60M |
| Hub | 15 systems x $1.3M of opt-in send-outs and overflow | $20M |
| Performing-lab JV | 1 JV, $30M outreach revenue consolidated | $30M |
| Total | | **$110M** |

Assumptions the upside needs, all at once:
1. Counsel clears the wholly-owned LLC plus vendor structure (§40.1, 424.73, EKRA). Probability about 75%.
2. At least 3 systems sit in each of 3 metros so route and billing density actually cut cost 8-12%. About 50%.
3. 15 of about 45 receptive stayers choose us over Orion, TriCore, Mayo/ARUP/Chi advice and national management offers (33% win rate), and most renew. About 25%.
4. Hospitals trust the hub firewall and send $1.3M each. About 50%.
5. A sponsor commits $50-60M after 6+ references. About 40%.
6. Payer pressure (UHC tier, Elevance, PAMA) continues and is not reversed.

Product of 1-5 taken as independent is about 2%. Assumptions 2 and 3 are correlated and the layers can substitute for each other (for example 25 managed systems without the JV), so I put the upside at about 5%, range 2-8%.

## 7. Moat (7 Powers)

- **Counter-positioning**: reachable now. Nationals cannot match a no-steering, no-noncompete, hospital-owns-everything contract without giving up the reason they buy and manage labs. Limited to the stayers who value it.
- **Scale economies**: reachable after 3 systems in a metro (courier routes, shared billing and denial engine).
- **Process power and data**: cross-system denial, payer-rate and leakage data; reachable around 40-100M requisitions.
- Switching costs: hospital owns TIN and contracts, so this is weak for us by design.

Pre-mortem, 2028 (A): (1) stayers do it themselves with Mayo or ARUP, or take a national's management offer; (2) our cost advantage is not real until density exists, so the guarantee bites early; (3) counsel or a regulator makes the structure heavier than the margin supports; (4) a national offers a cleaner management contract at cost; (5) we become acquisition bait before we have scale.

## 8. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test

**Riskiest assumption:** a non-selling health system will sign a 5-year open-book operating contract with a startup, at a price at least 8% below its own cost, instead of doing it in-house, buying advice, or taking a national's management offer. Demand and cost advantage in one sentence. Legal risk is second, and cheaper to test.

**Test (under $2k, 10-14 days):**
1. Build a one-page "Network and Margin Audit" template. Offer it free to 20 outreach directors, lab administrators or CFOs at systems with $15-50M outreach in two metros that are not Louisiana and not already under a national's management contract. Reach them through lab-management communities (CLMA, ADLM, CAP, Dark Report readership), not cold email alone.
2. Ask for a 30-minute call and the outreach commercial-function cost lines (couriers, PSCs, billing, client services) plus their UHC, Anthem and Elevance status.
3. Pull the Elevance and UHC provider notices and settle whether on-campus outreach tests are affected.
4. One hour with healthcare regulatory counsel (budget about $750-1,500) on the one-page structure: 100% hospital-owned LLC, vendor agreement at cost plus flat fees, hospital-employed salaried reps, hub opt-in.

**Pass:** at least 4 of 20 take the call, at least 3 share cost data, in at least 2 the commercial functions cost 14% or more of outreach revenue and the buyer would consider outsourcing billing and logistics at 8%+ below cost with SLAs, at least 1 will pay the $25k audit or sign a non-binding LOI, and counsel does not flag a blocker.
**Kill:** fewer than 2 will share data, or none would outsource at that price, or counsel's first read is that the structure fails §40.1 or EKRA.

# C08 refined: Last Mile (P022), strongest honest version after three critiques

Date: 2026-10-18. Tags: [S] sourced, [V] verified by me against the primary page this session, [D] my arithmetic on sourced inputs, [A] assumption, [C] taken from a critic's evidence and not re-opened by me.

## Verdict: DEAD as a $100M business (alive = false)

The critiques can be answered. Counterparty risk, the "below the bill" promise, attribution, the voluntary hand-back and the LPR dependency can each be fixed by changing the payer, pricing, product or sequencing. But every fix removes something that was supposed to make the business big, and none of them creates a defensible position. What is left is a parity-priced recovery desk for broken books. Its organic upside is about $84M of gross billing, about $40M net of agent pay and about $5-8M of EBITDA, only if four separate customer segments all land at the top of their ranges in year 7-8. Reaching $100M of billing requires buying a Resolvion-class forwarder with sponsor capital. That is a roll-up at single-digit margins and a different business, and I do not count it as a path for this idea. I am not shrinking it into a credit-union boutique to keep it "alive."

Two independent reasons:

1. **Size.** $100M of gross billing means being about as large as the largest independent forwarder. Resolvion's own page says 10,000+ cars recovered a month, 50,000+ new assignments a month, 600+ agents and partnerships with 80+ lenders [V]. That is about 120k recoveries a year, about $96M at $800 [D], an average of about 1,500 units per lender [D]. Resolvion took 22 years, a 2019 merger and PE money to get there [C]. A new entrant's $100M is "become Resolvion," which means winning 60-80 lender relationships, not 3-5 anchors.
2. **No power.** Locate is sold to everyone and is legally narrowing. Net-proceeds pricing is a contract clause that incumbents can match at no cost. Voluntary hand-back saves about $100 on one leg at high liability. Counter-positioning fails, scale economies favor the incumbents, and process power from pooled outcome data is 4+ years away and sits behind the rail owner's own data.

## The refined business (v2)

**Name.** Last Mile: an independent recovery desk for broken books.

**What it is.** A 14-day takeover of the repossession and remarketing chain for portfolios where the normal chain has failed or has no owner: the failed or stressed non-captive subprime lender's secured creditors and estate, and the third-party servicers who inherit such books. It re-papers open assignments, clears repossessed units stranded in agency yards, restarts placements through a multi-source locate stack with state-level compliance gating, pays agents in 2 days, and reports net proceeds per unit to the secured creditor.

**Wedge customer.** Warehouse-bank special-assets teams, ABS controlling parties and trustees, bankruptcy trustees and CROs, private-credit owners of whole-loan books, and the third-party servicers (Vervent, Westlake PM, Servicing Solutions class). Later: mid-market lenders with 5-20k repossessions a year that have no formal panel power.

**Payer and signer.** The workout head at the warehouse bank or fund, with the trustee, or the VP Recovery at the servicer. Paid from collections ahead of residuals [A, counsel].

**Pricing.** Per-unit base fee about $900, fuel-indexed, at parity or above. Transport, storage and title are at-cost pass-throughs with a small handling fee. Gross billing about $1,100 per unit including pass-throughs [A]. Net revenue after agent pay and pass-throughs about $520 [D]. Optional gain share, about 20% above a concurrent control, only inside a contractual randomized holdout and paid last.

**Moat, honestly.** Of the 7 Powers, none is reached before year 5. The best candidate is process power: pooled unit-level outcome data across broken books and several forwarders. It is real but small, slow, and sits behind the rail owner's own assignment data. Counter-positioning fails because incumbents lose nothing by matching. There is no cornered resource, because every locate input is sold non-exclusively. Scale economies in dispatch density favor the incumbents.

## Bottom-up market

- **Whole pool.** 1.7M (Cox, auction-delivered 2024 [S]) to about 2.95M (RDN annualized, Q3 2025 [S]) repossessions a year. Forwarders handle 66% [S, CFPB]. Fees $575-800 [S]. Forwarder fee pool $0.65-1.6B, spread about $0.5B [D, dossier]. Cox expects flat near 1.7M through 2029 [S]. 2026 assignment flow is softer [V].
- **Units for $100M [D].** Gross billing at $1,000-1,300 a unit needs 77k-100k units, 4-5% of all repossessions at 2.0M. Net revenue at $520 needs 192k units, about 10%. Compare Resolvion at about 120k.
- **Upside units by segment in year 7-8 [A]:**
  - A. Distress and orphan books: 15k. Basis: 2-4 mandates a year, about 40k loans each, an 18% repossession rate, about 7k units per mandate in year one, half won, with overlap in the tail.
  - B. Servicer channel: 15k. Basis: third-party servicers' books of roughly 120-180k loans at a 10% repossession rate give 12-18k units, plus new onboardings.
  - C. Mid-market challenger slices: 40k. Basis: about 30 lenders at about 1.3k units, against Resolvion's 1,500-unit average with about 80 lenders [V, D].
  - D. Credit union and regional bank reseller channel: 6k.
  - Total 76k units, about 3.8% of repossessions at 2.0M.
- **Upside:** 76k × $1,100 = **$84M gross**, about $40M net, EBITDA about $5-8M at 6-10% of gross. Misses $100M by about $16M, and the gap closes only by acquisition.
- **Median (year 5):** 10k units (4k + 2k + 3k + 1k) × $1,000 = **$10M gross**, about $5M net.

## Riskiest assumption

That distress creates **repeatable** demand (3+ mandates a year of 3,000+ units each) that goes to an unproven independent desk at parity price, rather than being a one-off Tricolor-type event whose seat is already held by successor servicers and incumbent forwarders. Everything in segments A and B, and the only place a randomized proof can happen, rests on it.

## Cheapest real-world test (2 weeks)

1. Pull the public record on Tricolor (Ch. 7 trustee filings), PrimaLend (Ch. 11 first-day declarations) and the ACC wind-down. Count vendors, units in yards and who was hired to recover them, how fast and at what terms.
2. Hold 20-25 calls: 6-8 servicer VPs Recovery (Vervent, Westlake PM, Servicing Solutions class), 6-8 warehouse-bank special-assets leads and ABS trustee corporate-trust officers, and 4-6 trustee-appointed CROs and restructuring advisers. Ask: who chose recovery vendors after the last failure; how many broken books did you handle in 2025-26 and how many are visible for 2027; would you place a 3,000-unit book with a new independent desk on a 14-day takeover; what are the pay terms and waterfall priority.
3. Ask two servicers for a one-page non-binding letter naming Last Mile an eligible recovery vendor on successor activation.
4. **Go** if 2 of 6 servicers or banks say yes with waterfall-senior payment and 4+ mandates a year are visible. **Kill** if fewer than 3 mandates a year are visible, or the seats are held under standing agreements.

## What would reverse the verdict

(a) A signed letter from a servicer or secured creditor naming Last Mile for 5,000+ units on waterfall-senior terms, with at least 4 mandates a year visible. (b) Counsel's written opinion that the enacted Kentucky text permits lender-agent LPR use, and a consented locate source that matches the DRN hit rate. (c) A lender RFP asking for net-dollar-per-defaulted-dollar pricing, with RDN and the big forwarders declining to bid. (d) Sponsor capital to buy a wounded forwarder. That last one is a different business and should be judged as a PE roll-up, not as this idea.

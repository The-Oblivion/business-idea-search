# C16 Dossier: Approved Buyer (sharpened pick: operator-led density consolidator of independent uniform and linen laundries, remedy-optional)

Pool P067 (US uniform, linen, workwear rental). Researched 2026-10-18 from about 45 searches and fetches, plus SEC filings pulled directly. "Gate" means the evidence-gate report for this idea. "Mine" marks my own arithmetic or judgment.

## 1. Bottom line

Verdict: weak. The sharpened pick is a real business but not a differentiated one. It is a standard private-equity roll-up of family laundries. Sellers exist, $100M of bought revenue is under 1% of the pool, and lenders are active. But at least ten funded buyers already do this, strategics pay synergy prices a financial buyer cannot match, and the FTC-remedy "option" that justified the original idea is worth close to zero in expectation. The revenue is bought, not earned: $100M of it costs about $130M of enterprise value (mine) and returns on that look like an ordinary 1.7x-2.7x equity multiple that depends on the exit multiple.

My cases for annual revenue in years 5-7: median $40M (gate said $45M, fair), realistic ceiling about $120M, remedy-driven $200M under 5% likely (mine). Overall score about 3.5/10.

## 2. Why-now: the trigger is real, the remedy is a lottery ticket with a bad payout

- **Status.** FTC Second Request issued 2026-06-11. Both parties certified substantial compliance 2026-10-02. A timing agreement bars closing before 2026-12-11 unless the FTC closes its investigation in writing. Cintas "expects" to close before year-end ([Cintas 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000072325426000047/ctas-20260831.htm)). Nothing public shows an FTC complaint, consent, or remedy proposal.
- **Cintas contractually declined remedies.** The merger agreement says Cintas "is not and will not be required" to sell or divest anything. In its December 2025 offer Cintas traded remedies for a $350M reverse fee and a duty to litigate. The outside date is 2027-01-10 and auto-extends twice by four months, to about September 2027 ([424B3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000114036126020376/ny20069194x3_424b3.htm)). So a package exists only if the FTC forces it and Cintas then chooses to settle rather than fight.
- **Precedent.** Cintas/G&K (2017) cleared after eight months with no divestitures ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/practices/experience/2017/03/cintas-secures-antitrust-clearance-for-22-billion-purchase-of-gk-services)).
- **Signals for a remedy.** The FTC is interviewing independents and regionals. One source flagged high concentration in Nashville, Atlanta, Miami and Los Angeles, said buyers will be hard to find, and called Vestis an unlikely buyer (single source: [ION/Dealreporter, 2026-05-06](https://ionanalytics.com/insights/dealreporter/ftc-interviewing-competitors-in-unifirst-cintas-merger-source/)). A March analysis expecting 20-30% of branches to be divested ([Antitrust Intelligence](https://antitrust-intelligence.com/cintas-5-5-billion-acquisition-of-unifirst-likely-to-hinge-on-divestitures/)) has no named author and reads the $350M fee as a remedy signal, which the 424B3 contradicts. Treat it as low reliability.
- **Who gets remedy packages.** Established operators. The Ferguson FTC takes divestitures to an upfront buyer with "a long track record" ([Synopsys/Ansys, Mondaq](https://admin.mondaq.com/unitedstates/antitrust-eu-competition/1635112/return-of-the-remedy-first-ftc-merger-settlement-under-second-trump-administration-in-synopsysansys)). DOJ's UnitedHealth/Amedisys package (164 sites, about $528M revenue) went to BrightSpring and Pennant ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/unitedhealth-amedisys-doj-settlement-merger/757086/)). Ascension/AmSurg sent seven centers in five metros to an existing operator and a physician group ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-31-ftc-requiring-divestitures-approves-final-consent-decree-in-ascensionamsurg-deal)). FTC's remedy study found whole-business buyers succeeded and staff scrutinize a buyer's financing and plan ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/ftc-issues-report-evaluating-merger-remedies)).
- **Legal misframe.** There is no "pre-clearance." A buyer is approved inside a specific consent. A new vehicle with three ex-executives and committed equity is exactly the profile staff discount.
- **My odds.** Any divestiture about 25-30%. This platform named as a buyer, given a divestiture, about 10% (Alsco, Prudential and PE-backed operators are likelier). Joint about 3%. The practical checkpoint is 2026-12-11.

## 4. Bottom-up market and deal supply

- **Payers.** Sponsor and lenders fund; the independent's family owner signs the sale.
- **Pool.** TRSA claims a $50B+ North American industry with 2,500+ facilities ([TRSA](https://trsa.org/about-commercial-laundry-linen-services)). A TRSA state sheet cites $27+B for the US ([PA fact sheet](https://www.trsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/022026_Pennsylvania-Fact_Sheet.pdf)). Independents hold about $11B (pool-file arithmetic; UniFirst FY25 revenue was $2.43B).
- **Universe.** Baird mailed its survey to about 500 independent firms and got 21 replies ([Baird 2Q26](https://www.trsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Baird-2026-2Q26-Textile-Rental-Survey-Trends.pdf.pdf)). A TRSA article says about two-thirds of its independent members have revenue of $15M or less and all but three are family-owned; my read is the "nearly 175" it cites is the member base, not the industry ([TRSA](https://www.trsa.org/magazine-article/trsa-independently-owned-and-operated/)). An undated aggregator lists 329 firms in industrial launderers and 522 in linen supply ([mewayz 812332](https://data.mewayz.com/industry/812332), [812331](https://data.mewayz.com/industry/812331)). I found no revenue distribution.
- **Inference (mine).** Route-based targets above $8M of revenue are probably 100-200 firms, not the gate's "300." If the median target is $7-10M, $100M needs 10-15 deals.
- **Pace.** Best-in-class acquirers do about three deals a year (ImageFIRST 25 in eight years; Alsco four in 18 months). A new team doing 10-15 deals needs 5-8 years, so $100M is an outer-edge outcome for a new team, not the median.

## 5. Price evidence and willingness to pay

- **Sellers have alternatives**: Alsco, Cintas, a dozen PE platforms, family succession. Unlike a service with no budget line, the payer is not captive.
- **Demand backdrop** ([Baird 2Q26](https://www.trsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Baird-2026-2Q26-Textile-Rental-Survey-Trends.pdf.pdf)): renewal pricing +3.1%, new-account pricing still "net competitive," 12-month growth outlook +3.6% against a ~4% long-run average, and employment-driven add/stops at their weakest since 2024.
- **Margins** (TRSA 2026 report, 2025 data): median pre-tax 8.1%, industrial 18.1% (up 7.1 points), healthcare 4.5% (down from 10.3%), F&B linen 11.9% ([TRSA](https://www.trsa.org/news/industry-performance-report-industrial-segment-leads-profit-growth/)). Swings of 6-7 points in a year are a leverage risk.
- **Broker multiples are unreliable.** CT Acquisitions quotes 5-9x EBITDA and says Cintas/UniFirst priced at "about 8x run-rate EBITDA" before synergies ([CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/how-to-sell-a-uniform-rental-business/)). UniFirst's FY25 EBITDA was $324.9M ($184.5M operating income plus $140.4M D&A, [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/717954/000119312525256222/unf-20250830.htm)). So $5.5B is about 16.9x trailing and 2.26x revenue; it is about 7.9x only if the full $375M of synergies is added.
- **Strategic comps.** UniFirst paid $300M headline (about $260M after tax benefits) for Clean Uniform with about $90M revenue: 2.9-3.3x ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/unifirst-acquiring-clean-uniform-for-300-million-details-unf)). Vestis is about 1.1x revenue and about 10x EBITDA (market cap about $1.87B per [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/price-vestis-nyse-vsts-stock-price-up-09-still-a-buy-2026-10-10/), net debt $1,205M per [Vestis Q3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001967649/000162828026055485/vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm), FY26 EBITDA guide $310-315M).
- **Entry price (mine).** UniFirst D&A is 5.8% of revenue, so independents at 14-24% EBITDA margins and 5-9x give 0.8-2.0x revenue. The gate's 0.8-1.2x only fits the low-margin end. $100M of revenue costs $80-200M, central $130M.
- **Seller anchoring.** The 2.3x and 2.9x prints are a headwind. The gate is right here. Route-dense strategics that fold stops into existing routes can outbid a financial buyer.

## 6. Unit economics, capital and illustrative returns

- **Density gap is real, closing it is hard.** Cintas uniform operating margin is 24.1% ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723254/000072325426000028/ctas-20260531.htm)). UniFirst earned 7.6% in FY25 and 3.6% in fiscal Q3 2026. Vestis earned a 12.2% EBITDA margin at 4.10x net leverage. Two national operators have not closed the gap, so "operator-led" is a claim, not a moat.
- **Capex.** UniFirst spent $107M in 39 weeks on $1.88B of revenue, 5.7% ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/717954/000162828026047621/unf-20260530.htm)). Consolidation plants cost money: Unitex's 130,000 sq ft Hartford conversion is about $9M for the building plus about $20M for fit-out ([Hartford Business](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/medical-linen-company-eyes-30m-hartford-expansion/); sources show $21M or $30M). Prudential is spending $40-50M on a 100,000 sq ft cleanroom plant ([Cleanroom Technology](https://cleanroomtechnology.com/prudential-breaks-ground-on-100-000-sqft-cleanroom-laundry)). Budget $15-30M per metro hub.
- **Debt.** A broker guide puts lower-middle-market unitranche at SOFR plus 650-900 bps and leverage at 3.4x senior and 4.2x total for $10-50M EV deals, citing GF Data and Lincoln (unverified: [CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/debt-financing/)). Unitranche all-in yields were 9.33% in Dec 2025 ([Houlihan Lokey](https://cdn.hl.com/pdf/2026/hl-us-private-credit-market-newsletter-january-2026.pdf)).
- **Illustrative platform (mine).** Buy $100M of revenue at 1.3x ($130M) with 18% EBITDA ($18M, 7.2x). Add $10M of fees and integration, 4.0x debt ($72M at about 9.5%, $6.8M interest), capex $5.7M, tax $1.5M. Free cash flow is about $4M a year; equity is about $68M. In year 6, revenue is about $124M at 3.6% growth and debt about $42M.
  - Exit at 7x EBITDA (about 1.3x revenue): equity about $114M, 1.7x, IRR about 9%.
  - Exit at 10x (Vestis-like, about 1.8x revenue): about 2.7x, IRR about 18%.
  - Exit at a Clean-style 2.9x revenue: above 4x, but this needs a synergy buyer.
- **Exit buyers.** Alsco (family-owned; the idea file calls it capital-limited, which I could not verify), Elis (walked from US talks citing financial discipline, [BNN Bloomberg](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/2024/10/04/elis-scraps-acquisition-talks-with-us-rivals-unifirst-vestis)), PE secondaries (CVC, Sterling and Calera show appetite), and Cintas/UniFirst only if the FTC allows. The merger arbitrage is real but not special.

## 7. Regulatory and liability landscape

- **HSR.** The 2026 threshold is $133.9M ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/01/HSR-Filing-Thresholds-Filing-Fees-and-Penalties-to-Increase-for-2026)). Independents' deals fall below it: no waiting period, but also no clearance, and the FTC can challenge after closing.
- **Environmental.** UniFirst's filings list consent-order cleanup sites (Woburn, Somerville, Springfield, Uvalde, Stockton, Williamstown) and accrue contingencies ([FY2019 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/717954/000156459019038460/unf-10k_20190831.htm)). A consultant notes former laundries often show solvent (PCE) conditions in Phase I reviews ([EEC](https://eecenvironmental.com/our-markets/industrial-laundries/)). Buy assets, run Phase I/II, and escrow. I found no remediation cost benchmark.
- **PFAS is not a forcing function.** EPA has finalized no PFAS effluent guideline ([CRS, 2026-09-30](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-30_IF12148_7411d6bc05ccbbc70ee4e9ed6f8ffb1569d3d172.html)), limits are local, and TRSA cites Michigan putting laundries at about 5% of PFAS waste ([TRSA](https://www.trsa.org/news/pfas-regulatory-update-risks-and-opportunities/)). The idea's "PFAS capex wall" should not be counted as seller motivation.
- **Labor.** TRSA reports operators worried about ICE I-9 audits and shrinking labor supply ([TRSA webinar](https://www.trsa.org/news/immigration-compliance-were-in-a-very-different-time/)).
- **Failure precedents.** Angelica went bankrupt in 2017 after losing its largest customer; its lender KKR bought it for about $160M by credit bid and cash ([Paul Weiss](https://paulweiss.com/practices/transactional/restructuring/news/kkr-completes-acquisition-of-angelica-corporation?id=24538)). Lighthouse Linen reorganized in 2026 and was recapitalized by Sundara ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/130863-lighthouse-linen-emerges-from-chapter-11-with-sundara-partners-control-i/)). Leveraged linen roll-ups can fail, which is also a source of distressed route books.

## 9. Scorecard against the round-1 failure modes

1. **Small market:** passes. $100M is under 1% of the pool.
2. **Hidden incumbents:** fails. At least ten funded acquirers and four strategics are active, and several are larger.
3. **Wrong payer or no budget line:** sellers are real but not captive. Capital is available.
4. **Legal premise:** fails. "Pre-cleared" is not a mechanism, and Cintas has contracted around remedies.
5. **Price exceeds value:** at risk. Seller expectations are set by strategic prints.
6. **Why-now used up:** half. The consolidation wave is real, but the remedy window closes with the Dec 11 to Q1 2027 decision.
7. **Shrinking to survive:** yes. The pick dropped the remedy insight and became a generic roll-up.

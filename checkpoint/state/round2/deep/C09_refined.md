# C09 refined: "Stafette" (working name, replaces Meisterwerk)

Written 2026-10-18 after three serious critiques of the "hold-forever Handwerk platform with owned origination". FX 1 EUR = 1.15 USD, so $100M = EUR 87M. Tags: [M] = from memory, not verified; "secondary" = seen only in a search summary or law-firm relay; "assumption" = my number, to be replaced by data from the test below.

## 0. Verdict in seven lines

1. **Alive, conditionally.** The original (60 whole micro-firms, hold-forever as the edge, EUR 15M seed) is dead. A rebuilt version survives and keeps a mechanical path to about $108M of year-7 revenue in the upside case.
2. **What changed.** The unit of purchase moves from "a micro firm" to "a customer book plus crew plus vans", absorbed into a dense local hub led by an equity-earning Meister. One Meister now covers EUR 3M+ of revenue instead of EUR 1.5M, which is what makes the arithmetic work.
3. **What the price now rests on.** Retained revenue, not a recurring-revenue hope. Half or more of the price is paid only if customers stay.
4. **Capital.** Staged EUR 4M, EUR 10M, EUR 16M of equity plus bank debt (about EUR 30M of equity for the upside case), not EUR 50-100M up front.
5. **Numbers.** Median case about $27M revenue at about 3.5% group EBIT. Upside about $108M revenue at about 6.6% hub EBIT (about EUR 6M). That is a capital-allocation business with a founder-carry outcome, not a software outcome.
6. **Riskiest assumption.** Owner-sellers with real, retainable B2B service books exist in volume and will take at most 0.4x of retained revenue with at least half contingent.
7. **Moat.** Weak to moderate and earned late: process power in small-ticket absorption, then local route density. "Hold forever" is a covenant, not a moat. Counter-positioning is withdrawn.

## 2. The refined business

### 2.1 One-liner
A Meister-partnered network of local building-service hubs (electrical and heating/plumbing service first) that buys the customer books, crews and vans of retiring and closing owner firms into dense hubs, financed in stages by steward-owned capital.

### 2.2 Why the unit of purchase changed (the math that carries the whole thesis)
Model (assumptions, all to be replaced by test data): direct costs (technician labour, material, vehicles) are 81% of revenue, so contribution is 19%; the platform shared-service fee is 3.5% of revenue; fixed overhead per hub is EUR 240k (Meister-Geschaeftsfuehrer EUR 76k loaded, two dispatch/admin staff EUR 96k, premises/IT/insurance EUR 68k). Hub EBIT = revenue x 15.5% - EUR 240k.

| Hub revenue | EBIT | Margin |
|---|---|---|
| EUR 1.2M | -54k | -4.5% |
| EUR 1.5M (a stand-alone micro firm) | -8k | -0.5% |
| EUR 2.0M | 70k | 3.5% |
| EUR 2.5M | 148k | 5.9% |
| EUR 3.4M (mature upside hub) | 287k | 8.4% |
| EUR 4.0M | 380k | 9.5% |

Break-even is EUR 1.55M. Each extra EUR 1M of absorbed, retained revenue adds about EUR 155k of EBIT because the Meister, dispatch and premises are already paid for. At a price of 0.30x (EUR 300k, half contingent) payback is about 1.9 years. Sensitivity of mature-hub margin at EUR 3.4M: contribution 15% gives 4.4%; 19% gives 8.4%; 23% gives 12.4%. The contribution figure on absorbed crews is the second-biggest unknown after seller willingness.

This is what US home-service roll-ups call route density. The difference from the critics' target is the buyer's cost per EUR 100-300k deal: HomeServe-type deal teams do not do tickets this small, a standard 10-day process can.

### 2.3 Who signs what
- **Seller (signer on the deal):** owner of an Elektro or SHK firm with 3-20 staff and no family or employee successor. Their alternative today is closing (about 54,000 Handwerk firms plan closure against 49,000 planning a handover, two years, DHZ/KfW) or selling to a neighbour. We do not chase owners who hold out for 0.87x of revenue (DHZ/KfW average expectation).
- **Meister-partner (signer on the equity ladder):** employed Meister or a Geselle finishing the Meister, becoming Geschaeftsfuehrer-Gesellschafter of one hub GmbH. Real shares, real management, so the HwO Sec. 7 Betriebsleiter test is met by the Geschaeftsfuehrer [M, confirm with HWK].
- **Revenue payer:** property managers and WEG-Verwalter, housing companies, small commercial and light-industrial sites paying service invoices. Consumers and public DGUV V3 frameworks are excluded from priced books (BGB 309 Nr. 9; Karlsruhe 2023 tender awarded on price alone).
- **Capital (signer):** CIO of a family office, insurer or Versorgungswerk for tranches B and C. Tranche A comes from steward-ownership and angel money.

### 2.4 First product
**"Nachfolge-Floor":** a written offer within 10 working days, on a standard contract set, to buy the customer book, crew and vehicles of a firm with no successor. Terms: price formula (below), every employee keeps the job and pay for 24 months (Sec. 613a BGB transfer, which here is the point), owner paid as consultant for 6-12 months for customer introductions, a joint customer letter, escrow of 10-15% of the upfront price for 12 months against pre-closing liabilities.
Three doors into a hub:
1. **EBO door:** an employed Meister wants to buy their boss's firm but cannot carry personal liability (KfW 077 limits). We fund the stake non-recourse beyond the shares and become the back office. Folds in the Teamkauf idea instead of competing with it.
2. **External-Meister door:** a Meister-partner plus a bought anchor book of about EUR 1.2M revenue.
3. **Opportunistic door:** an 8-20 staff firm with a Meister in place, only at or below 0.4x revenue. Not planned for, because HomeServe, GREENOX and PE chase these.

Customer-side origination (the channel PE does not have): ask Hausverwaltungen and housing companies which of their Elektro/SHK vendors are over 58 with no successor, and for a "continuity letter" committing the account to the successor for 24 months if crew and prices are unchanged. Shared channels (HWK Nachfolgemoderatoren, Innungen, free Steuerberater referrals) are secondary and are shared with HWP, Builtech and Konzmann.

### 2.5 Pricing
- **Book and crew:** 0.25-0.40x trailing-12-month revenue of contracts actually assigned and customers actually retained. 40-50% at closing, the rest paid quarterly over 24 months from retained gross profit with a true-up at month 12 and month 24. Hard ceiling 0.5x. This sits inside the FINANCE corridor of 0.25-0.50x for Bau/Handwerk under EUR 20M (KfW Fokus 526). The unproven recurring share now sits on the seller's side of the table, not ours.
- **Anchor firm:** 0.35-0.45x with the same contingent structure.
- **Meister-partner:** EUR 70-80k loaded salary (portal medians EUR 53-63k gross, so top of market), plus an equity ladder of about 5% a year to 30% at fair value, financed by a platform loan repaid from distributions. Tax and notary design needs a Steuerberater (Sec. 51a GmbHG information right; wage-tax treatment of cheap shares) [M].
- **Hub to platform:** 3.5% of revenue for accounting, payroll, scheduling software, procurement, insurance and compliance, and the M&A desk.
- **Customers:** market service rates. Raising retiring owners' underpriced contracts is a possible margin lever, not in the base case.

| Phase | When | What | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Weeks 0-8 | Seller-ledger sprint (section 9), 15 Hausverwaltung interviews, written answers from 2 HWK on mobile-crew hubs with no branch premises, 3 Meister-partner candidates | Test passes; no HWK says each absorbed crew needs its own full-time Meister |
| 1 | Months 3-24 | EUR 4M. 3 hubs in one dense cluster, about 3 books each | At month 12 retained revenue at least 70%; hub contribution at least 15% after fee; price paid at most 0.4x; one hub EBIT-positive by month 18 |

Region: not fixed. Rule: a dense belt where a 60-minute radius reaches many Hausverwaltungen and where a Meister co-founder already has a network.

### 2.7 Legal wrapper: making "hold forever" binding without the GmgV
The GmgV is only a Rahmenkonzept (4 Mar 2026), no draft bill. Steward ownership already works today through a veto-share foundation: a foundation holds a small voting stake that can block a sale of the group or changes to the articles, and investors take capped, non-voting profit rights (Noerr, Advant Beiten, Rose & Partner commentary, secondary). That gives sellers an enforceable continuity promise, gives LPs a capped cash yield (target 6-8% preferred), and answers "a side-letter anyone can undo". Cost: no flip exit for investors, so a redemption right at formula value after year 10 is the only liquidity.

## 4. Moat (Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers), honestly
- **Primary: process power, reachable in about 3 years.** A standard small-ticket absorption process: 10-day offer, template asset-purchase and Sec. 613a pack, customer-consent campaign, earn-out accounting, escrow. If it costs about EUR 8-12k per EUR 100-300k deal against an incumbent's deal overhead of EUR 50k+ (assumption), PE-backed platforms will not match it without changing how they work. This is an execution moat and it is copyable by a determined HomeServe.
- **Secondary: local scale economies in route density**, reachable cluster by cluster once a hub passes EUR 3M. The first buyer to reach 3-4 hubs in a radius becomes the default buyer of closing books there; the supply of books in a radius is finite.
- **Cornered resource (partial):** the Meister-partner equity ladder and the customer-side continuity channel. Neither is exclusive. PE can offer management incentive plans.
- **Not claimed:** counter-positioning, network effects, switching costs, brand.

## 5. Competitors and edge
HomeServe Deutschland (Brookfield): buys 20-60 staff SHK and electrical firms, keeps names; our edge is the unit. VINCI Energies / R+S, Konzmann (KLAR), Builtech (VDK), HWP (GRIP): bigger, project-heavy, not in micro books. MEISTEREHRE, SHK Ventures, GREENOX: closest peers; edge is hub absorption and customer-side origination, and honest risk is that they win the same sellers. svt/Ergon, Wtech (Waterland), Schindler, TKE, Kone: service specialists, avoided in Phase 1. Thermondo, Enpal, 1Komma5: single-family service, avoided. Free substitutes: closing, neighbour sale, EBO with KfW/KBG money, HWK Betriebsboerse, nexxt-change. Three searches this run found no source describing a startup that absorbs books into Meister-equity hubs; that is absence of evidence, not proof.

## 6. Bottom-up market
- **Sellers:** about 54,000 Handwerk closures over two years = 27,000 a year. Elektro (about 63,900 firms, unconfirmed) plus SHK (50,500) are about 20% of 570k Handwerk firms, so about 5,400 closures a year if proportional (assumption). 59.8% of Handwerk firms have fewer than 5 staff, so about 40% have 5+: about 2,200. If 30-35% have a B2B-heavy service book (assumption): **about 700 book-worthy sellers a year**, plus the undecided third of retiring owners.
- **Need in the upside case:** 35 anchors plus about 120 books over 7 years = about 22 deals a year = about 3% of 700. Share is not the constraint; HWK Dortmund data applied by the critics gives 800-1,600 outside sales a year in the 10+ staff tier alone.
- **Revenue composition at year 7 (upside):** anchors EUR 42M, absorbed books EUR 38M (120 x EUR 0.45M x 70% retained), organic EUR 14M = EUR 94M = $108M.
- **Customer side:** technical service is a growth pocket inside a EUR 68.7B German facility-services market, +9.6% in 2025 (Luenendonk, snippet). A mature hub of EUR 3.4M is about 25 technicians.

## 8. Capital plan
Tranche A EUR 4M (months 0-18): angels, a Meister-community syndicate, steward-ownership money (Purpose Evergreen Capital writes EUR 0.5-5M evergreen tickets, a lead from the dossier, not a commitment); Meister-partner stakes can use regional succession equity (NRW.BANK KBG silent partnership of EUR 25-250k in NRW; other states have equivalents [M]). Tranche B EUR 10M (months 24-40): insurer, Versorgungswerk or family office after three audited hubs. Tranche C EUR 16M plus debt (years 4-5). Buergschaftsbank cover (EUR 2M per borrower cap) helps hub-level debt, not the holding.

## 9. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test
**Assumption:** in one metro cluster, enough owner-led Elektro/SHK firms with real, retainable B2B service books and no successor will accept the book formula (0.25-0.40x retained revenue, 40-50% at closing, rest on retention, jobs guaranteed). If not, nothing else matters: no deals, no hubs, no capital.

**Test (days 1-14, near-zero cash, needs a German-speaking operator with Handwerk credibility):**
1. Days 1-3: build a list of 120 Elektro/SHK owners aged about 58+ in one cluster from the Handwerksrolle and Impressum data, HWK Betriebsboerse and nexxt-change listings. Draft a one-page formula offer.
2. Days 3-10: 60 owner calls (cold plus two Innung or HWK Nachfolgemoderator introductions) and 10 Hausverwaltung or housing-company interviews asking which vendors are aging out and whether they would sign a 24-month continuity letter.
3. Ask each interested owner for a 30-minute call and a redacted three-year ledger under NDA: revenue by customer type, share B2B repeat, top-10 customer share, headcount, owner's own hours.
**Pass:** at least 10 of 60 take the call; at least 4 send ledgers within 14 days; at least 2 say yes in writing, non-binding, to the formula; at least one ledger shows 55%+ B2B repeat revenue and top-10 customers under 40%; at least 3 of 10 property managers name an aging vendor and would sign a continuity letter.
**Fail (stop or downgrade):** under 2 ledgers; every owner insists on at least 0.7x in cash; B2B repeat share under 40% in every ledger.

## 10. Pre-mortem (2029) and what each failure costs
1. **Sellers will not take the formula or books are consumer-heavy.** Caught by the test and the month-12 gate. Fix: none; stop.
2. **Hub contribution is 15% or lower, or a Meister cannot be recruited or an HWK rejects the structure.** Caught by Phase 1 P&Ls and the HWK letters. Fix: plateau at a smaller number of hubs.
3. **No anchor LP.** Fix: stay at median size by design, self-fund from hub cash flows.
4. **A big buyer copies book absorption near our hubs.** Fix: speed in one cluster at a time, Meister lock-in, walk away above 0.5x.

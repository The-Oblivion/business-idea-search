# C11 Dossier: P062 "Outreach Without Selling" (gate pick "Outreach Partner Labs")

Researched 2026-10-18. Method: about 50 searches and fetches, plus primary filings read directly (Quest 10-K FY2024, Labcorp 10-K FY2021, CMS manual chapter 16, 42 CFR 424.73, XiFin 2026 report, Quest Q4 2025 prepared remarks, Sonic FY26 deck). Tags: (S) sourced, (A) my arithmetic or assumption, (U) unverified. Pages that returned 403 (Business Wire, Sonic's site, GenomeWeb) were used only through search summaries and are marked "search summary".

## 1. Verdict

**Weak, trending dead as a $100M business. I would not advance it as written.**

- The pool is real and large.
- The $100M revenue figure is arithmetically reachable only as consolidated JV revenue. That revenue earns about $8-15M of EBITDA and needs roughly $120M+ of equity at the multiples national buyers actually pay (A).
- The plan fails four checks:
  - The legal structure contradicts Medicare billing rules.
  - Capital is understated by about 2x.
  - The competitor set is far bigger than "none found".
  - The closest funded precedent (RDL, $250M committed in 2012) had stalled by 2015 after backing away from deals.
- My estimate (A), 5-7 years out:

| Case | Probability | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Bear | ~50% | No controlled JV closes; 2-4 management contracts, $2-6M revenue, or a pivot to advisory |
| Base | ~35% | One JV plus a few managed systems; $35-45M revenue, $3-5M EBITDA |
| Bull | ~15% | 2-3 JVs plus 8 managed systems; $100-130M revenue, $10-15M EBITDA, $120M+ invested |

- Median is about $8-12M, below the gate's $20M. Upside is about $100M of revenue but not $100M of value creation.

## 2. Industry metrics (step 0)

Outreach runs on:
- net revenue per test or requisition
- contribution margin %
- cash collected per dollar billed
- volume growth
- sale price as a multiple of revenue

## 3. Market and bottom-up (steps 1-2)

**The pool**
- Quest's investor day sizes hospital outreach at about $20B of a $58B physician channel across "600+ health systems", with about 20 Quest deals in a decade. (S) [Quest 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1022079/000094787125000321/ss4596431_ex9901.htm)
- That is about $33M per system (A).
- Program sizes are skewed. Chi's 2016 survey had a median of $12.8M and a mean of $24.9M. Contribution margin was 23-33%, defined as revenue less dedicated and incremental outreach costs. (S) [CLP survey summary](https://clpmag.com/resource-center/research/survey-laboratory-outreach-enjoys-favorable-profitability/)
- Hospital labs were 13.6% of 6,411 PAMA reporters, about 870 NPIs. These are NPIs, not systems. (S, computed) [CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/preliminary-calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-clinical-laboratory-fee-schedule-payment-rates)

**Flow of deals** (the real constraint)
- Quest and Labcorp closed at least 29 outreach deals from 2013 to 2024, all to the nationals, with prices undisclosed. (S) [G2 deal list](https://www.g2intelligence.com/dx-deals-outreach-deals-by-quest-labcorp-are-still-ramping-up/)
- Kaufman Hall counted about 2 transactions a year in 2013-2021 and 7 in 2022. (S) [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/finance-and-business-strategy/healthcare-business-trends/strategic-alternatives-abound-for-health-system-outreach-laboratories/)
- Labcorp's CEO said it had 13 new or closed hospital or regional lab deals in 2025. (S, search summary) [360Dx](https://www.360dx.com/clinical-lab-management/labcorp-reaffirms-outreach-acquisitions-jpm-key-growth-cites-strong-deal)
- So roughly 5-13 systems a year decide to monetize (A). If 10-25% would pick a partner over cash (A, my assumption), 1-3 contestable systems appear a year.

**Two ways to $100M**

| Path | Math (A) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Fees | 9% of a $35M program is about $3.2M a system; $100M needs about 32 systems, about 5% of all systems | At 1-3 contestable a year, 10-30 years |
| Consolidated JV revenue | About 3 JVs at $33M | Needs the equity in section 6 |

- Verdict on the pool: size is not the problem. Velocity, capital and win rate against cash buyers are.

(S) [Q4 2025 remarks](https://s2.q4cdn.com/390454341/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Quest-Diagnostics-2025-Q4-Year-End-Webcast-Script.pdf) This is the startup's pitch, from a bigger and better-funded seller.
- Labcorp:
  - It bought CHS for $195M and has Parkview pending. (S) [Labcorp](https://www.labcorp.com/education-events/press-releases/community-health-systems-announces-definitive-agreement-sell), [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/Labcorp-deal-Parkview-Health-outreach-lab-assets/805514/)
- Sonic USA:
  - It is flat on organic growth, and its operating review is ongoing. (S) [Sonic FY26](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260820/pdf/072zhg5rhzbt79.pdf)
  - It has hospital JVs such as ProMedica, where over 90% of testing is done at the JV core lab. (S, search summary) [Sonic](https://www.sonichealthcareusa.com/about-us/news/promedica-health-system-in-new-joint-venture-partnership/)
- ARUP and Mayo are free or cheap advisory substitutes. ARUP offers an outreach-value assessment. (S) [ARUP](https://www.aruplab.com/magnify26/commercial-laboratories-want-buy-your-lab-operations-heres-why-math-not-your-favor)
- Consultants: Chi Solutions, owned by PE-created Accumen, which sells shared-savings partnerships. (S) [Dark Report](https://www.darkintelligencegroup.com/the-dark-report/laboratory-management/accumen-acquires-chi-to-beef-up-lab-consulting/)

Orion is described as the state's largest independent lab and contributes operations, technology and logistics. FMOL had been using Labcorp. Terms are undisclosed. (S) [Healthcare Journal BR](https://www.healthcarejournalbr.com/node/3348), [Business Report](https://www.businessreport.com/business/orion-labs-fmol-health-partner-to-expand-services-across-the-state)

  - **Atalan.** Froedtert's WDL launched this with HealthEco in 2023 as a hospital-lab network. I found no member list or update. (S) [MLO](https://www.mlo-online.com/management/article/53074544/froedtert-health-wisconsin-diagnostic-laboratories-and-healtheco-launch-atalan)
  - **Mednet Services.** It was an older "direct and network-based outreach" operator partnered with ARUP. Current status unknown. (S) [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/arup-laboratories-signs-agreement-with-b-mednet-services-b-to-expand-its-outreach-services)
- PAML as the "independent operator":
  - It was owned by Providence and CHI, with about $300M of revenue and 1,600 staff when sold to Labcorp. (S) [Lund Report](https://www.thelundreport.org/content/paml-announces-sale-labcorp), [Labcorp](https://www.labcorp.com/labcorp-acquire-pathology-associates-medical-laboratories-providence-health-services-and-catholic)
  - Its end state was a sale by its hospital owners, not an independent success.

**In-house substitutes**
- Tucson Medical Center took outreach back after selling it in 2003. Bryan Health built its own program. (S) [ARUP](https://www.aruplab.com/magnify26/commercial-laboratories-want-buy-your-lab-operations-heres-why-math-not-your-favor)
- Kootenai Health had a PAML JV with a 50/50 revenue split. PAML ran preanalytics and billing while Kootenai tested. Labcorp bought PAML in 2017 and converted it into a testing agreement with a 5-year noncompete. Kootenai says it was "losing money", insourced in 2022 with Mayo Outreach Insights, and reports EBIDA up about $10M from FY2022 to FY2024. (S) [CAP Today](https://www.captodayonline.com/how-a-core-lab-took-back-its-outpatient-business/)
  - This is the best published case of this exact model. The operator took half the revenue, the sponsor was acquired and changed the terms, and the hospital left.

**Software:** MEDITECH outreach portal, hc1 and similar tools are commodity enablers, not competitors. (U)

## 5. Evidence of demand and willingness to pay

**Positive**
- Hospitals do pay for outside lab operations: Quest's Co-Lab grew about 22% a year.
- The JV template is accepted: Corewell, FMOL/Orion, ProMedica.
- Distress is real:
  - Median health-system margin was -0.6% in January 2026 (Strata via ARUP). (S)
  - 43.5% of 2025 healthcare M&A involved a distressed party. (S)
  - Labcorp's CEO says Medicaid cuts and the end of ACA subsidies may push systems to sell. (S, search summary)
- Outreach billing is bad:
  - For hospital outreach, "procedure not paid separately" was 61.2% of denials and duplicates 22.6%.
  - Appeals recover 0.03% of receipts at $48 each. (S) [XiFin 2026](https://media.xifin.com/prod/uploads/EWC-2026-XiFin-2026-Payor-Denial-Impact-Report.pdf)

**Negative**
- Every one of the 29+ listed outreach exits went to Quest or Labcorp. I found no US case of a system choosing a venture-backed operator over cash.
- I found no open RFP for an outreach operating partner (search limited to public web).
- Sellers skew distressed, and distressed sellers want cash, not a 49% stake:
  - Jefferson sold its lab business for $108M against $231M of operating loss (FY2023).
  - Tufts cut about 600 jobs after its sale.
  - CHS was pruning non-core assets. (S) [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/jefferson-sells-lab-business-for-108m/), [Digital Health News](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/chs-completes-194m-sale-of-outpatient-lab-assets-to-labcorp)
- The signer is the CFO. Budget exists in the sense that outreach P&L exists, but the alternative on the table is a check.

## 6. Unit economics and capital

**National analogs**

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Quest 2025 adjusted operating margin | 15.9% (S) [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1022079/000102207926000005/dgx123120258-kexhibit991.htm) |
| Quest Corewell Co-Lab | About $250M of 2026 revenue "at low single-digit margins" (S, Q4 2025 remarks) |
| Labcorp on outreach acquisitions | Margins "typically lower than the company average early on" (S) [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/labcorp-buys-baystate-health-outreach-lab/695687/) |

- A scaled, synergy-rich national earns 16%. Management-type revenue earns a few points. A startup does not beat that.

**Prices paid**
- Ascension: $400M for assets with about $150M of annual revenue, or 2.7x. (S) [Labcorp release](https://labcorp.com/labcorp-announces-2021-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-results), [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/920148/000092014822000015/lh-20211231.htm)
- The gate cites BioReference at 2.4x (not re-verified by me).
- Quest's 2024 outreach buys were $230M, $200M and $183M. Revenue was undisclosed. (S) [Quest 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1022079/000102207925000044/dgx-20241231.htm)
- Conflict to note: MedTech Dive says Quest paid "$2.2B" for outreach. The 10-K says $2.2B is the aggregate for all 2024 deals, including LifeLabs at about US$1B. The three outreach buys total $613M.

**Capital (A)**
- At 2.4-2.7x, a 51% stake in $100M of outreach revenue costs about $122-138M. The gate assumed $50-90M at 1.0-1.3x.
- Nationals can pay that because they cut costs by moving testing into their own hubs. Labcorp expected to "return its cost of capital by year two" on Ascension.
- A standalone operator cannot. At an 8-15% margin, $100M of revenue is worth $80-180M at 10-12x EBITDA (A), against $122-138M paid for 51% of it.
- RDL's CEO said the same thing in 2015: hospital lab payment levels differed from independent-lab levels, prices exceeded likely value, and RDL backed away from deals. (S) [G2](https://g2intelligence.com/?p=1172)

**Hospital's side of the fee (A)**
- A $33M program at 25% contribution is $8.3M.
- A 9% fee is $3.0M, or 36% of that contribution.
- The hospital's savings pool is 10-20% of addressable costs on vendor claims (Quest/Kaufman Hall). On about $25M of direct cost that is $2.5-5.0M. The fee consumes 60-120% of it.
- Selling at 2.7x pays about 10.7 years of contribution up front.
- Cost benchmarks for couriers, patient service centers and sales as a percent of revenue were not found, so the claim that 9% covers all commercial functions is unverified.

## 7. Legal and liability

1. **Medicare referral billing contradicts the structure.**
   - CMS manual ch. 16 §40.1 lets a referring lab bill for tests another lab performed only if:
     - it is in a rural hospital, or
     - the two are wholly owned by one another or by a third entity, or
     - it refers no more than 30% of its tests. (S) [CMS manual](https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/Downloads/clm104c16.pdf)
   - A 51/49 LLC that bills while the hospital core lab does 80-85% of testing meets none of these, unless the hospital is rural.
   - I found no disclosed structure where an LLC bills for tests done in the hospital's lab. The JVs I found build or use a JV-owned performing lab (Corewell's new lab, with billing mechanics in the interim undisclosed; ProMedica's JV core lab). Kootenai instead billed Labcorp as the reference lab, and Labcorp performs most of its own testing.
   - The fix pushes capital and equipment into the JV, or reduces the model to a management contract.
2. **Billing-agent pay.**
   - Medicare may pay an agent only if the agent's compensation "is not related in any way to the dollar amounts billed or collected". (S) [42 CFR 424.73](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/424.73)
   - That blocks a percent-of-collections fee where the operator receives payment. Paying into a provider-controlled account may avoid it (U).
3. **EKRA.**
   - Payments to third-party marketers are covered. Percent pay is not per se unlawful, but it falls outside the safe harbor. (S) [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/ninth-circuit-clarifies-permissible-marketing-compensation-under-ekra-laboratories-beware/)
   - An operator with sales staff paid by percent of revenue is the target profile. This binds only the Ninth Circuit.
4. **Payer enrollment cuts both ways.**
   - Anthem New Hampshire has excluded most hospital labs since 2010. Frisbie opened an independent lab company to get back in. (S) [Dark Report](https://www.darkintelligencegroup.com/the-dark-report/clinical-laboratory-trends/locked-out-of-payer-network-nh-hospital-opens-lab-company/)
   - UnitedHealthcare's July 2026 Preferred Lab Network lists Eurofins, Labcorp, Quest and specialty labs, with no hospital lab. (S) [UHC](https://www.uhcprovider.com/en/resource-library/news/2026/preferred-lab-providers-july.html)
   - Re-enrolling at independent-lab rates opens networks but removes the premium. Hospital outpatient departments are paid about 3x independent labs, with 60% of spend. (S) [HCCI](https://healthcostinstitute.org/all-hcci-reports/price-markups-for-clinical-labs-employer-based-insurance-pays-hospital-outpatient-departments-3x-more-than-physician-offices-and-independent-labs-for-identical-tests/) Chi found nationals' net revenue per test about 20% below typical outreach.
5. **Gaps:** tax-exempt JV control rules (UBIT, Rev. Rul. 98-15), state laws and whether commercial payers copy the Medicare referral rule were not researched. Counsel is required.

## 8. Why-now

**Real**
- CMS preliminary 2027 rates average about 16% lower, with a 15% annual cap over 2027-29 and final rates due in November 2026. (S) [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/preliminary-calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-clinical-laboratory-fee-schedule-payment-rates)
- The sale wave is live: Labcorp's 13 deals in 2025, and Kaufman Hall expects acceleration into 2026. (S) [Vizient/Kaufman Hall](https://www.vizient.com/insights/articles/behind-the-boom-in-health-system-lab-transactions)

**Weak**
- PAMA cuts have been delayed repeatedly. The 2026 appropriations act moved them to 2027, and the lab industry is lobbying for the RESULTS Act again in the lame duck. (S, search summary) [MDDI](https://www.mddionline.com/medical-device-markets/quest-diagnostics-urges-congress-to-act-on-lab-payment-reform-as-cuts-loom)
- Quest's cash spent on acquisitions fell from $2.16B in 2024 to $101M in 2025. It shifted to Co-Lab and JVs.
- The shock favors whoever has cash and hubs.

## 10. Moat assessment (step 9)

| Moat | Quest/Labcorp | Regional independents (Orion, Sonic) | The pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network effects | Moderate (density) | Weak | Weak |
| Switching costs | Strong (Kootenai had a 5-year noncompete; EMR/billing lock-in) | Moderate | Moderate if contracts are long |
| Scale economies | Strong (hubs, couriers) | Moderate | Weak |
| Intangibles (payer contracts, licenses) | Strong | Moderate | Weak at start |

- Structural vulnerability: the buyer's best alternative is cash, and a successful operator is acquired by Labcorp (PAML).
- Durable advantage: none found. The "control" pitch is the only difference, and Orion and Sonic already offer it.

## 11. What would change my mind

1. Signed LOIs from 2+ systems with at least $30M of outreach to accept equity rather than cash.
2. A healthcare counsel opinion that a 51/49 LLC can bill without owning the performing lab.
3. A sponsor LOI for $120M+ at under 1.5x revenue.
4. One payer letter of intent for preferred network status.

If these cannot be had, the surviving variant is advisory-led. That is the boutique outcome the round-2 rules say to call dead.

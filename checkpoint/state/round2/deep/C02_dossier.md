# C02 dossier: P061 "Fiscal-intermediary consolidator with a payroll-certainty SLA"

Research date 2026-10-18. Marks: (A) my arithmetic or assumption; † = read only in a search-result summary, page not opened; Louisiana is taken from the gate file because the memo returned 403.

## Verdict

The spend is real, mandated and signed by a state. But the fee is only about 2% of program spend, the only public cost data I found (state-reviewed Wisconsin summaries) shows the sector losing money at about 28,000 participants, the one $100M-class contract (New York) is locked until about 2030 and under federal attack, recent rebids went to multi-state incumbents, and private equity is already rolling up the same assets. I rate this **weak-to-moderate, not "strong"**. The gate's $20M median revenue is plausible. Profit at that size is near zero. The $110M upside needs conditions that do not exist today.

## 1. Industry metrics (Step 0)

What this industry runs on: admin fee per participant per month (PMPM), participants under contract, operating cost per participant-month, contract term and rebid cycle, working-capital and reserve rules, and past-performance eligibility.

## 2. Market: bottom-up

- **Pool.** The 2023 inventory counted 1,520,267 self-directing people and says California is 48% of them ([NCI deck](https://nci-ad.org/images/uploads/SarahTaubWebSeries_1.24_.25_.pdf)). California's IHSS (800,000+ recipients) runs on state and county payroll, not FMS vendors ([CHCF](https://www.chcf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/InHomeSupportiveServicesIntegrationManagedCare.pdf)†). So at most about 790,000 people are outside California (A); the gate's 0.7-1.0M is at or above that ceiling. New York's CDPAP is about 250,000 of them ([Lawyer Monthly](https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2026/06/justice-department-sues-ppl-new-york-medicaid-fraud/)). That leaves **about 540,000 outside CA and NY (A)**, an upper bound because it includes veteran-directed and non-FMS programs.
- **Price points.**
  - New York bid: $68.50 PMPM, per the DOJ complaint ([Lawyer Monthly](https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2026/06/justice-department-sues-ppl-new-york-medicaid-fraud/)).
  - Wisconsin: realized $76.50 in 2025 ([DHS](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/publications/p03612-4q-25.pdf)); the 2026 contract rate is $82.77 ([contract](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/iris/cy2026-iris-fea-contract.pdf)).
  - Colorado: $103.21 for both Palco and PPL ([HCPF](https://hcpf.colorado.gov/sites/hcpf/files/HCPF%20OM%2024-039%20Consumer-Directed%20Attendant%20Support%20Services%20%28CDASS%29%20%20Changes%20to%20Per-Member-Per-Month%20%28PMPM%29%20Rates.pdf)†).
  - Texas: $114.40 for consumer-directed services and $210.08 for STAR+PLUS plans ([TCHP](https://www.texaschildrenshealthplan.org/news/provider-alert/hhsc-publishes-payment-rates-financial-management-services-agencies)†, [UHC](https://www.uhcprovider.com/en/resource-library/news/2022/tx-star-plus-billing-change.html)†).
  - Louisiana: $105.88 ([memo](https://www.ldh.la.gov/assets/docs/OAAS/Support-Coordination-Memos/OAAS-SC-23-022-New-Service-SD-Financial-Management-Services-Admin-Fee-I-9-29-23.pdf), not re-verified).
  - PPL says pre-2025 New York fees ran $150-$1,050 ([PPL, self-interested](https://pplfirst.com/?p=20928)).
- **Pattern.** Competitive statewide awards price at $68-83. Only state rate-card or plan-paid programs reach $103-210.
- **Pool arithmetic (A).** 540,000 x $76-103 x 12 = $0.5-0.67B outside NY and CA. New York adds $0.17-0.21B (209,000-250,000 people at $68.50). The fee pool is **about $0.7-0.9B**, below the gate's $0.8-1.2B.
- **Headline contract values mislead.** Wisconsin's single-vendor contract is reported at $1.4B ([AOL/Center Square](https://www.aol.com/articles/questions-linger-wisconsin-medicaid-manager-160000138.html)). With 29,000 participants and $1.5B of 2026 cost ([WisPolitics](https://www.wispolitics.com/?p=419079)†), the vendor's fee is about $29M a year, roughly 2% (A). New York's roughly $9B program yields about $172-205M a year of fees (A), matching the "just over $1 billion over five years" report†.
- **Participants needed for $100M (A).** About 109,000 at $76.50, 101,000 at $82.77, or 81,000 at $103. That is **15-20% of the non-NY, non-CA pool**, or about half of New York's 209,000.
- **New York volume is unstable.** The state reported about 280,000 authorized before the switch, 209,000 registered with PPL by May 2025, and 75,000 moved to agency-based personal care ([NY DOH](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/press/releases/2025/2025-05-19_cdpap_update.htm)). FI-served consumers fell about 25% in one rebid.

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

- **Mandated spend, state signer.** MACPAC lists FMS as a core function of self-direction, including EVV operation ([MACPAC](https://macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/03_February-Slides_Self-Direction-for-Home-and-Community-Based-Services-Key-actors-in-program-administration.pdf)). Signers are state Medicaid or developmental-disability directors, or plans in Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Texas.
- **The procurement wave is real.**
  - New York: awarded 2024. Reportedly only 4 of 130+ bidders qualified ([City & State](https://www.cityandstateny.com/policy/2025/08/state-planned-give-no-bid-contract-ppl-lawmaker-claims/407621/)†).
  - West Virginia: 2024.
  - Wisconsin: three or four incumbent FEAs to one (press says three; the 2025 financials list four), start April 2027 ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/questions-linger-wisconsin-medicaid-manager-160000138.html)).
  - Kentucky: RFP due 2026-11-06. The two listings carry conflicting dates and no participant count ([Govly](https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16859242)). I could not size it.
  - Ohio DODD: closed 2026-01-14, award not found ([SamSearch](https://samsearch.co/gov-explore/sled/ddd-statewide-financial-management-services-for-the-department-of-developmental/SRC0000028354)†).
- **But winners are incumbents with past performance.** The New York RFP required a statewide FI contract in another state as of 2024-04-01 and a **$100M line of credit** ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/11/cdpap-fiscal-intermediary-takes-aim-at-dohs-competitive)). It also required being in business before 2012 ([HHCN](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2024/09/competition-heats-up-for-new-york-cdpap-contract/)). In every award I found, the winner already held statewide FMS contracts elsewhere (PPL, Tempus, Palco); no company without that record won.
- **Payroll certainty is already a contract term, not a differentiator.** Wisconsin treats a missed payroll deadline as grounds for fiscal corrective action and can withhold or recover fee payments ([contract](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/iris/cy2026-iris-fea-contract.pdf)). Connecticut bills would add penalties for late payroll ([SB 487](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2026/cbs/S/pdf/SB-0487.pdf); [SB 498 analysis](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2026/ba/pdf/2026SB-00498-R000487-BA.pdf)†). Passage is not confirmed. The demand signal is real, but it makes the SLA table stakes.
- **Evidence that fees can move.** Wisconsin's 2026 rate is $82.77 against $76.50 realized in 2025, about +8% (A). Plans in New York dispute PPL's request for $0.55 more per hour ([Spectrum](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nys/central-ny/politics/2025/12/11/n-y--health-plans-accuse-medicaid-home-care-company-of-violating-antitrust-laws)).

## 5. Unit economics and capital

**Wisconsin FEAs, the best hard analog** ([2025](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/publications/p03612-4q-25.pdf), [2024](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/publications/p03612-4q-24.pdf)). Dollars are per participant per month.

| FEA | 2025 revenue | 2025 operating cost | 2025 net | 2024 net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GT Independence (about 8,500 people) | $77.47 | $76.02 | +$1.46 | +$5.00 |
| iLife | $75.73 | $85.40 | -$9.67 | -$13.33 |
| Premier | $77.84 | $103.34 | -$25.51 | -$21.18 |
| Outreach | $76.12 | $94.39 | -$38.41 | +$1.99 |
| **All four (about 27,700 people)** | **$76.50** | **$84.87** | **-$9.53 (-12.5%)** | **-$9.03 (-11.8%)** |

- **Implication.** The best operators' cost ($71-76) leaves about 2-7% margin at $77. iLife, the largest at about 14,800 people, costs more per head than GT at about 8,500, so scale alone did not lower cost. A 10-15% margin at $76-83 requires cost of about $65-75. Only the two best Wisconsin results in 2024 ($71) fall inside that range, and only at the $83 price. New York's $68.50 is under the best Wisconsin cost. That implies either large scale economies or under-costing; DOJ alleges PPL misrepresented its costs (unproven).
- **Tempus** earned about $7.9M on $1.99B in 2023 (0.4%) with $23M of staff wages ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/042239746)). It is a nonprofit, so this is not a margin analog.
- **Regulatory capital is small.** Wisconsin requires working capital of at least 2% of revenue plus a restricted reserve at 8%, 4%, 3%, 2% and 1% across revenue tiers ([contract](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/iris/cy2026-iris-fea-contract.pdf)). At $100M revenue that is about $4M (A). It also requires $1M general and professional liability, D&O, umbrella and a fidelity bond.
- **Payroll float is the real capital need.** New York's roughly $36k per participant (A) implies about $59M a week for 85,000 people. A two-week backstop is about $120M, consistent with the $100M line New York demanded. PPL paid more than $240M in its first April weeks ([HHCN](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2025/04/new-york-consumers-home-care-workers-protest-cdpap/)†). I found no sourced commitment-fee pricing.
- **Acquisition cost.** I found no prices for any FMS deal; H.I.G.'s GT terms were undisclosed.

## 6. Regulatory and liability landscape

- **Tax.** An IRS Section 3504 agent is jointly and severally liable with each employer for employment taxes and penalties, using Form 2678 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/third-party-payer-arrangements-section-3504-agents)). The FI carries every participant-employer's tax risk.
- **Wage-hour.** Joint-employer status is contested. Talarico v. PPL went against PPL in the Third Circuit in 2020 and for PPL in an unpublished May 2026 opinion ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/home-care-workers-fail-in-joint-employer-unpaid-overtime-appeal)†). New York's Calderon class action settled for **$162M** ([Legal Aid](https://legalaidnyc.org/court-case/philip-calderon-v-public-partnerships-llc/)†). That is about 79-94% of one year of New York fee revenue (A). One report breaks it into $92M unused vacation, $40.5M general damages and $25M health insurance†.
- **Fraud.** DOJ seeks a receiver under 18 U.S.C. 1345. Wisconsin can suspend fee payments on a credible fraud allegation (42 CFR 455.23). CMS named personal care and HCBS for scrutiny in February 2026 ([HHCN](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/02/cms-targets-medicaid-personal-care-hcbs-in-sweeping-fraud-crackdown/)). All 48 responding states cross-check claims to EVV, and 21 use or plan AI ([KFF via HHCN](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/10/states-go-beyond-federal-requirements-to-fight-medicaid-home-care-fraud/)).
- **Political.** New York bills would restore multiple FIs; passage not confirmed ([HHCN](https://homehealthcarenews.com/2025/05/new-york-lawmakers-introduce-bill-to-reverse-cdpap-changes/)†). Wisconsin legislators want the PPL award paused. The single-vendor model is now politically contested, which cuts both ways for a challenger.

## 7. Is the why-now real?

Partly. Consolidation procurements are live, and New York cut its fee from a reported $150-$1,000+ to $68.50 (state and PPL figures, unaudited). But New York's day-one payroll failures, the DOJ suit and the settlement now make states cautious about single vendors and about new entrants. The $100M prize is a five-year contract from April 2025† with no rebid scheduled. A new New York slot needs a receivership or legislative reversal, both speculative.

## 9. Synthesis

**Moats.**
- Switching costs: **Moderate.** States do switch (West Virginia from PPL to Palco, Pennsylvania's managed-care waiver from PPL to Tempus in 2022 ([PA providers](https://www.paproviders.org/?p=25195)†), Colorado after Acumen left).
- Scale economies: **Unproven** below 30,000 people; New York's $68.50 suggests they exist at 200,000+, but that is unaudited.
- Intangibles (past performance, state certification): **Moderate**, and held by incumbents.
- Network effects: **None.**

**Scenarios (A, my judgment).**

| Scenario | Probability | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| Bull | 10% | A New York receivership or rebid, or two or three large state consolidations, gives $100M+ revenue. |
| Base | 50% | Acquire a small FI and win one or two mid-size states: $15-35M revenue at 0-6% margin. |
| Bear | 40% | Lose rebids to PPL, Tempus or Palco, or get outbid for targets by H.I.G.: $5-10M, thin. |

**Under the lessons filter:** the payer and budget line are real, and the legal premise is checked. Fails the hidden-incumbent test (funded consolidators already exist) and the fee is too thin to pay for a guaranteed-payroll SLA. Lesson 7 applies. The gate already shrank the idea from "30% under" to an SLA, and the SLA is a contract term.

**Cheap tests before any commitment.**
1. Obtain Wisconsin's RFP price scoring and the protest outcome.
2. Get Kentucky's participant count.
3. Ask two FI operators for cost per participant-month at 10,000 and 50,000 people.
4. Wait for the E.D.N.Y. ruling on the receiver request.

**Gaps.** No FMS deal prices. No vendor margins outside Wisconsin. No Ohio or Kentucky awards or sizes. No rejection-rate data. The Wisconsin award outcome is unknown.

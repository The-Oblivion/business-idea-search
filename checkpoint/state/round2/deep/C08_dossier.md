# C08 dossier: "Last Mile" (P022, idea 1), a locate-first, net-recovery operator for the default chain

Date: 2026-10-18. About 60 searches and fetches. Tags: [S] sourced, [D] my arithmetic on sourced inputs, [A] assumption. Where a page would not load and I relied on a search-result summary, I say so. CURepossession and Auto Remarketing are trade blogs: useful, but secondary and often vendor- or agent-slanted.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: wounded. Overall 3/10, upside 3/10.** The budget line is real and the pilot is cheap. But every step of the product is already sold by a funded incumbent, one of them aimed at the idea's exact first customers as of October 2025. The idea's headline number is misread. And $100M of net revenue needs roughly a fifth of the whole forwarder profit pool.

1. **The "29% recovery ratio" is not a failure rate** (section 2). It counts cures, cancellations and voluntary surrenders. It cannot carry the "invisible failure" story.
2. **No gap on the product** (section 3). Locate, forwarding and remarketing are sold today by Primeritus, Resolvion, Servicing Solutions, Location Services and DRN. Percent-of-recovery pricing and time-tiered fees already exist.
3. **The market is real but the idea's share is heroic** (section 4). $100M net is 7-21% of remarketable units and about 20% of the forwarder spread pool [D].
4. **The key input is owned by a competitor and is being regulated** (section 8): DRN plate data, and state LPR bills.
5. **Contingency recovery is a thin-margin business at scale** (section 6).

## 2. The 29% number, corrected

- RDN data as relayed by CURepossession: 2,422,883 unique assignments and 706,393 repossessions in Q2 2025, a 29% ratio. The author never defines the ratio and says "unique" means one assignment per vehicle. [S] https://curepossession.com/rdn-repossession-volume-report-q2-2025/
- The CFPB's data pilot (about 14.6% of the market) measures it properly. 27% of assignments were completed in Sept 2022, vs 38% in Sept 2019 and 41% pre-2020. Completions include voluntary surrenders (15-19% in 2018-19; 10-13% for subprime from Nov 2021). 25-34% of completed repossessions were later redeemed by the borrower (25% Dec 2019, 34% Dec 2021, 30% Nov 2022). Of accounts assigned, 54% were assigned only once (eight lenders). Average time from assignment to completion was 15.9-23.1 days. The report does not split non-completions into cures versus failed hunts. [S] https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_report-repossession-in-auto-finance_2025-01.pdf
- Dollar recovery is a different number: KBRA put non-prime ABS recoveries at 38.2% in 2024 [S, via search summary; page returned 403] https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250424955483/en/KBRA-Releases-Research-Tariffs-May-Offer-a-Temporary-Boost-to-U-S-Auto-ABS-Recoveries. Fitch had subprime recoveries at 39.5% in June 2026 and 38.0% in July 2026 [S] https://www.autoremarketing.com/?p=130785. **A 1.5-point move in one month** is the market noise any "+3 points" claim must beat. Only a randomized pilot can show it.
- Success-fee pay is already standard. Credit Acceptance says its repossession provider is paid on a contingency basis [S, search snippet of 10-K] https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885550/000088555026000047/cacc-20251231.htm. CPS assigns at day 60-90 to "an independent national repossession service" [S, read in 10-K] https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/889609/000168316826001856/cps_i10k-123125.htm.

## 4. Bottom-up market

**Units.** Cox: about 1.73M repossessions at auction in 2024 [S] https://www.auto123.com/en/news/vehicle-repossessions-rising-usa/72577, expected to stay near 1.7M through 2029 [S, search summary] https://www.autoremarketing.com/?p=71419. RDN (blog): 2,211,057 repossessions through Q3 2025, about 2.95M annualized [S, search summary] https://curepossession.com/top-ten-states-for-repossession-volume-ytd-q3-2025/. Range 1.7-3.0M. Forwarders handled 66% of completions by Dec 2022 (CFPB, above). Remarketable (not redeemed) is roughly 70%: 1.2-2.1M [D].

**Fees.** Forwarder bills of $575 (Westlake, Feb 2018) to $800 (Chase 2018) against agent pay of $300 (2018) to $365 (A1 Nationwide, Aug 2025) [S] https://curepossession.com/a1-nationwide-raises-the-standard-in-fees-and-agency-protections/. I found no 2025 forwarder rate card. The idea's $825 is from a 2019-era editorial.

**Pool.** 0.66 x 1.7-3.0M x $575-800 = **$0.65-1.6B of forwarder fees**; spread over agent pay is about $0.5B at the midpoint [D]. The idea's $2.6-4.3B "chain cost" adds transport, auction and title, which Last Mile cannot capture.

**Path to $100M.** The idea's $650 net per unit needs transport ($250) outside Last Mile's bill. If transport sits inside the $1,000 bill, net is about $400 [D].

| Case | Units for $100M | Share of remarketable units |
|---|---|---|
| Gross $1,000/unit | 100k | 5-8% |
| Net $650 | 154k | 7-13% |
| Net $400 | 250k | 12-21% |

Net $100M is also about 20% of the $0.5B spread pool [D].

**Payers.** I could not source a lender count; the gate's 20-25 reachable non-captive lenders is plausible but unsourced. Sizing: Exeter had about 720k contracts and $13.6B at Sept 2025 [S, search summary of SEC filings] https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2049379/000092963826001229/a10k.htm, so at an assumed 8-12% annual repossession rate it alone generates 58-86k repossessions [A]. CPS ($3.69B average portfolio, 7.8% net charge-offs) is plausibly 20-25k [A]. A $100M gross target is about 20% of the entire flow of 20 lenders averaging 25k [D]. Resolvion advises lenders to run 3-4 vendors and reallocate share quarterly, with the top 1-2 getting most [S] https://resolvion.com/repo-assignment-management-strategy-best-practices/. So it means being a top-two vendor at most of them.

**Credit unions are a tiny wedge.** NCUA shows $485B of CU auto loans, 0.81% delinquent (Q2 2026; $485B is my sum of used and new) [S] https://ncua.gov/files/publications/analysis/quarterly-data-summary-2026-Q2.pdf, against subprime ABS delinquency of 5.8-6.1% (Fitch above). Philadelphia FCU, a $1B+ CU, assigned 15-20 vehicles a month in 2017 (about 200 a year, or $0.2M of gross billing). "CUs with $1-5B auto books" are not 8-30k-repossession accounts.

## 5. Demand and willingness to pay

- **For:** lenders already pay for speed (Chase tiers). Recoveries are historically weak (above). Vendors claim 7-35% recovery lifts, but all are self-reported with no method. A Resolvion case claims $4.0MM a year of net auction proceeds from moving to a day-90 skip rotation (own claim).
- **Against:** I found **no public RFP, no contract and no lender statement** asking for net-proceeds pricing. Lenders buy a service they already understand at a per-unit price. The pain has a soft side: CFPB found lenders typically bill repossession costs to borrowers, and forwarder use was associated with higher fees to consumers.
- **"Voluntary hand-back costs a fraction" is overstated.** A1 pays agents $265 for a voluntary versus $365 for an involuntary, a 27% saving on one leg. There is no evidence of a condition benefit.
- **Free substitutes:** lender-direct DRNsights, existing 3-4 vendor panels with quarterly reallocation, and in-house teams.

## 6. Unit economics analogs

- **Forwarder spread.** Chase's $675-800 against a $300 agent payment is a 56-63% gross spread [D]. Agents say they need about $125 of revenue per case [S, Levison, Resolvion's CEO, 2018] https://www.autoremarketing.com/?p=48284. Forwarder overhead (case managers, compliance, insurance, technology) comes out of the spread.
- **Contingency recovery analog.** Performant (outcome-fee recovery, healthcare and student loans) guided to $117-122M of 2024 healthcare revenue but only $4-5M of adjusted EBITDA, and its payers could reset the fee basis (the Department of Education moved to a flat $1,710 per rehabilitated loan) [S] https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1550695/000155069524000024/pfmt-20231231.htm (10-K) and 2024 guidance via search summary https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241106255017/en. At $100M of revenue a recovery operator earns single-digit EBITDA margins, so $100M gross is worth $5-10M of profit, not a venture outcome [A].
- **Net-proceeds pricing adds variance.** Lift share moves with the wholesale index (Cox cut its year-end Manheim index forecast from +2% to +0.2% in Oct 2026; the Sept index of 205.9, down 0.6% year over year, is from a search summary [S] https://www.coxautoinc.com/press-releases/q3-2026-muvvi-press-release/). The operator eats downside when values fall and the lender sees windfall as its own.

## 7. Capital

- **Insurance and licensing.** Wrongful-repossession GL of $1M/$2M, on-hook $100K, garagekeepers $350K recommended; a forwarder cannot technically buy garagekeepers and uses a property-of-others policy [S] https://resolvion.com/managing-risk-understanding-insurance-coverages/. Illinois requires $1M/$3M for agencies [S] https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/022504220K90.htm.
- **Scale needs sponsor capital.** The two scaled forwarders have PE owners (Kinderhook; SBJ and TZP), and Primeritus's CARS deal alone was a $102.5M facility. A 50-agency pilot is cheap. A national operator is not [A].
- **Working capital.** Net-proceeds settlement waits on an auction sale after a 16-23 day average chase (CFPB), plus hold periods. I found no days-to-sale figure; I estimate $3-5M to pilot and staff, and $20-40M to reach $25M of billings [A].

## 8. Regulatory and liability

- **Breach of peace sits on the secured party.** An Illinois court awarded $24K+ against a repo company in Oct 2025 [S] https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2025/10/14/judge-awards-24k-after-repo-found-to-breach-the-peace/. A California court kept a bank in a wrongful-repossession case in Dec 2025 [S] https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2025/12/29/judge-denies-mtd-in-fdcpa-rfdcpa-case-over-repossession/. A Nevada footnote says secured parties answer for agents [S] https://swlaw.com/publication/nevada-court-of-appeals-clarifies-what-constitutes-a-breach-of-the-peace-when-repossessing-vehicles. An operator paid on net proceeds will be asked whether the incentive rewards aggressive grabs.
- **Licensing.** Michigan's Supreme Court held in June 2014 that forwarding is a collection activity [S] https://curepossession.com/michigan-supreme-court-rules-against-forwarders; Illinois exempts pure forwarders [S] https://lig.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/022504220K30.htm; California is the strictest. The "scheduled hand-back" step means contacting borrowers, which likely brings state collection licensing and Reg F/TCPA exposure [A, counsel needed].
- **LPR (the locate input).** Easing: California's appeals court affirmed summary judgment for DRN on July 20, 2026, requiring actual harm [S] https://www.passwordprotectedlaw.com/2026/07/california-court-rules-automated-license-plate-recognition-law-requires-actual-harm/. Tightening: Arkansas's AG said in Dec 2023 that LPR for repossession is illegal [S] https://hudsoncook.com/article/arkansas-limits-use-of-license-plate-readers-for-repossession. Kentucky HB 58 (90-day retention, limits on commercial use, borrower disclosure from 2027) awaited the governor in April 2026; I did not confirm its outcome [S] https://curepossession.com/kentucky-bill-seriously-threatens-lpr-data-access-for-the-repossession-industry/. Colorado's repossession bill failed in 2026 [S] https://curepossession.com/colorado-lpr-bill-dies-but-a-trend-is-developing/.
- **Federal vacuum, political heat.** The CFPB rescinded its repossession bulletin in 2025 [S, search summary] https://niada.com/dashboard/?p=5928 and Senator Warren sent 12 letters in Feb 2026 [S] https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/with-trump-sidelining-cfpb-warren-launches-probe-into-the-auto-lending-industry-as-car-repossessions-skyrocket. Enforcement shifts to state AGs and private suits [S] https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/insights-finance-cfs-yir-auto-loan-origination-and-servicing.

## 9. Why now: half real

Real: repossessions have sat at the highest level since 2009 for two years (Cox ~1.7M at auction, flat through 2029). Fitch subprime delinquency was 5.80% at mid-2026 vs 5.60% at mid-2025 (6.13% in July), and annualized subprime net loss 8.42% vs 8.17% [S] https://www.autoremarketing.com/?p=130785. Wholesale values are soft, and Cox's mid-September deck reportedly notes the Fed's first hike since 2023 (search summary; Cox link above). Used up: this is a plateau, not a shock. Subprime delinquency eased from 6.50% at year-end 2025 to 5.80% at mid-2026 (same Fitch source). The Tricolor-era investor-reporting demand is unsupported by anything I found.

## 11. Verdict and what would change it

As specified, this is a forwarder with a better contract. The customers it can win cheaply (CUs) are too small, the customers that are big are served by incumbents and in-house teams, and the one differentiator (net-proceeds pricing) is a term sheet, not a moat. The only route to $100M I can see is **buying forwarders**: capital-heavy and thin-margin. The idea should not be narrowed into a CU boutique; that is the "shrink to survive" failure.

**Would raise my score:** a randomized pilot of 3,000+ accounts at one 20k+ lender showing 3+ points of net-dollar recovery at p<0.05, plus a written commitment of 30%+ of that lender's flow. Also a DRN-independent locate source.

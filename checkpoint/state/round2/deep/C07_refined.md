# C07 refined: "Day 21" (P108, idea 7) after three fatal critiques

Date: 2026-10-18. Tags: [V] verified by me this session in primary text, [C] taken from a critic or the dossier and not re-read, [A] my assumption or arithmetic.

## Verdict: DEAD on the $100M test (alive = false)

I looked for a change of wedge, payer, price, product, sequencing or moat that keeps a credible path to $100M a year. There isn't one that survives the evidence. The honest upside is about $55M, and it needs four independent breaks to go the right way. The honest median is about $4-5M. I am not narrowing this into a boutique to keep it alive. The lessons.md rule applies: call it dead.

Five facts decide it:

1. **The court mandate is stayed.** [V] On 2026-09-18 a Fifth Circuit panel (Smith, Southwick, Douglas) granted Texas a stay pending appeal in Ward v. Muth, No. 26-50619. I read the order. It says "all of the stay factors favor HHSC", that HHSC is "likely to succeed on the merits" on three grounds (Jackson v. Indiana probably does not apply to pre-hospitalization detainees; there is no substantive-due-process right to a 21-day limit; the waitlist is likely to pass rational-basis review "with flying colors"), and that the class will not be harmed by waiting out the four-year clock. A footnote calls it a preliminary ruling and leaves the merits to another panel. The dossier ("no ruling as of Oct 18") and the gate ("main risk is the Fifth Circuit") both missed that the risk had already landed. Without the order Texas has no legal duty to buy a bed.
2. **The new-contract demand is already assigned.** [V] HHSC's FY2028-29 request (agency submission 2026-09-04) puts $256.4M (FY28) and $258.9M (FY29) in exceptional item 4 to operate capacity at named counterparties: Texas Behavioral Health Center, Montgomery County, Permian Basin, Doctors Hospital at Renaissance (up to 286 beds) and Victoria County plus Baptist Beaumont (up to 132 beds). The only open contract-bed ask is item 7: 65 beds for extended forensic commitments, inside a $22.4M-a-year item that also pays for Forensic Support Teams. At $697 a bed-day that is about $15.7M a year [A]. The text also says "contracted entities have expressed interest", which means the incumbents are already in line. It is a request, not an appropriation.
3. **The contract is not financeable.** [C, consistent across two critics] HHSC's one public forensic-bed contract (HHS001336800001, 16 geriatric beds) requires a licensed hospital with Joint Commission or CMS-deemed accreditation, allows termination for convenience at any time, guarantees no quantity, caps spend, and puts routine medical costs inside the rate. No lender or sale-leaseback buyer funds buildings against that. A start-up cannot sell the product without a licensed host.
4. **The buyer owns or grants.** [C] Texas has put more than $2.5B into 680+ owned beds and $239M into grants. Georgia ($409M plus a $20.7M jail unit) and Nevada ($399M, 298 beds, finishing 2029; I confirmed the headline) are building. Colorado's Special Master says contract beds should never be the primary strategy, and its decree ends 2027-12-01 [C]. I searched for a 2026 RFP or award for a private operator of 100+ forensic beds in any state and found none.
5. **Capital markets have passed on the scaled version.** [C] Wellpath's behavioral unit ($425M revenue) drew about 70 NDAs and no qualifying outside bid, and went to its lenders on a $395M credit bid, about 0.93x revenue. At 8-12% EBITDA, $100M of revenue is $8-12M of EBITDA.

The only structural insight that survives is real but small. [V, LAR] The state hospital forensic share is 71% (up from 47% in FY2015) and forensic length of stay rose from 182 to 261 days. Contracted beds are designed for 60-180 day restoration, so extended-commitment patients sit in state beds and block admissions (average transfer wait 107.7 days). [C] The opinion records zero contracted beds offline against 237 state beds offline for staffing. That supports "operators fill beds the state cannot staff", but only the buyer's own plans decide how many beds it outsources, and the LAR has assigned them.

## The strongest honest version (what I would pitch if forced to)

**Forensic program operator for licensed host hospitals.** The company supplies the forensic clinical program (restoration curriculum, court liaison, security protocols, forensic psychiatry and psychology staffing) inside a Joint Commission-accredited psychiatric hospital or hospital-district shell that already holds the license and the building. It is paid per bed-day through the host.

- **First product:** a 40-65 bed extended-forensic-commitment unit in Texas, matching exactly the 65-bed item 7 ask.
- **Customer and signer:** HHSC (Associate Commissioner for State Hospitals approves ramp plans; the Legislature funds it). The host hospital is the contract party. HHSC must approve the subcontract in writing.
- **Price:** HHSC's anchor is about $697 a bed-day for full-service hospital beds. Colorado pays $1,050 for contracted hospital beds and $421 for jail-based beds [C]. I assume a $650-750 all-in rate, 95% occupancy and a 55-65% share to the operator [A].
- **Capital:** a few million dollars of working capital, no real estate.
- **Moat:** none reachable at scale. A track record plus a host relationship is a weak cornered resource and anyone can copy it. Switching costs are voided by termination for convenience.

**Upside assumptions (all four are needed; I put the joint probability under 5%):**
1. The 90th Legislature funds item 7 and adds about 100 more contract beds. A new entrant wins or hosts about 80 of 165. Roughly $19M at $697 and 95% occupancy.
2. By year 4-5, the Texas unit gives enough past performance to win one Florida-style, five-year competitive operating contract for a state-owned facility, about 150 beds at roughly $380 a bed-day. Roughly $20M. The $380 is my escalation of GEO's 2006 SFETC figure ($24M a year for 200 beds); I did not find a current value. Florida's ITN 2425 012 for SFETC (term 2026-07-01 to 2031-06-30) shows this is how Florida buys, but I found no award and the incumbents have held these contracts for about 20 years.
3. One more state funds contract beds despite the Special Master. About 50 beds at about $1,050. Roughly $18M.
4. The Fifth Circuit stay does not turn into a Texas "no duty" ruling that also drains political will in other states.

## Riskiest assumption

That there is unassigned, competitively awarded contract-bed demand a non-incumbent can win in the 2027-28 cycle, and that a licensed host will put its license behind an outside operator. Everything else is downstream of that.

## Two-week test

Cost: a few hundred dollars and about 30 hours.
- **Day 1:** file a Texas Public Information Act request to HHSC for every private inpatient restoration and forensic contract (vendor, rate, beds, term, occupancy, termination language) and any planned solicitation for the item 7 beds. Responses may run past two weeks. The calls below don't wait on it.
- **Days 1-12:** call 10 people who sign or advise: HHSC state hospitals contract management; the House and Senate Article II budget staff; the Texas Conference of Urban Counties (which commented on the LAR); two to three hospital-district or private-hospital CEOs who hold, or could hold, a Joint Commission-accredited psychiatric license; the Montgomery County administrator; Colorado's forensic office on its contract-bed asks.
- **Ask:** "Will the 65 extended-commitment beds go out for competitive solicitation, who is eligible, and would you host an outside forensic operator?"
- **Go:** one licensed host signs a non-binding letter of intent AND an HHSC or legislative source confirms on the record a solicitation for at least 100 unassigned contract beds within 12 months.
- **Stop:** anything else. Without those two things the business is a manager waiting on named incumbents, and I would not spend more than the records request.

## Tripwires that would reopen it

1. The Fifth Circuit merits panel affirms, or the stay is vacated or narrowed.
2. The 2027 General Appropriations Act funds 200+ new contracted forensic beds through an open solicitation (decided March-May 2027).
3. A firm multi-year contract form with a payout on early termination, or a lender term sheet against an HHSC contract.
4. A second state issues a funded RFP for 200+ private forensic beds with no in-house build competing.
5. A licensed, accredited host with a balance sheet commits to the build.

## The only $100M-shaped route is outside this brief

The scale precedent exists, but it is owned by incumbents: Recovery Solutions (about 3,700 staff, 70 facilities, lender-owned and debt-free [C]) and GEO Care. A $100M forensic operator in this niche is built by acquiring a scaled operator, not by entering with a wedge. That is a roughly $150-400M capital question for a PE or strategic buyer, with unknown availability. It is not a founder business, and I'm flagging it only so nobody mistakes "the category is large" for "this entry is large".

# C03 dossier: Neutral HCBS operating system for D-SNP-aligned Medicaid plans (pool P061, idea 5)

Researched 2026-10-18. Tags: (S) read in a source this session, (A) my arithmetic or assumption, (U) unverified. Where a page would not load, I say the figure came through a search summary. About 55 searches, fetches and filing pulls.

## 1. Bottom line

Wounded, and the upside case does not hold. The category is real and the buyer has a budget line: CareBridge reached 115,000 patients and a reported $2.6B of 2023 revenue in about four years and was bought for a reported $2.7B. But four things weaken the pick.

1. **The "neutral vacuum" is unproven, and the sharpened wedge is already occupied.** I found no rival plan leaving CareBridge. Elevance now says CareBridge supports "nearly 200,000 patients" and moved into D-SNPs in 2025.
2. **The gate missed independent competitors** that sell to the same buyer: CareCentrix, Cityblock plus Homeward, and HHAeXchange's payer tools.
3. **The 5% net margin behind the 67-90k member path is at the top of observed analogs** (3.4% for Carelon Services, 5-8% for Evolent).
4. **The "savings only" pilot cannot reach $100M** (section 3).

Revenue test: $100M of gross premium needs only about 4,000 fully capitated members, so the revenue rule is not binding. Profit is what matters, and $100M of profit needs about 67,000 at-risk members, or 3.6% of all managed-LTSS users (A). My read: the gate's $20M median is fair, and the $100M upside is under 10% likely (judgment). The credible large outcome is a strategic sale to a plan, not a standalone $100M-profit company.

## 3. Market: bottom-up

- **Managed-LTSS users, July 2024 (S):** 1,426,227 in comprehensive managed care plus 429,894 in MLTSS-only plans = 1,856,121 ([CMS dataset via API](https://data.medicaid.gov/api/1/datastore/query/5394bcab-c748-5e4b-af07-b5bf77ed3aa3/0)). State-reported, includes nursing-facility users, Medicaid-only and duals; CMS warns definitions differ.
- **Concentration (S):** NY 346,892 (mostly NY MLTC-only plans), CA 312,162, TX 152,030, FL 139,572, PA 131,370 = 1,082,026, or 58%. Only 25 states report any.
- **Spend per user (S/A):** MACPAC FY2023: 6.6M LTSS users, $329.0B (38.7% of $850.1B benefit spending), about $49.8k each across all services ([MACPAC](https://www.macpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/EXHIBIT-20.-Distribution-of-Medicaid-Enrollment-and-Benefit-Spending-by-Users-and-Non-Users-of-Long-Term-Services-and-Supports-FY-2023.pdf)). The idea's $30k of managed HCBS spend per member is plausible.
- **Aligned D-SNP pool (S):** 6,084,997 D-SNP enrollees, of which 1,975,250 HIDE and 743,683 FIDE, so 2,718,933 integrated ([HMA](https://www.healthmanagement.com/blog/in-focus/hma-resource-provides-key-insights-about-the-evolving-medicare-medicaid-integration-landscape/)). The 2027 rule limits certain D-SNPs to members of an affiliated Medicaid plan. HCBS use among them: not found. If 15-25% use HCBS (U), the aligned HCBS pool is 0.4-0.7M, and 67k members is 10-16% of it (A).
- **Reachable payers (A/U):** about 6-10 non-Elevance parents have more than 50k LTSS lives (Centene, UHC, Molina, Humana, CVS/Aetna, AmeriHealth Caritas, large Blues), plus NY local plans. Not sourced as a count.
- **Revenue per contract (A):** a 10,000-member regional deal (Cityblock-Meridian size) at $30k is $300M of managed spend, or $9-15M of profit at 3-5%. A 5,000-member pilot is $4.5-7.5M. $100M needs 7-11 such deals.
- **Three ways to count $100M (A):**
  - Gross premium at about $25k per capitated member: about 4,000 members. Profit is $3-8M.
  - Net margin at 3% / 5% / 8% of $30k: about 111k / 67k / 42k members.
  - Shared savings only: 20% of 5-10% savings is $300-600 per member, so 167k-333k members, or 9-18% of all managed-LTSS users. **Dead as a standalone path.**

## 4. Demand and willingness to pay

- **Strong precedent (S):** CareBridge's $140M 2022 round was led by Oak HC/FT with Anthem, Optum Ventures, CVS Ventures and HLM (for Centene). Its CEO said those plans oversee about 60% of HCBS members. It took risk "based on a member's historic spending" and employed no home workers ([BHB](https://bhbusiness.com/2022/06/16/how-carebridge-won-over-investors-mcos-in-140m-funding-round/)). It grew from about 25k to about 115k patients in about a year ([HCI](https://www.hcinnovationgroup.com/policy-value-based-care/medicare-medicaid/news/53071689/medicaid-focused-carebridge-tops-inc-magazines-fastest-growing-list)).
- **Rivals did buy from it (S):** a 2023 Iowa deck says CareBridge was a subcontractor of all three Iowa plans: Amerigroup (Elevance), Iowa Total Care (Centene) and Molina ([Iowa HHS](https://hhs.iowa.gov/media/535/download)). Iowa also asked to pause CareBridge's member outreach because of confusion. Iowa's 2026 EVV materials still show all plans using CareBridge EVV (search summary; Iowa's EVV model may be partly state-driven).
- **Rivals buy from neutral vendors (S):** Centene's four Cityblock deals. The one visible first-contract size is 10,000 lives, not 70,000.
- **No evidence of the neutral demand itself (negative finding):** I found no RFP, posted contract, plan statement or press report from Centene, UHC, Molina, Humana or CVS seeking an HCBS manager free of Elevance, and none of a plan leaving CareBridge since the Dec 2024 close. Elevance's CEO said it would keep serving all customers (Fierce, via search summary). Whether CareBridge still serves rivals after the close: not verifiable.
- **No mandate forces vendor spend (S):** EVV is done. The 80/20 pass-through stays on the books for July 2030 ([LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/serialpost/cms-significant-medicaid-rules-addressing-access-and-quality/)). The 2027 D-SNP alignment rules raise plan interest in integrated management, but they do not require a vendor.

## 5. Unit-economics analogs

- **Carelon Services segment (S, [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm)):** 2025 revenue $28,316M, operating gain $960M = 3.4%. 2024: $17,961M and $717M = 4.0%. Premiums (risk contracts) rose from $1,679M (2023) to $2,630M to $6,315M (2025). About 75% of revenue is affiliated ($21,175M). The segment recognizes risk revenue gross: a PMPM premium with responsibility for claims cost ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm)).
- **Evolent (neutral risk-bearing platform, via search summary of the [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1628908/000162890826000014/a2025q4exhibit991.htm)):** 2025 revenue $1,876M, adjusted EBITDA $151M (8.1%), $398M Q4 goodwill impairment. 2026 guidance $110-140M EBITDA on $2.4-2.6B, about 5%.
- **CareBridge exit (S/A):** about 1.0x reported 2023 revenue. If its margin resembled the two analogs (3-8%), the price is roughly 13-35x operating profit, which prices growth, not current earnings (A).
- **Revenue per member differs by model (S/A):** CareBridge about $22.6k ($2.6B over 115k, a mixed definition); Cityblock about $11k ($2.2B over 200k).
- **Implication:** 5% of $30k is a good-year profit rate, not a fee. The gate's $1,100-1,500 per member holds only for a full-risk book, which carries volatility and capital needs (sections 6 and 7).

## 6. Capital needs

- CareBridge raised $180M of equity across two rounds before its exit ([BHB](https://bhbusiness.com/2022/06/16/how-carebridge-won-over-investors-mcos-in-140m-funding-round/)). It had about 500 employees at the deal ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/finance/elevance-health-scoop-value-based-care-company-carebridge), via search summary).
- My estimate for a non-anchored entrant: $100-200M to reach about 30-50k members (A). That covers clinical and EVV-data build, plus risk reserves, stop-loss and any surety or solvency capital a state requires. Plans pay capitation before providers are paid, so working capital is a smaller issue than variance.
- **Structural problem (A):** the sharpened design funds the vendor with 2-3 plans' co-investment. In 2022 Anthem was an investor; in 2024 Elevance was the buyer. Plan co-investors get first look at an exit, and a plan seat on the governance board is the opposite of neutrality.

## 7. Regulatory and liability landscape

- **Plan stays on the hook (S):** under [42 CFR 438.230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/438.230), a plan keeps ultimate responsibility, and delegation must be revocable. State, CMS and OIG get audit rights for 10 years. Plans will price that into contracts and favor vendors they can fire or absorb.
- **Licensure when taking capitated risk (S, examples only):** Arkansas treats a licensed risk-based provider organization as an insurer ([Ark. Rule 117](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arkansas/054-00-17-Ark-Code-R-002)). California's Knox-Keene scope was widened in 2019. Delaware certifies risk-bearing entities. CMS counts entities paid a fixed periodic amount as sub-capitated ([CMS](https://www.medicaid.gov/tmsis/dataguide/t-msis-coding-blog/cms-technical-instructions-reporting-sub-capitation-payments-and-encounters-associated-with-sub-capitation-payments-from-managed-care-plans/)). I found no HCBS-specific rule, so each state is a separate licensing check.
- **Hours and authorization liability (S/U):** Arkansas's ARChoices algorithm cut home-care hours; a court found notice failures in 2018, and the vendor's tool mishandled diabetes and cerebral palsy ([Arkansas Online](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2018/may/24/dhs-found-in-contempt-over-rule-changes-1/); vendor-liability ruling not found). *Lokken v. UnitedHealth* over nH Predict is in discovery (secondary source). If the product steers hours or networks, due-process and fair-hearing exposure falls on the plan and lands on the vendor by indemnity.
- **Savings capture (S):** CHCS says HCBS quality savings tend to accrue to Medicare ([CHCS](https://www.chcs.org/early-state-efforts-to-advance-value-in-medicaid-managed-long-term-services-and-supports-programs/)). That is why FIDE/HIDE alignment is the right buyer, and also why the buyer pool is small. A California budget document also warns that capitation rebasing passes plan savings back to the state in later years (undated; U).
- **Evidence base:** the only outcome numbers I found for this model are Elevance's, with no methods.

## 8. Is the why-now real?

- **Real and live:** plan Medicaid margins are squeezed. Molina guides a 92.6% medical cost ratio and calls 2026 a "trough year" for Medicaid margins ([S&P](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/8/us-health-insurers-raise-outlooks-despite-q2-cost-rises-shifting-membership-105000456)). H.R.1 state-directed-payment cuts start Jan 1, 2028, and 15 states plan new cost containment in FY2026 ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/states-management-of-medicaid-home-care-spending-ahead-of-h-r-1-effects/)). Integrated D-SNP alignment is in force: 22 states have applicable integrated plans, up from 14.
- **Half-used:** the Elevance purchase closed 22 months ago and nothing I found shows rivals defecting. The shock that was meant to open the neutral slot has not visibly produced demand. Tight margins also cut vendor budgets and push plans to in-source or use free HHAX tools.
- **Already occupied:** Elevance extended CareBridge to D-SNPs in 2025, the sharpened wedge.

## 10. Kill tests and gaps

- **Three plan calls (the idea's own test):** ask Centene, UHC and Molina LTSS leads whether they would move at-risk HCBS lives from CareBridge or in-house to a neutral vendor, at what share, and on what terms (savings only or delegated risk).
- **Pull CareBridge subcontractor lists** from state Medicaid sites (Iowa, Tennessee, Texas, Ohio, Arizona) to see whether non-Elevance plans still use it.
- **Get CareCentrix Medicaid LTSS terms and size.** This is the closest independent comparable and I could not find its numbers.
- **HCBS use among FIDE/HIDE members:** not found; it decides the pool size.
- **Not found:** plan-to-vendor fee benchmarks; sales cycle length for a neutral entrant; any independent outcome study of CareBridge; whether CareBridge keeps contracts with Centene, UHC, Molina, Humana or CVS after the close.
- **Source caveats:** the $2.6B and $2.7B figures are press-reported (one outlet cites $4B+ revenue). The CMS counts are state-reported. Some items came only through search summaries: CareCentrix's guaranteed-savings model, Evolent's results, and the Papa, Optum, Iowa-EVV and Fierce items.

**Call:** do not pursue as a standalone $100M-profit company. If pursued at all, test as a regional, contract-anchored risk manager built to be acquired. Say plainly that the defensible version is smaller than the pitch.

Honest overall read: the money in this pool is mostly indemnity (about $100B of losses) and agent commissions (about $20B), and a new intermediary gets a 1-6% slice of either. So every idea below needs 13-22% of its own sub-pool for $100M, and none is a clean venture-scale winner. The two with the best first-customer logic are 3 (a fresh state order, one small incumbent, three stackable payers) and 5 (commission income that accumulates, with a regulator pushing carriers to soften exits). Idea 6 does not reach $100M on its own and is best built as the data layer of idea 3. Idea 7 has the sharpest economics but no insurer has yet been seen paying for it.

## 1. Coverage Continuity Desk (INSIDER: servicer hazard-loss / insurance-tracking head, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** Charge servicers a flat per-loan fee to keep every escrowed borrower's policy alive and cure non-renewals before force-placement, replacing "free" tracking that is funded by lender-placed premium.
- **Customer:** Servicing operations at the largest mortgage servicers. First 10: three mid-size nonbank servicers (0.5-3M loans) with heavy CA, FL, TX, CO books, reached through MBA servicing forums and compliance heads who already field force-placement complaints.
- **Payer and signer:** SVP Servicing Operations (insurance, escrow and hazard loss), with the Chief Compliance Officer co-signing (Reg X force-placed rules). Budget lines: escrow and loss-draft processing (MBA: $11 a loan, S), complaint handling, and default-servicing cost. Fee (A): $6 a loan a year.
- **Problem:** A servicer sees the premium jump, the non-renewal notice and the lapse before the borrower acts, but routes the case to a tracking vendor whose economics run on lender-placed insurance: Assurant tracks 34M+ loans and places about 2% (S). NAIC calls LPI a "reverse competition" market because the payer is not the chooser (S). Every cure is revenue the tracker loses. Servicers cannot shop for borrowers or earn a commission without RESPA exposure (hazard insurance is a settlement service, S via summary), so nobody is paid to keep the borrower on a normal policy.
- **Solution:** Fee-for-service, no commissions: (1) evidence matching so borrowers who renewed (new policy number, new agent) are not force-placed; (2) outreach inside the non-renewal period, not the 45-day force-place window; (3) placement through a licensed partner with the servicer receiving nothing per policy; (4) premium-shock and escrow-shortage calls; (5) carrier-failure playbook; (6) mortgagee-clause re-papering after servicing transfers. Legal premise to check: RESPA Section 8 and affiliated-business rules (12 CFR 1024.15; payment for services actually performed), Reg X 1024.37 notice timing, state agency licensing for the placement partner.
- **Why now:** About 2.03M nonrenewals a year (A) and +96-216% since 2018 (S); FAIR Plan +29.1% on Oct 15 (S); State Farm's no-nonrenewal stay ends Dec 31 2026 (S, news); DFPI is now reading servicer behaviour (Oct 15 order, S); Rocket must pick one insurance stack for about 10M loans after the Mr. Cooper merger (A), a rare buying event; Assurant is still adding clients (Freedom, Q2 2026), so the incumbent is entrenched but visibly dependent on LPI growth.
- **Wedge:** One mid-size servicer's CA/FL/TX non-renewal cohort: contact within 10 days of the carrier letter, report borrowers kept on voluntary coverage and LPI placements avoided.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Four to five servicers with about 18M loans (Rocket/Mr. Cooper about 10M, S, plus two or three others at 2-4M) x $6 = $108M. By loan count that is about 30% of roughly 60M loans (heroic as a share), but it needs only four or five signatures. Fee is below the $11 MBA line it substitutes for.
- **Who does it today:** Assurant (tracking and LPI), National General/Allstate (TrackGuard LPI claims), ICE/Black Knight insurance services (U), Matic (embedded shopping, commission model), Hippo-style servicer partnerships, in-house hazard-loss teams.
- **Why they lose:** Assurant cannot cut LPI without cutting its own profit pool (non-cat loss ratio about 35%, S). Matic is paid by carriers per bound policy, so it cannot be the servicer's neutral compliance operator and it triggers the referral-fee question. In-house teams are cost centres with no data on what happens after a cure.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning against the LPI-funded tracker, plus switching costs from servicing-platform integration (12-18 month conversions) and process power from a cure-rate dataset.
- **Earned secret (U):** The valuable window is the weeks between a non-renewal letter and the lapse, while the borrower still has an agent and a carrier will still quote. After force-placement the borrower is on E&S or FAIR at 2-3x the price and the servicer is in a complaint. Also, a share of "lapses" are matching failures, not real lapses; validate on ten servicers' LPI refund reports.
- **First 90 days:** Sign one pilot servicer; ingest 12 months of LPI placements and refunds to quantify erroneous placements; run outreach on 5,000 non-renewal cases; get a written RESPA/Reg X memo for the partner structure.

## 2. Placement Desk: pay for the agent's hour (INSIDER: independent agent in California WUI zones, 15+ years)

- **One-liner:** An exchange that lets an agent submit a distressed home once, documents the declinations, and gets it quoted and bound across FAIR, E&S and take-out carriers, because the shortage in this market is agent minutes, not capacity.
- **Customer:** Independent agencies holding distressed books (CA first, then FL, TX, CO), plus the carriers that want that volume. First 10: 15 IIABCal-type agencies with 100+ FAIR policies each; three admitted carriers with SIS writing commitments; three E&S MGAs.
- **Payer and signer:** Carriers pay a bind fee or wholesale split (signer: VP Distribution; budget: new-business acquisition, 12-15% commission plus marketing). Agencies pay a seat (A: $300-500 a month). FAIR Plan pays nothing.
- **Problem:** (U, insider estimate) A distressed submission takes 2-4 hours across 6-10 portals. FAIR averages about $2,930 a policy (A), so a 7% new commission is about $205 and a 3% renewal about $88 (S rates, A dollars). At $50-70 an hour loaded cost, renewals are underwater and new business is break-even. Agents drop service; policyholders orphan. Carriers with appetite cannot reach agents' attention; the Clearinghouse reaches policyholders only through the broker of record (U).
- **Solution:** One ACORD-plus-photos-plus-mitigation-evidence packet; automatic California diligent-search/declination documentation for E&S; appetite matching; parallel quote and bind by API or portal automation; agent keeps the retail commission, the desk takes the wholesale split or a bind fee; renewal servicing for orphaned FAIR policies at near-zero marginal cost. Legal premise to check: surplus-lines broker licensing and the diligent-search rule, FAIR producer rules, BOR letters.
- **Why now:** FAIR +29.1% and commission cuts start Oct 15 (S); E&S homeowners +29.5% to $4.14B and California surplus policies over 300k, 90% urban, with falling average new premium (S): the "boomerang" flow back to admitted carriers as SIS rates become adequate; Citizens down to 266k (S) so Florida carriers want growth; cat reinsurance down 15-25% (S) so capacity exists; State Farm's stay ends Dec 31 (S).
- **Wedge:** FAIR-to-admitted take-outs and E&S placements for 15 agencies. Prove 300 binds, then add Florida and Texas carriers.
- **Path to $100M (A):** E&S homeowners $4.14B growing 15% a year gives about $6.3B in 2029; a 6% wholesale/bind take is about $378M; add 144k FAIR exits a year (24k in two months annualised) x $450 desk revenue = $65M; add orphan servicing about $12M. Pool about $455M; $100M is about 22% of it. Heroic. At 10% share the desk makes about $45M.
- **Who does it today:** Bridge Specialty (digital personal-lines marketplace, Apr 2026), CRC, Amwins, RT Specialty, Burns & Wilcox, Young Alfred/Credible ($10M Series A), Novella and Pathpoint (commercial only), Matic (origination), Kin/Openly/Slide (carriers), the California FAIR Clearinghouse.
- **Why they lose:** Wholesalers' cost structure assumes $5k+ commercial premiums; FAIR cannot market; D2C aggregators earn per lead and have no hard-risk carriers; carriers' captive channels cannot use an agent exchange.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Network economies (agents, carriers) and process power from portal automation; main threat is Applied/Vertafore/EZLynx bundling a rater.
- **Earned secret (U):** Agents are not refusing distressed risks because markets are closed; they refuse because the hours do not pay. Paying the agent's time (carrier-funded bind fees) frees more capacity than any new carrier.
- **First 90 days:** Sign 15 agencies and six carriers; automate five portals; run 300 submissions with documented time saved; publish minutes-per-bind.

## 3. Proceeds Desk: custody, draws and contractor fast-pay for insurance money (PATTERN: float and spread on payment rails; construction pay-apps and retainage)

- **One-liner:** Take the monitored loss-draft file off the servicer: hold the proceeds in a bank-partner account, release by machine-checked milestones, and pay contractors in two days for a small fee.
- **Customer:** Servicers and depository lenders holding insurance proceeds. First 10: California credit unions and community banks with LA-fire loans (they owe DFPI a report by about Oct 29), plus two nonbank servicers.
- **Payer and signer:** Head of Loss Draft / Hazard Loss (signer: SVP Servicing Operations, with the Chief Compliance Officer) from the loss-draft processing line ($11 a loan, S) and compliance remediation. Contractors pay the fast-pay fee. Insurers can pay per evidence packet for RC-holdback releases.
- **Problem:** Proceeds are payable jointly to borrower and servicer; large claims are "monitored" and released in thirds after inspections by a $100 third-party inspector who cannot read a schedule, while the contractor cannot start without money. California now has AB 493 (interest on held proceeds), a July order on holds over 10 days, and an Oct 15 reporting order; 160+ complaints; 16,000+ structures destroyed; fewer than a dozen rebuilt one year on (S). Disputes over percent complete are the stated cause.
- **Solution:** (1) Digital multi-party endorsement (the iink layer); (2) a custodial FBO account per claim at a partner bank, with interest passed through; (3) release rules encoded from the servicer's policy and checked against permit-status feeds, dated geotagged photos, contractor licence and insurance, and statutory lien waivers; (4) contractor fast-pay on approved draws at 1-2% (A); (5) later, a completion guaranty and a soft-cost bridge secured on the proceeds. Legal premise to check: money transmission (use a bank partner), security-instrument terms (restore if feasible, S), state inspection-before-release rules (Massachusetts example, S), CA lien-waiver forms.
- **Why now:** Oct 15 2026 order, AB 493, Isaias (Oct 10, $2.5-4.5B insured), and the March 2026 GSE rule accepting ACV roofs, which widens depreciation gaps on hail claims (S).
- **Wedge:** California fire loan books: median days from check receipt to homeowner disbursement under 10, with interest ledger and a DFPI-ready export. Then monitored claims nationally.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Losses about $102B x 60% mortgaged x 50% monitored = $30.5B of proceeds a year (A; if monitored is 30% the pool falls to about $340M). Servicer fee: about 305k files x $600 = $183M. Contractor fast-pay: $21B of draws x 1.5% = $320M. Float spread 0.5% on about $12.7B average balance = $64M. Pool about $567M; add completion guaranty (about $80M) and bridge interest (about $100M) for about $750M. $100M is 13-18% blended share. Heroic, but three payers stack.
- **Who does it today:** iink (check capture and multi-party payments; $14.8M raised), DIMONT (loss-draft service), National General/Assurant LPI claims, servicers' own loss-draft teams, The Hub/CORE (nonprofit escrow oversight in LA), Built Technologies and title-company disbursers (construction lending, not insurance money).
- **Why they lose:** iink moves checks but does not decide releases, has no contractor credit and is small. Servicers cannot underwrite contractors or hold interest-bearing accounts cheaply under AB 493. The Hub is local and philanthropic.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Switching costs (servicer integrations, DFPI audit trail) and process power; contractor draw history becomes credit data (cornered resource).
- **Earned secret (U):** The money is not slow because servicers are lazy; it is deadlocked. The inspector's percent-complete is a judgment call, and no one will pre-fund. Replace the judgment with evidence and release becomes mechanical. Outside California, servicers also earn float on these balances, so expect resistance there until states copy AB 493.
- **First 90 days:** Sign two CA lenders with open fire files; process 200 draws at cost; connect LA County permit data; sign one bank partner; agree contractor fast-pay terms with 20 rebuild contractors.

## 4. Closing Desk: outcome-priced total-loss operation with a regulatory warranty (PATTERN: outcome-priced replacement of a high-volume labour function)

- **One-liner:** Take a carrier's major-loss and total-loss files end to end (contents, ALE, debris, dwelling scope, mortgagee) for a fixed fee per closed file, with a cap-limited warranty on statutory clocks.
- **Customer:** VP Property Claims at mid-size carriers, reciprocals, Florida domestics and residual plans. First 10: 100 open LA total-loss files at a mid-size carrier or the FAIR Plan; a Florida domestic after Isaias.
- **Payer and signer:** Chief Claims Officer; budget: outsourced LAE (adjusting and TPA fees). Compliance officer co-signs because the warranty reduces exam exposure.
- **Problem:** Total losses run as separate queues (dwelling, contents, ALE, debris, mortgagee), each with a different adjuster; reassignment resets clocks, and CDI listed repeated adjuster reassignment, late status letters and underpayments among the violations in a 220-file sample (S). Penalties are small ($10,000 per willful violation) but a one-year licence suspension is also sought (S). New SB 495 rule: advance of 60% of contents limit without inventory in declared emergencies (S; the cap is stated as $250k and $350k in different sources).
- **Solution:** One case owner per file, a clock engine for every statutory deadline, templates for status and ALE-extension letters, SB 495 advance workflow, contents valuation, vendor routing, and an indemnity for penalties up to a cap. Surge mode with pre-credentialed adjusters on retainer. Priced per closed file with a cycle-time bonus.
- **Why now:** CDI Accusation (May 4), LA County suit (Aug 31), ALE ends Jan 7 2027, and the Sept 2026 mitigation rule add clocks. State Farm's own file is 78% closed at about 20 months (S), a number every carrier can now be compared with.
- **Wedge:** Contents plus ALE workflow on open Palisades/Eaton files, priced per closed file.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Outsourced adjusting about $11.7B (S, all lines) x 35% property = $4.1B; x 40% major/cat/total-loss = $1.6B; $100M is about 6%. Cross-check: about 150k major-loss files a year x $6,000 = $900M; $100M is about 11%.
- **Who does it today:** Sedgwick (property split into five product lines, desk outsourcing accepted), Crawford ($1.27B revenue), Pilot, Frontline, Engle Martin, Alacrity; policyholder-side Claim Architect; AI newcomers (Curant.ai $3.1M seed, Sprout.ai). Carriers' own major-loss units.
- **Why they lose:** IA firms bill by the hour or file, so automation shrinks their revenue; their adjusters are 1099s shared across firms; none sells a compliance warranty.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Process power and switching costs (delegated authority, carrier-system integration); scale economies in surge capacity.
- **Earned secret (U):** Total losses are slow because five queues restart each other, not because estimating is hard. One owner and a clock engine halves reassignments, and the warranty is only priceable once the clocks are automated.
- **First 90 days:** Take 100 open files at fixed price; measure reassignments, days to ALE decision, days to contents payment; get an E&O carrier to quote the warranty.
- **Cliché flag:** "AI claims" is on the cliché list; the differentiators are the warranty and total-loss scope, not FNOL.

## 5. Soft-Landing Exchange: outplacement for non-renewed policyholders (DILEMMA: the exiting carrier cannot refer its customer to a competitor)

- **One-liner:** Sell the leaving carrier a consent-based service that moves its non-renewed policyholders to quoted, bindable offers from other carriers, and keep the resulting agent-of-record commission.
- **Customer:** Carriers cutting exposure in CA, FL, TX, CO, LA; regulators who want softer exits. First 10: State Farm General's slated nonrenewals for 2027 (U: about 72,000 announced in 2024), plus Travelers, Chubb, Nationwide, USAA, Farmers, Allstate (S: all reported as pulling back in high-risk areas).
- **Payer and signer:** Exiting carrier (signer: VP Customer/Regulatory Affairs; budget: nonrenewal handling, call centre, complaint and regulatory-risk cost, about $30 a policy, A); buyer carriers via commission (about 12% of premium, A).
- **Problem:** A non-renewal is legally a termination, not a referral. The captive agent cannot place elsewhere, the carrier cannot recommend a competitor, and the policyholder starts from zero with 75 days (U) to find coverage. About 2.03M policies a year (A) are non-renewed; most dissolve into the market and the buyer carriers' acquisition value (about $500 a policy, A) is never captured.
- **Solution:** The notice carries an opt-in link; on consent the desk becomes agent of record, pre-qualifies the home against 5-10 appetites using the carrier's own data, shows bindable offers in 24 hours, and handles BOR and mortgagee changes. Exiting carrier gets a regulator-ready report. Legal premise to check: policyholder data sharing consent (CA Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act, U), nonrenewal notice content rules, any CDI view on carrier-paid referral services, agent licensing.
- **Why now:** State Farm's stay ends Dec 31 2026 (S, news); NAIC data show nonrenewals rising 96-216% (S); CDI and the Governor are publicly measuring harm to homeowners (S); FAIR is trying to depopulate and growth has slowed to 1.9% (S).
- **Wedge:** One carrier's 2027 slated cohort, handled as a managed transfer.
- **Path to $100M (A):** 2.03M nonrenewals x 60% re-placeable = 1.22M a year. At 15% share that is 183k placements a year; with 85% retention the in-force book after five years is about 678k policies. At $2,400 premium x 12% commission x 70% after local-agent splits = $200 a policy, revenue is about $135M in year five. Needs about 15% of the flow.
- **Who does it today:** Nobody sells to the exiting carrier that I found. Adjacent: Policygenius, Insurify, The Zebra (leads), Matic (origination), Young Alfred, Bridge Specialty (agents), the FAIR Clearinghouse, retail agents.
- **Why they lose:** Aggregators are paid per lead by buyers and have no access to the exiting carrier's data or notice. Captive agents are contractually stuck. The FAIR Clearinghouse only serves FAIR policies.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Counter-positioning (a service no carrier or aggregator can offer), cornered resource (carrier-originated cohorts), and recurring commission that accumulates.
- **Earned secret (U):** Carriers lose more from the exit story than from the exit: complaints, legislators, press. They pay for outplacement for laid-off employees; the same logic applies to customers.
- **First 90 days:** Offer one carrier a free pilot on 5,000 non-renewed policies; measure rebind rate and time to bind; get a CDI informal view.

## 6. Draw and Price Oracle (DILEMMA: Verisk sells to both sides) - weakest of the eight, flagged

- **One-liner:** A conflict-free percent-complete and paid-invoice price standard that servicers, insurers, contractors and appraisers can all accept.
- **Customer:** Servicers (draw approvals), insurers (RC holdback and ALE evidence), public adjusters, contractors, appraisal umpires. First 10: the lenders from idea 3 plus two PA firms.
- **Payer and signer:** Servicer loss-draft head (inspection line), carrier property-claims VP (reserving and disputes), PA principal, litigation counsel.
- **Problem:** Xactimate's list is the de facto price standard and contractors and advocates say it lags real costs, especially after a surge (S, advocacy sources). Verisk also sells to carriers and is buying a roofer CRM; the FTC's theory is that it could foreclose rivals (S). Nobody neutral can say what a line item cost or how complete a job is.
- **Solution:** Photo, permit and invoice ingestion scores percent complete and flags overbilling; paid-invoice index by line item, ZIP and month; auditable evidence packets for supplements and appraisals.
- **Why now:** Verisk/AccuLynx ruling and FTC review; LA price disputes; reinsurers and regulators asking about demand surge.
- **Wedge:** Use idea 3's draws as the data source; sell packets to LA public adjusters.
- **Path to $100M (A):** Servicer inspections: about 305k monitored files x 3 x $100 = $92M at 100% share. Contractor/PA seats: 100k x $600 = $60M. Carrier benchmark licences: 25 x $1M = $25M. Packets: about $15M. Pool about $190M; $100M needs over 50%. It does not reach $100M alone; treat as a data layer of idea 3 whose value shows up as higher release rates there.
- **Who does it today:** Verisk Xactware (Xactimate; 22 of the top 25 US insurers claimed by Xactware), Symbility/Cotality, in-house price lists, expert witnesses.
- **Why they lose:** Verisk cannot publish a price that contradicts its list or undercuts its carrier customers. Cotality has the same customer mix.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (paid-invoice data) only if idea 3 wins first.
- **Earned secret (U):** Disputes are rarely about the dollar amount of a line; they are about the date of the price and the percent complete. A dated paid-invoice index ends the argument.
- **First 90 days:** Load 500 LA invoices; compare to the Xactimate list for the same month; publish the gap.

## 7. Rebuild Velocity: owner's rep priced on ALE months saved (SHOCK: the Jan 7 2027 ALE cliff)

- **One-liner:** An insurer-paid, homeowner-trusted rebuild coordinator (permits, design, bids, code upgrades, draw readiness) paid a share of the ALE months it saves.
- **Customer:** Carriers with open total-loss files in LA and, later, hurricane, tornado and hail total losses. First 10: three carriers with at least 100 open LA files; FAIR Plan.
- **Payer and signer:** VP Property Claims (budget: ALE and indemnity), with servicers contributing for draw readiness and homeowners paying a small fee.
- **Problem:** The meter runs on the carrier: fewer than a dozen homes rebuilt one year on (S); 1,153 permits issued by Jan 2 2026 (S); ALE ends Jan 7 2027 for many, extendable 12 months on reasonable diligence (S); LA County's suit alleges caps (S). Survivors are first-time builders; soft costs are $200k-350k+ before a shovel (marketing source, U); insurers cannot manage construction and servicers will not release money without progress.
- **Solution:** A neutral coordinator that takes the homeowner from payout to permitted start, produces dated progress evidence that satisfies the "reasonable diligence" test, and lines up draws with the servicer. Paid 40% of ALE months saved, estimated at $5,000 a month (A, no ALE data found), plus a homeowner fee. Legal premise to check (decisive): whether an insurer paying this is steering, an inducement or unlicensed contracting (California anti-rebating, U); the cleanest structure is an opt-in benefit tied to ALE extension.
- **Why now:** Jan 7 2027, 38% of survivors out or near out (S), the LA County suit (S), and the state's attention on payouts (S).
- **Wedge:** 500 LA households with the longest-open ALE; guarantee four months saved or no fee.
- **Path to $100M (A):** About 13,000 homes destroyed (S) x 55% insured primary residences with ALE needing extension = about 7,150; a 12-month extension at $5k a month is about $430M of insurer cost (A). Saving four months is $20k a household; at $10k revenue a household (insurer 40% share plus homeowner and servicer fees), LA is about $70M, spread over two to three years. Nationally (A) about 60k major rebuilds a year x $10k = $600M; $100M is about 17%. Heroic, and LA is one-time.
- **Who does it today:** Homebound (design-build with permitting and insurance advisors), The Hub/CORE (project management and escrow oversight), LA County One-Stop Permit Centers (free), Habitat plus Foothill Catalog (pre-approved plans), Samara/Steadfast and Cover (modular), Claim Architect (policyholder-side estimates), RockRose (mitigation). No insurer-funded programme found.
- **Why they lose:** Each is paid by the homeowner or by charity; none is paid by the party carrying the meter, and none is neutral enough to certify diligence.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Cornered resource (permit and contractor throughput data) and process power; weak until an insurer signs.
- **Earned secret (U):** The biggest controllable cost in a total loss is time to rebuild, not the dwelling number, and nobody owns it. The insurer wants it owned but cannot own construction.
- **First 90 days:** Offer three carriers a no-fee pilot on 100 files; measure months from payout to permit; get counsel's view on the structure.

## 8. Defensible-Space Network (SHOCK: Zone 0 adoption, the Sept 4 mitigation FAQ and a shrinking CAL FIRE inspection budget)

- **One-liner:** A route-dense subscription that keeps 0-5 ft and 100 ft zones compliant every year and produces an inspection-grade record that insurers and fire departments accept.
- **Customer:** Homeowners in very high fire zones and SRA; HOAs; insurers that want fewer high-risk renewals. First 10: 500 homes in one LRA city with an active fire department inspection programme (LA County charges $151 a parcel, S).
- **Payer and signer:** Homeowner (subscription, A: $1,000 a year); HOA board (signer: president or treasurer); insurers co-fund for high-score policies (U, to be tested).
- **Problem:** The 100-ft law has applied since 2005 (S). Zone 0 rules were adopted Aug 19 2026 for close to 2M homes (S), existing homes in 3-5 years (S, conflicting). CAL FIRE can do only 129,206 inspections a year against a 250,000 goal (S). Insurers must credit mitigation and offer free inspections (S). Compliance decays within a year; no one sells upkeep with proof.
- **Solution:** Annual or quarterly crews, goats and chippers on routes, with before-and-after geotagged evidence stored for the fire department and insurers; certificate packet for sale (AB 38 certificate, U) and for rating credits. Add Zone 0 conversions when deadlines bite.
- **Why now:** Zone 0 adoption, Sept 4 FAQ, the inspection capacity gap and unfunded loans (SB 894, no appropriation, S) mean homeowners pay and need proof.
- **Wedge:** One city, 500 homes, a letter from the fire department accepting the evidence format.
- **Path to $100M (A):** 100,000 homes x $1,000 a year = $100M, which is 5% of the roughly 2M Zone 0 homes. Needs multi-state expansion for safety.
- **Who does it today:** Local tree and landscape firms (thousands), goat grazers, RockRose (roll-up of tree-trimming and roofing firms; $12.5M Series A, planning a $30-40M credit facility, S), BurnBot (Mercury-backed), private fire-defence contractors for insurers (U), Fire Safe Councils, CAL FIRE and local fire departments.
- **Why they lose:** Local firms cannot document to an insurer's standard or build routes across towns. RockRose is a carrier/broker first and early.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** Scale economies (route density), switching costs (the compliance record), and a possible standard if fire departments accept the format.
- **Earned secret (U):** Compliance is an annual re-grow problem, not a one-time retrofit, so it is a subscription. A homeowner who clears once drifts out of compliance in 12 months.
- **First 90 days:** Sign the fire department's evidence format; run 100 homes; test whether two insurers will credit or co-pay; price at $75-100 a month.
- **Whiplash flag:** Existing-home Zone 0 deadlines slip to 2029 or later; the business should stand on the existing 100-ft law and insurer credits first.

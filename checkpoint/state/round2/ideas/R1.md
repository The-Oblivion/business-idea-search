# R1: Rescue pass over round 1 (insights re-aimed at bigger payers)

Date: 2026-10-18. Inputs read: lessons.md, evolution_ranking.csv (top 60), all 25 finalist files, deep_validation_results.json, refined pitches. 15 searches plus about 10 page fetches (several returned 403 or unreadable PDFs; those claims are marked "via search summary").

Verdict: 2 of the 3 allowed slots filled. Both are at the edge of the $100M bar, not comfortably over it, and the path-to-$100M sections say exactly what has to be true. Everything else I tested either died on a named competitor or had no forcing mandate (see the last section).

## 1. Large-Load Queue Bureau: reality scores that price the $50,000-per-MW deposit

Round-1 source: I0079-M2 "Phantom Pipeline Audits for Utility Rate-Case Intervenors" (finalist). Supporting: I0773 / H01 (phantom-load registry), I0996-M1, I0365-M1, I0836-M2 (the "getting power for large loads" cluster).

**One-liner.** A furnisher-funded bureau that sees the same data-center project in every utility and ISO queue, scores how much of each megawatt is physically real, and uses that score two ways: to verify applicants for grid operators, and to underwrite the interconnection deposits that now forfeit 80% when a project falls out.

**The insight that stays.** From I0079-M2: duplicate counting is only visible from outside the queues. Each utility sees only its own list; an auditor working in several states sees the same project in three pipelines. Round 1 sold that as a $40-120K expert exhibit to intervenors. The re-aim: the same "is this megawatt real?" number is now a loss-given-default input, because Texas put a price and a forfeiture rule on every phantom megawatt.

**What changed (facts, with how well I verified them).**
- Texas SB 6 applies to sites over 75 MW. PUCT's proposed rule (read at Foley, Mar 31, 2026) requires proof of site control and $50,000 per MW of requested peak demand at the Intermediate Agreement stage. Accepted forms in that article: cash, a guaranty from a BBB-/Baa3 entity, or a letter of credit from an A-/A3 U.S. bank. Surety bonds were not listed.
- PUCT approved the ERCOT Batch Zero framework on June 18, 2026 (via search summaries of Sheppard Mullin, Mondaq and ERCOT). Default security is $50,000/MW. If a load withdraws or misses a milestone by six months, 20% of the remaining security is refunded and 80% goes to the transmission provider's rate base. A Sept 7 report said refundability rules under 16 TAC 25.194 were still not formally adopted when the July 10 deposit deadline passed. Refund terms may still move.
- ERCOT tracks about 474 GW of large-load requests, about 90% data centers (ERCOT June 2026 deck, via search summary; another analysis says 438 GW).
- ERCOT's September 2026 update, via search summary (the PDF would not open): conditional classification of 204 projects / 66.4 GW as base load and 158 projects / 127.9 GW as studied load, so 362 projects and about 194 GW. At $50,000/MW that is a ceiling of about $9.7B of security (my arithmetic; ERCOT staff said the numbers are a ceiling and do not yet reflect actual posting).
- Governor Abbott's Aug 3, 2026 directive ordered a verification and audit. ERCOT expects it to cover roughly 250-300 projects and about 200 GW, using requests for information on site control, load forecasts, financial assurance and ownership, to finish by December 2026. Loads that give materially false information or do not respond are ineligible (Pillsbury, Gibson Dunn, via search summary). The study report and MW allocations are due April 9, 2027.
- FERC issued show-cause orders on June 18, 2026 to all six FERC-jurisdictional RTOs/ISOs on large-load interconnection, naming speculative or duplicative requests as a concern (Orrick, via search summary). Responses were due Aug 17; I did not find the outcome.
- Round-1 file claims I did NOT re-verify: an EPRI survey of 10 utilities, and Exelon's load estimate falling about 40% after stricter collateral.

**Customer and signer.**
- Bureau: Director/VP of Large Load Interconnection or Transmission Planning at a utility, or the grid-planning VP at an ISO. Funded from interconnection study-fee revenue and planning budgets. Applicants (developer CFO or head of power) pay a per-application "verified ready" fee.
- Credit layer: developer CFO signs the credit-support fee; an infrastructure-credit fund MD signs the capital.

**Product.**
1. Per-project evidence file: recorded deeds/options/memoranda, rezoning agendas, air permits and generator counts, water filings, tax-abatement agreements, LLC/principal cross-matching, equipment lead-time feasibility. Output: probability-weighted realized MW with confidence bands.
2. Cross-queue duplicate registry: utilities and ISOs contribute hashed site/principal identifiers and get duplicate flags and scores back (the credit-bureau "furnish to receive" model).
3. "Verified ready" attestation applicants can hand to an ISO in response to an RFI.
4. Credit layer: use the score and realized-outcome history to underwrite and arrange deposit funding from infrastructure-credit capital. Start as the underwriter/sub-adviser to a licensed manager, not on own balance sheet.

**Path to $100M (every count and price marked A is my assumption).**
- Data/verification layer: 7 ISOs/RTOs x $2M (A) = $14M; 100 utilities with 1 GW+ queues x $400K (A) = $40M; 2,000 applications a year x $15-20K (A) = $30-40M; 150 lender/insurer/fund seats x $100K (A) = $15M. Sum is about $100M at 100% penetration, so a realistic 50-60% is $50-65M.
- Credit layer: assume 20-30% of the ERCOT ceiling needs third-party funding, so $1.9-2.9B per cycle. At 4-5% platform economics (origination plus management over about two years, A) the whole pool is $80-145M per cycle. A 20% share is $16-29M per cycle. It needs repeated batches in ERCOT plus 2-3 other markets copying the rule.
- To clear $100M a year you need both layers: roughly 50% of the data pool and 20-25% of third-party deposit funding across 3+ markets. If only the data layer works, the ceiling is about $50-80M. That is heroic share on the data side unless an ISO or FERC makes cross-queue verification standard.

**Who does it today.**
- ERCOT, PUCT and transmission-provider staff run the RFIs and audit by hand, queue by queue. I found no cross-utility registry.
- Market-intelligence vendors sell to developers and investors: Enverus (publishes Batch Zero readiness analysis; I could not open the page), Cleanview, SynMax Vulcan, Epoch AI hub (the last three from round 1 files).
- Deposit finance: Dynamix Capital Partners / Staubach Capital / Soda Springs "SSSC Batch Zero Fund", $95M, about 1.7 GW across two Texas sites (read at angelinvestorsnetwork.com; single trade source, no fee or return disclosed). Banks supply letters of credit to rated names.
- Law firms advise on documentation. Utility-aligned engineering consultancies do load studies.

**Why they lose.** Consultancies see one client's queue and are conflicted when the target is that client. Intelligence vendors have no furnisher data and no outcome labels, and their buyers are the developers being scored. The Dynamix fund is a two-site, one-state vehicle with no published selection method. Banks only take rated names.

**Moat.** Cornered resource and network: queue contributions plus realized/withdrawn outcome labels let the score calibrate in a way scraping the public trail cannot. Counter-positioning against conflicted consultancies. Switching costs once scores become RFI inputs or covenant terms. Honest weakness: the public-trail layer is copyable; the moat only exists if utilities/ISOs actually furnish data.

**Legal/premise checks still open.** (1) Refundability rules not final. (2) Whether ERCOT/utilities allow a third party to fund or hold an interest tied to a load allocation. (3) Whether surety is an accepted form (not listed). (4) Utility confidentiality of queue data and NDAs with hyperscalers. (5) Securities/lending licensing for the credit layer; partner with a licensed manager.

**First 90 days.** Weeks 1-3: build a public-trail scorecard for the Texas list (ERCOT large-load reports, county records, TCEQ air permits, tax-abatement filings, PUCT filings). Weeks 3-8: score the 40-60 largest projects, publish an anonymized aggregate "phantom ratio." Weeks 6-12: sell 3-5 paid pilots: one deposit-funder underwriting file ($25-40K), one intervenor docket audit ($40-120K, the round-1 channel), one developer answering an ERCOT RFI ($15-25K); propose a hashed duplicate-check pilot to one utility planning group. Kill test: if no utility/ISO will furnish data and no fund pays for underwriting by day 90, it is an audit shop, not a bureau.

## 2. Independent payment-accuracy ledger for state SNAP and Medicaid agencies (gainshare)

Round-1 source: I1240-M3 "Unit Tests for Benefit Law: Eligibility System Conformance" (finalist). Supporting: I1240 (worker drills), I1240-M2 (QC reviewers), I0573-M1 (as the demand evidence that cash-worker packets are not a health-center budget line).

**One-liner.** An independent, state-signed answer key for benefit law, run as a continuous conformance and re-adjudication service that finds the systematic logic errors behind a state's SNAP error rate, priced partly as a share of the cost-share the state avoids.

**The insight that stays.** From I1240-M3: independence is the product (like UL or a ratings agency); vendors grade their own homework; the expensive error is one the system makes identically thousands of times (North Carolina's stacked shelter deduction, fixed by a one-click prompt used more than 20,000 times, per the round-1 file); the cornered resource is the adjudicated, state-signed answer key. Round 1 priced it at $30-60M ARR in test packs. The re-aim changes the payer's frame from an IT test budget to the cost-share bill.

**What changed (verified).**
- FY2025 national SNAP payment error rate: 10.62%, about $10.1B improper payments. 41 states plus DC were above 6%; nine below (search summaries; USDA release June 24, 2026).
- H.R.1 cost share starts FY2028 (Oct 1, 2027): states under 6% pay nothing; 6-8% pay 5%; 8-10% pay 10%; over 10% pay 15% of benefit costs (Ballotpedia, opened). For FY2028 a state may use its FY2025 or FY2026 rate; from FY2029 the rate from three years earlier applies, so FY2026 sets FY2029 and FY2027, the year that began Oct 1, 2026, sets FY2030 (Ballotpedia, opened). States above 13.34% can delay.
- Exposure examples: California about $1.9B a year (CBPP), Florida about $1B at 12.97% (WUSF, opened), Texas $750M at 8.32% and $375M if under 8% (Texas A&M), Alabama up to $265M, Missouri about $150M (search summaries). FFIS's $9.4B a year nationwide at FY2025 rates is a single-source figure I could not trace.
- Budget line exists: Florida's FY2026-27 budget has a $4M line item for DCF to procure an AI vendor for "data analytics and machine learning on SNAP eligibility determinations," selection due Sept 1 (WUSF, opened; the capability language "identifying and correcting erroneous determinations, identifying root causes" is from a Florida Phoenix summary I could not open). I found no award announcement.
- Admin cost share moves to 75% state from FY2027 (round-1 file; not re-verified).
- Medicaid side: 43 states must start work requirements Jan 1, 2027; most states plan to use incumbent vendors; CMS negotiated discounted vendor rates; federal match is 90% for build and 75% for operations (CBPP, KFF via search summaries). This side is harder to enter.

**Customer and signer.** Commissioner/Secretary of the state human-services agency, with the SNAP director and state CIO, and the governor's budget office as co-sponsor. Funded by legislative appropriation and by the avoided cost-share.

**Product.** (1) H.R.1 conformance packs: about 250 synthetic households per rule change with state-signed expected outcomes, run in the state's UAT (the round-1 product). (2) Weekly re-adjudication of a statistically valid sample of the state's own closed cases against the answer key, using a rules engine plus an LLM, predicting what federal QC will find and root-causing by rule, system module and office. (3) A fix loop: system change tickets with test evidence, plus worker drills built from the same corpus. Price: fixed fee for (1)-(2) plus a gainshare on cost-share tier avoided.

**Path to $100M (assumptions marked A).**
- Fixed-fee path: 41 states x $2.5-4M (A; Florida's $4M is the only anchor) = $105-165M for SNAP alone, so $100M would need most of it. Not credible alone.
- Gainshare path: moving California 15% to 10% avoids about $630M a year (derived from $1.9B x 5/15); Florida 15% to 10% about $330M; Texas 10% to 5% $375M (sourced). Roughly $1.3B a year across three states; a 5% gainshare is about $65M. Add 5-7 mid-size states at $100-300M avoided each and the total is $90-140M.
- This needs three things to be true, none proven: the tooling really lowers error rates by 2-4 points; states may legally pay gainshare (federal cost-allowability and state procurement rules, unverified); and you win 8-10 large-state procurements of 12-18 months each. Call it heroic. It is the weakest of the two on the $100M bar.

**Who does it today.**
- Maximus "Accuracy Assistant," a SNAP-specific suite using predictive analytics to flag inconsistencies; Maximus calls SNAP an emerging opportunity and described an expanded role with an unnamed long-time state customer. It works inside live cases.
- The integrators that build and maintain eligibility systems (Deloitte, Accenture, Gainwell, Conduent, Tyler and others). Searches found no SNAP error-reduction award for Deloitte or Conduent, which proves little.
- Code for America and PolicyEngine (nonprofit / open source rules engine). Florida's selected AI vendor (unnamed).
- The round-1 competitor check for this idea was partial (its search budget ran out); no startup was found, which is thin evidence.

**Why they lose.** Integrators build the logic that causes systematic errors and earn on change orders, so they cannot sell independence. Maximus assists workers in live cases rather than testing the system against an independent answer key. Time-and-materials contractors cannot copy an outcome price without cannibalizing themselves. Honest risk: Maximus or an integrator can bolt this on or buy a startup.

**Moat.** Cornered resource: the state-signed answer-key corpus compounds across 40+ states and every rule change. Standard: state RFPs and USDA QC calibration cite the suite as an acceptance test. Weakness: PolicyEngine is open source and answer keys can be reproduced.

**First 90 days.** Build H.R.1 Conformance Pack v1 for SNAP boundary rules from public manuals and USDA guidance; send a free QC-risk memo to the policy directors of 2-3 states with system releases due Oct 2026-Jan 2027 and an error rate in the 8-13% band; watch state procurement portals for SNAP error-rate RFPs (Florida's is closed). Kill test: no paid pilot of $40K or more, and no named budget line, by day 90 means it is a small test-pack shop.

## Considered and rejected (so round 2 does not re-run them)

- **Collateral verification for asset-based finance** (I0218-M2, I0338-M3, I0365-M1). The shock is real (Tricolor, First Brands) and the pool is huge (about $6.1T of ABF per one source). But it is already being built: Setpoint PledgeCheck (double-pledge uniqueness; backed by Citi and Wells per its release), Lenvi ALVIN (loan fingerprints), Iridium Credit ($3M raise; invoice verification with debtor confirmations), Wolters Kluwer digital lending infrastructure. Nothing forces the spend; verification is a voluntary fee line. Dead on "someone already does it" plus no mandate.
- **Lead lines** (I0463-M3 / M1). Mandate holds: state guidance says unknown lines must be identified by Nov 1, 2027 and replacement runs to 2037; I found no 2026 EPA change. But it is a one-time inventory crunch of about $60-70 per line services work, BlueConduit, VODA and Trinnex already sell into it, and I did not size unknown lines or funding. Not shown to reach $100M a year durably.
- **Cure Desk / Medicaid work requirements** (I0573-M1). Payer is wrong: states use incumbent vendors, CMS negotiated vendor discounts, wage-data matching handles most people. The health-center budget line still does not exist. The state-payer version is folded into idea 2.
- **Failed-part subrogation bounty** (I0689). Interesting insight (the plumber is the first notice of loss for the product), but my one search could not size property/product subrogation recoveries (only auto: $31.2B in 2021 per one claims-investigation firm). Not verified; worth a dedicated pass in round 2.
- **Judged on round-1 files only, not searched this pass:** tariff clawback (I0236) and tariff engineering (I0769): crowded with brokers, Big 4 and trade counsel, and the legal premise of surcharge pass-through is unchecked; work-permit gap desk (I1062-M2): political and legal-services constraints, large incumbents; scribe audit (I0389): payment-integrity incumbents (Cotiviti, Optum) and the critics called it a feature; trade-materials price agency (I0068-M3) and pharmacy reimbursement index (I1266-M3): incumbents (Verisk Xactimate pricing, RSMeans, PBM audit firms) and no forcing mandate; supervised-hours bench (I0844): clinics' willingness to pay per associate is untested and large telehealth groups can internalize it.

# P136 - Private-school-choice money: state ESAs, vouchers, tax-credit scholarships, and the federal credit from 2027 (round 2 ideas)

Written 2026-10-18. Eight ideas, two per lens. Every number is tagged: (S) = sourced from a page or document I opened or a search result I read, (A) = my arithmetic or assumption, (U) = unverified (memory, secondary summary, or an insider hypothesis I could not confirm). About 33 searches, 9 page fetches and a read of the 181-page Treasury/IRS proposed-regulation text (REG-117199-25) went into this. Sources at the bottom. Research log and version backups: `round2/backups/P136/`.

## Verdict on the pool (read first)

- The pool is **thin at the layer everyone looks at**. Running a state ESA is a low-margin admin contract: Iowa's Odyssey fee is 25 cents per $100 of qualified spending (S), Louisiana pays $143.50 per account (S), Texas caps the administrator at 5% of $1B, so up to $50M (S, advocacy-group reading), Florida caps the administrator at 3% (S). The incumbent ClassWallet, after seven years in 37 states, has a third-party revenue band of $10-50M (S, Dealroom, paywalled). A pure "ESA platform" cannot reach $100M. I killed those ideas (list at the bottom).
- The money that **scales with dollars instead of accounts** is in four places: (1) the new federal credit (IRC 25F) from 1 Jan 2027, where Treasury itself projects $26B a year from 11M donors by 2030 (S) against my grounded central case of about $5B (A); (2) float on unspent balances; (3) credit to schools that wait months for choice money; (4) standards and ledgers that every program must use.
- **Seven of the eight ideas need more than 25% of their sub-pool in my central case.** I say so in each one. Ideas 3, 4 and 5 are really one company (wallet + host + ledger); I list them separately because each has a different first customer and a different failure mode.
- Pool-file corrections found this session: the pool says 27 states opted in; the IRS list shows 30 (as of 14 Sep 2026, S). The pool says the SGO spend test is 90%; the proposed regulation keeps 90% of income on scholarships and adds an 85%-of-activities safe harbor for the organization (S). The pool lists Odyssey with a $10M Series A; a $74M Series B is reported (S, date unconfirmed) and Odyssey says it serves 10 states and has distributed more than $2B to 200K+ students (S). The pool says SGO infrastructure "does not exist at scale"; FACTS announced Compass on 15 Oct 2026 and already supports 120+ SGOs (S).

## Pool facts used (checked this session)

- (S) Pool size: $10.6B allocated in the latest year, +29% (EdChoice, via pool file). Florida $3.7B; Arizona >$1.1B and 101,128 students on 1 Jun 2026 (Arizona Capitol Times; ABC15).
- (S) Federal credit, IRC 25F: nonrefundable, up to $1,700 per individual ($3,400 joint, each spouse separately), contributions from 1 Jan 2027, 5-year carryforward, donors may give to an SGO in any participating state, Form 8525 with an IRS donor number, cash only. 30 states participate (IRS list, 14 Sep 2026). Proposed and temporary regulations issued 1 Oct 2026; comments due 1 Dec 2026; hearing 15 Dec 2026; states must submit SGO lists by 15 Feb 2027.
- (S) Treasury/IRS impact estimates in the proposed regulation: 600-700 SGOs by 2030; more than 11M taxpayers giving $26B a year; 2M full-time ($12,000) or 5M part-time ($4,500) scholarships; about 9M students eligible; household income test is 300% of area median gross income and Treasury itself says substantially all households with children in participating states fall under it; income verification costs about $100 each (Equifax Work Number $46.95 per employer record x 2); a safe harbor "saves $540M annually" of verification cost; the 85% activity safe harbor adds about 450 SGOs and about $4B a year of contributions.
- (S) SGO rules that create new forced workflows: 90% of income spent on scholarships by the end of the taxable year after receipt (cash method, "spent when paid"); no earmarking for a particular student; 10+ scholarships to students not all at the same school; segregated 25F account per state; annual independent audit (committee allowed at $500k or less of receipts); donor acknowledgments due 31 Jan; contribution reports to the IRS due 28 Feb; "reasonable procedures" including systems to detect duplicate awards to the same student for the same expense; tuition and fees must be paid directly to the school; other vendors only if verified and unrelated.
- (S) **Qualified digital wallet**: a third-party platform that pre-approves vendors, tracks approved expenses and pays vendors directly or requires timely receipts. If the SGO does not keep ownership of funds moved into it, the SGO is treated as having spent them on the transfer date for the 90% test. This is the hinge for ideas 3 and 5.
- (S) Texas TEFA: more than 85,000 students confirmed for 2026-27 (Comptroller, 3 Aug 2026); about 274,000 applications; about 153,000 waitlisted in one analysis (another source says about 107,000 enrolled after waitlist moves); about 2,700 private schools approved; average private-school award $10,474, up to $30,000 for students with IEPs; Comptroller has asked lawmakers to double funding to $2B. Private-school awards are paid 25% on 1 Jul, 25% on 1 Oct and 50% on 1 Feb 2027.
- (S) Arizona oversight: Treasurer RFI 26A (issued 27 May 2026, responses due 29 Jul 2026) is the first step to replacing or upgrading the ESA financial platform; the ESA director's top ask is an "audit management software system"; the Auditor General found ADE's assigned-for-audit share of purchases ranged 6.5%-23.8%. 12News: about 84,000 of about 385,000 reviewed purchases (Dec 2024-Sep 2025) were unallowable (about 20% by count, 18,000+ accounts); ADE's dollar-weighted sample says 1.9% of dollars were unallowable and 0.3% fraudulent. A credit union, not the state, flagged one ghost-student scheme (50 applications, 43 non-existent children, $110,258).
- (S) Arizona's mature individual tax-credit program (STOs, since 1997): FY2023 individual donations about $86.2M ($49.9M original + $36.3M switcher), FY2022 about $112.5M (down about 23% in a year); corporate $118M; individual cap $769 single / $1,535 joint for 2025.
- (S) HealthEquity (the HSA custodian analog), fiscal year ended 31 Jan 2026: revenue $1.31B, custodial $636.8M, service $485.0M, interchange $191.6M; 17.8M accounts; $36.5B of HSA assets, $17.5B of it cash at about 3.5% yield.
- (S) National Student Clearinghouse (the registry analog): FY2024 revenue $102.5M, 96% program-service revenue.
- (S) Schools: about 29,730 US private schools and 4.7M students (NCES via secondary, 2021); about 75,000 microschools and 1.5M students (National Microschooling Center, May 2026; RAND range 750k-2.1M), median 20 students; half of microschools get more than a quarter of tuition from state choice programs; Catholic schools 5,829 and 1,674,907 students, 18% on choice programs, nearly 40% with waiting lists, 63 closed and 24 opened in 2024-25. ## Shared sizing assumptions (A unless marked)

- Central ECCA case: AZ individual STO giving of $86-112M over roughly 3.3M AZ returns is $26-34 a return; times about 160M US returns is $4.2-5.4B; I use **$5B a year by 2030** (central), with Treasury's **$26B** as the upside case. Arizona is the most mature individual-credit state, so this is a ceiling for a mature market, not a 2027 number. A realistic 2027-28 ramp is $1-2B.
- State programs (ESA, voucher, credit) grow from $10.6B (S) to about $16B by 2029 (A, about 15% a year).
- Blended ECCA scholarship size: Treasury's two sizes are $12,000 and $4,500 (S); I use $8,000 blended (A) for counting recipients.

# INSIDER

## 1. The national ESA provider network ("Forage + CAQH + Headway, for ESA vendors")

**Persona:** state ESA program director with 15+ years, the person who approves vendors and answers the legislature when a tutor is not paid.

**One-liner:** A payer-agnostic credential, one-invoice billing and two-day payout for the tutors, therapists, pod leaders and curriculum sellers who are paid out of ESA and scholarship wallets in every state.

- **Customer:** independent providers and small vendors working in ESA states; the family-facing directory is a by-product.
- **Payer and signer:** the provider pays a subscription ($29-99 a month) plus 1.5% for two-day payout (A); states, SGOs and wallet platforms pay $10-25 per credential check (A). Signer: the provider owner; at the state, the ESA director or treasurer.
- **Problem:** Every program keeps its own vendor list: Tennessee's was built with Student First Technologies, North Carolina runs a state portal, Florida has MyScholarShop, others run through ClassWallet or Odyssey (S). Indiana providers do a state credential check and then a separate ClassWallet onboarding (S). EdChoice reports vendor payments taking 7-30 days and administrator approvals up to 21 days (S). The states do not actually know who their vendors are: Arizona deactivated more than 900 tutors in 2023 for accreditation, and a 2025 report found no cross-check against disciplinary records (S). Texas celebrated 2,400 vendors at launch while the marketplace listed about 1,200 on 7 July (S).
- **Earned secret:** the binding constraint in an ESA is willing, credentialed supply, not family demand or money. A director cannot fix it because each state is bound to its own platform vendor and its own statute, and the platforms are rivals who will not share credentials with each other. (That directors want a portable credential is an insider hypothesis, U.)
- **Solution:** (a) a reusable provider record (licence, background check, disciplinary cross-check, accreditation, W-9); (b) adapters to every wallet and portal so the provider sends one invoice and gets one remittance; (c) the network pays the provider in two days and collects from the wallet (a small factoring spread on state-backed money); (d) a verified-status directory for families.
- **Why now:** Texas, Tennessee, Alabama, Indiana and others launched or expanded in 2025-26; from 1 Jan 2027 the federal credit adds 600-700 SGOs, each with its own approved-vendor rule (S). The NPRM requires SGOs to pay other vendors directly only if "verified" as appropriate providers and unrelated to the student (S), which is a credentialing mandate.
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** therapists, tutors and pod leaders in Texas and Arizona with $50k-$500k a year of ESA revenue; two states' ESA offices willing to accept the network credential as a pre-check (Texas says it "rarely rejected vendors", S).
- **Path to $100M:** non-tuition vendor spend is about 41% of Arizona ESA dollars (tuition and fees 58.7%, ADE via House testimony, S; EdChoice's older cohorts show 83% tuition, S, so I use 35%). 60% of state flows are ESA-type (A): $16B x 60% x 35% = **$3.4B** by 2029, plus ECCA vendor-paid scholarships (part-time scholarships paid to tutors and therapists) of about 40% of $5B = **$2B** (A). Total about **$5.4B** of provider billings; at a 5% take that is $270M of pool, so **$100M needs about 37% of it**. Cross-check: 40,000 active providers x $2,500 a year. In Treasury's case ($26B) the pool is about $690M and the share needed is 14%.
- **Who does it today:** ClassWallet marketplace and directory, Odyssey vendor marketplace, Student First Technologies (Tennessee), MyScholarShop (Step Up, Florida), state portals (NC, IN); Outschool and Varsity Tutors sit inside ClassWallet's North Carolina marketplace (S). Forage (YC; $40M Series B; accepts SNAP, WIC, HSA, FSA online) has no ESA product I could find, but Intuit Ventures says it is built to expand to other restricted-spend categories (S). Headway and Alma credential therapists for insurers, not for ESAs (U). No national directory exists (S).
- **Why they lose:** each platform is paid by one state and wants an exclusive vendor list; none will export a credential that makes its rival's job easier. A neutral network that providers pay for gets the whole supply side.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** network economies on the supply side plus switching costs (billing history, payout advance).
- **Risks:** a platform denies API access; Forage or ClassWallet copies it; the factoring spread needs a warehouse line; states may refuse a private credential.
- **First 90 days:** onboard 200 providers in Texas and Arizona; build adapters to ClassWallet and Odyssey vendor portals; measure days-to-paid versus the 7-30 day baseline; ask one state office to accept the credential.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5/10.

## 2. School payments and capital OS ("Toast for private schools and microschools")

**Persona:** private-school business manager with 25 years, the person who runs collections and meets payroll.

**One-liner:** Multi-payer tuition acceptance with automatic reconciliation, instant payout, and working-capital advances repaid by sweeping the choice money before it reaches the school's bank.

- **Customer:** private schools and microschools that take ESA, voucher and SGO money (the count of such schools is unknown; there are about 29,730 private schools and about 75,000 microschools, S).
- **Payer and signer:** the school pays 0.5-0.8% on volume plus $150-300 a month and a fee on advances; signer: head of school or business manager (microschool: the founder).
- **Problem:** Choice money does not arrive on the payroll calendar. Texas private-school awards arrive 25% on 1 Jul, 25% on 1 Oct and **50% on 1 Feb 2027** (S). A school with 100 TEFA students at $10,474 (S) is owed about $524k that lands six months after teachers start. Arizona stopped authorizing payments to schools and vendors in November 2022 and later released about $22M of overdue payments, including to a small school that had been shopping for a bank loan (S). Banks will not lend against it: the receivable is a family-controlled, audit-exposed state account, not an invoice.
- **Earned secret:** the business manager's real job is chasing four to six payers per family, and the loss event is not the family defaulting but a payer freezing or clawing back. The NPRM now requires schools to return any SGO payment in excess of the student's costs or paid in error (S). A lender that watches the payment calendar and the wallet can price this risk; a bank cannot. (The "spreadsheet of remittances" detail is an insider hypothesis, U.)
- **Solution:** (1) accept ESA wallets, SGO, voucher, 529 and parent ACH or card on one invoice, with mid-year withdrawal and refund handling; (2) pay the school the next day; (3) advance against scheduled payments, underwritten on payer calendar, enrollment contracts and payer history, repaid by payment direction through the wallet partner; (4) later, family gap loans and facilities loans.
- **Why now:** TEFA, Alabama, Indiana and Florida expansions; 31% of Catholic schools already take choice money (S); federal wallets from 2027.
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** ten Texas private schools and microschools with TEFA students (an Alpine school said it was on the vendor list but unsure what is required, S); a pilot warehouse of $2-3M (A).
- **Path to $100M:** choice-funded tuition volume on the platform of **$5B** (about 30% of $16B state flows, A) x 0.6% net = $30M; 6,000 schools x $2,400 SaaS = $14M; advances: 30% of that volume financed for an average of four months = **$500M average outstanding** x 14% yield = $70M. Total about **$114M**. That needs about 6,000 schools and 30% of choice-funded tuition volume, which is heroic.
- **Who does it today:** FACTS/Nelnet (about 12,000 schools, $9B of tuition, S) and its new Compass for SGOs; Building Hope's Lending Lab (bridge loans for ESA timing; microschools up to 100 students) and a $675k Stand Together/Building Hope microschool loan fund that requires a signed lease (S); Charter School Capital (receivable sales for charters, S); ClassWallet and Odyssey pay vendors directly but do not lend.
- **Why they lose:** FACTS earns on family-paid fees and card processing and sells SIS lock-in; a payer-side lockbox that diverts card volume is against its model, and its lending sits in a student-loan group. The CDFIs are grant-capped. Platforms cannot lend against state money they administer. This is the idea FACTS can most credibly copy; the edge is risk pricing off payment calendars and speed.
- **Moat:** switching costs (billing + payments + capital in one login), process power in underwriting (payer behavior data), scale economies in cost of funds.
- **Risks:** state audit freezes (Arizona 2022); an Idaho-style termination of a platform (S); consumer-finance rules on family loans; FACTS response.
- **First 90 days:** sign ten schools; negotiate payment-direction with one wallet partner; stand up a $2-3M facility from a specialty lender.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5/10.

# PATTERN

## 3. The K-12 education wallet ("HealthEquity for scholarship dollars")

**Pattern transferred:** float and spread on payment rails without credit risk (HSA custodian), combined with mandate-created rails (the new "qualified digital wallet").

**One-liner:** A bank-partnered wallet and custody product for the 600-700 new SGOs: they transfer scholarship dollars to it on day one (which counts as "spent" for the 90% test), it holds them until families spend them, pays vendors directly, and shares the yield.

- **Customer:** SGOs, including dioceses, school networks and community foundations that become SGOs.
- **Payer and signer:** the SGO pays a wallet fee out of its 10-15% overhead room; vendors pay for fast pay and interchange; the provider keeps a negotiated share of the yield. Signer: SGO executive director with the board treasurer.
- **Why it has not happened yet:** the proposed regulation defined the wallet on 1 Oct 2026; comments close 1 Dec; final rules are likely 2027 (S). Existing platforms were built to be hired by a state.
- **Earned secret (structural, not insider):** the NPRM says that if the SGO does not retain ownership, funds moved into a qualified wallet count as spent on the transfer date (S). Donations pile up in Q4 but scholarships are paid Aug-Jun, so an SGO that wants to meet its 90% test early has every reason to move cash into a wallet immediately. Whoever runs the wallet becomes the custodian of the whole float. Floats on state money are politically dangerous (Idaho ended Odyssey's contract in Oct 2024 after reports of non-educational purchases and interest earned, S), but ECCA money is private SGO money governed by contract.
- **Solution:** FBO accounts at a sponsor bank (pass-through insurance), ACH and direct pay to schools, debit and marketplace for non-tuition items, receipts and reimbursement controls that the NPRM requires, SKU-level eligibility checks (the HSA/FSA IIAS model, required of non-healthcare merchants since 2006-08, S), vendor network from idea 1, a yield-sharing schedule published to SGOs, and later 529 K-12 and family top-ups.
- **Path to $100M:** central ECCA $5B a year: donations arrive 60% in Q4 (A), average holding of about seven months, so **average balance about $2.9B** x 3.5% yield (HealthEquity's cash yield, S) x 60% kept = **$61M**; plus 0.5% servicing on $5B disbursed = $25M; plus 1.7% interchange on the 25% that is card or marketplace spend = $21M. Total about **$107M, which needs essentially all of the central case**. In Treasury's $26B case the same math gives about $560M, so $100M needs about 18%. HealthEquity earns about $36 of custodial revenue per account (S). Honest read: the highest ceiling here, the least certain volume.
- **Who does it today:** ClassWallet (runs Missouri's privately funded EAO programs on a shared wallet; one EAO reports $26.2M distributed to 57 schools, S), Odyssey (ACH after proof of delivery plus a marketplace, S), FACTS Compass (fundraising and enrollment first, more in 2027, S), ACE Scholarships (services), banks and BaaS providers (U).
- **Why they lose:** state-contract SaaS vendors price per account, cannot keep yield on state money, and sell through RFPs; serving 600 small nonprofits needs self-serve KYB, a sponsor bank and a treasury product they have not built (U, I cannot see their contracts).
- **Moat:** scale economies in cash and cost of funds, switching costs (SGO records and vendor network), a bank relationship as a cornered resource.
- **Risks:** final rules could require the SGO to keep ownership or interest; money-transmission licensing; SGOs demand most of the yield; rate cuts; Idaho-style political fights; ClassWallet copies it.
- **First 90 days:** file a comment letter by 1 Dec asking Treasury to confirm wallet transfers and clarify who owns interest; sign a sponsor bank; pilot with three SGOs (including one Missouri-style EAO).
- **Outside-the-box score:** 7/10.

## 4. The SGO host ("Fidelity Charitable for scholarships")

**Pattern transferred:** wrapper intermediary on pass-through flows: carry the legal status and compliance risk that neither donors nor affinity groups want to carry, and book the flow (the digest's caution: always size the net take).

**One-liner:** A registered multi-state SGO that hosts branded affiliate funds for dioceses, school associations and networks that want their own donors but cannot run 25F compliance.

- **Customer:** affiliates: dioceses and Catholic school foundations (5,829 Catholic schools, S), Christian-school associations, Jewish day-school federations, charter-adjacent networks, community foundations.
- **Payer and signer:** the host SGO (a nonprofit) contracts a service company at fair market value for marketing, donor care, awarding, verification and compliance, paid from the host's overhead room (10% of income, up to 15% of activities with the safe harbor, S). Signer: host board; affiliate side, the foundation director.
- **Why not yet:** the credit starts 1 Jan 2027; vendors sell software to existing SGOs, not a legal wrapper.
- **Earned secret:** giving to school scholarship funds is affinity-driven (parish, school, denomination), not cold-digital, and affiliates will not hand their donors to a statewide SGO that competes for them. (Insider hypothesis, U; Arizona's individual giving fell 23% in a year even with 28 years of habit, S, which is why affinity matters.) Every affiliate needs the same compliance: 501(c)(3), segregated 25F account per state, audits, IRS portal, Jan 31 and Feb 28 filings (S). The multistate safe harbor lets one SGO serve many states (S).
- **Legal premise to check:** no earmarking for a particular student and 10+ scholarships to students at more than one school (S) are tests at the host, so affiliate "funds" can brand and solicit but cannot direct awards; whether a school-specific brand is allowed is a question for counsel (U). Use the comment period (to 1 Dec) to ask. Whether donor-advised-fund grants can qualify is not addressed in the NPRM (U).
- **Path to $100M:** fee of 8% of hosted gifts (covers marketing and operations, inside the overhead cap, A) = $100M needs **$1.25B hosted**, which is 25% of the $5B central case or 5% of Treasury's $26B.
- **Who does it today:** FACTS Compass (120+ SGOs and group programs, S), Odyssey (SGO suite, S), ClassWallet, ACE Scholarships (all-in-one services, S), LearningSpring, Vouchive, AwardSpring (600+ organizations), Foundant (S); statewide nonprofits such as Step Up (3% cap) act as SGOs themselves. I found no white-label host.
- **Why they lose:** software vendors cannot be a charity; incumbent SGOs will not host rivals' donors; affinity groups will not give up brand control.
- **Moat:** regulatory status in 30 states (cornered resource), scale economies in compliance, affinity-brand switching costs.
- **Risks:** counsel says branded funds violate the earmark or same-school tests; private-benefit and inurement rules on the service company; donors refuse; central-case volume.
- **First 90 days:** line up three affiliates (a diocesan foundation, a Christian-school association, a day-school federation); get a legal opinion; file the comment letter; choose a state of formation.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 6/10.

# DILEMMA

## 5. The neutral eligibility and anti-duplication clearinghouse ("National Student Clearinghouse for K-12 scholarships")

**What incumbents structurally cannot do:** the platforms (FACTS, Odyssey, ClassWallet, Step Up) are direct rivals and will not pool award data; each state is bound to its own statute; each SGO sees only its own awards. The proposed rule now requires every SGO to run "reasonable procedures", including systems to detect duplicate awards to the same student for the same expense (S).

**One-liner:** A consent-based ledger of who was awarded what for which expense across SGOs, state ESAs and vouchers, plus a reusable eligibility credential verified once.

- **Customer and payer:** SGOs (compliance officers), wallet platforms, state agencies. Per-recipient annual fee of $12, per-credential fee of $10, plus an integrity-analytics subscription for states (A). Signer: SGO CFO or compliance lead; state ESA director.
- **Problem:** duplicate awards and misuse are named by stakeholders as the main fraud sources in existing programs (S). A student can hold an ESA, two SGO awards and a 529 draw against one tuition bill. Treasury prices income verification at about $100 per household (S); each SGO and state repeats it.
- **Earned secret:** the only cross-program control today is a phone call between program staff (insider hypothesis, U). Odyssey's SGO page mentions income, residency and identity checks and fraud controls but not anti-duplication, and FACTS Compass's first release does not mention it either (S).
- **Solution:** a neutral party under family consent holds the award ledger and eligibility credential; platforms and SGOs call an API before awarding; one verification is reusable across SGOs (categorical eligibility, direct income documents, safe-harbor flags per the NPRM); states get dashboards. The long-run asset is the dataset and the standard.
- **Why now:** the NPRM appeared on 1 Oct 2026; SGOs must stand up procedures for 2027; 600-700 SGOs by 2030 (S).
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** the ten largest new SGOs in Texas, Florida and Georgia plus one platform partner for duplicate checks; the Texas waitlist (about 153,000, S) as the first eligibility pool.
- **Path to $100M:** central case: 0.63M ECCA recipients ($5B / $8,000) + about 2.5M state-program recipients (A) = 3.1M x $12 = $37M; 2.5M credentials x $10 = $25M; 30 states x $1M of analytics = $30M. Total about **$92M, which needs nearly every recipient on the rail**, a winner-take-most standard. Treasury's case: about 3.5M ECCA recipients + 2.5M = 6M x $12 = $72M + 5M credentials x $10 = $50M + $30M = $152M, so about 66% of recipients. Analog: the National Student Clearinghouse reaches $102.5M at 3,600 institutions (S).
- **Who does it today:** nothing cross-platform found. In-house checks at each SGO and platform; Equifax Work Number at $46.95 per record (S); IRS IVES and payroll-data aggregators (U); the National Student Clearinghouse for postsecondary only.
- **Why they lose:** rivals will not share, states cannot cross lines, and a nonprofit SGO has no budget for a data product under its overhead cap.
- **Moat:** network economies and a standard; switching costs; a data asset no single platform can build.
- **Risks:** a nonprofit or SGO-funded consortium offers it free; Treasury drops or softens the duplication language; student-data privacy law (U); platforms refuse API calls; the price per check collapses.
- **First 90 days:** comment on the NPRM asking Treasury to name a shared registry as a reasonable procedure; build the ledger API with two SGOs; sign a platform partner.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 7/10.

## 6. The family-facing funding stack and net-price front door ("Common App for choice dollars")

**What incumbents structurally cannot do:** each payer can only promote its own pot. State agencies cannot advertise SGO scholarships or other states' programs; each SGO markets only itself; schools market sticker tuition; ClassWallet and Odyssey are paid by states; FACTS Grant & Aid is paid by schools and sees only aid.

**One-liner:** One free application and net-price screen that shows a family every ESA, voucher, SGO scholarship and school aid it qualifies for, school by school, and submits once; paid per funded student by SGOs and per enrolled student by schools.

- **Customer and payer:** families use it free; SGOs pay about $100 per awarded student (cheaper than the $100 income verification Treasury assumes, S); schools pay about $300 per new enrolled student (A). Signer: SGO development director; school head or admissions director.
- **Problem:** Texas had about 274,000 applications for about 85,000-107,000 funded seats (S) and awards of $10,474 against private tuition that is often higher (A); families cannot see net price after stacking. NPRM's own stacking rule (the $1,700 cap applies after state credits) is a donor problem, but the family-side version is the same complexity (S). Arizona private schools raised tuition after ESAs (Hechinger headline, S), so sticker price is no guide.
- **Earned secret:** the family does not need more school reviews; it needs the stack. (Hypothesis, U.)
- **Solution:** eligibility engine and net-price calculator across programs; single intake that routes to each payer and reuses the credential from idea 5; waitlist exchange (Texas holds a waitlist of about 153,000, S; Texas participates in the federal credit, S, so SGO dollars from 2027 can fund waitlisted children).
- **Why now:** Texas's waitlist meets the first federal-credit scholarship season (2027-28).
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** Texas waitlisted families and three Texas SGOs; ten private schools with seats.
- **Path to $100M:** 0.6M funded students a year through the platform x $100 = $60M, plus 0.15M new-to-school students x $300 = $45M. Total **$105M, needing about 55% of the roughly 1.1M newly funded students each year (A), which is heroic.** Treat as a funnel that feeds ideas 3 and 5 rather than a standalone business.
- **Who does it today:** state school-finder portals, Odyssey and ClassWallet application flows, Step Up's own application, FACTS Grant & Aid (S); Niche, GreatSchools, SchoolMint-type enrollment tools (U).
- **Why they lose:** no payer can show the others' money; the neutral party can.
- **Moat:** two-sided network economies; data on net price.
- **Risks:** disintermediation by schools; states restrict third-party applications; thin moat; a score of 4 reflects that a smart generalist would think of it in five minutes.
- **First 90 days:** Texas waitlist pilot with one SGO and ten schools.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 4/10.

# SHOCK

## 7. The permitted-seat platform ("E-occupancy landlord and turnkey seats")

**Shock:** TEFA launched 1 Jul 2026 with $1B; about 274,000 applications against about 85,000-107,000 funded; about 153,000 waitlisted; the Comptroller wants $2B (S). Money is abundant, seats are not. **Pattern too:** policy money flows first to whoever already holds permitted physical capacity (the CoreCivic/GEO lesson in the digest).

**One-liner:** Convert closing school buildings and church education wings into permitted 100-seat schools once, then sell turnkey seats to microschool operators on long leases backed by the ESA and SGO payment stream.

- **Customer:** microschool and small-school operators (75,000 microschools, median 20 students, S) and, on the supply side, church trustees and diocesan CFOs.
- **Payer and signer:** operator pays about $2,900 per seat a year (28% of a $10.5k award, A) for facility, furniture, insurance, safety systems and payment rail; signer: operator founder. Landlord side: pastor or diocesan CFO.
- **Problem:** microschools are limited by occupancy classification and zoning. Utah's 2026 law caps a facility at 100 students and ties enrollment to occupancy codes (S); a Utah city requires Group E (educational) occupancy for a micro-education entity (S); Louisiana's draft text says a lease does not change occupancy classification (U, bill draft). Funders such as the Stand Together/Building Hope microschool loan fund require a signed commercial lease first (S).
- **Earned secret:** the scarce asset is a permitted building, not a teacher or a curriculum. Dioceses are closing schools (63 Catholic closures in 2024-25, S) while ESA demand exceeds seats in the same metros. (Facility scarcity at the margin is an inference, U: nearly 40% of Catholic schools have waiting lists, S, but others have room.)
- **Solution:** master-lease or acquire; do the occupancy, fire and zoning conversion once; deliver standardized 100-seat sites to operators (Prenda-style, Acton-style, local founders); collect rent through the wallet sweep from idea 3.
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** three Texas sites, then Arizona, Florida and Tennessee; two operators; one lender for conversion capex.
- **Path to $100M:** **34,000 seats x $2,900 = $99M**, which is 340 sites x 100 seats and about 22% of Texas's waitlist alone (A). Capital need: $15-30M of working capital and conversion capex in the master-lease model (A), far more if buying.
- **Who does it today:** operators lease churches one at a time; Prenda is home-based and asset-light; KaiPod takes a 10% revenue share for 2 years and charges $249 to join (S); CDFIs and Building Hope finance after a lease exists (S); churches lease directly. I found no scaled "school-use" landlord.
- **Why they lose:** asset-light operators cannot hold leases or conversion risk (their investors fund software); dioceses cannot operate; CDFIs lend only after a lease; commercial landlords will not convert space for a 20-student tenant.
- **Moat:** cornered resource (permitted buildings in the right metros), process power in permitting.
- **Risks:** political durability of ESA funding is the master risk; child-safety liability (abuse and molestation coverage); local zoning fights; church governance; capital intensity.
- **First 90 days:** map 150 closing or under-used school buildings in Texas; sign three sites and two operators; price conversion capex with an architect and a fire marshal.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 7/10.

## 8. The credit concierge ("the free planning tool for tax professionals and payroll, paid by SGOs")

**Shock:** the federal credit starts 1 Jan 2027; Treasury expects 11M donors and $26B (S). **Pattern transferred:** a free tool for a profession, paid by the suppliers who want that profession's attention (OpenEvidence/Doximity).

**One-liner:** Make a $1,700 gift cost the donor nothing by planning it with their CPA or payroll team: capacity, state-credit stacking, W-4 offset, payroll deduction.

- **Customer:** tax preparers, CPAs and enrolled agents; payroll and HR administrators at employers in the 30 participating states; donors via both.
- **Payer and signer:** SGOs pay the platform about 6% of each completed gift (A); the tool is free to CPAs and employers. Signer: SGO development director or executive director.
- **Problem:** the credit is nonrefundable, so donors need $1,700 of liability; the donor pays cash before the credit; stacking with state credits changes the net outcome; the gift must be made in the tax year (timing detail U). The NPRM itself estimates 46.5M taxpayers could use both state and federal credits and at 10% take-up about 3.1M taxpayers (and 1.55M more married filers) would net up to $1,700 or $3,400 (S).
- **Earned secret:** the decision is made by the advisor, in Q3-Q4, not by the donor, in Feb-April. TurboTax-time is after the deadline (U). Payroll deduction is an explicitly allowed contribution method (S), and W-4 Step 3 lets an employee add "the value of any other credits" to reduce withholding (S), so a payroll gift can be made cash-neutral; Treasury has not endorsed that use (U; the 74 commentary is a proposal). Arizona individual giving dropped about 23% from FY2022 to FY2023 (S), so the habit must be built.
- **Solution:** a free calculator for CPAs (client-by-client credit capacity, state stacking, carryforward, W-4 or estimated-tax adjustment, SGO shortlist by state need), an employer payroll-giving connector, donor-facing flow with Form 8525 numbers and Jan 31 acknowledgments handled for the SGO. No commissions to CPAs (AICPA rules on commissions to attest clients, U), flat SGO-paid fee on completed gifts.
- **Wedge and first 10 customers:** 200 CPA firms in Texas, Florida and Georgia; three payroll or PEO partners; five SGOs to pay.
- **Path to $100M:** central $5B x 30% intermediated = $1.5B x 6% = **$90M**; $100M needs about 33% of all central-case giving through the tool. In Treasury's case $26B x 30% x 4% = $312M.
- **Who does it today:** the SGOs' own donor teams; workplace-giving platforms (Benevity and others) with no SGO support found (S); payroll providers (none found); Intuit (U). No planning tool exists.
- **Why they lose:** SGOs cannot afford tax-advisor outreach inside 10-15% overhead; giving platforms do not give tax advice; payroll companies avoid charity; tax software is too late in the year.
- **Moat:** distribution into 100k+ preparer workflows (switching costs), data on donor capacity.
- **Risks:** IRS withholding guidance; donors fickle; CPAs distrust a vendor paid by SGOs; central-case volume; Treasury caps what SGOs may spend on solicitation inside the 85% test.
- **First 90 days:** sign three SGOs as payers; build the calculator; recruit 50 CPA firms by 15 Dec; file a comment asking Treasury to confirm that payroll-deduction gifts plus W-4 Step 3 are an acceptable cash-neutral pattern.
- **Outside-the-box score:** 5/10.

## Considered and killed

- **Recovery-audit contractor for ESA improper payments.** ADE's dollar-weighted rate is 1.9% unallowable and 0.3% fraudulent (S). On $10.6B (S) plus $5B of ECCA (A) that is about $300M of improper spend; a 12.5% Medicaid-style contingency (Connecticut proposes 17.46%, S) is under $40M even at 100% capture. Dead as a $100M business.
- **A pure ESA state-platform entrant.** Fees are 0.25%, $143.50 a head or 3-5% caps (S); the market leader's revenue band is $10-50M (S). Odyssey ($74M Series B reported) and ClassWallet already own the recompetes; Arizona's RFI 26A is real but the prize is a few $M a state.
- **SGO operating software.** FACTS Compass, Odyssey, ClassWallet, ACE, LearningSpring, Vouchive, AwardSpring and Foundant are all there (S). Folded into ideas 3, 4 and 5 as layers on top.
- **Income-verification API alone.** Treasury's $100 vs a plausible $10 price; about 3M checks is about $30M (A). Folded into idea 5.
- **IIAS-style SKU eligibility standard alone.** A few dollars per account; used as a moat in idea 3.
- **Diocesan enrollment-as-a-service.** Nearly 40% of Catholic schools already have waiting lists and enrollment is flat at about 1.67M (S); no empty-seat glut to fill.
- **Pediatric-therapy practice roll-up funded by ESA.** Disability awards average about $10k (FL $9.7-9.9k; TX most about $10.3k, up to $30k) (S); the $30k case is rare.
- **Microschool network operator.** Prenda has about 10,000 students (S); even at a $2,199 platform fee (U) that is about $22M.
- **Accreditor for microschools.** Needs about 40k schools at $2.5k to reach $100M (A); share is heroic.

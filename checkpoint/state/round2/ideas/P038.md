## Pool anchors (re-based on newer data than pools_selected.json)

Source: NEI "Nuclear Costs in Context, 2025 Cost Data" (EUCG data, published August 2026), which supersedes the 2023-based $26B in pools_selected.json.

- Total generating cost $36.46/MWh in 2025 (+5.1% year on year). Fuel $5.95, capital $9.69 (= $7.59B, +23.9%), operating $20.82.
- Industry labor is "nearly $8.8B", more than half of operating cost.
- Derived by me: $7.59B / $9.69 per MWh implies ~783 TWh. So total ~$28.6B/yr, operating ~$16.3B, non-labor operating ~$7.5B, fuel ~$4.7B.
- **Third-party spend (non-labor operating + capital + fuel) is ~$19.8B/yr**, spread across ~25 owner-operators and 55 sites. The other ~$8.8B is in-house labor.
- Uprate capital spend: $66M (2022) to $365M (2025). NEI cites NRC: 30 uprates / 2,291 MWe expected by 2030. NRC's own expected-applications table totals ~2.4 GW through 2032. Remaining potential is estimated at 3-8 GW.
- Merchant plants $31.47/MWh vs cost-of-service plants $40.31 (capital $5.15 vs $13.19). 100 reactors have initial license renewal, 25 have second renewal.
- Hyperscaler money: Constellation-Google, 890 MW of uprates at 11 units for "more than $4.3B" (about $4.8k/kW, not like-for-like). Vistra $4.2B DOE loan for 3 plants / 433 MW. DOE conditional loans of $17.5B (June 2026) for long-lead items for up to 10 AP1000s.

Honest read of the pool: the money is real but fragmented. Software-only per-site subscriptions (Nuclearn-style) cap at tens of millions. The paths that credibly reach $100M either (a) take a share of a labor line (engineering, contingent labor), (b) become certified capacity, or (c) become a standard that many competitors must accept.

## Ideas considered and killed (so nobody re-derives them)

- **Spent-fuel damages claims rail.** ~$11B paid cumulatively (Nov 2023), advocacy figure ~$800M/yr, 16 cases pending. A 10% fee on 100% share is <$80M, and law firms already hold the relationships.
- **Pooled spares exchange.** Pooled Inventory Management (PIM) has run since 1980 with every US licensee participating ($247M of critical components, undated Southern document). The rapidpartsmart network is "used by every nuclear utility in North America". Free substitute plus small.
- **Nuclear parts marketplace / group purchasing.** USA Inc. is a non-profit co-op (9 stations shown on its site) targeting $100M of member value by 2030. A 1-2% take on $20B needs 30-60% share.
- **Deregulation-savings ESCO.** NRC's own regulatory analyses say the EO 14300 rules save little. ALARA elimination: $9.53M/yr industry ($10.5M/yr with NRC and Agreement States). Security modernization: $45.2M/yr annualized at 7%. Licensing overhaul: $15-22M/yr (secondhand). The deregulation shock is not a money pool. Do not build on it.
- **Security automation.** Fleet security was ~$1.15B in 2017. The NRC rule saves $45M/yr, the topic is sensitive, and the final rule is not due before 2027.
- **Fleet-as-a-service for one-plant operators.** The owner-level cost gap swung from $0.59/MWh (2023) to $6.57/MWh (2025). The structural single-unit penalty ($45.77 vs $34.36) is fixed site cost that cannot be shared. Unstable and unshareable.
- **Counterfeit/suspect item detection.** Per-event revenue, under $20M.

## 1. [insider] Signed-Off: the QA-accountable AI engineering house

**One-liner.** Sell plants finished, signed, Appendix B-compliant engineering deliverables at a fixed price per deliverable. They are produced by an AI pipeline that sits inside a qualified quality program. Do not sell another AI tool, and do not sell more hourly engineers.

**Insider.** NQA-1 lead auditor plus plant licensing engineer with 15+ years in the job.

**Customer.** Design and fleet engineering organizations at the ~25 owner-operators. Start with single-site and restart operators that have thin benches (e.g., Energy Northwest, Evergy/Wolf Creek, Ameren/Callaway, Holtec/Palisades, Constellation/Crane, NextEra/Duane Arnold; names from background knowledge).

**Payer and signer.** Contracted engineering and supplemental-staff lines in O&M, plus project engineering inside the $7.59B capital budget. Signer: Director of Design Engineering or VP Engineering. Supply Chain adds the firm to the Approved Supplier List, and the QA director must accept the quality program.

**Problem.** Capital spend jumped 24% in 2025 and ~30 uprates, three restarts and 20+ license renewals queue behind a shrinking engineer pool. The work product must be traceable, independently verified and issued by an organization under 10 CFR 50 Appendix B. That includes 50.59 screenings and evaluations, calculation revisions, engineering change packages and license-amendment sections. AI tools exist (Nuclearn lists an "Engineering AI" product), but a tool's output cannot be used on safety-related work until a qualified organization owns it. So engineers redo it by hand and the overflow goes out hourly to Sargent & Lundy-class firms.

**Solution.** An engineering supplier with four parts:
- Its own NQA-1/Appendix B program, listed on customers' Approved Supplier Lists after a NUPIC-style joint audit.
- A version-locked AI drafting and checking stack, verified under NQA-1 software QA.
- Nuclear-qualified engineers who review and sign every output.
- Fixed price per deliverable with a rework guarantee.

Over time the verified calculation and evaluation templates per plant design become a library.

**Why now.** Capital +24% in 2025 and rising uprate capex meet a thin engineer bench. A DOE/INL/ANL/Microsoft/Everstar demo turned a 4-6 week license-application drafting job into one day (208-page draft). Hyperscaler contracts let owners pay for capital but not for engineers.

**Wedge.** 50.59 screenings and calculation revisions (high volume, bounded) for 2-3 single-site operators. The plant's own engineer signs the first pilots (quality-related scope) while the QA program and ASL listing are built. Faster: buy or white-label a 30-100 person ASL-listed engineering boutique and run its QA program. First 10 customers: single-plant operators plus the three restarts. EUCG counts 10 "one plant" operators.

**Path to $100M.** ASSUMPTION: no published figure for outsourced nuclear engineering exists.
- Outsourced engineering is ~10% of capital ($0.76B) plus ~5% of non-labor operating ($0.37B), about $1.1B/yr.
- Add the uprate/restart wave: 2.3 GW x ~$4.8k/kW is ~$11B, and 8-10% engineering is ~$1B over 5 years, about $200M/yr.
- The pool is ~$1.3-1.4B/yr by 2028-30. $100M is ~7% of it, about 400 billable-engineer equivalents at ~$250k. Because deliverables are priced at a discount to hourly, volume share is ~10-12%.
- Upside: ~25 funded reactor developers each spend tens of millions on engineering and licensing (inference).
- A software-only subscription across ~70 facilities caps at ~$35-70M (my arithmetic at $0.5-1M per site), which is why the service model is the path.

**Who does it today.**
- Hourly engineering firms: Sargent & Lundy (ENR #1 in nuclear plants, eight times in a decade), Enercon (self-described top 3), Jensen Hughes (ENR #4), plus Framatome, Westinghouse, Bechtel and Aptim.
- Owner in-house design engineering.
- AI tools: Nuclearn ($10.5M Series A, "over 70 facilities", annual per-product subscription), Atomic Canyon ($7M seed, Diablo Canyon), Everstar ($4M pre-seed, Gordian).

**Why they lose.**
- Hourly firms cannot cut hours without cutting revenue.
- Tool vendors do not carry QA accountability or an engineer's signature.
- Frontier labs will not hold an Appendix B program.
- Owners will not accept unowned output.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Switching costs plus process power. ASL status takes 12-24 months of audits. Each owner's QA acceptance and per-plant verified templates lock in. Counter-positioning against hourly billers.

**Earned secret.** Nobody in nuclear buys AI; they buy a name on the signature block. The binding constraint is who is accountable under Appendix B, not model quality, which is why every AI product stays stuck on search and screening.

**First 90 days.** Interview 15 design-engineering directors and QA managers, plus 2 ASL-listed boutiques (for acquisition or white-label). Write the NQA-1 software-QA plan. Run a paid pilot of ~20 50.59 screenings with 1-2 single-site operators, plant engineer signing, and measure hours saved and rework rate. Schedule the supplier audit. Legal gate: Appendix B Criterion VII makes the licensee responsible for its suppliers. State PE-licensing rules for engineering services apply. No NRC rule bars AI-assisted analysis if it is QA-controlled (to confirm with counsel).

**Outside-the-box score:** 7.

## 2. [insider] Dose Ledger: the nuclear traveler network

**One-liner.** A W-2 employer-of-record network of radiation-protection technicians, welders, pipefitters, NDE and I&C techs that chains workers across every outage in the country. Each worker carries a portable credential and dose-headroom ledger, so plants get badged crews in days and workers get ~40 weeks of work a year.

**Insider.** Outage manager plus senior RP technician.

**Customer.** Outage managers and site-support directors at ~55 sites. Also the integrated outage primes (Westinghouse, Framatome, GE Vernova Hitachi) that subcontract labor. Later: restarts and new-build owners.

**Payer and signer.** Supplemental-labor lines in outage O&M. Signer: Outage Manager or Director of Site Support, with Supply Chain executing the MSA.

**Problem.**
- A refueling outage brings 1,000-1,400 temporary workers (Entergy ANO 1,300; Columbia Generating Station 1,400 for a 40-day outage; Davis-Besse 1,000+).
- Outage seasons cluster, so every plant bids for the same people at once.
- The senior contract RP technician pool shrank ~37% in three years (Health Physics, 2022).
- Travelers idle between seasons and quit.
- Each site re-badges a worker (background, fitness-for-duty, training, dose history), and that sits on the outage critical path.

**Solution.**
- Employer-of-record payroll.
- A credential vault: certifications, training, access status, respirator fit.
- A dose-headroom ledger: remaining annual dose, so planners see who can take a high-dose job.
- A scheduler that chains workers across plants' outage calendars.
- Pre-clearance workflows so site badging takes days.

**Why now.** Hyperscaler-priced power raises the value of an outage day to ~$1.6-2.4M for a 1.1 GW unit at $60-90/MWh (arithmetic), so labor readiness is the cheapest lever. Restarts (Palisades, Crane, Duane Arnold) and new builds compete for the same people. NRC's July 2026 ALARA proposal keeps dose limits unchanged but may reduce RP hours.

**Wedge.** Senior RP technicians, the scarcest trade. Spring 2027 outage season (March-May) for 2-3 single-site operators or one integrated prime. First 10 customers: single-site operators plus one OEM outage organization.

**Path to $100M.**
- ~50 outages/yr (inference from 94 reactors on 18-24 month cycles) x 1,000-1,400 temporary workers is ~50-70k worker-outages.
- At ~300-350 hours x ~$75/hr blended bill rate (Vogtle outage posting: senior RPT $39.52-44.72/hr), that is ~$22-26k per worker-outage. Outage temp labor is ~$1.2-1.6B/yr. Cross-check: older trade figures put direct outage cost at $300-800k/day x ~38 days, or $11-30M per outage.
- $100M of billings is ~4,000-4,500 worker-outages, ~7% share.
- Gross margin is only ~15-20%, so this is a $100M-revenue business, not a $100M-profit one, unless the ledger, training, payroll and housing layers earn more.

**Who does it today.**
- Integrated outage primes: Westinghouse (10-year integrated outage contract with Xcel), Framatome (integrated outage services for FENOC), GE Vernova Hitachi (>$70M Browns Ferry outage services contract).
- Direct hiring: Westinghouse career postings for outage technicians.
- Job boards: NukeWorker.
- A long tail of regional craft and RP staffing firms (not individually verified).
- I found no funded startup doing credentialing or outage staffing for nuclear.

**Why they lose.** Staffing firms are paid per head per outage and see only their own customers' schedules. Merchant rivals do not share outage calendars, so nobody can chain a worker into 40 weeks. Badging is rebuilt site by site.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Network economies (two-sided: workers and plants) plus cornered resource (the best few thousand travelers' loyalty and their dose/credential histories). Commodity staffing is the failure mode.

**Earned secret.** The shortage is a synchronization and paperwork problem as much as a headcount problem. Outage windows cluster, so travelers idle and quit. A worker's remaining annual dose headroom and badge status, not skills, decide who can take a job. Whoever sees every schedule sells continuity and speed. (Hypothesis to validate in the first 20 interviews.)

**First 90 days.** Interview 20 outage managers/RP supervisors and 30 travelers. Build the credential and dose ledger MVP with ~100 RP techs. Sign an LOI with one single-site operator or integrated prime for spring 2027. Register as an employer of record (W-2, workers' comp, staffing licensing). Legal gate: 10 CFR 73.56 access authorization and Part 26 fitness-for-duty remain licensee-run programs. Part 20 dose-record rules apply. Worker classification (W-2 vs 1099) must be W-2.

**Outside-the-box score:** 4.

## 3. [pattern] Certified Cell: Hadrian for nuclear (the automated NQA-1 parts factory)

**One-liner.** Transfer the defense neo-prime playbook (Hadrian-style automated precision-machining factories selling into supply-constrained primes) into nuclear. Build a factory whose machine-control and inspection systems write the Appendix B/NQA-1 paper trail as they cut, so certified capacity becomes the product.

**Pattern.** "Neo-primes and the defense supply-base rebuild" plus "Policy money flows to whoever holds permitted physical capacity."

**Customer.** Westinghouse and its AP1000 supply base (DOE long-lead loans), BWXT, Curtiss-Wright, valve and pump OEMs, reactor developers (TerraPower, X-energy, Kairos), and fleet owners (replacement internals, fasteners, supports, fittings).

**Payer and signer.** Chief Supply Chain Officer or VP Procurement at the OEM/developer, from component procurement in capital budgets. Fleet side: Director of Supply Chain with engineering acceptance.

**Problem.** Few shops hold ASME N-type certificates or NQA-1 programs, so a new-build and uprate wave hits a thin sub-tier. DOE conditional loans of $17.5B (up to five loans, two reactors each) are meant to pull long-lead purchasing forward by up to three years. Valar and Radiant are hiring supplier-quality engineers because the qualified base is small. Section 232 tariffs (April 2026: 25% on full value for most metal derivatives, 50% for near-all-metal) push buyers toward domestic content.

**Solution.** A high-mix, low-volume automated machining and inspection factory.
- Material heat traceability, in-process inspection and certificates are generated by the system.
- Hold ASME certification and an NQA-1 certificate.
- Pre-dedicated stock of fleet long-tail parts.
- Anchor customer offtake before building.

**Why now.** First sustained nuclear order book in 40 years, federal loans financing long-lead buys, tariffs, and defense-tech investors who have proven the factory model but have not looked at nuclear.

**Wedge.** Fasteners, valve and pump internals, supports and instrument fittings for AP1000 and developer orders, qualified through one anchor customer. Fleet long tail follows. First 10 customers: Westinghouse tier-1s, BWXT, 3-4 reactor developers, and Curtiss-Wright/Flowserve-class OEMs short of capacity.

**Path to $100M.**
- ASSUMPTION: 8-12% of the $17.5B long-lead value is machined or fabricated sub-tier content outside the big forgings, about $1.4-2.1B over ~6 years, or $230-350M/yr.
- Add the fleet long tail: parts are ~25% of ($7.59B capital + $7.5B non-labor operating), ~$3.8B, of which 20-25% is OEM-unsupported (Rolls-Royce study), about $0.8-0.9B.
- $100M is one large factory (~150-200 cells) and ~15-20% of the combined flow. Capital need ~$50-100M.
- **Honest verdict:** big only with an anchor offtake from Westinghouse, BWXT or a developer.

**Who does it today.**
- Dedicators and distributors that do not make parts: Park Nuclear (Oak Ridge dedication facility), ATC Nuclear, Nuclear Logistics.
- OEM aftermarket: Curtiss-Wright (commercial nuclear is a stated growth driver; ~$300M base unverified), Flowserve, Velan, Crane.
- Family-owned qualified machine shops.
- Kairos built its own U-stamp vessel shop.
- Hadrian itself is defense/aerospace.

**Why they lose.** OEMs prioritize new-build and cannot add capacity fast. Family shops cannot fund automation plus QA overhead. Dedicators do not manufacture.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Cornered resource (certified, audited capacity that takes 18-24 months to replicate) plus process power (automated records) plus scale.

**Earned secret.** The scarce thing is not machine time but certified, auditable lots. The paperwork twin of a safety-related part costs more than the metal. A factory that generates the paperwork automatically turns a compliance tax into a moat. Caution: 10 CFR 50.69 can move up to 75% of safety-related components (EPRI pilot result) out of special treatment, so target Class 1/2, pressure-boundary and new-build content, not the whole fleet tail.

**First 90 days.** Pick the 50 part families with the most backorders from two Westinghouse-qualified suppliers and one developer. Secure an anchor LOI. Identify a state with an existing ASME-certified shop to partner with or acquire. Draft the NQA-1 program. Apply for state/DOE incentives. Legal gate: ASME Section III/NCA-3800 certification, Part 21 reporting, customer ASL audits, Buy-American/domestic-content rules.

**Outside-the-box score:** 6.

## 4. [pattern] One Audit: a supplier-paid shared accreditation for nuclear (Nadcap for nuclear)

**One-liner.** Aerospace solved duplicate supplier audits in 1990 with Nadcap: suppliers pay once, every prime accepts. Do the same for the nuclear supply chain across competing primes and ~25 funded developers.

**Pattern.** "Mandate-created compliance rails" (Appendix B Criterion VII makes every licensee evaluate suppliers) plus "flip which side pays."

**Customer.** Subscribers: Westinghouse, Bechtel, Fluor, BWXT, Curtiss-Wright, TVA, X-energy, TerraPower, Kairos. Payers of accreditation: the suppliers themselves (machine shops, foundries, welders, testing labs).

**Payer and signer.** Supplier President/QA Manager pays fees. Subscribers' Chief Supply Chain Officers pay annual subscriptions from supplier-quality budgets.

**Problem.** Every developer builds its own supplier-quality team (Valar and Radiant job posts list supplier qualification, audits and dedication protocols). They re-audit the same shops. NUPIC (utility-run joint audits, formed 1989, relied on by the NRC) covers utilities, not competing OEMs, EPCs and developers.

**Solution.** A neutral registry plus audit program.
- One standard checklist mapped to NQA-1, ASME NCA-3800 and ISO 19443.
- Accredited auditors.
- A shared eAuditNet-style database.
- A "QA program in a box" for aerospace and oil-and-gas shops converting to nuclear.
- Optional dedication testing labs.

**Why now.** DOE-financed AP1000 long-lead buying and ~25 funded developers racing DOE and NRC clocks all need qualified suppliers at once.

**Wedge.** Sell the "QA program in a box" and shared assessments to 3-5 founding subscribers (one OEM, one EPC, two developers) who pay for the first 25 supplier assessments. Convert to accreditation when a utility QA organization accepts it as survey input.

**Path to $100M.** Nadcap's precedent: >1,350 accredited suppliers by 2007, >6,900 active accreditations and 53 subscribing OEMs by 2015-16, ~40% fewer audits (PRI-commissioned survey). ASSUMPTION (no published count found): 2,500-3,500 US and allied nuclear-qualified or aspiring suppliers x $20-30k/yr = $50-105M, plus ~60 primes/developers x $250k = $15M, plus QA-in-a-box services. **Stretch:** it needs near-universal adoption and non-US scope (Canada, UK, Poland).

**Who does it today.** NUPIC (utility joint audits), the ASME N-stamp and NQA-1 certificate programs, DOE's own consolidated audit program, third-party inspection firms (not individually verified), and each developer's in-house team.

**Why they lose.** NUPIC's members are utilities that cannot extend audit liability to competing vendors. Competing primes will not share audit findings with each other. ASME certifies capability but not ongoing process performance. No incumbent has a neutral position.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Network economies and switching costs once primes require the accreditation. Counter-positioning against each prime's in-house audit teams.

**Earned secret.** The fix is not technology but a neutral party that two competing primes both trust, with the supplier paying once. Boeing and Airbus both subscribed to Nadcap for exactly that reason.

**First 90 days.** Sign 3 founding subscribers. Recruit 6 ex-NUPIC lead auditors. Run 10 pilot assessments. Ask 1 utility QA organization to confirm acceptability as survey input. Legal gate: antitrust review of shared-audit information exchange (Nadcap and PRI precedent), and licensee acceptance under Appendix B Criterion VII.

**Outside-the-box score:** 6.

## 5. [dilemma] Day Rate: the performance-priced outage prime

**One-liner.** A challenger outage prime that earns a base fee plus a bonus or penalty per outage day against a P50 baseline, backed by its own critical-path dataset. The incumbent primes cannot copy it because they earn on emergent work.

**Insider.** Outage manager.

**Customer.** Site VPs and fleet outage directors, starting with single-site and mid-size operators whose integrated outage contracts come up for renewal.

**Payer and signer.** Outage O&M and capital budgets. Signer: Chief Nuclear Officer or Site VP. For merchant owners the CFO cares because a day of a 1.1 GW unit is ~$1.6-2.4M (arithmetic at $60-90/MWh).

**Problem.** Outage contracts are typically fixed-price for baseline scope and time-and-materials for the rest (BWXT 10-K language), so emergent work is the incumbents' profit center. Average outage length was ~38 days in an older NEI figure, while individual operators showed 19-26 day outages in 2008-2011 SEC filings. (Constellation reported 86 planned refueling outage days in Q2 2026 vs 41 a year earlier; that is a lumpy count, not a duration.) Trade figures put direct cost at $300-800k/day, plus ~$1M/day replacement power, and hyperscaler pricing pushes that higher.

**Solution.** A fixed-fee-plus-days-saved integrated outage contract.
- Outage control software (critical path, work-package readiness, mobilization, laydown).
- A dataset of the schedule actuals.
- Downside beyond a cap reinsured or capitalized.
- Pairs with idea 2's labor network.

**Why now.** The price of power doubled the value of a day while incumbents' contracts are long-term and fixed. Owners now have a reason to look at renewal.

**Wedge.** Offer a "critical-path logistics" sub-scope (containment access, laydown, mobilization) with gain-share on days saved for spring 2027, then expand to full prime at renewal.

**Path to $100M.**
- ~50 outages/yr x ~$25-30M each = $1.3-1.5B/yr of outage spend. Four outages a year as prime is $100-120M of mostly pass-through revenue at a 10-15% fee, ~8% of outages.
- The prize: fleet outage-days ~1,750/yr (50 x 35). Pulling the slow half from 40 to 28 days saves ~300 days x $1.6-2.4M = $480-720M/yr. A 20% performance fee is $95-145M.
- The two stack.

**Who does it today.** Westinghouse (10-year integrated outage services contract with Xcel), Framatome (integrated outage services for FENOC), GE Vernova Hitachi (>$70M Browns Ferry outage services), BWXT-type fixed-scope contractors, Day & Zimmermann (background knowledge, not verified), plus owners' own fleet outage organizations (Constellation).

**Why they lose.** Time-and-materials emergent work is margin. Fixed-price scope is already priced against risk. The OEMs are not balance-sheet risk takers on schedule, and none will pay penalties for days.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Counter-positioning plus cornered resource (a critical-path dataset incumbents will not build) plus scale.

**Earned secret.** Outage length is mostly decided before the outage starts (work-package readiness at T-12 weeks, labor mobilization, tooling, crane and laydown constraints), not during execution. (Hypothesis to validate in the first 10 interviews; I found no source for this.)

**First 90 days.** Build a duration model from NRC's daily Power Reactor Status reports (public, 30 years). Interview 10 outage managers. Pitch a "critical-path logistics" gain-share pilot to 3 operators for spring 2027. Legal gate: services-contract liquidated damages are not insurance. Avoid cash-for-lost-generation structures, which can be classified as insurance (round 1's NY DFS lesson).

**Outside-the-box score:** 6.

## 6. [dilemma] Second Source: HEICO for nuclear plants

**One-liner.** An OEM-neutral second source for the long tail of obsolete safety-related parts, with the reverse-engineering, test and dedication package built once and sold to every plant. The OEMs cannot do it because sole-source aftermarket is where their margin is.

**Insider.** Procurement and commercial-grade dedication engineer.

**Customer.** Procurement engineering and supply chain at ~55 sites.

**Payer and signer.** Materials and parts lines in O&M and capital. Signer: Director of Supply Chain with Site Engineering acceptance.

**Problem.** A Rolls-Royce study estimates 20-25% of nuclear plant components are no longer supported by the original equipment provider, and a plant relies on >100,000 parts. The same commercial item is dedicated separately by each plant that buys it. OEMs have no reason to fix that.

**Solution.**
- Reverse-engineer and test equivalents for obsolete I&C cards, relays, transmitters, valve and pump internals.
- Build one dedication package per part, accepted across the fleet through engineering equivalency.
- Hold pre-dedicated stock.
- Sell at 30-40% below the OEM.
- Keep a library of qualified alternates as the data asset.

**Why now.** Fleet life extensions to 80 years plus uprates increase parts demand. AI-assisted reverse engineering and additive manufacturing cut engineering cost per part.

**Wedge.** The 200 most common obsolete part numbers across the top 8 plant designs, starting with two operators' obsolescence lists. First 10 customers: operators with active obsolescence programs.

**Path to $100M.**
- ASSUMPTION: parts are ~25% of $15.1B (capital + non-labor operating), ~$3.8B/yr. OEM-unsupported 20-25% is $0.75-0.95B.
- A second source priced at ~65% of OEM needs $154M of OEM-equivalent volume for $100M of revenue, ~16-20% of the exposed pool. **Stretch.**
- Existing proof that $100M+ is possible in this space: Curtiss-Wright's commercial nuclear unit (reported ~$300M, unverified).

**Who does it today.** Park Nuclear (dedication, seismic and material testing, reverse engineering, new Oak Ridge facility), ATC Nuclear, Nuclear Logistics, Curtiss-Wright, Westinghouse and Framatome dedication services, Nuclearn "Parts AI", the Nuclear Utility Obsolescence Group, USA Inc. co-op, the PIM pool and the rapidpartsmart network.

**Why they lose.**
- OEMs will not release drawings or price at cost.
- Co-ops are non-profit and carry no inventory or liability.
- Mid-size dedicators are sub-scale on engineering.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Counter-positioning against OEM aftermarket plus cornered resource (qualified alternates library with accepted dedication packages).

**Earned secret.** The dedication package, not the part, is the product. Caution: 10 CFR 50.69 can move up to 75% of safety-related components (EPRI pilot) out of special treatment, shrinking the nuclear-grade premium where it is adopted. The surviving premium is Class 1/2 and pressure boundary.

**First 90 days.** Obtain 20 obsolete part numbers from two plants. Produce dedication packages. Have 3 CGD engineers confirm acceptability. Take counsel on OEM drawing, patent and trade-secret exposure. Legal gate: Appendix B, Part 21, engineering equivalency; reverse engineering faces IP and contract limits. The aviation PMA analog (HEICO) is background knowledge, not verified here.

**Outside-the-box score:** 4.

## 7. [shock] Uprate Kit: the fixed-price uprate integrator

**One-liner.** Become the integrator-of-record for hyperscaler-funded uprates. Productize standard uprate kits per plant type and sell megawatts delivered at a fixed price and date.

**Shock.** Hyperscaler PPAs (Google-Constellation 890 MW across 11 units; Meta-Vistra; Amazon-Constellation Calvert Cliffs ~190 MW) plus DOE financing created the first large uprate wave since 2012.

**Customer.** Owners with uprate pipelines in NRC's expected-applications table: Constellation, Duke (Brunswick, McGuire, Catawba), PSEG (Salem), Southern (Hatch), Vistra.

**Payer and signer.** Uprate capital project budgets (hyperscaler-backed). Signer: VP Nuclear Engineering/Projects and the CFO. DOE LPO is the lender where loans exist.

**Problem.** Uprate capex went from $66M (2022) to $365M (2025) and is heading to billions. NRC expects 30 uprates / ~2.3-2.4 GW by 2030-32 against 3-8 GW of potential. Constellation's $4.3B for 890 MW is ~$4.8k/kW, versus Browns Ferry's $475M for 14% over 3 units (2018-19) at roughly $1,000/kW. These are not like-for-like, but the spread suggests sole-source pricing, scope creep or both. Main turbines, generators, pumps and transformers carry long lead times.

**Solution.** Feasibility screen for each of 94 reactors, standard retrofit kits by plant type, LAR plan, competitive procurement of long-lead items, reserved equipment slots, and a fixed price per MW delivered with schedule guarantees. Optional revenue share on incremental MWh.

**Why now.** The first time in 14 years that uprate money exists. The first projects will fix the template.

**Wedge.** A paid feasibility and cost-review package for 3-5 owners using public data (172 prior uprates). Turns into engineering and procurement for the cheapest 2-3 units.

**Path to $100M.**
- Pool: 2.4 GW x $4.8k/kW is ~$11.5B through 2032, about $1.6B/yr. At 8 GW it is ~$38B, $4-5B/yr.
- **As engineering and licensing only (~11%):** $180M/yr in the base case, so $100M needs ~55% share. Not viable.
- **As fixed-price integrator-of-record (revenue gross of equipment):** $100M is 2-6% of capex flow. This is the only version that works.
- Capital need and warranty risk are high.
- **Stretch.** It also needs owners to give up OEM sole-source relationships.

**Who does it today.** OEM uprate packages (Westinghouse, GE Vernova Hitachi, Framatome), turbine OEMs (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi), engineers (Sargent & Lundy, Bechtel, Burns & McDonnell), and owner in-house programs (Constellation). No funded startup found.

**Why they lose.** OEMs bundle uprates with fuel and services at sole-source prices and will not standardize across competitors' designs. Engineers bill hourly. Owners' in-house teams are the bottleneck.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Process power (repeatable kits) plus scale economies (reserved equipment slots) plus switching costs (licensing basis).

**Earned secret.** Uprate cost sits in a handful of long-lead secondary-side retrofits and licensing analyses, not reactor physics. (Unverified: to be tested by the first three cost reviews.)

**First 90 days.** Build the public-data headroom screen. Contact the 5 owners on the NRC expected-uprate list. Sell 1-2 paid feasibility and cost reviews. Legal gate: NRC LAR process, OEM IP licenses, DOE domestic-content guidance (INL, March 2026).

**Outside-the-box score:** 5.

## 8. [shock] Clock Keeper: pay-on-approval licensing for the 12- and 18-month NRC clocks

**One-liner.** Executive Order 14300's clocks (18 months for new reactors, 12 months for renewals, enforced through fee-recovery limits) move the bottleneck to the applicant. Sell a fixed-price, pay-on-approval licensing submission and RAI-response service with a schedule guarantee.

**Shock.** EO 14300 (May 2025), NRC reorganization (implement by end of September 2026), a cluster of final rules September-November 2026, TVA Clinch River construction permit approved in 14 months (2026-09-29).

**Customer.** Reactor developers (~25 funded), restarts, and fleet owners filing uprate and license-renewal amendments.

**Payer and signer.** Regulatory affairs and licensing budgets plus project capital. Signer: VP Licensing or Chief Regulatory Officer.

**Problem.** With reviews capped, the NRC's defense against a missed clock is early requests for additional information (RAIs) and strict docketing. The applicant's critical path becomes completeness at docketing and RAI turnaround. Preparing applications takes tens of millions and months.

**Solution.** A licensing factory: structured regulatory "building blocks", a library of RAIs and accepted responses by topic from the NRC's public ADAMS record, former NRC reviewers for mock docketing, and a RAI-response SWAT team. Priced per submission and paid on approval within the clock.

**Why now.** The clocks are new, the application volume is rising, and AI-drafting has just been shown by the DOE/INL/ANL/Microsoft/Everstar test to compress drafting to a day.

**Wedge.** Free mock docketing reviews for 3 applicants due in 2027, converting one to pay-on-approval. First 10 customers: developers and restarts with 2027 filings.

**Path to $100M.**
- ASSUMPTION: ~10 new-reactor applications per year x $30M outsourced preparation = ~$300M/yr.
- Fleet licensing actions: ~6 uprate LARs/yr x $20M, ~5 renewals/yr x $25M, plus ~80 smaller actions/yr x $1.5M = ~$365M/yr.
- Total ~$0.65B/yr. $100M is ~15% of it. **Stretch.** The pool is concentrated in ~35 applicants per year.
- The NRC's own estimate of industry savings from licensing reform is only $15-22M/yr, so the market is applicant preparation spend, not NRC savings.

**Who does it today.** Licensing consultants (Enercon, Sargent & Lundy, Jensen Hughes, ERIN, Framatome, Westinghouse), law firms (Morgan Lewis), in-house licensing. AI tools: Everstar (Gordian, $4M pre-seed, DOE test), Nuclearn, Atomic Canyon.

**Why they lose.** Consultants and law firms bill hourly and will not take approval risk. Tool vendors sell software, not accountability. The reference-design owners (Westinghouse, GE Vernova Hitachi, TerraPower) keep their own adaptation kits.

**Moat (7 Powers).** Process power only. All RAIs are public, so there is no data moat. Weak. Best treated as an extension of idea 1.

**Earned secret.** The NRC clock is enforced through fee-recovery caps, so speed is set by completeness at docketing and the days an applicant takes to answer RAIs, and most RAIs repeat recurring topics (to be validated against ADAMS). It is a process-and-accountability business.

**First 90 days.** Ingest the ADAMS corpus of RAIs and responses for uprates, renewals and construction permits. Build a topic-level RAI predictor. Offer 3 free mock docketing reviews. Convert one to pay-on-approval. Legal gate: 10 CFR 50.9 completeness and accuracy, ABA-style fee-sharing limits if lawyers are involved, and the "practice of law" boundary.

**Outside-the-box score:** 4.

## 1. Approved-Claim Notes (insider: restoration/roofing owner, 20 years)

**One-liner.** Advance 80-90% of an invoice within 48 hours of the carrier approving it, underwritten on the carrier's obligation and repaid into a controlled account, not on the contractor's credit.

- **Customer.** Insurance-driven roofing and restoration contractors ($1-20M revenue; (A) 15-25k firms) and the PE platforms and franchise systems that roll them up.
- **Payer and signer.** Contractor pays 2.5-3.5% per advance (about 13-18% APR at 70 days) from its financing expense; signer is the owner or controller. Warehouse lines, later forward-flow credit funds, supply the capital.
- **Problem.** 60-90 day waits (S), checks held by homeowners and mortgagees, and general factors will not touch the paper because they cannot tell approved from pending or whether the check will be intercepted. The mortgagee's lien is older than the contractor's invoice (U).
- **Solution.** Ingest the approved estimate and carrier/TPA status, signed direction-to-pay, mortgagee status and license/permit match; advance within 48 hours; route repayment to a controlled account (ties to idea 5); price by carrier pay-speed data.
- **Why now.** Volume -20% in 2025 raised carrier scrutiny and delay; digital endorsement rails (Checkbook/sureti, iink) now exist; credit funds want short, carrier-credit paper (A).
- **Wedge.** Storm-heavy roofers (TX, CO, MN) and TPA-dependent water-mitigation shops; first product only on carrier-approved invoices with a direction-to-pay. Not recoverable depreciation, which ACV schedules are erasing.
- **Path to $100M.** At about 3% per advance, $100M needs about $3.3B a year of advances, 6% of the $57B contractor billings (A), about $630M outstanding at a 70-day turn. That is roughly 3-5k active contractors at $0.7-1M advanced a year, 15-25% of insurance-heavy firms (A). Honest caveat: this is a lender. After about 7.5% cost of funds and 0.5-1% losses, $100M of revenue is about $20-35M of pre-tax profit; the value is the pay-speed dataset.
- **Who does it today.** National Claims Funding, ClaimPay, iink's advance program, sureti/Checkbook, general factors (Triumph, Bluevine, Fundbox), distributor trade credit, contractors' own cash.
- **Why they lose.** They sit outside the carrier and servicer payment flow, cannot control the payment destination and have no cross-carrier pay data; sureti raised $3.25M in total.
- **Moat.** Scale economies (cost of capital) plus cornered resource (cross-carrier pay-speed and dispute data).
- **Earned secret.** What predicts payment is not the contractor's credit but four documents: approved estimate acknowledged by the carrier, signed direction-to-pay, a mortgagee path, and a license/permit match.
- **Legal premise.** Do not rely on assignment of benefits (restricted or banned in places such as Florida); use receivable purchase plus direction-to-pay; check state commercial-financing disclosure laws; deductible waivers are treated as fraud in TX (U).
- **First 90 days.** Collect 24 months of invoice and payment history from 10 contractors (free AR dashboard) to model days-to-pay by carrier; line up a $5M warehouse; fund $1-2M on approved estimates with direction-to-pay; add controlled-account repayment. Target 25 contractors, $10M advanced, loss under 1%.

## 2. Restoration RCM (insider: restoration billing manager / estimator)

**One-liner.** R1 for roofers and restorers: take over estimates, supplements, TPA invoice submission and collections, paid a percentage of what is actually collected.

- **Customer.** Insurance-heavy roofing and restoration firms, franchise groups and PE platforms with $3-300M of carrier-paid billings.
- **Payer and signer.** Contractor pays 3-5% of collected carrier/TPA dollars out of office staff, offshore estimating desks and collections cost; signer is the owner/GM or the platform CFO.
- **Problem.** Getting paid is the top concern (S). Each TPA has its own pricing programs, documentation and portal; carriers are buying AI to cut invoices (NovaData); 64% of restoration firms depend on TPAs (S). Money leaks through short-pays and supplements nobody follows up (U).
- **Solution.** Managed service plus software: build estimates and supplements to each carrier/TPA's rules, submit, chase, dispute, and book the outcome. Pricing is zero base fee and a share of collections.
- **Why now.** AI desk review on the carrier side makes defense necessary; LLMs make the labor cheap enough for percentage pricing; volume -20% pushes carriers to scrutinize every line.
- **Wedge.** PE-backed and franchise groups with 5-30 locations in TX, FL, CO; start on TPA-program water jobs.
- **Path to $100M.** At 3.5-4% of collections, $100M needs about $2.5-2.9B collected, 5% of the $57B (A): about 12 PE platforms at $100-250M each plus 700 independents at $2-3M. Low share, services-heavy margins at first.
- **Who does it today.** In-house office staff, offshore estimate/supplement desks, Rebuild (software, $13M), Xcelerate, Cotality DASH, JobNimbus/AccuLynx, Albiware. Healthcare RCM firms have not entered restoration (nothing found).
- **Why they lose.** Software sells seats and leaves the labor with the contractor; offshore desks bill hours and see one contractor's files; Rebuild focuses on estimate generation. Nobody owns the collected-dollar outcome across carriers.
- **Moat.** Cornered resource (cross-carrier short-pay and denial data) plus process power.
- **Earned secret.** The money is not in winning the job but in the invoice that survives desk review. Roughly 15-25% of a restoration firm's AR sits in "pending supplement" purgatory because nobody owns follow-up (U).
- **First 90 days.** Five design partners with $10-50M of carrier billings; take supplements, invoicing and AR follow-up at 4% of collected; log every short-pay reason; show a collected-rate lift of 5-8 points and 15 fewer days of DSO on matched jobs.

## 3. Storm Pro Co-op (pattern: free tool paid by suppliers, plus group purchasing)

**One-liner.** A free scope-and-supplement engine for insurance contractors, funded by manufacturers and distributors who want complete systems specified, with a buying group on the same members.

- **Customer.** 25-40k insurance-driven roofers and restorers (A), starting with PE-backed regional platforms.
- **Payer and signer.** Manufacturers (VP Residential Roofing / contractor marketing) and distributors (SVP Pro sales) pay sponsorship and 1.5-2% admin fees on member purchases; budget lines are loyalty rebates, co-op marketing and technical services.
- **Problem.** Insurer scopes omit code-required and warranty-required components; contractors lack time and citations; manufacturers lose system sales; the top three roofers hold under 5% of the market (S), so no contractor has buying power.
- **Solution.** A free estimate and supplement engine citing code, manufacturer installation requirements and carrier guidelines, exporting to Xactimate. Sponsors flagged and included only where code or warranty requires them. A buying group routes member volume to chosen distributors.
- **Why now.** Claims -20% and ACV schedules (about -30% severity) shrink insured volume, so suppliers fight for attach rates; QXO bought Beacon and Home Depot bought SRS, so national programs have central buyers.
- **Wedge.** One manufacturer sponsor, one product family, one region; 300 contractors via three PE-backed platforms.
- **Path to $100M.** About $5B of member purchases at 1.7% = $85M plus $15-20M sponsorship. $5B is about 25% of the roughly $20B of insured-job materials (A), heroic; co-ops of that size exist in hardware (U).
- **Who does it today.** Rebuild, Xcelerate, JobNimbus/AccuLynx, EagleView/Hover measurements, GAF Rewards and Owens Corning programs, SRS One and Beacon Pro+, regional buying groups.
- **Why they lose.** Software vendors charge contractors, which caps adoption; manufacturer loyalty programs reward volume, not claim-readiness; distributors cannot be neutral between brands.
- **Moat.** Network economies (member volume buys better terms, which attracts members) plus scale economies.
- **Earned secret.** System warranties require matching components, so a sponsor is not buying bias; it is paying for complete code and warranty citations that adjusters pay when asked. Roughly 25-40% of supplement dollars are such items (U).
- **Legal premise.** Sponsor disclosure, steering and fraud-flag risk with carriers; no healthcare-style GPO safe harbor exists for construction (U).
- **First 90 days.** Build the code-and-manufacturer library for shingle systems in one region; sign one founding sponsor with a written disclosure policy; run 500 jobs and measure supplement lift.

## 4. Pay-in-Five Managed Repair (pattern: outcome-priced replacement of a measurable labor function)

**One-liner.** Managed repair paid for the days of displacement it removes, with contractors paid within five days of a verified milestone.

- **Customer.** Regional and mid-size homeowners carriers and residual-market plans with displaced households (fire, major water, smoke).
- **Payer and signer.** VP Property Claims / Chief Claims Officer; program fee from LAE plus a share of ALE (Coverage D) savings.
- **Problem.** ALE runs by the day (policies cap it around 20-30% of dwelling, (S)); AB 1795 now bars ending ALE before remediation; DRP networks are paid per assignment or by contractor referral fees, not for days saved; contractors wait 60-90 days and slow the job.
- **Solution.** Dispatch vetted contractors, pre-agree scope and price menus, run the project clock (permit, materials, trades), pay contractors in five days from escrow and balance sheet, and price against ALE-days saved versus a matched cohort.
- **Why now.** AB 1795; volume -20% pushes carriers to outcome-priced vendors; Crawford's network shows about $3B a year of managed-repair demand (company); Alacrity's unit changed hands (Jan 2026).
- **Wedge.** Water and smoke losses (short scope, fast cycle) in TX and CA with two regional carriers and 40 contractors.
- **Path to $100M.** About $3.3B of managed volume at a 3% fee = $100M, 4% of the pool and roughly Crawford's present scale. Gain-share on top: ALE is (A, unverified) 6-9% of indemnity, $5-7B; 20% of days saved at a 25% share is $250-350M of pool.
- **Who does it today.** Crawford Contractor Connection, Altimeter Solutions Group, HOMEE, Sedgwick, ServPro/Belfor franchise programs, carriers' own DRPs.
- **Why they lose.** Referral and assignment fees pay them for volume, not speed, and they cannot fund fast pay; carriers cannot run contractor cash; contractors cannot carry 60-90 days. Counter-positioning: we give up referral fees and charge for days saved.
- **Moat.** Counter-positioning plus switching costs (carrier integration).
- **Earned secret (carrier claims-ops).** Nobody owns the repair clock. ALE is paid on receipts by adjusters while the days between loss and first trade on site, and between completion and final payment, belong to no one.
- **Legal premise.** State steering and option-to-repair rules; prime-contractor licensing if we contract the work.
- **First 90 days.** A 100-claim pilot with a matched control at one carrier; pre-load 30 contractors with five-day terms; measure ALE-days to first trade on site and to final payment.

## 5. Claims Escrow Co. (dilemma: the neutral custodian everyone needs)

**One-liner.** The title company for insurance repair money.

- **Customer.** Mortgage servicers and sub-servicers (loss-draft and compliance) and homeowners carriers; contractors and homeowners are the beneficiaries.
- **Payer and signer.** Servicers: SVP Servicing / head of loss draft plus the chief compliance officer; carriers: VP Claims Ops. Budget lines are servicing operations, compliance and (carriers) ALE; fees are a draw fee, a share of float and an indemnity premium.
- **Problem.** About 61% of owner-occupied homes carry a mortgage (S); carriers fear double indemnity if they bypass the mortgagee; servicers fear misapplied funds, now owe at least 2% in California (S) and face a 10-business-day benchmark and a data order due 29 Oct 2026 (S); manual first draws meet SLA only 22% of the time (U).
- **Solution.** A licensed escrow/trust company with bonded, insured custody. Proceeds go into a claim escrow; servicers set release rules; funds go out against milestones to contractors, suppliers (materials first, with lien waivers) and homeowners. A "claim protection letter" indemnifies servicer and carrier, as closing protection letters do in title, backed by a surety or insurer.
- **Why now.** AB 493, the July 2026 letters, the 15 Oct order, AB 1842 and bills in MA/HI turned custody from free float into a regulated liability; the GSE guide already gives servicers latitude.
- **Wedge.** California large-loss and total-loss claims at one top-25 servicer or sub-servicer plus the FAIR Plan or a CA carrier, 100 files first.
- **Path to $100M.** (A) Loss-draft-sized proceeds about $30B of the $50B mortgage-linked. A 0.4% draw fee is $120M and net float ($30B x 75/365 x 2%) is $123M, so $243M at 100% share. Adding contractor-payment protection on the non-mortgaged $32B at 0.4% = $128M brings the pool to about $370M. $100M is 27-40% of the pool, **heroic: utility or nothing**. Title and CCC (auto, about $1B revenue, (U)) show claims and closing networks concentrate.
- **Who does it today.** iink ($2.5B+ processed, (U)), sureti/Checkbook ($3.25M raised), One Inc ClaimsPay (carrier-side), lender construction-draw software (e.g. Built, (U)), servicers in-house. No title or escrow company handles claim proceeds (nothing found).
- **Why they lose.** Each is one-sided: iink is a servicer deposit tool, sureti bypasses lenders without their consent, One Inc pays but does not hold against milestones; none issues a protection letter.
- **Moat.** Network economies and switching costs, with a licensing barrier.
- **Earned secret (servicer loss-draft).** Ops managers will never speed up on their own, since every fast release is personal liability, but they will outsource to a party that signs an indemnity. And float stopped being free.
- **Legal premise.** State escrow/trust licensing and money-transmitter exemptions (agent of payee); mortgage clause law; RESPA; CA AB 493.
- **First 90 days.** Pick the charter route (CA escrow license plus partner bank and surety); sign one sub-servicer for a 100-file CA pilot with its release rules coded; produce an AB 493-style interest and days-to-release report.

## 6. Mid-Band Advocate (dilemma: the walls between professions)

**One-liner.** A licensed, software-run public adjuster for the $5-25k claims no PA takes.

- **Customer.** Homeowners with $5-25k wind, hail and water claims, (A) about 55% of claims, 1.9-2.4M a year.
- **Payer and signer.** The homeowner pays an 8-10% contingent fee on the settlement, within state caps, out of proceeds; no outside budget is needed.
- **Problem.** PA economics fail below roughly $25k (U) on acquisition cost and adjuster hours; contractors may not negotiate coverage (illegal in places such as TX, FL, (U)); lawyers will not take small cases; carriers are the adverse party. The first offer becomes the settlement, and ACV schedules and scrutiny widen the gap.
- **Solution.** Licensed in all 50 states (acquire small PA firms for licenses). Software reads the policy, estimate and photos, finds formulaic entitlements (overhead and profit, matching, code upgrade, labor depreciation, ALE, contents), drafts the presentation and a licensed adjuster signs.
- **Why now.** ACV schedules cut roof payouts; carriers are deploying AI to estimate and trim; LLMs can read policies cheaply.
- **Wedge.** TX and FL hail and wind roofs, a free scope tool (idea 3) for contractors, no referral fees.
- **Path to $100M.** 100k signed claims at about $1,200 = $120M, roughly 4-5% of the mid-band and 2.5-3% of all claims (A). The binding constraint is customer acquisition cost, not market size.
- **Who does it today.** Thousands of PA firms (fees 5-20%), consumer claim law firms, claim-fee advances (National Claims Funding), United Policyholders. No funded policyholder-side AI PA turned up in one search, which is not conclusive.
- **Why they lose.** Hourly or relationship-based PAs cannot hit one hour per claim; contractors cannot negotiate; carriers cannot advocate.
- **Moat.** Process power, cornered resource (claims outcome data) and scale in licensing.
- **Earned secret (public adjuster).** The missing dollars in mid-band claims are formulaic, $1-4k each (U). The economics break on adjuster time, not fee: six hours fails, one hour works.
- **Legal premise.** Per-state PA licensing, fee caps (TX 10%, FL 10/20%), solicitation limits, no fee-splitting with contractors, and no legal advice (UPL).
- **First 90 days.** License in TX, FL, CO and CA; build the entitlement checker on 500 historic mid-band files; run 100 claims at an 8% fee cap and measure uplift over the first offer.

## 7. Fire and Smoke Program (shock: AB 1642 / AB 1795)

**One-liner.** The clearance authority and program manager for smoke, soot, lead and asbestos claims.

- **Customer.** Homeowners carriers and the CA FAIR Plan, later all fire claims.
- **Payer and signer.** Head of property claims at an LA-exposed carrier or the FAIR Plan; budget is Coverage A/C indemnity (testing, remediation) and ALE, paid as a program fee per file.
- **Problem.** About 13,000 LA smoke claims (S). AB 1642 and AB 1795 require protocol testing and remediation, full cleanup, and no ALE cut-off until clearance. Insurers are said to stall contamination claims a year on (S); LA County sued State Farm.
- **Solution.** One program: intake, protocol sampling by accredited assessors (a network of industrial hygienists, lead and asbestos consultants), lab results, remediation scope at pre-agreed prices through vetted contractors with five-day pay (idea 4), independent clearance test, and a clearance certificate that ends ALE and releases funds.
- **Why now.** Signed 15 Sep 2026; the effective date was not confirmed (U); ALE meters are running.
- **Wedge.** 200 LA smoke files from the FAIR Plan or a regional carrier.
- **Path to $100M.** LA alone: 13,000 files at about $6.5k (A: $4k testing and clearance plus 10% of $25k remediation) = $85M over about two years, maybe half capturable. National: fire is 21.6% of $82B = $17.7B (A, if the ISO share is by dollars); smoke, soot and contamination on non-total fires about 30-35% = $5-6B; an 8-9% fee is $450-540M, so $100M is about 20% and **depends on other wildfire states copying the standard (U)**.
- **Who does it today.** Restoration contractors that clear their own work (Belfor, ServPro), industrial-hygiene firms and labs, TPAs, DTSC guidance.
- **Why they lose.** Contractors cannot certify their own work, labs have no program or payment control, and carriers and TPAs gain by delay. Nobody owns the end-to-end clearance outcome.
- **Moat.** Cornered resource (accredited experts and lab throughput) plus standard-setting.
- **Earned secret.** Under AB 1795 the clearance test is what stops the ALE meter. The test result is worth thousands of dollars a month to the carrier, and each side distrusts the other's lab.
- **Legal premise.** Lead, asbestos and industrial-hygiene credentialing; conflict-of-interest rules between tester and remediator.
- **First 90 days.** Recruit 10 assessors and two labs in LA; write the clearance protocol to AB 1642 with a toxicologist; clear 100 homes and report ALE-days saved.

## 8. Certified Complete (shock: California's servicer regime plus GSE latitude)

**One-liner.** A neutral completion grade that releases the next loss-draft installment and ends the holdback and ALE clock.

- **Customer.** Servicer and sub-servicer loss-draft teams and carrier property claims; later underwriters.
- **Payer and signer.** Servicer EVP Servicing Ops / head of loss draft (inspection vendor budget); carrier VP Property Claims (reinspection, holdback release).
- **Problem.** Remaining funds follow periodic inspections (S); manual first draws meet SLA 22% (U); every party to "done" is conflicted: contractor wants paid, carrier wants to stop ALE and holdback, homeowner wants the cash, servicer wants no liability.
- **Solution.** A third-party grade built from device-attested milestone capture (tear-off, dry-in, rough-in, final), permit finals, lien waivers and a license check, with risk-based sampling by licensed inspectors and a bonded guarantee behind the grade. The servicer codes its policy ("release on grade X"); carriers accept it for holdback and ALE.
- **Why now.** The 10-business-day benchmark and 29 Oct reporting order; AB 493 interest; AB 1795 makes clearance legally meaningful; AI-doctored images push toward attested capture (U).
- **Wedge.** A 60-day shadow pilot on 200 open California loss drafts at one sub-servicer.
- **Path to $100M.** (A) Servicer files about 1.2M (4M claims x 60.7% x about half above the loss-draft threshold) x 2.2 inspections x $90 = $240M; carrier holdback and final reinspection 0.8M x $75 = $60M; clearance and post-remediation testing 600k x $350 = $210M (overlaps idea 7); repair-history lookups 20M x $1 = $20M. Pool about $530M, so $100M is about 19%.
- **Who does it today.** Servicers' in-house teams and field-inspection vendors, iink (digital loss draft), capture apps (Encircle, CompanyCam, Hover), IH labs, carrier reinspection staff.
- **Why they lose.** Inspectors are paid per visit; capture apps are tools, not accepted standards; contractors cannot self-certify; carriers are conflicted. A grader both sides accept is a chokepoint, as PSA is in cards.
- **Moat.** Network economies and standard, plus cornered resource (verification outcomes become a repair-history asset).
- **Earned secret (servicer loss-draft).** Servicers inspect not because rules require it (Fannie's current text lets them release a large first draw) but because no one will vouch for the contractor. The fear is liens and unfinished work, not workmanship, so a lien waiver, permit final and license check plus a party that stands behind the grade removes most visits.
- **Legal premise.** State inspector licensing, GSE acceptance, liability and privacy.
- **First 90 days.** Shadow-grade 200 loss drafts and compare to what the servicer released and inspected; if the grade predicts every decision, convert to a per-draw fee; get one top-10 carrier to accept the certificate for holdback or ALE release on 100 claims.

## Notes for breeding

Ideas 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8 are layers of one contractor and servicer graph (capital, back office, tools, custody, verification). Any of them alone is contested; the combination (escrow plus verification plus pay-speed data) is what could be defensible.

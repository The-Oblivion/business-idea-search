# C02: Qualified-challenger fiscal agent: buy the ticket in choice markets, bid the statewide auctions later

**One-liner:** Buy two or three owner-operated Medicaid fiscal agents in enrollment and rate-card markets to acquire the scarce asset (multi-state qualification), run them on rented software with a measured exception-rate program, then bid mid-size statewide RFPs and plan-delegated contracts.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $25M/yr; upside $103M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C02_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C02_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (serious): The entry ticket is held by funded incumbents, and capital is flooding in. New York's RFP required a statewide FI contract elsewhere and a $100M line of credit (dossier, Holland & Knight). Every award the dossier found went to a multi-state incumbent. The 'buy a small FI for past performance' route then competes with H.I.G. (GT Independence, 50k+ participants, Nov 2025) and General Atlantic, which closed a roughly $3B purchase of TEAM Services from Alpine in April 2026 at about 10x EBITDA. TEAM sells financial management services for consumer-directed care. I could not confirm that Acumen sits inside TEAM; the dossier's link rests on an unopened CB Insights page. Acumen itself reports 22-23 states and 100+ programs. A first-time consolidator buys against 10x-multiple sponsors. The only cheap targets are the money-losing ones, since the four Wisconsin FEAs lost 12.5% in aggregate. A new entrant would end up buying either an overpriced or a sick asset.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (serious): The 'payroll-certainty SLA' is not the vendor's to deliver, and no buyer pays a premium for it. Connecticut is the live test: GT Independence (the 'best operator' in this dossier) still had hundreds of aides with missing, delayed or partial pay in Nov 2025 to Feb 2026. GT says it 'can only execute payments when the state green-lights the funds'. The state (DSS) blames its own incomplete or miscalculated authorization files, plus employers exceeding monthly hours (10% of self-directing employers). The union won a partial settlement of just under $1M in backpay, and the state, not the vendor, was left holding the political problem. If the FI guarantees payroll anyway, it becomes an unsecured lender against hours the state later refuses to reimburse. Arithmetic (A): if just 1% of payroll fronted is unreimbursable, that is about $30 per participant-month at New York's ~$36k per participant-year, or 37% of an $80 fee. In Wisconsin (~$51.7k per participant-year) it is about $43, or 56% of the $76.50 fee. The buyer side also already treats payroll timeliness as a penalty clause, not a priced feature (Wisconsin's contract allows withholding fees for missed payroll; Connecticut has 'enhanced contract monitoring' and a 2026 late-pay penalty bill). In Kentucky, the one live RFP, the state's own FAQ says financial management agencies may bill claims before paying PDS employees, so there is no payroll float for a backstop to cover. Pitch failure mode: the SLA is either un-backable (state-caused misses) or un-monetizable (table stakes).
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (serious): TIMING: the why-now is mostly spent and the one open door is not enterable. As of 2026-10-18: New York is locked to PPL (sole FI since 2025-04-01, award upheld on appeal Oct 2025, no rebid found). West Virginia was awarded in 2024. Wisconsin gave PPL a notice of intent on 2026-01-29 with a protest review still unresolved on a DHS page last revised 2026-10-08, and transfers not before 2027. Ohio DODD closed 2026-01-14 and Alabama FMS RFP 2026-03 closed in June 2025 with multi-award and no award found. That leaves Kentucky (due 2026-11-06, 19 days from now): the listing is a re-procurement of a 2018 RFP, carries stale 2018 text, names no incumbent, participant count or value, and Kentucky is mid-transition of PDS oversight to waiver operating agencies by 2027-01-01. A not-yet-formed company cannot assemble past performance, an EVV integration, a bid lead and a funded payroll line in 19 days. The realistic cycle is RFP to first revenue of about 2 years (Wisconsin: RFP S-1640, NOI Jan 2026, protest open 8+ months, go-live 2027), all pre-revenue burn. Entry requires past performance, which means buying an FI, which means bidding against H.I.G. (GT Independence, Nov 2025), DW Healthcare/Linden (PPL, $425M refinanced in 2025) and General Atlantic (bought TEAM Services, a home-care and payroll platform that includes FMS, for about $3B including debt at about 10x EBITDA, April 2026).

## Second-round critic: wounded
- **Is the upside believable?** No. The $103M case needs at least five things to hold together and several are weakly evidenced or contradicted.
(1) $150 blended PMPM on 28k choice participants. The rate-card evidence is $103 (CO), $114-121 (TX; proposed $120.55 in 2024), and $106 (LA). The only prices near $150 are Minnesota's, where the fee is participant-paid out of the care budget and tiers down to $50-155 at the smaller nonprofit providers. A realistic $110-120 on that segment gives about $37-40M, not $50M, and the total falls to about $90M with NY and about $80M without it.
(2) 79k participants in 7 years. That is 1.6x the footprint GT built over about 21 years with H.I.G. capital, delivered here by a handful of acquisitions of 2-6k-participant operators plus unspecified organic growth in markets where PPL, Palco, GT and Acumen already operate.
(3) Winning two mid-size statewide RFPs as a first-time winner while the plan itself says about one competitive award occurs per year nationally.
(4) A New York slot opening through unconfirmed litigation or legislation.
(5) $60-75M of capital available for a business whose median case yields about 5-8% on invested capital.
The probabilities of 8-10% for the upside, about 45% for the median and 40-45% for the bear are reasonably candid, but the upside is a joint event of several low-probability conditions, so 8-10% is probably generous. And even if the upside is hit, the return is roughly 1.4-2.1x over about 7 years. The upside is not believable and, if achieved, not attractive on a capital basis. The idea is worth only the two-week test and a harder look at Minnesota, Texas and Colorado specifics before anything else.
- **Remaining holes:** The premium-price evidence is cherry-picked and partly mis-specified. In Minnesota's CFSS budget model the FMS fee is negotiated with each participant and paid out of the participant's own service budget, not by the state or a plan (MN DHS provider pages, search summary). It is tiered by budget size, not flat. Listed fees run $50-150 at MRCI, $110-150 at Lifeworks, $135-155 at PICS, $50 then $165 at GT, $165 at Accra and $200-400 at Aspen. The '$165-250' figure is the top of the range, and the small nonprofit operators the plan wants to buy charge less. A $150 blended fee on 28k participants has no support, and the 'payer and signer' section is wrong for the single biggest line in the upside. | Colorado is mislabeled as an enrollment market. HCPF ran a 2023-24 RFP and awarded FMS contracts for five years starting 2024-07-01 (HCPF IM 24-003, search summary; PDF returned 403, so awardee names are unconfirmed). Participants choose only among the awarded vendors, which in 2025 were Palco and PPL at $103.21 each. A new entrant can reach Colorado only by buying a contract holder, which is the closed-auction problem the rewrite claims to avoid. This is the same objection critique 2 raised about rate-card states, and it is not answered. | In Texas, where open enrollment is real (HHSC takes FMSA applications year-round and contracts noncompetitively), entry is free. The qualification therefore needs no purchase. What a buyer pays 1x revenue for is a book of participants who can switch FMSA at will, plus the seller's referral relationships. The rewrite's own selling point, participant choice, is what makes the acquired asset non-sticky and key-person dependent. A fixed rate with free entry also means every funded entrant competes for the same margin, and Texas FMSA counts and profitability are not sized. | Rate cards are revocable and tied to budgets. Colorado applied a 1.6% across-the-board cut in Oct 2025 (the FMS PMPM was untouched that time). Texas sets its rate by biennial review. Critique 2 listed state cuts to self-direction in MD, MO, ID, IN and CO. The rewrite files policy risk as 'not fixable' and then concentrates the thesis in budget-authority, family-caregiver-heavy programs, which are the segment most exposed to the federal and state fraud and cut push. | The acquisition math contradicts itself. The plan buys at about 1.0x revenue and calls that 6x EBITDA, which implies sellers earn about 17% EBITDA. The median case then earns 5-8% EBITDA on about $25M revenue, or $1.3-2M. On roughly $24-26M invested (about $20M of acquired revenue at 1.0x, plus about $4M of reserves, plus bid costs), that is 12-20x EBITDA actually paid. An exit at 8-10x would return $10-20M on $25M, a capital loss. The 'median' case is effectively a bear case, and the real success probability is the 8-10% upside only. This is my arithmetic from the rewrite's own figures.

## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** Small, non-sponsor-owned fiscal agents exist in choice and rate-card markets that earn at least 15-20% gross margin at $110+ per participant-month and will sell at a price their cash flow can carry (about 1.0x revenue, about 6x EBITDA, both assumptions). The qualification ticket, the ladder to statewide RFPs and the whole upside depend on buying that ticket cheaply. No FMS-only deal price has been found by me or by any critic.
- **2-week test:** Seller-side price and cost discovery, no capital. Days 1-2: list 25 owner-operated or nonprofit FMS operators from the Minnesota DHS FMS provider pages, Texas HHSC FMSA choice lists and MCO lists, and Colorado, Michigan and Virginia lists, excluding GT, Acumen/TEAM, PPL and Consumer Direct Care Network; pull Form 990s for the nonprofits. Days 2-12: contact all 25, aiming for 8-10 conversations plus 2 healthcare-services M&A advisers; ask six fixed questions (participants and states, revenue PMPM, cost PMPM, EBITDA margin, any payroll-tax or wage-hour claims, willingness to sell and the multiple they have been told). Days 12-14: apply thresholds written in advance. Pass: at least 3 operators show cost at or under $90 at $110+ revenue and at least 2 would consider a sale at or under 1.0x revenue. Kill (alive becomes false): median ask above 1.5x revenue, or reported margins under 5% at $110+, or fewer than 5 of 25 will share numbers. In between: shrink the premium assumption and rerun the cases. Second gate at month 6: a signed LOI at or under 1.0x revenue plus a committed equity partner, or stop.

# C16: Regional Partner Group: rollover-first anchor-and-fill consolidator of independent uniform and linen laundries

**One-liner:** Buy one anchor family laundry per region with the owner family rolling equity and running the region, fill the cluster with tuck-ins at non-synergy prices, and fund it deal by deal until closed deals unlock committed capital.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $45M/yr; upside $130M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C16_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C16_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (serious): The sharpened pitch (operator-led, family-to-family alternative to the nationals) is already being run by better-credentialed operators, and the dossier missed the closest one. Wildman Business Group (family-owned, Warsaw IN) has nine locations, 13,500+ customers and 500 staff. It announced Springfield Laundry on 2025-10-13 with succession as the stated reason. It keeps acquired names, branding and sales teams, and says it wants to be the 'alternative succession plan to large nationals' for independents. Alsco closed Churchill (Sep 2024), MacIntosh (Feb 2025), Topper (Jun 2025) and Advanced Linen (Mar 2026). The Advanced Linen co-owner said he and his partners 'carefully chose' Alsco as the company they trust with their customers. Behind them sit at least ten funded buyers (Surge, Falcon, Quintex, PureStar/Cornell, Sterling/HLSG, CVC/Unitex, Calera/ImageFIRST). KPMG told TRSA in Feb 2026 that strategic buyers dominate most deals to add route density. A new vehicle with three hired executives, no closed deals and no reference customers is the least credible bidder a family owner will see. Strategics fold stops into existing routes, so they outbid it on the best targets. Cintas paid $164.5M cash for FY26 tuck-ins that added about 0.6% (~$62M) to revenue growth, roughly 1.3x-2.7x revenue by my arithmetic given part-year effects. UniFirst paid 2.9-3.3x for Clean Uniform. The platform therefore inherits adverse selection: targets with no strategic fit, deferred capex or key-person risk. 'Density' also cannot be bought. A metro has one to three independents, and the first deal in each metro has no density while the incumbent there values it at a synergy price.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (serious): The seller-preference edge is asserted, not evidenced, and the evidence points the other way. The sharpened pick says it wins on 'seller preference and post-deal retention', but sellers name a different criterion: trust to look after their customers. In the one 2026 deal I could open, Advanced Linen's co-owner said they 'carefully chose Alsco' as the company they trust to take care of their customers. Alsco is a fifth-generation family firm that has done four deals in 18 months. A newly formed vehicle has no track record, and its three ex-regional presidents come from the competitors the sellers fight every day. CT Acquisitions (a buy-side broker) says a competitive process with more than one strategic 'almost always lifts the price', and the strategic prints are far above the platform's 0.8-1.2x revenue: UniFirst/Clean Uniform at 2.9-3.3x, Cintas/UniFirst at 2.26x. A seller who sells to the lowest bidder because of a rep equity pool is the opposite of how the evidence says owners behave. The cheap test (20 owner calls) has not been run, so the pitch is a hypothesis.
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (serious): The remedy option is dead on mechanism and timing, so the sharpened pick is just a generic roll-up. Cintas proposed in Dec 2025 that it not be required to engage in antitrust remedies, and the merger agreement obliges both sides to defend any challenge in court. The outside date is Jan 10, 2027, extendable to Sept 10, 2027. That makes clearance or a litigated fight more likely than a negotiated divestiture package. As of the latest filings and trade coverage (Oct 2026) no divestiture or consent is mentioned anywhere; the only reporting is one anonymous Dealreporter source saying buyers will be hard to find. Earliest closing is Dec 11, so the platform has about 53 days to get committed equity, a lender, officers and FTC-credible financing in place. It has none of them today. The obvious buyers of any package are established operators that already close tuck-ins: Alsco (four deals in about 18 months), Prudential, and PE platforms such as ImageFIRST, PureStar, Sterling's HLSG and Unitex/CVC. Cintas picks the buyer and staff scrutinize the buyer's financing and track record (dossier sourcing). A new vehicle with three ex-executives is the profile staff discount. The dossier's own odds put this platform being named a buyer at about 3%.

## Second-round critic: wounded
- **Is the upside believable?** Only as a low-probability tail, and the 10% the rewrite assigns is generous. The arithmetic is internally consistent: about 12 deals, about $113M bought, about $12M organic, about 3.5x and 21% IRR. Most of that return is multiple expansion from roughly 6x to 9-10x, plus 3-4 margin points of synergy. The size premium is real. GF Data 2025 sponsor deals run about 6.3x at $10-25M EV, 7.4x at $25-50M and 8.8x at $50-100M. But 9-10x at about $200M EV is at or above the top of that curve, and above the roughly 8x run-rate CT Acquisitions cites for Cintas/UniFirst. At 8.8x my rough recompute gives about 3.1x and 19%.\n\nOther weak points:\n- Pace: two closings a year from a first-time team that needs a new equity raise for each deal, versus ImageFIRST's roughly 3 a year with Calera behind it.\n- Coverage: it needs essentially every independent in two 150-mile regions to sell to this buyer.\n- Arithmetic: 'two regions' implies two $15-25M anchors, but the build counts one anchor plus 11 tuck-ins at $8M.\n- A plug: the 'extra 5-10% from a second platform or released-merger accounts' (about $6-13M) rests on the same customer-flight effect the why-now section says is not evidenced.\n- Churn of 7% or less is unsourced.\n- The named exit buyers are thin: Wildman looks too small, Alsco and Wildman are the same rivals who outbid the platform for targets, and ImageFIRST's 2023 exit was a continuation fund.\n\nThe tail is also not weighted against the sector's failure record (Kleen, Angelica, Patriot Linen, Lighthouse Linen). The distribution as stated, about 8% at the median and about 10% for the upside, is a sub-hurdle bet. Without evidence that sellers pick this buyer, I would rate the idea wounded. It dies if the two-week owner test, the adviser comps or the 60-day IOI gate fail.
- **Remaining holes:** The new edge is the PE-platform default. Falcon/Century Linen (owner stays as significant minority holder and CEO), A&M Capital/Atlantic City Linen (Goldberg family rolls its whole stake and stays co-CEOs) and Calera's ImageFIRST/Faultless (family keeps equity) already offer rollover plus a family role. The pitch's own line that it found no public rollover terms for Wildman or Alsco skips the PE buyers where rollover is routine. The offer is also worse on price (5.5-6.5x vs a roughly 8x strategic print), cash at close (65-80%) and certainty (deal-by-deal equity, no track record). Any seller who accepts has a reason other than the offer, which is adverse selection. | Entry price is not a discount. GF Data 2025 sponsor deals (read secondhand through a Vistage republication) show $10-25M EV at about 5.9-6.7x, $25-50M at 7.4x and $50-100M at 8.8x. The anchor targets ($14-26M EV) sit exactly in the first band. 'Non-synergy price' is just the going LMM sponsor price that PE platforms with rollover can also pay. | The team does not exist and is not funded. 'Three ex-executives' became an unnamed 'operator co-founder' who runs all 25 owner calls. Independent-sponsor economics (Citrin Cooperman 2025 via vendor summaries: closing fee about 2% of EV with 40-80% typically rolled; management fee about 5% of EBITDA, roughly $190K on a $3.75M-EBITDA anchor) give near-zero cash for a year or more. Platform overhead is never quantified in the median case, although critic 2 flagged about $3.5M (12% of revenue at $30M). A $1.5-2M overhead on the anchor turns 6x into roughly 10x on post-overhead EBITDA until scale arrives. | The deal-flow math uses the unfiltered pool. '8% of 20-25 deals a year' (an unsourced estimate) ignores the wedge's own filters: $15-25M revenue, family-owned, owner 58+, outside Wildman's five states, outside FTC-flagged metros, no natural strategic fold-in, and 3-6 independents within 150 miles. After those filters the annual flow is probably a handful of deals, and two closings a year could be a large share of it. The upside case (anchor plus 11 tuck-ins across two regions) means buying essentially every independent within 150 miles, each of whom has Alsco, Wildman or PE alternatives. A 150-mile radius is also not route density. | Internal contradiction in the structure. The family keeps the real estate on a 10-15 year leaseback, a fixed obligation lenders treat as debt-like. The margin lever is later plant consolidation (hub-and-spoke), which would strand leases the platform signed. The family regional-president role ends at year 3, just as consolidation starts. The 24-month retention earn-out also conflicts with the plan to change plants and routes. The 'operating bench' that rivals cannot rent is therefore a 3-year, post-close asset. It does nothing to win the first deal.

## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** A repeatable flow of $8-25M family-owned laundries will sell to a new, financed buyer at or below about 6.5x normalized EBITDA (about 0.9-1.0x revenue) with 20-35% rollover, instead of to Alsco, Wildman or a PE platform at strategic prices. Nobody found a closed 2025-26 multiple for a sub-$15M independent, and my model shows 7.5x entry cuts IRR to 12.5% (median case about 4%).
- **2-week test:** Days 1-3: list 60 family-owned independents with $8-30M revenue from the TRSA directory and state associations. Days 3-12: hold 25 owner conversations through the operator co-founder and TRSA peers, giving first a free confidential options report (worth to a cash strategic, a PE platform, a rollover partner) in exchange for three years of pounds, revenue and EBITDA; ask who has approached them, at what multiple, and whether they would take cash versus rollover plus earn-out. Days 5-14: three calls with M&A advisers (IMG, EdgePoint, Stephens) on closed 2025-26 sub-$15M comps, and two lenders on 3.5-4.0x for a $3M-EBITDA anchor. Pass: at least 5 of 25 engage or share financials (baselines: cold outreach 1-5%, Baird 21 of about 500); at least 3 owners name or accept a price at or below about 7x EBITDA or 1.2x revenue; advisers cite at least 2 closed sub-$15M deals at or below 1.2x revenue; at least 1 lender signals 3.5x or more. Then run the 60-day gate of 3 signed IOIs. Kill (alive=false, do not narrow): fewer than 2 engage, or every number named is 8x or 1.5x revenue and up, or advisers say two-strategic processes are the norm.

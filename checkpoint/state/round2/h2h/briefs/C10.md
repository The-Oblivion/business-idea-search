# C10: Signed-Off v2: buy-and-build, AI-leveraged nuclear engineering house sold through the buyer co-op

**One-liner:** A buy-and-build nuclear engineering house that sells finished, independently verified calculation revisions and change packages per deliverable, at a discount tied to measured hours saved, produced by a two-tier bench with AI-assisted drafting and bought at orphan prices rather than auction prices.

**Revenue cases (5-7 years):** median $25M/yr; upside $110M/yr.

**Full refined pitch:** round2/deep/C10_refined.md · **Research dossier:** round2/deep/C10_dossier.md

## What the adversarial critics said (severity, then top objection)
- **Competition / incumbents** (serious): The customers are already locked up by incumbents who are now richly funded, and several of the named 'first 10' are not open doors. Enercon says on its own site it holds 'Engineer of Choice contracts at over 90% of nuclear power plants across the US' and 'projects for every nuclear utility'. It was merged with Pond in April 2026 into a 2,700-person firm, with a reported (unconfirmed) $1.25B of Arlington equity and add-ons planned. Sargent & Lundy (~4,700 staff, growing 14.5%) holds engineer-of-choice roles at Exelon/Constellation, Diablo Canyon (via Fluor) and the Utilities Service Alliance. Of the pitched wedge accounts: Energy Northwest has had Black & Veatch as engineer of choice since 2015 (~80 projects, 200+ B&V staff; snippet, the page 404'd on fetch). Wolf Creek runs an in-house Nuclear Engineering group (job postings). Holtec describes itself as vertically integrated (design, engineering, manufacturing) and is IPO-ing, NextEra says it is doing Duane Arnold's engineering evaluations itself, and I found no outside engineer named for Crane. Allied Power already holds Constellation's fleetwide staff-augmentation contract through Aug 2028 with a 5-year option. The incumbents also have a structural answer to 'hourly firms cannot cut hours'. They are capacity-constrained (S&L is hiring ~15% a year), so AI gains flow to margin and backlog, not price cuts. They hold years of actual hours per calculation and 50.59 at every plant, so they can quote a fixed price per bounded deliverable better than a new entrant can (my inference). They can match the pricing on exactly the narrow scopes Signed-Off wants, without ever changing their core model.
- **Customer / pricing / go-to-market** (serious): The first-customer list is not open to a bilateral fixed-price pilot, because owners buy engineering through multi-year alliances and a buying co-op. Energy Northwest (Columbia), one of the named first customers, belongs to the Utilities Service Alliance (USA Inc.). In January 2020 USA made Sargent & Lundy its Engineer of Choice for up to five years, covering 'engineering modification packages, calculations, equipment specifications'. That is the exact deliverable set Signed-Off sells. USA added GSE as a specialty Engineer of Choice (2019, expanded 2023) and took on Enercon and Blue Wave AI (an AI vendor) as 2026 supplier partners. PSEG gives S&L the majority of its modification design work (2017 alliance), and Enercon holds a blanket engineering contract with Exelon. Holtec (Palisades) is a vertically integrated engineering company that sells engineering consulting itself (SEC filing). So the entry point is a co-op panel rebid every few years, or a subcontract under an incumbent, not a deal with a Director of Design Engineering. USA's stated lever is to find 'measurable duplications and similarities' across member work. That is the template-library moat Signed-Off claims, and here the buyer co-op and its incumbents are already pursuing it for the buyer's benefit. The 'first 10 customers' list shrinks and is mostly pre-contracted. The idea file says the target names came 'from background knowledge'. I could not verify who serves Wolf Creek or Callaway, and I do not know whether S&L's USA term was re-awarded.
- **Execution / regulation / capital / timing** (serious): The core product (AI-produced, fixed price, rework guarantee) is hard to insure and cannot be self-insured, and it buys the customer no regulatory relief. Appendix B leaves the licensee responsible for the QA program (dossier, eCFR), so the owner's QA director gets cheaper work, not transferred accountability. The downside is badly asymmetric. A 50.59 evaluation is priced at roughly $30-50k (dossier), while a day of lost output on a 1.1 GW unit is worth $1.6-2.4M (the idea file's own arithmetic). That is a 30-80x ratio per day of delay [inference]. Berkley has published an 'Absolute' AI exclusion aimed at D&O, E&O and fiduciary lines. It reaches claims 'arising out of' any use of AI, and I could not confirm its approval status for design-professional policies. The ISO generative-AI exclusions for general liability took effect 1 Jan 2026 [S]. If the pitch is 'AI does the work', the E&O policy that Part 21 and owner contracts will demand may exclude exactly that claim. Vogtle/Summer is the cautionary precedent for fixed-price promises in nuclear. Fixed-price protection was only as good as the contractor's balance sheet, and the owners' letter of credit was about 1% of project cost [S]. A leveraged roll-up with a thin balance sheet cannot credibly guarantee safety-related deliverables.

## Second-round critic: wounded
- **Is the upside believable?** No. The $110M upside needs about nine conditions to hold together. The largest are: (a) a first-time team raises $60-75M and closes five bilateral deals at 6.5x EBITDA or less in a market where nuclear-only funds are buying; (b) AI cuts total accepted hours by 30% or more on calculation-grade work, for which there is no evidence beyond narrative drafting by non-specialists; (c) 14 new fixed-price accounts averaging $2.5M, won from entrenched alliances with 6-18 month cycles (Nuclearn's own comparable); (d) customers and ASL status survive the ownership change; (e) E&O is available at about 3% of revenue; and (f) incumbents do not match the pricing for two years. Even with generous odds on each and positive correlation, I get about 1-3% joint probability, not 5-8%.\n\nMore than half the upside revenue ($65M) is bought, not won, and does not depend on AI at all, so the upside is mostly multiple arbitrage. The rewrite's own sensitivity shows this: at 9-12x EBITDA the upside falls to $50-60M. The $25M median is itself an ordinary boutique roll-up, which is what the critiques predicted.\n\nTo its credit, the rewrite is candid about the probability, and its market-share math is modest (7.7% of the pool, about 7% of the reachable pool for the won portion). ## Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test
- **Riskiest assumption:** On real calculation-grade work (calculation revisions and design-change packages), AI-assisted production plus independent verification cuts total accepted hours by 30% or more with 90%+ first-pass acceptance. It is the only thing separating this from the Pelican/Arlington roll-up, it sets price, margin and hiring need, and the evidence is drafting-stage only (INL: about 50% on narrative drafting by non-specialists but hallucinations and a 10,500 vs 5,500 cc/s leak-rate error; Westinghouse: 25%, sponsored). Nobody has measured calculations or design changes. Demand for a discount is comparatively likely given USA's stated savings goals.
- **2-week test:** A public-record bake-off for under $10k over about 13 days. Days 1-3: build 12 difficulty-matched tasks from public docket material (UFSAR chapters, NRC-docketed licensing calculations, public 50.59 summaries): 4 screening/evaluation write-ups, 4 calculation revisions with changed inputs, 4 change-package technical sections. Days 4-10: one experienced ex-licensee design engineer (about 25 hours) does half the pairs manually, time-logged (Arm A) and the matched half AI-assisted with every number recomputed, then a second qualified person verifies (about 12 more hours), counting all hours (Arm B). Days 11-13: two ex-QA/licensing reviewers blind-score every package against an owner-style acceptance checklist. Pass: Arm B total hours at or below 70% of Arm A, first-pass acceptance at or above 90%, zero uncaught critical numerical errors. Kill: hours cut under 15% or any uncaught critical error; 15-30% means treat as the median case and price the discount at half the cut. In parallel and free: submit the USA supplier-partner form, make 10 calls to design-engineering leaders (are bounded scopes outside the alliance, would QA accept an AI-as-unverified-draft procedure), get 3 broker E&O indications with AI disclosed, and request 3 orphan-boutique teasers.

# C13 war game: Run-Base Shrinker v2 (founding on Monday 26 Oct 2026)

War-gamed 24 Oct 2026. About 45 searches and fetches. Evidence tags: **[primary]** I opened the document itself (city memo, filing, press release, job page); **[summary]** a search-tool summary I did not open in full; **[assumption]** my arithmetic or guess; **[model]** from the refined pitch's simulation (`deep/C13_refined.md` sections 5.3-5.6).

---

## 0. Bottom line

- The invoice-line rule is clean and the buyers are real. Ten named organizations below each have a dated event (a closed deal, a separation, a posted "application rationalization" job, a city program) and a budget now.
- The funded model's economics are poor. Per unit, payback is about 16 months; per client, cash payback is about 4 years on my ramp math (section 5). Realization evidence is weak: a health-system panel realized about 25-30% of target, and Austin has verified $8.1M against $49-142M identified (6-17%).
- The strongest threat is not a startup. It is Rimini Street (3,132 clients, $123M cash, already sells the fastest invoice-line cut in the market) plus Flexera (Thoma Bravo-owned, saying "execution" is the next step).
- Start only as the lean version (paid X-ray plus co-funded execution at 15%) with an insider co-founder. The full "we fund the engineering" company needs $28M peak cash in the model, plan $45-60M.

## 1. The strongest incumbent and funded rival

### 1.1 Rimini Street (strongest incumbent): it already sells the thing the Shrinker wants to bill on

What they sell today **[primary: Q2 2026 release, 30 Jul 2026]**:
- Revenue $111.1M in Q2 2026 (+6.7%), annualized recurring revenue $412.8M, **3,132 active clients**, 90% revenue retention, remaining performance obligations $636.9M, cash $123.4M against $48.4M debt.
- U.S. revenue was $48.4M, **down 1.6%**. Growth is international. That makes U.S. cost-reduction offers attractive to them.
- Describes itself as "the leading third-party support provider for Oracle, SAP and VMware software" and says 66% of VMware users are using or considering third-party support (survey teaser on the same page).
- Sells "managed services" and Rimini Smart Path (stay on your current ERP release, no forced upgrade); launched Rimini Govern for AI on 30 Jul 2026.
- Its published Managed Services Optimization datasheet **[primary: PDF text]** is a five-week consulting engagement that analyzes AMS ticket data, models ROI and delivers a roadmap; one cited SAP AMS client got an estimated 30-40% ticket reduction. It is steered toward "trusted partners like Rimini Street". No price is stated.

Why they are the right rival to fear:
1. Their core product moves a vendor support invoice line down immediately (the Q2 release features a client at 50% annual maintenance savings; I did not verify a general rate). That is a "line drops" outcome with no retirement work. It competes for the same CFO savings and the same first-year wins the Shrinker needs for Wave 1.
2. They already sit in the room with the CIO and CFO at 3,000+ SAP, Oracle and VMware shops.
3. After the Shrinker shows a CFO-signed ledger that works, Rimini can bundle it for free into a multi-year support contract: "we will cut your support line and retire the apps behind it, shared savings, no extra fee." They have the cash and the RPO to absorb that.

Likely response once the Shrinker has traction (my forecast, **[assumption]**): within two quarters they (a) add a shared-savings or "savings guarantee" rider to support renewals, (b) turn Managed Services Optimization into a funded, outcome-priced program with a 15-20% share, and (c) pitch the Shrinker's design partners with "we are already on your invoice."

**Counter-move:**
1. Do not fight on lines Rimini touches (SAP, Oracle, VMware support). Price a support swap as a "negotiation-only" unit at 15-20%, or refer it for a fee. A verified invoice drop is a verified invoice drop; make the Shrinker the neutral party that audits Rimini's savings on the client's ledger.
2. Aim at what Rimini cannot sell: the long tail (Austin has 1,184 distinct applications), SaaS, hosting and cloud, non-SAP/Oracle third-party maintenance, post-merger duplicate apps.
3. Publish the open ledger protocol early, so "Internal Audit signed the Shrinker ledger" becomes the standard evidence for any support-swap savings, including Rimini's.
4. Lock design partners into multi-wave agreements with 36-month scope exclusivity on retire and consolidate work.

### 1.2 Flexera (strongest funded software rival)

- Owned by Thoma Bravo; bought ProsperOps and Chaos Genius in Jan 2026 and earlier Spot, Snow Software, BDNA. CEO Jim Ryan: "organisations need more than dashboards, they need execution" **[primary: itassetmanagement.net, 7 Jan 2026]**. Its application rationalization product supports retire, retain, replace, consolidate decisions and dependency mapping **[summary]**.
- ProsperOps' fee is a share of realized cloud savings (rate not public; a third party guesses 30-35%, unverified). So Flexera owns the exact pricing template and a decade of IT-asset data in thousands of enterprises.
- Likely response: ship free "rationalization agents" in the ITAM suite and sign SI partners to deliver on shared savings. Software cannot get an application owner to sign off, but it can make the identification step free.
- Counter: ingest Flexera and ServiceNow data rather than building discovery; sell what software does not do (owner sign-off, archive and retention sign-off, contract exit, CFO sweep, guarantee). Become a Flexera execution partner if they ask.

## 5. Unit economics

### 5.1 Price (sources)

| Item | Price | Source / reason |
|---|---|---|
| Estate X-ray | $75-150k fixed, credited against first-year fees if a program is signed within 6 months | SaaS-buying services quote $30-250k plus 15-30% of validated savings (vendor-authored guide, via dossier) |
| Share, executed retirement we fund | 30% for 36 months, 25% from wave 3 on | Redress is 25% for negotiation only (primary) |
| Share, negotiation-only lines | 20% | Redress 25% ceiling |
| Share, co-funded (client pays engineering at cost plus 10%) | 15% | Lean variant |
| Tail | 10% of still-verified reduction, months 37-84 | About 0.7% of spend, the ISG governance price quoted by the critics (unverified) |

Blended 26% for 3 years plus 8% tail. The client keeps about 81% of every dollar over seven years (refined pitch). Austin test: tier A "execution-dependent" savings of $22-65M a year at 30% would be $6.6-19.5M a year of fee; the city's budget plan is $5.8M, then $14.2M, so a 30% fee on the budgeted savings is $1.7M, then $4.3M a year.

### 5.2 Per-client math (median client, $45M fee-eligible external application run base [assumption])

| Line | 7-year $M | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2.6 | 6% reduction at year 3, 7% at year 5 ($3.15M run-rate savings); 26% share; 8% tail [model] |
| Engineering we fund | 1.1 | 0.35 x annual saving [model] |
| Delivery and ops | 0.45 | [model] |
| Gross margin | 1.05 (40%) | revenue less engineering and ops |
| CAC | 0.24 | $0.15M + 0.2% of base [model] |
| Contribution | 0.7 (27%) | After CAC |

CAC sanity check [assumption]: one senior hunter at about $300k loaded plus advisor fees and travel, closing 1.5 program agreements a year on a 9-18 month cycle, is $0.2-0.25M each, partly offset by X-ray margin (X-ray $100k price less about $55k delivery cost for 3 people for 4 weeks).

**Payback.**
- Unit level: engineering cost 0.35 x saving, divided by a 26% share, is 1.35 years (about 16 months). Units above 0.45 x saving miss the 18-month hurdle and go to co-funding.
- Client level (my ramp math [assumption]): cash out is CAC $0.24M at month 0, engineering $1.1M over months 3-18, ops $5k a month; fees start around month 9 and ramp to about $55k a month by month 36. Cumulative cash turns positive at roughly month 50, **about 4 years from signing**. That is the real number behind "thin-margin services company".
- Lean variant (client funds engineering at cost plus 10%, we take 15% plus X-ray margin): no engineering float; contribution is roughly 55-60% on about $1.6M of seven-year revenue; client-level payback about 12-18 months.

### 5.3 Capital

- **To first revenue (a paid X-ray, month 2-4):** about $70-150k if founders are unpaid: advisor $30k, counsel and templates $15-25k, tools and travel $10-20k, broker submissions free. First revenue $50-150k.
- **To first success-fee dollar (month 9-15), lean variant:** about $450k gross spend (three X-rays' delivery $165k, legal $25k, insurance $40k, SOC 2 Type I $30k, tools and travel $40k, advisor $60k, two founder stipends $90k) against about $300k of X-ray revenue, so **about $150k net** [assumption; premiums and SOC 2 prices not sourced].
- **Full funded model:** peak cumulative cash about $28M (median) or $32M (upside) in the model; plan $45-60M with receivable timing and bad debt. Lender terms are unverified: revenue-based financing quotes run 15-40% APR (marketing blogs), and performance-based receivables are typically advanced at 80-90% of invoice (one government-contracting source). No lender priced contingent IT-savings receivables.

## 6. Biggest risk, mitigation, founder fit

**Biggest risk: owners do not release the money.**
- Health-system panel: 20% of applications targeted, "maybe 5% or 6%" realized; about $1M of labor to save $200k of licences **[primary: healthsystemcio 16 Jul 2026]**.
- Austin: $49-142M identified, $8.1M verified so far (6-17%), $5.8M budgeted for next year, and the city says collecting the data alone took over 4 months **[primary]**.
- If the fee only starts when an invoice line drops, every funded unit that stalls is dead capital. Related blockers: multi-year VMware terms (Metropolitan Council to 2031), acquirers that "honor existing contracts" (Prime), and carriers excluding AI work (Berkley has introduced an absolute AI exclusion on E&O products **[summary]**).

**Mitigation:**
1. Fund nothing before a CFO-signed program agreement and written owner sign-off on at least 50% of Wave 1 dollars.
2. Wave 1 is only lines with invoice end or renewal dates inside 9 months (licence, SaaS, hosting, support), where engineering is near zero.
3. Cap exposure at about $250k of funded engineering per client until the first audited invoice drop.
4. Co-fund (15%) anything above 0.45 x saving.
5. Paid X-ray and the Day-14 test screen out accounts that cannot sweep a budget line.
6. Human-led, AI-assisted work; no autonomous writes to production; client's own change process; 60-90 day scream test.

**Founder fit.** The founding team needs (a) a credible enterprise insider: 10+ years in IT finance, ITAM or vendor management, or a former AMS delivery head, with a CFO-level network; (b) an engineering lead who has run decommissioning and data-archive work; (c) someone who can negotiate contracts and share-of-savings terms with a Controller. One young or outside founder cannot get a CFO to hand over AP exports and sign a 3-year fee; if the founder is that person, the insider co-founder is not optional. Domain exposure to M&A integration offices or PE portfolio operations is the fastest way in.

---

## 7. Verdict

A real wedge with named buyers and a cheap test, but a thin, capital-hungry, services-like business whose funded version pays back in about four years per client. Run the 14-day test. Proceed only as the lean, co-funded variant with an insider co-founder, expecting a $5-20M firm, not a venture outcome.

# C04 War-Game: Time-to-Power Desk (matched-book seller of date-certain 40-200 MVA transformers)

Date: 2026-10-24 (Saturday). Scenario: founding Monday 2026-10-26. About 55 searches and fetches; all dated facts below are from pages fetched or searched on 10/24. Aggregator-only items are marked **(agg)**; items I could not confirm are in the last section.

## 0. Bottom line

- The gap is real only for one buyer type: a private buyer with a signed tenant or energization date (data-center owner, EPC, IPP). For them a month of delay costs several times the price of the transformer. They are also the buyers who already use direct OEM frames, stock dealers, and temporary units (see Lancium/Mortenson below).
- Public power, which is where almost all of the *verifiable* demand evidence is, buys by RFP/IFB at about the benchmark price and pays little for speed. The best revealed price of time I found is Harbor Springs, MI: +3.4% to save roughly 2 to 3.5 years.
- Realistic outcome: a 3-to-8-person sourcing boutique that earns fees and a thin dealer spread, break-even near 11 to 12 units a year (about $25M revenue). That matches the "median case" in the refined pitch, not the $120M upside.
- Start only with the 2-week test and a fee-only structure. Do not buy inventory before a written buyer commitment and a named OEM channel exist.

### The strongest response: JCL Energy, backstopped by Sunbelt Solomon/TJC

Once the Desk shows a repeatable 8-12% premium on date-certain units, the likely sequence:

1. **Month 0-3, price match on the reserved tier.** JCL already runs "reserve capacity now". It can quote the reserved tier at +2-3% over benchmark because it already holds the customer relationship and its overhead is spread over a distribution business. My unit math (section 5) shows the reserved tier earns about 4.8% gross before ~$58k of direct costs per unit. At +2.5% the tier is loss-making. The Desk cannot win that fight.
2. **Month 3-9, take the cancelled-unit supply.** JCL and Sunbelt Solomon are the natural buyers of cancelled and over-ordered units: they already carry inventory, hold lender lines, and are known to the sellers (MARS Transformers, Belyea and JCL already list 30/40/50 MVA units, some "in production, available 2027", on Surplus Record: https://surplusrecord.com/listing/30-40-50-mva-34500y-primary-13800-delta-secondary-prolec-3-units-in-production-1089339/ ). They can pay sellers faster and with no learning curve.
3. **Month 6-12, bundle.** Sunbelt Solomon adds the bridge rental unit (to 230 kV / 50 MVA) to every sale, so the buyer gets power in weeks while the permanent unit is built. A startup cannot match this.
4. **Quanta/EPC bundling.** Programmatic EPC buyers get slots inside Quanta's $500-700M transformer build-out, removing the biggest private buyers from the Desk's pool.

### Counter-move (what to do so the response does not kill the business)

- **Do not compete as a stock dealer.** Sell *into* JCL/Sunbelt. Become their off-book sourcing arm for cancelled and over-ordered 60-200 MVA units, for a finder's fee. The refined pitch already concedes that the realistic exit is acquisition.
- **Pick the band where stock dealers are thin.** JCL's own engineered-substation page shows 30-34 weeks production for custom units, and the in-stock substation range I could verify tops out far below 100 MVA (JCL "commonly stocks 300-5000 kVA" for substations per its pages). The 60-200 MVA band with a specific impedance/BIL/vector group is build-to-order for them.
- **Sell contractual date certainty, not units.** Dealers sell "ready to ship". The Desk sells a dated delivery with liquidated damages, factory-acceptance witness, origin attestations, and a refund clause if DOE rules strand the unit. Charge for the contract (a reservation fee that is credited on order), not for the metal.
- **Avoid the reserved tier as a profit center** (it is only an option fee that feeds the funnel).
- **Use the open public solicitations as free market intelligence.** Every RFP award tabulation shows the incumbents' price and weeks (for example Lubbock's April 2025 award at $29.3k per MVA and 60 weeks; section 5).

## 4. Quit conditions (measurable, dated)

1. **By Fri 2026-11-13 (test).** Stop if any of: (a) 3 or more of 6 dealers quote 20 weeks or less at 8% or less over benchmark on at least 2 of 3 specs; (b) fewer than 5 of 20 buyers name a documented transformer-critical delay of 6 months or more; (c) no buyer gives a written indication at 8%+ or a reservation fee.
2. **By Fri 2026-12-18 (supply).** Stop the principal/pooled-deposit model if zero OEMs or OEM agents will state in writing that a buyer deposit can be tied to a production slot and that a unit can be transferred. Fallback is fee-only brokerage, which I would treat as a side business, not a company.
3. **By Fri 2027-01-22 (day 90).** Stop if no executed deal of at least $1.5M or paid reservation fee, or if the qualified pipeline is under $15M.
4. **By Fri 2027-04-30.** Stop if cumulative closed revenue is under $6M (about 3 units), or realized gross margin on closed units is under 5%, or cash burn exceeds $400k with under $150k of fees earned.
5. **By Sat 2027-07-31.** Drop the principal model if no unit has been sourced at 10% or more below replacement cost (no floor-tier supply). Without that tier the model loses money (section 5).
6. **External triggers (any time).** Quoted lead times for new 40-100 MVA units fall under 70 weeks from at least 3 OEMs (Rystad already says growth in prices and lead times has "largely stalled"), or benchmark price falls more than 10% from about $32.5k per MVA.

## 5. Unit economics (show the math)

### Anchors (sourced)
- **Price per MVA.** Lubbock, April 2025: two 50 MVA for $2,928,000 = **$29,280 per MVA**, 60-week delivery (Electric Utility Board agenda; summarized in my search, video index https://lubbocktx.new.swagit.com/videos/340301 ). Rystad: recent European awards for 50-100 MVA $30-35k per MVA (https://powerpeakdigest.com/global-transformer-supply-constraints-ease-rystad/ ). I use **$32.5k per MVA**, so a 60 MVA unit = **$1.95M**.
- **Price of speed, observed.** Harbor Springs, MI: $1,323,270 (up to 1.5 years) vs $1,279,391 (3.5-5 years) = **+3.4%** for roughly 2 to 3.5 years saved (https://transformer-technology.com/harbor-springs-advances-6-million-electrical-substation-project-with-transformer-purchase-transformer-technology-news/ ). Bolt Electrical claims 12-15% over factory cost for 12-14 months (POWER Magazine; hard to verify).
- **Payment milestones.** Mt. Pleasant, UT: 30% at order, 30% at drawing approval, 40% at shipment (https://sanpetemessenger.com/mt-pleasant-approves-major-transformer-purchase-delays-a-fire-billing-ordinance/ ).
- **Cost of money.** Fed raised to 3.75-4.00% on 2026-09-16; SOFR about 3.87-3.90% in early October (https://www.sofrrate.com/sofr-rate/2026-10 ). Assume a warehouse line at SOFR + 400 bp = **about 7.9%**.

### Price and margin by tier (60 MVA, benchmark cost $1.95M)

| Tier | Sale price | Purchase cost | Gross profit | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floor (cancelled finished unit, bought 12% below replacement), +17.5% over benchmark | $2.29M | $1.716M | $0.574M | 25% |
| Reassigned (in production for a cancelled buyer), +10% | $2.145M | $1.95M | $0.195M | 9.1% |
| Reserved (pooled deposit), +5% (the 2-3% fee is credited at order, not extra revenue) | $2.05M | $1.95M | $0.098M | 4.8% |

### Direct costs per unit (assumptions, mine)
- Liquidated-damages and warranty reserve 1% of price: about $21k.
- Cargo/storage insurance and LC fees (1.5% a year on about 10% of price for about 1.5 years): about $9k.
- Factory-acceptance witness and independent testing: about $15k.
- Financing carry on units not fully prepaid: about $10k average (floor-tier units held about 3 months at 7.9%: about $34k each, plus about $35k of storage/retest).
- Transport and installation: pass-through to the buyer (FOB/delivered priced separately).
- Total about $55-58k per non-floor unit; about $100k per floor unit.

### Blended year-3 case (12 units/yr: 2 floor, 4 reassigned, 6 reserved)
- Revenue = 2 x $2.29M + 4 x $2.145M + 6 x $2.05M = **$25.5M**.
- Gross profit = 2 x $0.574M + 4 x $0.195M + 6 x $0.098M = **$2.5M (9.9%)**.
- Direct costs about $0.78M, so contribution about **$1.7M (6.8% of revenue)**.
- **Without the floor tier** (cancelled units never materialize: 0 floor, 4 reassigned, 8 reserved): revenue $25.0M, gross profit $1.56M (6.2%), contribution about **$0.9M**.
- Opex for an 8-person team: about **$1.6M** (principal $200k, two commercial/sourcing leads $340k, transformer engineer $190k, fractional counsel $120k, finance $150k, logistics/PM $140k, travel and events $150k, E&O and product insurance $120k, legal/accounting $120k, software $40k; all assumptions).
- **Break-even about 11 to 12 units a year** (about $25M revenue) with the floor tier; about 22 units without it. A 25-person team (about $5M) needs about 36 units with the floor tier.
- This matches the second-round critic's 7-10% blended gross margin, not the refined pitch's 11-13%.

### Price match test (the incumbent response)
If JCL/Sunbelt force the reserved tier to +2.5%: gross profit about $49k vs about $58k direct cost = **negative**. The reserved tier cannot be the profit center.

### Fee-only brokerage path (first 6 months)
- Assume a 3% agent commission paid by the OEM/dealer on a $2.145M unit = **$64k per unit** (3-5% is a typical capital-equipment agent range; not sourced).
- Two founders at about $0.3M a year of costs break even at about 5 units; a 3-person team ($0.65M) at about 10 units.

### CAC, payback, capital
- **CAC.** Public-power path: about 60-100 hours of proposal work per bid ($15-25k with counsel) at an assumed 1-in-5 win rate = about $100k per win. Private path: 4 industry events ($40k), one BD hire ($170k over 9 months), 3 customers = about $70k each. **Blended CAC about $85k per first-time customer** (estimate).
- **Payback.** A customer taking 2 units a year yields about 2 x $145k = $290k contribution. CAC / annual contribution = about 3.5 months of contribution flow, but cash arrives 9 to 18 months after first contact (delivery of a reassigned or reserved unit), so **realistic payback is 12-18 months** from first contact.
- **Gross margin.** 6-10% blended (25% only on the floor tier, which is unproven).
- **Capital to first revenue.** Fee-only brokered deal: about **$150k** (counsel $40-60k, E&O insurance $25k, travel $30k, entity/registrations $10k, runway). First matched-principal deal: about **$1.0-1.5M** (bid and performance security, LC support, and 2-3 months of team cost) because the buyer's 30% deposit passes to the OEM. The refined pitch's upside needs about **$25-30M of equity plus a $50-60M warehouse line**; I would not plan for that until the supply-side proof exists.
- **Market check.** At $2.15M a unit, $120M of revenue is about 56 units a year. The refined pitch's own reachable-payer count is about $335M a year; the second-round critic is right that $120M is a heroic share.

### Value of time for the private buyer (why the segment exists)
My assumption, not sourced: a 50 MW IT-load facility with a signed tenant at about $100-150/kW-month earns about $5-7.5M a month. A one-month delay costs 2.5x to 4x the price of a $2M transformer, so a private buyer on the critical path can rationally pay 15-30%. Public power, with no tenant and RFP rules, rationally pays 1-4%. PJM's own milestone data put supply chain at 23% of delay requests against 29% for permitting, so the transformer is the critical path for perhaps a quarter of delayed projects (secondary source).

---

## 6. Biggest risk, mitigation, founder fit

**Biggest risk: no defensible premium and no guaranteed supply.** The buyers who would pay (private owners on a signed-tenant clock) already have direct OEM relationships, JCL/Sunbelt stock, Quanta/EPC bundles, and temporary units (Lancium). The buyers who are plentiful and reachable (public power) pay about benchmark. On the supply side, no source shows an OEM will accept a newcomer's pooled-deposit reservation or consent to a unit transfer; Hitachi's reported behavior is direct end-user frameworks. Cancelled units may be double-orders that never became built units.

**Mitigation.**
1. The 2-week test and the 11/13 and 12/18 gates before any inventory.
2. Fee-only brokerage first; hold title only against a signed buyer PO and deposit.
3. Aim at the private-owner segment (Aligned-type OFE buyers, EPCs like Mortenson) and use public RFPs only for credibility and market data.
4. Make the OEM/agent letter (slot-to-deposit tie and unit-transfer consent) the first deliverable of Phase 3.
5. Plan the exit as a sourcing partner to JCL/Sunbelt/Quanta, not a stand-alone dealer.

**Founder fit.**
- A two-to-three person team: (1) a 15-year-plus transformer sales or utility/EPC substation procurement veteran who knows impedance, BIL, vector group, approved-plant lists and has 100-plus buyer contacts (ex-OEM sales at Hitachi, Prolec, Hyundai, Virginia Transformer, Siemens Energy; or ex-utility supply chain); (2) a trade-finance/commercial person who can structure LCs, bid and performance bonds, and a warehouse line (ex-JCL/Sunbelt/lessor); (3) fractional energy-regulatory counsel (DPA, EO 14420, BABA).
- A first-time founder without industry insiders cannot sell $2M sole-source units to utilities. Pair with a veteran or apprentice at a dealer/OEM for 12 to 18 months first.

---

## 7. Verdict (80 words max)

Thin and crowded. The gap exists only for private buyers with a signed tenant, where a month of delay costs several times a unit's price; public power buys by RFP and paid just 3.4% to save years at Harbor Springs. JCL already sells "reserve capacity now" and Sunbelt Solomon has the capital. Realistic result is a fee-driven boutique, break-even near 12 units a year. Start only with an industry co-founder and the two-week test.

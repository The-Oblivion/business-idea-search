# C09 war-game: "Stafette" (Meister-partnered hubs that buy the books and crews of closing Elektro/SHK firms)

Written 2026-10-24 (Saturday). "Monday" below means 2026-10-26. FX 1 EUR = 1.15 USD. About 45 searches and fetches this run. Tags: **[read]** = I opened the page or PDF; **[snippet]** = seen only in a search-result summary; **[assumption]** = my number; **[M]** = from memory, not verified.

## 0. Verdict (after war-gaming)

Not attractive to start as specified. Deal flow is real and fast: at least six German SHK or electrical-type firms entered preliminary insolvency between 1 and 22 Oct 2026 alone. But ROM Technik (Zech Group) absorbed a 55-person electrical firm in 46 days, HomeServe Deutschland (about EUR 100M revenue) already owns the back office and the capital, the pitch's 19% contribution holds only above roughly 69% technician utilisation (in a market where ZDH reports a deepening slump), and the no-exit equity for tranches B and C has no identified source. The only version worth starting is a 12-week, under-EUR 40k deal-flow test led by a German-speaking Meister. The revenue upside ($108M) is a EUR 6M-EBIT business, not a software outcome.

## 1. Strongest rival and its response

### 1.1 Who it is: HomeServe Deutschland (Brookfield)

What they already sell and own (all verifiable today):
- 24 businesses, about 1,300 employees after buying Ernst Wohlfeil (Rheinstetten) on 15 Oct 2025 [read, Grant Thornton]. Then Scheer Heizung und Sanitaer (Hamburg area, about 50 staff) on 16 Mar 2026 with the stated plan of long-term growth "with the existing management" [snippet, Grant Thornton]. Earlier: Raff (Stuttgart), Scheel (Itzehoe), Saling (Klein-Winternheim), Eckel (Oldenburg), Nikoley (Ginsheim-Gustavsburg) in 2023 [read, Pinsent Masons].
- Revenue "ca. 100 Millionen Euro" and a **Frankfurt service center of about 100 people** that is the contact point for the SHK businesses [read, HomeServe job posting via remotely.de].
- A **Partnernetz** of independent trade firms: a full-time "Manager Partnernetz" role exists to identify, win and develop craft businesses (plumbing, heating, electrical) as service partners, with quality reviews and loyalty events [read, same posting; posting is no longer live, so the network size is not public].
- Promise to sellers: firms keep name, logo and contacts; HomeServe supplies digitalisation, recruiting, purchasing and marketing [read, Grant Thornton]. A Vaillant partnership is shown on the German CEO's profile [snippet].
- Brookfield took the HomeServe group private in Jan 2023 for GBP 4.08B [snippet, Globe and Mail / City AM].

Second threat, for estates and crews: **ROM Technik / Zech Group**. ROM has 2,600+ staff, sits in a family group of about 12,000 [read ROM press release; snippet for Zech]. Timeline on Rueckert + Mueller (Karlsruhe electrical, 55 staff): preliminary administrator PLUTA appointed 16 Mar 2026, proceedings opened and asset deal closed 1 May 2026 (46 days), 49 staff taken over, announced 11 May [read, ROM release]. Zech did the same with QUINtec in 2021 (41 staff) and with SCHNEIDER Engineering in 2026 [snippet]. This is exactly the "buy the crew out of distress" move, done faster than a startup can raise a cheque.

Small peers (not the real threat): MEISTEREHRE lists **3 firms and 31 employees** in Bavaria (Pullach HQ; Halm Bad Reichenhall and Muenchen, Kargl Holzkirchen) and a Bad Reichenhall succession job with a 60-200k salary band and "Beteiligung" [read, meisterehre.de and join.com snippet]. GREENOX (founded 2023) bought DUERR Elektrotechnik (Ofterdingen) on 1 Jul 2025, owner stays on, and opened a Munich site [snippet]. Neither shows capital depth.

### 1.2 How HomeServe responds once Stafette shows traction in a cluster
1. **Cheapest copy, a partner offer instead of a purchase.** Their Partnernetz plus a 100-person dispatch center lets them tell a retiring owner "keep your firm, take our jobs, we guarantee volume", with zero purchase price. That attacks our core formula (we pay 0.25-0.40x, half contingent) with a free alternative, and it needs no Betriebsleiter per tuck-in.
2. **Lower the size floor and raise the bid.** They already bought a 50-staff firm in March 2026. Brookfield cost of capital against our steward-owned equity.
3. **Poach the Meister-partners.** A management incentive plan is cheap to offer and our equity ladder (5% a year to 30%) takes years to pay.
4. **Lock the Hausverwaltung channel** with a service-level offer through the Frankfurt center before we collect continuity letters.

### 1.3 Counter-move (what Stafette does differently, in order of strength)
- **Fight where they are thin.** HomeServe's German focus is "private Endkunden und Wohnungsbau" SHK [read, Pinsent Masons]. Stafette leads with **Elektro and B2B service for Verwalter and small commercial sites**, and avoids their footprints (Stuttgart, Karlsruhe/Rheinstetten, Itzehoe, Hamburg, Oldenburg, Rheinhessen) for the first cluster. Default first cluster: Ostbayern/Regensburg or Suedniedersachsen/Goslar, where live estates exist and none of HomeServe, MEISTEREHRE or GREENOX is listed [inferred from the footprints above].
- **Be the lighter process under EUR 150k.** A standard asset-purchase pack, Sec. 613a BGB letters, escrow and earn-out accounting, with an offer in 10 working days. PE-backed groups run EUR 50k+ deal overhead per file [assumption from the refined pitch].
- **Make the Meister equity real.** Real shares in the hub GmbH versus a bonus plan.
- **Walk away above 0.5x** of retained revenue and let the strategics overpay.
- **Treat HomeServe/ROM as the exit and the overflow buyer**, not only the enemy. A hub that fails the 15% contribution gate gets sold to them. Hold-forever is a covenant, not a moat (the pitch already concedes this).
- Honest limit: none of this is a legal barrier. A determined HomeServe wins any single contested seller.

## 4. Quit conditions (measurable, dated)

1. **By 20 Nov 2026:** fewer than 2 signed NDAs with administrators or owners AND fewer than 4 owner conversations from 60 calls. Stop calling and rework the offer once; second miss means stop.
2. **By 18 Dec 2026:** no written non-binding "yes" to the formula from any owner or administrator, or every counterparty insists on 0.7x or more in cash. Stop.
3. **By 22 Jan 2027 (day 88):** (a) no signed asset-purchase agreement or at least 2 signed LOIs; or (b) B2B repeat revenue below 40% in every ledger seen; or (c) both HWKs say each absorbed crew needs its own full-time Meister. Any one of (b) or (c) is a stop. (a) is a stop unless (b) and (c) are clean and one deal is within 3 weeks of signing.
4. **By 31 Mar 2027:** under EUR 1.0M of tranche-A commitments. Stop buying; return to the Meister-led micro version or close.
5. **Contest test, running:** if 3 consecutive estate or closer processes go to ROM, HomeServe, GREENOX or a similar bidder at more than 0.5x retained revenue, stop bidding in that cluster.
6. **Month-12 gate (about Q4 2027, from first closing):** retained revenue under 70%, or hub contribution under 15% after the 3.5% fee, or price paid above 0.4x. Stop buying new books; manage the existing hubs for cash.
7. **By 30 Jun 2027:** no hub operating with positive monthly contribution. Stop.

## 5. Unit economics

### 5.1 Summary
- **What we pay:** 0.25-0.40x trailing-12-month revenue of contracts actually assigned and retained; 40-50% at closing; hard ceiling 0.5x. The KfW Fokus 526 realised-deal corridor for Bau/Handwerk under EUR 20M revenue is 0.25-0.50x, while owners expect about 0.87x [read, dossier].
- **What a hub sells:** service at EUR 75-95 per hour net. Ranges found: Elektro EUR 65-95, SHK EUR 70-100 net; under EUR 60 is hardly viable for a Meister firm [snippet, clean-invoice 2026].
- **Platform take:** 3.5% of hub revenue for accounting, payroll, scheduling, procurement, insurance and the M&A desk [pitch].
- **Typical book:** EUR 0.45M trailing revenue, 70% retained at month 12 (pitch assumption, not sourced).

### 5.2 Deal math (my arithmetic)
- Price ceiling at 0.30x: 0.30 x EUR 450k = EUR 135k. At 70% retention the contingent part scales, so paid price = EUR 94.5k. Upfront 45% of the ceiling = **EUR 60.8k**.
- Incremental EBIT of the retained revenue when overhead is already covered: EUR 450k x 70% x (contribution 19% - fee 3.5%) = **EUR 48.8k a year**.
- **CAC (cost per closed book):** funnel from the pass test is 120 listed, 60 called, 10 conversations, 4 ledgers, 2 yeses, about 1 close in the first run. Cost to close in the first run is about EUR 26k (two founders for 8 weeks at EUR 4k a month = EUR 16k opportunity cost plus EUR 10k legal and diligence). At a steady 1 close per 4 ledgers: EUR 10k legal and diligence + 4 x EUR 1.5k ledger work = **about EUR 16k**. The pitch's gate is under EUR 12k per book; that is an assumption that I cannot confirm and the first run will miss it.
- **Payback** (price + CAC = EUR 110.5k over incremental EBIT): 27 months at 19% contribution. Range 20 months (24.7%) to 65 months (10%).

### 5.3 Where the 19% contribution comes from (and fails)
Inputs: net rate EUR 80/h [sourced range above]; 1,650 attended hours a year [assumption]; loaded wage EUR 61.1k = EUR 22/h gross x 2,028 paid hours x 1.37 on-costs (the 22/h and 37% are the inputs used in a vendor's published calculator) [snippet, streit-software]; van EUR 13k and tools EUR 2k [assumption]; materials 22% of revenue at 30% gross margin [assumption].

| Billable utilisation of attended hours | Revenue per technician | Contribution | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 55% | EUR 93k | EUR 2.6k | 2.8% |
| 60% | EUR 102k | EUR 9.8k | 9.6% |
| 65% | EUR 110k | EUR 16.9k | 15.4% |
| **69%** | EUR 117k | EUR 22.7k | **19.4%** |
| 75% | EUR 127k | EUR 31.3k | 24.6% |

So the pitch's 19% is not invented out of thin air but it sits on one knife-edge variable. Utilisation of 60% (what a falling order book looks like) gives about 10%. Rate sensitivity at 65% utilisation: EUR 70/h gives 5.5%, EUR 85/h gives 19.5%. ZDH reports business conditions and expectations "stark" down in Q2 2026 [snippet]. The Busch-Jaeger/Heckner operating comparison (2022 data, a self-selected sample) puts average electrical-firm return at only 4-5% of annual output after tax and owner pay [snippet]. Hub EBIT at EUR 3.4M revenue: contribution 10% gives -0.6%, 15% gives 4.4%, 19% gives 8.4%, 23% gives 12.4% (EUR 240k hub overhead and 3.5% fee as in the pitch).

### 5.4 Platform level
- A 5-person central team (2 founders, deal/legal, finance/payroll, ops) at about EUR 450k a year needs EUR 12.9M of hub revenue to break even on the 3.5% fee.
- Median path (EUR 0.9M, 2.4M, 5.4M, 9.4M, 14.0M, 19.0M, 24.0M in years 1-7): net central cost -EUR 419k, -366k, -261k, -121k, then +40k in year 5. **Cumulative central burn peaks at about -EUR 1.17M in year 4**, before ramp losses on hubs. The pitch's tranche A of EUR 4M covers it; the story that "the fee funds the central team" is true only from year 5.
- Gross margin, in the sense the pitch uses: hub contribution after materials, technician wages and vehicles = 19% base case (range 10-25%). Platform fee margin is mostly cost-covering, not profit.

### 5.5 Capital to first revenue
- **To a signed first deal:** under EUR 40k (Section 3 budget) plus founder time.
- **To the first hub with revenue (about EUR 420k):** EUR 61k upfront for a book, EUR 25k legal and escrow set-up, EUR 150k working capital (wages go out before invoices come in; for an estate the Insolvenzgeld window ends after three months), EUR 120k hub overhead for the first 6 months (Meister at EUR 76k loaded, dispatch, premises) and EUR 40k buffer. This is my estimate [assumption]. Gate-by-gate capital in the pitch is EUR 4M (3 hubs, months 3-24), EUR 10M (months 24-48), EUR 16M plus debt (years 4-7).

## 6. Biggest risk, mitigation, and who can win

**Biggest risk: you only get the books that nobody with a back office wants, and they leave.** Evidence: the electrical firm in the Munich Innung listing lost about 27% of revenue in two years; strategics (ROM, HomeServe, GREENOX) take the better estates within weeks; BGB 309 Nr. 9 lets private maintenance customers leave on one month's notice (from the critics' evidence); WEG administrators need about three comparative quotes for larger jobs, so continuity is not automatic. Retention below 70% and utilisation below 65% together push contribution under 15%, and you only learn it around month 12, after the capital is spent.

**Mitigation:**
1. Pay on retained revenue (45% contingent, 24 months, escrow 10-15% of upfront) so the seller carries the retention risk. Already in the pitch.
2. Exclude consumer and tendered books from the priced base; price only B2B repeat revenue with top-10 customers under 40%.
3. Measure utilisation and rate before closing from the ledger and technician time sheets; make 65% utilisation a condition of the bid.
4. Cap exposure per cluster at 3 hubs until the month-12 gate passes.
5. Keep the quit conditions in Section 4 binding.

**Founder fit.** Two people who already hold what the pitch lacks:
- **A German-speaking Elektro or SHK Meister (or Betriebswirt des Handwerks) who has run a firm of 10+ staff**, with a local Innung/HWK network and the licence to be the registered Geschaeftsfuehrer of a hub (Sec. 7 HwO). Sellers and administrators will not talk to anyone else about their crews.
- **A deal and paper operator** who has closed small asset purchases (law firm, transaction services, or a search fund), can handle Sec. 613a BGB, earn-outs and escrow, and can build the cash model.
A solo founder with no German, no Handwerk credentials and no Meister partner cannot execute Section 3. The capital side (a first-time operator raising no-exit equity at a 6-8% yield) stays the likeliest cause of death even with the right team.

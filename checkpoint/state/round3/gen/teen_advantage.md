# Round 3 / Lens: businesses where being 18 is an advantage

Written late Oct 2026. About 28 searches. Every competitor named below came out of a search unless I mark it "from memory". "Not found" means my search did not surface it. It does not prove nothing exists.

## How I ranked

I ranked on the brief's six tests, in this order: first paying customer in 60-90 days under about $10k, plausible buyer, no license wall, uses his edges, credible path to $10M+, not crowded. Three of the eight ideas (3, 4, 8) share one asset: a verified 18+ panel recruited from his network. Build that once and it feeds all three.

Guardrails applied to every idea: all workers, creators and panelists are 18+; no gambling, lending or crypto brands as customers; no card "breaks" or mystery-box lotteries; no academic-dishonesty angles; no promised outcomes on money matters.

## 1. Campus Signup Crew (a pay-per-activated-user network of verified 18-24 reps)

**One-liner:** A marketplace where startups and DTC brands pay per verified, activated student user, and a network of vetted 18-24 reps (starting with his high school's last four graduating classes, now on about 50 campuses) earns it by sharing links in the group chats they already live in.

- **Customer:** Seed to Series A consumer apps, AI and SaaS tools courting students, and DTC brands that need 18-24 users.
- **Who pays:** The founder or growth lead, from the performance budget. $3-12 per activated user, with the platform keeping 30-40%. $1,000 minimum prepaid campaign. Whop Content Rewards uses the same $1,000 floor, so brands are used to it.
- **Problem:** Everyone wants Gen Z users, and every serious brand ends up building its own rep program.
  - SHEIN reports 17,000+ active ambassadors and over $30M paid in commissions ([PR Asia](https://en.prnasia.com/releases/global/shein-expands-campus-ambassador-program-with-back-to-school-campus-tour-at-seven-universities-544384.shtml)).
  - Fizz paid reps about $60 for a 3-hour shift ([Ithacan](https://theithacan.org/49642/news/fizz-hires-ic-students-to-market-app-on-campus/)).
  - BeReal reportedly paid $30 per referral ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/q/how-did-bereal-get-so-popular/)).
  - A small startup cannot run contracts, payouts, fraud checks and attribution for 50 reps.
- **Solution:** A web app. A brand posts a campaign defined by an activation event (for example ".edu signup plus finished onboarding"). Each rep gets a unique link or code. Attribution comes from a webhook or postback (RevenueCat, PostHog, Stripe). The brand sees only verified, activated users. There is a 14-day holdback to catch fraud, instant payouts through Stripe Connect, 1099s, and FTC disclosure text built into every asset. Reps share through Instagram stories, GroupMe, Discord and dorm chats, not tabling.
- **Why now:**
  - AI and SaaS companies are in a student land-grab (ambassador clubs such as Clay's, free student plans).
  - Paid-social CAC for 18-22s keeps climbing.
  - Founders are younger and trust peers.
- **Founder edge:**
  - It is his own network: classmates, older siblings and the graduating classes ahead of him.
  - Buyers are young founders who respond to a peer.
  - His mentor teaches entrepreneurship at UF, a 50,000-student campus for the first pilot.
  - He builds the tracker and payout rails in days.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Week 1: landing page plus a one-link tracker.
  2. Recruit 40 reps across about 10 campuses, with a $25 bonus after their first 5 activations.
  3. DM 100 founders who launched a student-relevant consumer app on Product Hunt, X or TrustMRR in the last 60 days. The offer is "first 100 activated students at $5, pay only on results."
  4. Ask the mentor for 3 intros to UF-linked founders.
  Expect 3-5 customers at $500-2,000 each within 30-45 days.
- **Cost to first revenue:** About $1,500 (rep seed bonuses, a lawyer to review the contractor agreement, domain).
- **Path to big:** $10M of revenue at a 35% take needs about $29M a year of brand spend, or roughly 5M activations at $6, across about 300 brands at $100k a year. The network is the moat: verified reps, fraud history and instant payouts. Later it moves beyond campuses to any 18-24 audience and to pay-per-subscriber. Share needed is meaningful but not heroic. SHEIN alone has paid out $30M.
- **Who does it today:**
  - In-house programs: SHEIN, Fizz, BeReal, Clay Clubs.
  - Software that brands run themselves: [Nextbee](https://blog.nextbee.com/2026/01/12/student-ambassador-programs-how-to-dominate-campus-marketing-2026-complete-playbook/), BrandChamp, Ambassify (the last two from memory).
  - WayUp's campus rep program (about $1 per verified signup; [WayUp](https://www.wayup.com/guide/community/dont-want-miss-wayup-campus-rep-program/)).
  - UNiDAYS and Student Beans, which sell student-discount marketing to big brands ([LBB](https://www.lbbonline.com/news/unidays-hits-20m-verified-student-and-graduate-members)).
  - Whop and Vyro, which pay clippers per view, not per signup.
  - I found no open pay-per-activated-signup marketplace for small brands.
- **What kills it:** Fake signups. Rep contractor-classification rules in CA and NJ. Brands that will not pay for activation-based metrics. Mitigations are .edu verification, the holdback, and exclusion of lending, gambling and crypto brands.

## 2. Live Host Network (hosts for hire for Whatnot and TikTok Shop live sellers)

**One-liner:** A bench of trained, on-camera 18+ hosts who run live selling shows for collectibles, thrift, beauty and fashion sellers on Whatnot and TikTok Shop, paid out of a share of hosted-show sales. Add a post-show profit dashboard later.

- **Customer:** Serious live sellers (cards, vintage, beauty, jewelry, women's fashion) who already go live 3-4 times a week and cannot add more hours.
- **Who pays:** The seller. Either 12-20% of hosted-show GMV, or $20-35 an hour plus 5-8% commission, with the first show free.
- **Problem:** Live selling is a performance job. The best sellers burn out, and inventory sits idle between shows.
  - Whatnot sellers who go live 3-4 times a week average over $13,000 a month, and one in eight is full-time ([Wikipedia/press summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whatnot)).
  - Whatnot drove about $8B of live sales in 2025 ([Exciting Commerce](https://excitingcommerce.de/2026/01/29/qvc-aufgepasst-whatnot-knackt-8-mrd-im-live-shopping/)).
  - eMarketer projects US livestream retail at $19.76B in 2026, up 35%. TikTok Live sales more than doubled in H1 2026 ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/whatnot-aims-make-live-shopping-mainstream-with-its-first-national-campaign)).
- **Solution:**
  - Audition and train 18+ hosts, drawn from theater, debate, streamers and sales-minded classmates.
  - Match hosts to sellers by category.
  - Give every show a run-of-show, an auction pacing script and comment moderation.
  - Track results from the seller's own payout export.
  - Later add AI co-host tooling and auto-clips.
  - Hosted shows are fixed-price and auction singles only. No breaks and no mystery formats.
- **Why now:** Whatnot was valued at $11.5B at its Series F ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/whatnot-raises-225-million-as-investors-bet-on-live-shopping/)) and reportedly raised a $545M Series G in August 2026 ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.nz/news/business/whatnot-raises-545-million-us-dollars-in-series-g-round/2026081043031)). On-camera labor is the bottleneck for growth.
- **Founder edge:** Hosts are the product, and an authentic Gen Z voice is the thing that sells. He knows who in his world can talk for three hours. He is fluent in both platforms and can build the tooling quickly.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Pull 200 sellers from public Whatnot pages (an [Apify scraper](https://apify.com/blackfalcondata/whatnot-scraper) exists), filtered to those with 3+ shows a week.
  2. DM each: "I'll host your next show free; you pay only if sales beat your average."
  3. Recruit 10 hosts. Expect 3 sellers within 2 weeks.
- **Cost to first revenue:** Under $500 (ring light, accounts, a few sample purchases).
- **Path to big:** $10M of revenue at a 15% take needs about $70M of hosted GMV, roughly 0.35% of US livestream retail. Then it layers on a seller P&L dashboard, a talent roster with exclusives, and show financing. This is a services wedge. The scalable assets are the host roster, the data on what converts, and the software that standardizes shows.
- **Who does it today:**
  - TikTok Shop live-operation agencies and job postings paying hosts $16-50 an hour plus commission ([NetInfluencer posting](https://www.netinfluencer.com/jobs/tiktok-livestream-host-part-time-at-superordinary/), [Monster posting](https://www.monster.com/job-openings/tiktok-live-host-sugar-land-tx--228b9978-41b7-4b02-accc-596f94103e90)).
  - Whatnot seller coaches.
  - I found no Whatnot-specific host marketplace and no published agency rate card.
  - Whatnot has Series E money earmarked for seller tools and could add a co-host feature.
- **What kills it:** Platform terms on third-party hosts. The pending arbitration claims that card breaks are unlawful lotteries (avoid breaks entirely). Thin margins if hosts demand hourly pay. A seller who just takes the trained host in-house.

## 3. Hook Lab (an 18-24 verified taste panel for apps and DTC brands: "ask 100 real Gen Zers in 4 hours")

**One-liner:** Self-serve testing of hooks, names, onboarding screens and paywall copy with a paid, identity-verified panel of 18-24s, returned as a ranked one-pager with verbatims.

- **Customer:** Indie and seed-stage consumer app founders, DTC marketers, creators.
- **Who pays:** The founder or marketer. $99-499 per test, or $299 a month for 5 tests.
- **Problem:** AI made creative nearly free, so the scarce thing is knowing what lands with 18-24s. RevenueCat counts about 14,700 new subscription apps launched in January 2026, up from about 2,000 in January 2022 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)). Testing with live ad spend takes days and burns cash. Generic survey panels are cheap (Pollfish base is $0.95 a response, plus add-ons per demographic; [Pollfish](https://www.pollfish.com/pricing)) but are not Gen Z-native and give no qualitative "why".
- **Solution:**
  - 18+ panelists verified by phone and .edu, recruited from school and campus networks, paid instantly.
  - Test types: hook A/B ("keep watching or swipe, and why"), name and logo, paywall copy, onboarding walkthrough, a "cringe score".
  - An AI synthesis step produces the one-pager.
  - He publishes a monthly "Gen Z Pulse" poll for press and inbound.
  - Later, aggregated Gen Z preference labels are sold to creative-AI vendors and to AI interview platforms that need participants.
- **Why now:**
  - Funded AI interviewing platforms prove buyers pay for fast customer voice: Listen Labs has raised about $100M and is in talks at a $1.5B valuation ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/listen-labs-69-million-series-b/), [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/143721-listen-labs-targets-125m-series-c-at-1-5b-valuation/)); Outset raised a $17M Series A ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/outset-17-million-series-a-raised-for-helping-enterprises-scale)).
  - Both aim at enterprise.
  - Both need participants.
- **Founder edge:** He is the demographic and his network is the panel. "18-year-old runs a Gen Z poll" is a story journalists use. He can prove accuracy by testing hooks that later get real ad data.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Build in about 10 days.
  2. Offer 20 founders from Product Hunt, TrustMRR and r/SideProject a free test: "I'll test your hook on 100 Gen Zers; tell me if it predicted your ad results."
  3. Turn the 5 best into a public case study.
  4. Sell the first paid tests at $149.
- **Cost to first revenue:** About $1,500 ($150 of payouts per 100-person test, plus about $450 of ID checks at $1.50 each).
- **Path to big:** RevenueCat's report covers about 115,000 subscription apps. If 10% buy at $300 a month, that is 11,500 teams, or about $41M a year, before DTC brands and creators. The upside route is supplying panels to the AI interview platforms and selling preference data to AI media vendors.
- **Who does it today:**
  - Survey panels: Pollfish, Attest, Prolific.
  - Message testing: Wynter.
  - Interview recruiting: UserTesting, Respondent, User Interviews.
  - Youth polling: [Generation Lab](https://generationlab.org/about), which works with Axios and has launched a product paying Gen Z $50+ a month for phone data ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/20/gen-z-data-privacy-generation-lab-verb-ai)).
  - AI interviewing: Listen Labs, Outset.
  - TikTok and Meta's own in-platform tests.
  - This is the most crowded idea in the set on its adjacent edges. The only wedge is Gen Z-only, fast, SMB self-serve.
- **What kills it:** Founders who just run $50 of TikTok ads and learn more. Panel quality drift. Weak repeat purchase.

## 4. Retainer Rail (flat-retainer creators with performance attribution, built for subscription apps)

**One-liner:** The operating system for the Cal AI recipe: hundreds of mid and micro creators on monthly retainers, tracked by promo codes tied to trials and subscribers, paid and compliance-checked automatically.

- **Customer:** Subscription-app founders and growth leads, roughly $5k-100k MRR apps.
- **Who pays:** The app. $500-1,500 a month software fee, plus creator retainers passed through at +20%.
- **Problem:** Cal AI put about 250 mid and micro creators on monthly retainers, spent mid six figures in a month, bootstrapped past $30M in annual revenue, and was acquired by MyFitnessPal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens), [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/calai.app/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)). Existing UGC marketplaces sell one-off videos: Billo from $99 a video, Insense from $500 a month plus fees, Collabstr about 10% from brands plus 15% from creators (competitor-reported) ([Youdji](https://youdji.com/en/blog/influee-competitors)). None are built around retainers, promo-code attribution and compliance for apps.
- **Solution:**
  - A creator marketplace (5k-100k followers, 18+).
  - Retainer contracts ($150-800 a month for 8 posts).
  - A brief generator.
  - Post verification by link monitoring.
  - App Store or Stripe promo codes attributed through RevenueCat or Adapty webhooks.
  - Bonuses per trial start.
  - Automatic payouts and 1099s.
  - FTC-disclosure checks.
  - Niche packs (fitness, study, gaming, productivity).
- **Why now:** Launch volume has exploded, AI apps earn about 41% more per payer, and hard paywalls convert far better than freemium (10.7% vs 2.1% download-to-paid by day 35; [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)). Everyone wants Cal AI's growth and almost nobody can run it.
- **Founder edge:** He is the same age as Cal AI's founders and can recruit creators from his own world. The founder story is press-friendly.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Seed 30 creators by DM.
  2. Pitch 20 app founders on X and Reddit: "10 creators, 30 days, $2,500 pass-through plus a $500 fee, with promo-code attribution."
  3. Run the first pilots by spreadsheet before building the software.
  Expect 3 customers in 45 days.
- **Cost to first revenue:** About $3,000, mostly float on creator retainers.
- **Path to big:** 300 apps spending an average of $15k a month through the rail is $54M a year of GMV. At a 20% take, that is $10.8M. Cal AI alone spent mid six figures in a single month.
- **Who does it today:**
  - UGC and influencer marketplaces: Billo, Insense, Collabstr, JoinBrands, soona, Influee ([Wisernotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/ugc-platforms/)).
  - Clipping marketplaces: Whop Content Rewards (about $0.50-1.50 per 1,000 views, $1,000 minimum campaign) and Vyro ([Opus Pro](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards)).
  - Creator-tracking tools (Shortimize, Virlo, from memory).
  - Cal AI running it in-house.
  - Crowded. The wedge is retainer operations plus attribution, for subscription apps only.
- **What kills it:** Creators who prefer one-off fees. FTC disclosure exposure. Apps that churn out of the program once they plateau. Whop or Insense adding retainers.

## 5. Collector Desk (profit and procurement OS for live sellers and card dealers)

**One-liner:** Upload your Whatnot, eBay and TikTok payout exports and get per-show and per-item P&L, "what to buy for the next show," grading-ROI calculations and a tax-ready export.

- **Customer:** Full-time and serious part-time sellers of cards, Pokemon, sports cards, sneakers and vintage, plus card shops.
- **Who pays:** The seller. $39-79 a month.
- **Problem:** Whatnot's fees stack on top of shipping, supplies, grading and losses. Sacra estimates 8% commission plus 2.9% + $0.30 (2024 figures, treat as dated; [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whatnot)). Existing tools cover inventory sync (TCGSync, CSV-based) and extension analytics (Resellbot's Whatnot Spy), but not the buy-side: per-show profit, return on inventory, grading expected value. One industry guide argues profitability depends on buying discipline, not sales velocity ([Sacra chat](https://sacra.com/chat/h/68577c91-82ba-4359-aa1d-b54c64204b63)).
- **Solution:**
  - Ingest payout CSVs, shipping labels and receipts (photo to ledger).
  - Per-show and per-item P&L.
  - Sell-through-based buying recommendations.
  - PSA and BGS grading expected-value calculator.
  - Sales-tax and 1099-K export.
  - Later, partner-financed inventory advances.
- **Why now:** Whatnot passed all of 2025's volume within six months of 2026 and raised a reported $545M Series G in August 2026. Hobbyist-built tools can win: Collectr bootstrapped a card app to eight-figure revenue and 4M users ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/how-collectr-bootstrapped-a-trading-card-hobby-into-an-eight-figure-business/)).
- **Founder edge:** Gen Z collector and reseller fluency, and fast building. It is the weakest "being 18" edge in the set. Its strengths are speed to revenue and a product that speaks for itself.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Post in r/whatnot, r/PokemonTCG and seller Discords: "Send me your last 3 payout CSVs and I'll send back a show-by-show P&L."
  2. Convert the best responders at $39 a month.
  3. Recruit 3 classmates who flip cards.
- **Cost to first revenue:** Under $800.
- **Path to big:** 15,000 paying sellers at about $55 a month is $9.9M a year, and I have no sourced seller count. The risk is that Whatnot builds this natively, since Series E money was earmarked for seller tools. Multi-platform coverage and financing are the hedge.
- **Who does it today:** TCGSync Storefront Pro ([TCGSync](https://tcgsync.com/integrations/whatnot)), Resellbot's Whatnot Spy ([Chrome store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/cmldgpnfifckadehdgjfjdcbdcapamaf)), Apify scrapers, Whatnot's own analytics, Collectr (collector portfolios, not seller P&L).
- **What kills it:** Whatnot shipping a native P&L. API or terms changes. Sellers who just use a spreadsheet.

## 6. Aid Ledger (a crowdsourced, verified database of what students actually got offered)

**One-liner:** A Levels.fyi-style database where admitted students upload anonymized financial aid letters, and families see what similar students were really offered by each college, plus an honest four-year cost view.

- **Customer:** Families of admitted students and the students themselves (18+ account holders). Later, college enrollment offices.
- **Who pays:** Families, $29-49 once, unlocking the comparison data. Later, colleges, via a subscription for anonymized competitive-offer intelligence.
- **Problem:** Aid letters are hard to compare and the funding picture just got worse. Parent PLUS borrowing is capped at $20,000 a year and $65,000 total per student from July 1, 2026, leaving gaps like $14,500 on an $85,000 cost of attendance ([The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/79823/new-parent-plus-limits-take-effect-july-1-what-families-need-to-plan-for-fall/)). Free comparison calculators exist ([Award Advisor](https://ohe.mn.gov/student-resources/other-resources/award-advisor), [Scholarships360](https://scholarships360.org/financial-aid/award-letter-comparison-tool/)), but a crowdsourced database of real offers did not turn up in my search.
- **Solution:**
  - Upload flow with fields stripped to school, aid type amounts, major, stats band and household income band.
  - A "true four-year cost" estimator.
  - Alerts on policy traps (outside scholarships that reduce institutional aid, renewal conditions).
  - Honest appeal odds. Do not sell appeal letters as a result-promising product.
  - Aggregate by school and profile.
- **Why now:** The Parent PLUS caps. Colleges are anxious about yield: recruiting one first-year student can cost thousands, and purchased leads run about $140-200 each ([ActiveProspect](https://activeprospect.com/blog/education-lead-aggregators), [Scoir](https://www.scoir.com/hubfs/College%20Solutions/Cost%20of%20Traditional%20Recruiting%20Infographic.pdf)). Colleges already pay vendors such as RNL for discount-rate optimization ([RNL](https://www.ruffalonl.com/optimizing-yield/advanced-financial-aid-management-leveraging-solutions/)).
- **Founder edge:** He is an applicant now, and his friends are posting results in Discords and Reddit. Peers trust a peer to look at their numbers.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. In December, collect 200+ ED and EA aid letters from classmates, siblings and r/ApplyingToCollege posters.
  2. Sell the comparison at $29 to 10 ED and EA families in mid-December through January.
  3. Use the real data for a TikTok and Reddit launch before Decision Day.
- **Cost to first revenue:** About $600.
- **Path to big:** Roughly 3M first-year students a year (my estimate, not sourced). A 5% share at $39 is about $5.9M. The bigger prize is a data subscription at 300 colleges and $30k each, about $9M. Together that is $10-15M but needs scale and cold enterprise sales.
- **Who does it today:** Edmit (2017, comparison tool that floated a 10-20% savings fee; [EdSurge](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2017-11-21-modeled-on-zillow-edmit-wants-to-help-families-make-financially-savvier-college-decisions)), Award Advisor (free), Discover's comparison tool, Scholarships360 (free), paid consultants at $850-4,000 ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/features/do-you-need-a-financial-aid-consultant)), RNL and EAB for colleges.
- **What kills it:** Families will not pay for what free tools and the school aid office provide. Appeals succeed rarely (one consultancy blog citing a 2020 survey said about 6%), so do not pitch appeals as the value. Cold-start data volume. Privacy handling of sensitive financial data. No lender referrals, which would pull in regulated lead-gen.

## 7. Intern Roof (verified-offer-letter intern and short-term sublet marketplace)

**One-liner:** A safe place for summer interns to sublet. Both sides are verified (.edu plus offer letter or lease permission), deposits sit in escrow until check-in, and sublease templates are state-checked.

- **Customer:** Summer interns (18+) in about 10 big intern cities, and students subletting their leases.
- **Who pays:** Charge on completed booking only. Subletter pays about 5% of rent, intern pays a $39-49 protection fee. About $150-300 per booking.
- **Problem:** Sublet fraud is routine. University of Illinois police have documented fake sublets via Reddit and Craigslist with $1,100-1,200 deposits taken by CashApp or wire ([UIUC Police](https://police.illinois.edu/fake-apartment-sublet/)). The default is Facebook groups. NACE projects employers will hire about 3.9% more interns this year, with 81% increasing or holding steady ([NACE](https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/employers-plan-to-expand-summer-intern-hiring-this-year)).
- **Solution:**
  - Offer-letter and .edu verification.
  - Landlord-permission upload.
  - Escrow of deposit and first month's rent until check-in.
  - State-specific sublease templates reviewed by a lawyer.
  - Intern-company roommate matching (with fair-housing-safe filters).
- **Why now:** Persistent scams, rising AI-generated fake listings, steady intern hiring. Most prior attempts were small or dead.
- **Founder edge:** Peer trust and Reddit and Discord fluency. The weak spot is that a high school senior has no college or intern network yet, so the first supply comes from the 2024-26 alumni of his school.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Seed 2-3 university subleasing groups through older siblings and friends: post verified sublets, free verification for the first 50.
  2. Take the first fee from February to April 2027, when searching peaks.
  This is realistically 90-150 days, not 60.
- **Cost to first revenue:** About $3,500 including attorney review.
- **Legal premise to check before launch:** California's Business and Professions Code section 10167 licenses "prepaid rental listing services" where tenants must pay an advance fee to obtain listings ([statute](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_10167)). Design the product as free to browse, charging only on a completed booking. Other states may differ. Escrow can trigger money-transmitter rules, so use Stripe Connect or a licensed escrow partner. Get a real-estate attorney.
- **Path to big:** NACE does not publish a headcount, so the sizing is my estimate. Assume 1-2M US interns, 30% needing housing, 10% booking through the platform: 30-60k bookings at about $300 is $9-18M. Then it extends to co-ops, study abroad and grad students.
- **Who does it today:** Small and fragmented: Roomsie ([PoPville](https://www.popville.com/2019/04/roomsie-affordable-convenient-housing-for-summer-interns-and-students/)), Ohana (employer partnerships; [Santa Clara](https://thesantaclara.org/blog/ohana-aims-to-ease-summer-subleasing)), Capstay ([ARLnow](https://www.arlnow.com/2017/05/01/startup-offers-intern-housing-solutions-in-crystal-city/)), Homey, Dormsy, the Wharton Crashpad project ([Wharton](https://fisher.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CrashPad-Final-report.pdf)), SubLite, plus Facebook groups and Reddit. No dominant player surfaced.
- **What kills it:** Two-sided cold start. Licensing. A single high-profile scam on the platform. Seasonality (Feb-May).

## 8. Vibe Arena (a game that produces Gen Z preference data for AI media models) - the moonshot

**One-liner:** A free game ("Real or AI?", "Would you post this?", "Pick the better clip") where verified 18+ players earn cash-outs, and every tap becomes demographic-tagged preference data sold to AI image, video and music companies. A public Gen Z leaderboard of AI models doubles as press.

- **Customer:** Small and mid gen-AI media startups, AI-UGC ad tools, evaluation teams.
- **Who pays:** The head of evals or the founder. $2-20k pilots, or about $0.01-0.05 per labeled comparison with demographic filters.
- **Problem:** AI media models need to know what looks real or cringe to 18-24s. The big human-data players use expert contractors:
  - Mercor reportedly passed a $2B gross run-rate ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/137121-mercor-doubles-to-2b-gross-revenue-run-rate-as-ai-labs-buy-expert-data/)).
  - Handshake's AI-training business neared $1B ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/127345-handshakes-arr-crosses-1b-as-ai-training-revenue-surges/)).
  - Contractor costs run 60-70% of gross for these firms.
  - LMArena has launched a paid evaluations service ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/state-of-evals-lmarenas-17b-vision)).
  - Rapidata raised an $8.5M seed for micro-task preference data ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/rapidata-8-5-million-closed-for-scaling-global-human-feedback-network)).
  - A Gen Z-native, game-wrapped, demographically tagged stream is my read of the gap, not something I verified.
- **Solution:**
  - A mobile and web game with streaks, leaderboards and cash-out.
  - 18+ verification and explicit data-use consent.
  - Consented demographic tags (gamer, sports fan, creator).
  - Datasets and a live "Arena" evaluation API.
  - A public leaderboard that ranks the major video and image models by Gen Z preference.
- **Why now:** AI media flood, commercialization of crowd evaluation, and funding for human feedback networks.
- **Founder edge:** Game design and accounts-plus-economy experience from House Money, distribution to Gen Z gamers and sports fans, and fast building. A leaderboard is a story.
- **First 10 customers:**
  1. Build a minimal "Real or AI?" game.
  2. Collect about 20,000 votes from classmates, Discords and Reddit in 3 weeks.
  3. Publish a free Gen Z preference leaderboard for 6 video models.
  4. Email 50 AI media startups with a sample dataset.
  5. Aim for 1-3 pilots at $2,500. This is slower and less certain than ideas 1-7.
- **Cost to first revenue:** About $3,500 (stimulus generation credits, rater payouts, hosting).
- **Path to big:** The human-data market is already measured in billions: Mercor and Handshake are each at $1B-plus run-rate. A $20M slice of preference and evaluation spend is a plausible target. Margins are thin unless payouts stay small, and the buyers' trust has to be earned.
- **Who does it today:** Rapidata, LMArena, Artificial Analysis, Prolific, Mercor, Handshake AI, plus Surge and Scale (from memory).
- **What kills it:** Labs buying only from established vendors. Rater fraud. Privacy and content rules (no NSFW stimuli, 18+ only, honest consent).

## Ideas I looked at and killed

- **Teen AI-safety evaluation.** The topic is hot (Common Sense rated ChatGPT for Teens "Unacceptable Risk" on Oct 7, 2026; [GPB](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/10/09/chatgpt-for-teens-has-special-safeguards-watchdog-group-finds-most-dont-work)), but incumbents include Alice, Thorn, Common Sense and Vals ([Vals](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/teen-safety)), and good testing involves minors and harmful content.
- **Clipping marketplace.** Crowded. Whop and Vyro pay about $0.50-1.50 per 1,000 views and the supply side is saturated.
- **Greek-life dues and merch software.** OmegaFi, myGreek (3% + 30 cents, free to chapters), PayDues are incumbents ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/mygreek)), and GreekLink is out of business.
- **Campus newsletter network.** The Tab sold for GBP 750,000 and Her Campus took 400+ chapters to a reported $15-20M of revenue. Slow, ops-heavy, and he is not on a campus yet.
- **Unibuddy-style ambassador chat for colleges.** Unibuddy has 600+ university partners and quote-only enterprise sales; an 18-year-old cannot sell it cold.
- **Student-discount verification rail.** UNiDAYS and Student Beans own it.
- **Sports-betting tools and free-to-play pick'em.** Fails the guardrails or the "not gambling-adjacent" spirit.
- **AI essay coach, AI tutor.** On the clichés list, and drifts toward academic dishonesty.

# F07 refined: Hard-Hat Stays / Per Diem Keeper

Written 2026-10-25 after two critic passes. Verdict: **DEAD as a flagship (alive = false).** Re-verified this pass: Texas 22 TAC 535.4(m)-(o) and 535.5 (Cornell copy, read through a fetch summary, so currency unverified), the Abilene Furnished Finder crew listings, and the Elmdale and Frontier lease-up status. Nothing I found rescues the idea; two things made the critics' case stronger.

## 1. Verdict in five lines

1. The side with the problem (workers) will not pay, and the side that would pay (operators) mostly has no problem or is locked into contractor deals.
2. The only fee that earns real money looks like unlicensed brokerage in Texas. The fee that avoids that is the $199-a-year commodity Furnished Finder already sells.
3. The only route to $10M+ runs through contractor-paid group booking, which needs float, insurance and enterprise credibility Asher does not have.
4. The demand shock is real (documented in 2026), which is why this was worth checking, but real demand is not the same as a buyer he can serve.
5. Narrowing it until it is legal and sells makes it a side hustle. The brief says call that dead.

## 2. Answers to the critics

### Fatal critic

| # | Objection | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Payer does not have the problem (scarce beds) | **Concede.** Owners with beds do not buy customer acquisition. Iowa campgrounds are about 100% full with a waitlist of about 20, and a Tye, TX park reportedly filled in 13 days (seller brochure). A lead engine sells to people who already have more demand than beds. |
| 2 | Non-full operators are contractor-locked | **Concede, and it is the structural point the dossier missed.** Dunn Village had 30 RV spots and 90 mobile-home residents against capacity of up to 500 in mid-Feb 2026, paid by a Turner per diem. Elmdale's roughly 1,000-unit park is "approved tenants only". Target Hospitality's new Hyper/Scale unit has signed three data-center projects worth $331M over two years. The empty beds are sold through the channels he cannot enter. |
| 3 | Price anchor is zero | **Concede.** Furnished Finder is $199 a year with no commissions. I re-confirmed six Abilene listings (949831, 972596, 972652, 977622, 968203, 1008704) worded for data-center crews and work teams. The Dyrt markets commission-free bookings. Facebook and roadside signs cost nothing. A $500-1,500 listing is 2.5-7.5 times the anchor. |
| 4 | A target customer already built a competitor | **Partly contest.** StayNELA is a small site with about 7-10 listings and is not a real threat. But it is evidence that operators route around third parties, so I count it against the idea, not for it. |
| 5 | Fee leaks and cannot be audited | **Concede.** A confirmed-move-in fee depends on a full operator admitting the move-in came from him, and renewals go direct after month one. This pushes him to a flat fee, which runs into objection 3. |
| 6 | Legal structure squeezes him to the weakest price point | **Concede, and I re-checked the rule text.** 535.4(m) requires a license for a fee to assist someone in locating property to rent, including a service that finds apartments or homes. (n) covers a rental-vacancy compilation fee that is contingent on rental (an advance fee that must be refunded counts as contingent). (o) covers compensation for advertising for others, accepting the inquiries and referring them to the owner. 535.5 has no publisher exemption; the listing-service carve-out is for trade associations and similar organizations. Whether RV pads and motel stays count as "lease" is untested, which is a gap, not a clearance. |
| 7 | Worker-side precedent is a graveyard | **Concede.** Worker Homes pitched "keep more of your per diem" in 2019 and is still a one-employee "in development" profile. WorkBnB's founder said workers "don't book where they stay" and the site is now six invite-only properties. The money (LodgeLink: $43.5M of bookings in Q2 2026 at a 12.4% net take) is on the contractor side. |
| 8 | Timing: Abilene crested | **Concede for Abilene, contest for Hyperion.** Crusoe topped out the final Abilene building and Oracle/OpenAI dropped a 600MW expansion (Oracle disputes the framing). Hyperion has a longer runway (Meta peak above 7,500 workers) but a moratorium on new RV parks, which lowers operators' need for leads, and its supply is informal landowner camps. He would arrive in Q1 2027 at the earliest. The 30-50% cancellation statistic traces to a consulting blog and I give it no weight. |
| 9 | Google as sole traffic channel | **Contest the severity, concede the point.** Programmatic "keyword in city" templates are exposed, and the unique data a page needs (rent, parking, commute for RV pads) is data nobody publishes. But paid search and a few hand-built town pages avoid the penalty, so this alone would not kill it. |
| 10 | No moat, ceiling single-digit millions | **Concede.** GSA rates are public, Furnished Finder could add a crew filter quickly, and LodgeLink already issues subcontractor access codes. See the arithmetic in section 4. |

### Serious critic

- **Licensing (535.4(n) and (o)), including the flat-fee fallback:** Conceded, same evidence as row 6. His own 4-week test (ads, then qualified texts or calls to him, then forwarding to operators) is the (o) fact pattern as soon as any operator pays. The only safe form is a pure directory with contact details straight to the owner, which is Furnished Finder.
- **Customers full, commission-averse, window closing:** Conceded. $50-100 on a $550-1,000 month is 5-13%, which is the band operators already resist.
- **Hyperion supply quality and the "verified" claim:** Conceded. He cannot inspect anything from a school desk, so a "verified" badge is a misrepresentation risk for a teenager with no insurance.
- **Roommates.com / Section 230:** Contest the severity. It only bites if he writes housemate-preference questionnaires, so dropping roommate matching removes it. I would drop it.
- **Texas SB 140 text-message registration:** Unverified on his facts and a pending challenge. Moot if he sends no marketing texts, so avoid them.
- **Traveler distribution unproven:** Conceded. No advertiser program on Energy Job Shop, TradesmenUp or TradeGig, and no trades-housing Facebook group found. The 25% self-arranging figure is an assumption.
- **Founder fit and bandwidth:** Conceded. The freight-audit service is built and unsold because his bottleneck is cold outreach and closing, not building. This adds a third thing that depends on the same missing skill.

## 3. Variants I tried to make survive

| Variant | Who pays | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Flat-fee verified directory | Operators | $199 anchor, free alternatives, full operators; the safe legal form is the commodity |
| Pay per confirmed 30-day move-in | Operators | Likely needs a Texas license; unauditable; leaks after month one |
| "Per Diem Keeper" worker app | Workers | Price hunters who pocket the difference; precedent is a dead one-person startup; edges into tax advice |
| Lease-up service for new park developers | Developers | Their units are sold to contractors and hyperscaler staff; they have their own channels; same license question |
| Contractor crew desk (LodgeLink model) | GCs, subs, staffing firms | The only big pool, and it fails founder fit: float, insurance, enterprise credibility, 24/7 support |
| Market-intel or data product on build towns | Park developers, lenders | A few hundred buyers who will not buy from an 18-year-old; boutique |

No row is both defensible for Asher and big. That is the finding.

## 4. Ceiling arithmetic (mine, assumptions stated)

- **Directory:** 40 towns x 45 operators x 20% penetration = 360 paying listings. At $199 that is about $72K a year; at $500-1,500 (which the anchor does not support) it is $0.18-0.54M.
- **Contractor desk:** LodgeLink's Q2 2026 net revenue of $5.4M on $43.5M of bookings is a 12.4% take. $10M of net revenue needs about $80M a year of bookings, which at $100-155 per bed-night is roughly 1,400-2,200 beds continuously booked. Target Hospitality's $250M contract for about 1,100 people implies about $155 per bed-night (dossier estimate, includes services). That is a balance-sheet and insurance business.

## 5. What I would do instead

Default: do not spend school-year hours on this. The unsold freight-audit service is closer to a first dollar than anything here.

If curiosity wins, run a **two-week, zero-dollar, phone-only gate** (about 10 hours, no ads, no texts, no money collected, no inquiry forwarding):

1. Days 1-2: build a call sheet of 40 operators and 10 contractor-side contacts. The 10 named contacts are in the structured output.
2. Days 3-14: ask three questions of operators. "What was your occupancy over the last 30 days, and how did your last vacancy get filled?" "Furnished Finder is $199 a year and Facebook is free; why would you pay me anything?" "Who decides where the crew sleeps at your biggest tenants?"
3. Days 8-14: five calls to contractor-side people (a sub's office manager, staffing recruiters, Hard Hat Housing, LodgeLink supplier team). Ask who books housing, what the budget line is, and whether a small sub would use a self-serve tool.
4. Day 14 scorecard. **Pass only if all hold:** at least 8 of 25 reached operators report at least 10% vacancy that is not contractor-locked; at least 3 say they would prepay at least $300 for something Furnished Finder does not give them; at least 2 contractor-side contacts name a budget line a small sub controls.
5. If it passes, spend $300-600 on a one-hour Texas real-estate attorney consult on 535.4(m)-(o) before anything is sold. If it fails, stop and write down the answers.

## 6. Carry-forward seeds (untested, not rescues of F07)

- **Lodging-spend audit for trades subcontractors**, the sister of the freight audit he already built: flat diagnostic plus contingency on savings between what a sub pays for crew lodging and what comparable 30+ day supply costs. It has the same credibility problem with enterprise buyers, and I have not checked whether any sub has a lodging line to audit. Screen it before believing it.
- **Call-sheet answers from the gate** are free market intelligence for any other idea that touches data-center construction labor.

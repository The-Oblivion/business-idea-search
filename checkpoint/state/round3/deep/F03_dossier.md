# F03 Dossier: Closed-Loop Paper (weekly personalized math packets, no screen for the child)

Researched late October 2026. Evidence tags: **[P]** primary or official page, **[S]** secondary (blog, aggregator, vendor marketing), **[U]** unverified or could not open. "My arithmetic" means I computed it from cited inputs.

## 1. Bottom line

No hard wall: no licence, no age problem, first dollar plausible in about 5 weeks for under $1k. But the evidence does not yet support a venture-scale business, and three things pull it toward "weak":

1. **The price is out of band for consumer education software.** Education apps have a median monthly price of $9.99 and a median yearly price of $44.99 [P: [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)]. $29/month ($348/year, my arithmetic) is about 3x the monthly median and about 27% of the $1,295 that HSLDA's 2024 survey of 4,000+ families found elementary homeschoolers spend per child per year on everything [S: [Good and Beautiful summary](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/blogs/education/how-much-does-homeschooling-cost) of [HSLDA](https://hslda.org/post/the-costs-of-homeschooling-today); the HSLDA page itself hid the dollar figures behind a login].
2. **The pieces are already cheap or free, and a new entrant shipped nearly the same feature set this fall** (MathSprout, below).
3. **Distribution to 30-45-year-old parents is the hard part**, and nothing found suggests his Gen Z edge helps there.

The upside is real but slow: ESA money (Section 5) is the only structural reason the $29-60 price might clear. It is a month-6+ lever, not a launch plan.

## 2. Competitors and free substitutes

**Closest to the whole loop (photo/scan of work, diagnosis, next practice):**
- **MathSprout** (SEDX LLC, iPhone/iPad): generates printable worksheets K-12, parent photographs the finished sheet, per-problem grading plus "skills that need more practice." Free to generate and print; photo grading is 100 gradings for $4.99; the parent must supply their own Claude API key. No ratings yet; listing shows v1.0 on Sep 1 and v1.0.1 on Sep 27 (no year shown, but the app ID suggests 2026) [P: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6787323908)]. This is the clearest evidence that the core loop is a weekend project. It does not show a weekly adaptive packet or a skill graph.
- **AI Math Coach**: uploads a photo of classroom problems and turns it into practice sheets, tailors to the child's pace, saves attempts for parents; free trial then from $9.97/month [S: [listing](https://thedailyworkflow.com/service/AI%20Math%20Coach)].
- **Extra Math** (grades 2-5, about EUR 0.99, photo to printable similar problems) [S: App Store summary via search; page returned 404 on direct fetch, so details are **[U]**: [link](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/extra-math/id6670766648)].
- **iWorksheetz** advertises upload of a textbook page or worksheet photo, AI grading and progress tracking, aimed at exam boards (CBSE, IB, AP) rather than US homeschool [S: [site](https://iworksheetz.app/)]. **GradeIt AI** (parent/teacher photo grader, no next-sheet logic found) [S: [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.mathcenter.gradeit&hl=en)]. **AfterBell** (photo to game, PreK-3, screen-based) [S: [site](https://afterbell.ai/)].
- **OpenEduCat**: error analysis of photographed work (procedural, conceptual or careless) plus a worksheet generator with answer keys and PDF export; teacher-facing, 15-day free trial, no prices posted [P: [page](https://openeducat.org/ai/tools/math-problem-solver/)].
- **Excellence Learning (YC W25)**: "US parents are our primary customers"; web and iPad; daily courses with detailed worksheet feedback ("super Kumon"); 4 people, San Francisco; pricing and funding not stated [P: [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/excellence-learning)]. Screen-based, so the paper positioning differentiates, but it is the best-placed funded rival for the same parent.
- **Frizzle** (YC): teacher-facing photo grading of handwritten math [P: [YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OFP-frizzle-ai-grading-for-math-teachers)]. **Chiron** (YC): iPad app reading handwriting live, 2 founders, London [P: [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/chiron)].
- **HomeschoolFox**: premium add-on with a printable daily skill sheet (6-12 problems, Math or Language Arts), grade-matched, nothing about adapting to performance or photo input; price not listed [P: [docs](https://homeschoolfox.com/docs/activities/worksheets)].

**Price anchors:**
- **Kumon**: one center's policy effective June 1, 2025 is $190/month for one subject, $375 for math plus reading, $80 registration, $30 materials [P: [center policy](https://form.jotform.com/221807970629060)]. Kumon Connect (tablet and stylus; paper fallback) rolled out March 2024 [S: [search summary](https://www.franchise.org/2024/03/kumon-connect-brings-even-more-personalization-to-learning/)]. Franchise Times lists 1,689 US units and $630M global sales [S: [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/top-400-2025/127-kumon-math-reading-centers/article_c04ed739-470f-4bb1-b84c-11290d56a21b.html)]. I found no current US enrollment; the last hard figure was 283,330 subject enrollments in 2017 [U: from a [2017 Kumon release](https://www.kumonfranchise.com/us-en/assets/pressrelease/release2017-0111.html) that I did not open].
- **Mathnasium**: about $150-500/month, $99-199 enrollment [S: [competitor blog, treat with care](https://www.myengineeringbuddy.com/blog/mathnasium-reviews-pricing-alternatives-guide/)].
- **Thinkster**: $60/$100/$130 per month for K-5 tiers per a review last edited in 2019 [S: [Cathy Duffy](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/math-supplements/math-supplements-for-all-grade-levels/thinkster-math)]; other 2026 sources say $53-64 entry [S: [search summary](https://www.homeschool.com/?p=149952)].
- **Math Academy** $49/month beta pricing, "regular" $79-99 [S: [site via search](https://www.mathacademy.com)]. **IXL** about $9.95/month or $79/year for one subject [S: [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-ixl-cost/)]. **Khanmigo** parent plan $4/month [P: [Khan support](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/nl/articles/25921448458893)], and it accepts photo uploads of handwritten work in Tutor Me [P: [Khan help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/46962842660749)]. **Monster Math** $59.99/year [P: [help page](https://help.monstermath.app/is-monster-math-a-free-app-cmclrj1y4004r2c4au79l06sz.md)].

**The real substitute is free.** A parent photographs the page, uploads it to Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, and asks for similar problems with an answer key; one 2026 parent guide walks through exactly this, and says Claude's artifacts print best [S: [AI Tools for Kids](https://aitoolsforkids.com/blog/ai-homework-practice-exercises)]. The same guide reports roughly 95% arithmetic accuracy in its own tests and tells parents to check every key. What the DIY route lacks is memory, a vetted item bank and a weekly habit, which is the only defensible product wedge.

## 3. Willingness to pay and the buyer

- Parents do pay for paper-based structure: Kumon at $175-190 per subject is the proof [P: [center policy](https://form.jotform.com/221807970629060)]. But the same buyers call it "prohibitively expensive ($200 a month)" and want Kumon's daily repetition without the center; commenters on one homeschool blog named no price they would pay for a replacement, and cited a $10 workbook [S: [Trivium Pursuit thread](https://triviumpursuit.com/replacement-for-kumon/)]. I found no direct evidence that parents would pay $29 for this specific loop. **Reddit was inaccessible** (the search tool blocks reddit.com and direct JSON calls returned nothing), so there is no r/homeschool evidence either way.
- Consumer education subscriptions retain modestly: median first-renewal 56% for monthly plans (interquartile 48-66%) and 24% for annual plans [P: [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category/)].
- Homeschool parents lean on blogs and reviews: one marketing source cites a survey where over 60% use homeschool blogs to choose curriculum (2021-22, secondary) [S: [iHomeschool Network](https://ihomeschoolnetwork.com/market-homeschool-audience/)].
- One 2023 poll found 44% of home-school educators had used ChatGPT [S: [KSL/Deseret](https://www.ksl.com/article/50723308/what-do-home-school-educators-think-about-chatgpt)], so the DIY substitute is already known to this audience.

## 4. Technical risk: reading children's work

- Multimodal models graded 288 handwritten arithmetic responses from Ghanaian middle schoolers at about 95% accuracy (kappa 0.90) but made mistakes humans would not; on 150 US elementary illustrations agreement dropped to kappa 0.20 [P: [Henkel et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05538)].
- Handwriting transcription is the repeated bottleneck: FERMAT found accuracy improves when handwriting is replaced with printed text [S: [ACL 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.acl-long.720)]; ScratchMath (1,720 samples of Chinese primary and middle school scratchwork) found 16 models well short of human experts on error explanation [S: [arXiv 2603.24961](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24961)].
- **No published benchmark covers grade 2-6 children's digits in answer boxes.** The sharpened design (system-made packets, page IDs, fixed boxes) is the right mitigation but untested. Cheap design rule: generate problems from code templates so every answer key is exact, and use the model only to read digits and write the parent note.

## 5. ESA and public money

- EdChoice's 2026 table lists 70 programs; I summed about 1.49M students, with 19 ESA and 4 tax-credit-ESA programs [P: [EdChoice](https://www.edchoice.org/2026-edchoice-participation-rankings/)]. Arizona ESA: 96,802 there, 100,208 on the state counter in January 2026 [S: [Daily Signal/state data via search](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/01/30/arizona-esa-growth-skyrockets-while-criticisms-crash/)].
- **Florida PEP is the strongest pool**: 78,617 funded students in 2025-26, average award $7,700-8,500, and in 2024-25 families spent $223.5M of $269.9M on "Curriculum / Course - Instructional Material" [P: [Step Up fact sheet](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/wp-content/uploads/26.5-PEP.pdf)]. Not everyone who applies is funded, and some parents say the funding has pushed curriculum prices up [S: [WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/education/2026-08-13/some-florida-homeschool-parents-are-denied-state-funding-but-why)]. Vendor entry runs through MyScholarShop: interest form, onboarding call, provider application, Ariba account, test orders, live catalog; fees not published [P: [Step Up vendors](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/schools-and-providers/vendors/)].
- **Arizona**: 14,793 registered vendors and 7,295 tutors as of October 2025; the department's main stated vetting is a high-school-diploma attestation for tutors [P: [Arizona Capitol Times](http://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2025/10/04/parents-left-to-fill-the-gap-in-esa-vendor-vetting/)]. The superintendent said he may seek fingerprinting for vendors who "engage personally with students" [P: [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/state/superintendent-tom-horne-may-seek-extra-requirement-from-arizonas-esa-vendors)]. A vendor-side guide says a parent can file a vendor-approval request in ClassWallet and most legitimate providers clear in a few weeks [S: [Prodigy guide](https://prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/arizona-esa-homeschool-2026-guide)]. A parent-pull route is the lowest-friction entry I found.
- **Texas TEFA** (new July 2026): homeschool families get only $2,000 a year, versus up to $10,474 for private school; about 73,000 accounts funded July 1 and about 2,400 vendors and 54,000 products listed, with no listing fees [P: [Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/about/media-center/news/20260630-nearly-73000-texas-education-freedom-accounts-to-receive-initial-funding-july-1-1782833580152)]. A 10% cap on hardware plus software (about $200 for homeschoolers) is reported, and no source says whether a PDF/software subscription counts [S: [Outschool](https://outschool.com/esa/approved-list), [search summary](https://outschool.com/esa-guides/tefa-approved-expenses)]. At $348 a year the product would eat 17% of a Texas homeschool award (my arithmetic).
- **Credential lane**: Tennessee requires an educator licence or accredited facility for tutoring and I found no instructional-materials exemption in its vendor rules [S: [TN ESA RFA](https://tn.gov/content/dam/tn/education/legal/solicitations/RFA_2024-2025_ESA_Service_Providers.pdf)]; Texas says anyone who teaches a child must meet credential rules [S: [EduBracket](https://edubracket.com/articles/how-to-become-an-esa-approved-vendor-2026)]; Utah wants an EIN, business licence and articles [same source]. A paper-packet product must stay classified as materials, not tutoring. I found no official Georgia or Missouri guidance.

## 6. Reaching the first 10-15 families

- **Family and local first.** The gate's list (relatives, co-ops, Kumon/Mathnasium parents) needs no platform permission and is the right pilot channel.
- **Affiliates**: HomeschoolFox pays 30% recurring for 12 months with a 60-day cookie, paid monthly via PayPal above $25 [P: [partners page](https://homeschoolfox.com/partners)]. Big curriculum brands pay far less (Sonlight and BookShark 6%, Calvert 4%) [S: [Lasso directory](https://getlasso.co/niche/homeschool/)]. At roughly 10% monthly churn, a 30% share costs about $87 per family over the year (my arithmetic).
- **Review ecosystem**: Homeschool Review Crew reviewers keep products and must post on a public blog, page or channel; vendor submission terms not found [S: [Old Schoolhouse](https://www.theoldschoolhouse.com/join-the-homeschool-review-crew/)].
- **Facebook groups** mostly restrict promotion to a pinned thread or admin approval [S: [general pattern, not homeschool-specific](https://www.eofire.com/what-no-self-promotion-means-facebook-group-guidelines/)]. The biggest US group I could size, Hip Homeschool Moms, had 36,544 members in September 2024 [S: [HHM](https://hiphomeschoolmoms.com/our-community-and-reach/)].
- **Conventions**: Great Homeschool Conventions inline booths were $600-750 for the 2025 season, new-exhibitor applications open each July [S: [GHC](https://greathomeschoolconventions.com/exhibitors)]; HEAV 2027 first booth $845-1,045 [S: [HEAV](https://heav.org/convention/exhibitor-information/)]. Too late and too costly for the first 90 days.
- **Microschools and pods** are a possible bulk buyer: about 75,000 microschools serving about 1.5M students by one nonprofit's estimate, median 20 students, 87% ages 5-11 [S: [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/what-you-need-to-know--about-microschools/827601/)]. Whether they buy per-child practice software is **[U]**.

## 7. Legal and policy

- The amended COPPA rule has been fully enforceable since April 22, 2026; it requires written data-retention policies, and photos of children remain personal information [S: [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1771468/enforcement-begins-soon-for-significant-coppa-rule-amendments)]. The FTC's FAQ says COPPA does not cover information adults upload that pertains to children, unless the service is directed to children [P: [FTC FAQ F.4](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)]; that FAQ predates the 2025 amendments, so confirm it is still current. Here the child never touches the product, which helps. Plan: parent-only accounts, no child names or faces, short retention, one hour of counsel. State children's-privacy laws were **not** researched.
- Screen-time bills in 14+ states and enacted K-5 limits in Iowa, Utah, Tennessee and Alabama [S: [Stateline summary](https://stateline.org/?p=17970)] are a marketing tailwind for "paper," but they regulate schools, not home purchases.

## 8. Unit economics (my estimates)

- Claude Sonnet 5.5 lists at $2 input and $10 output per million tokens [P: [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)]. Assuming about 6 scanned pages per week, 12k input and 5k output tokens, model cost is about $0.08 per child per week, roughly $0.35 a month. At $29 the margin is above 90% before payment fees, support and the hired reviewer.
- Not researched: print-and-mail cost for the $59 upgrade, payment-processor fees, sales tax on digital goods.
- Path to $10M: 30,000 families x $348 = about $10.4M (my arithmetic). Against about 3.4M US homeschoolers (NHERI estimate; NCES says 3.4% of students) [S: [search summary](https://aop.com/blogs/featured-articles/homeschool-statistics-in-the-us)], that is a low-single-digit share of homeschool families, but the sales channel is fragmented and the price is above norm.

## 9. Why now

For: paper backlash in schools; ESA expansion (Texas live July 2026, a federal scholarship tax credit starting January 1, 2027 with 31 states opted in per [Outschool](https://outschool.com/esa/approved-list), a vendor claim); models now read arithmetic handwriting at near-human accuracy. Against: those same models make the DIY and cheap-app versions trivial, and MathSprout appeared in the last two months.

## 10. Verdict and test design

**Weak to moderate; keep only as a cheap test.** The paper-first, closed-loop framing is differentiated from Excellence Learning, Thinkster and Math Academy, but not from a determined parent with a chatbot, and the price needs a story. Decisive test (about 4-6 weeks, under $1k): 10-15 paid families at two price cells ($19 vs $29), a DIY-ChatGPT control group, and a pre-registered kill rule: fewer than 5 pay, or half stop scanning by week 4, or weekly scan completion below 60%. Add one ESA probe: have one Arizona or Florida family request the product as a vendor and time the approval. Track digit-read accuracy on real child pages before building anything else.

## Gaps
No Reddit or Facebook thread evidence; no price tested with real parents; Excellence Learning and Extra Math details unverified; no kid-handwriting benchmark; TEFA software-cap treatment unknown; print-and-mail costs, state privacy law and Review Crew vendor terms not found.

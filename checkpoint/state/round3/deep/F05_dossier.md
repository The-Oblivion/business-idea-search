# F05 Dossier: Credit Concierge (federal scholarship tax credit, IRC 25F)

Researched 2026-10-25. Confirmed facts carry a link. "Inference" marks my own reasoning. "Not verified" marks claims I could not source.

## 1. Bottom line

The credit is real and the policy shock is real. The gate's "weak" verdict is, if anything, too kind to the pitch as written. Three findings drive that:

1. The incumbents are already doing each revenue leg: ACE, FACTS/Nelnet, CSF, Workplace Scholarships and AFC are active in the SGO, advisor and employer/W-4 legs (sections 3 and 4).
2. The best price anchor I found is ACE paying advisors 3% (about $51 on a $1,700 gift). That is far below the cost of buying donors with ads (section 5).
3. Credible take-up estimates are 4 to 17 times smaller than the headline "$26B a year" (section 2).

My read: a good free calculator is a feasible weekend-to-six-week build, but it is a commodity. As a business it is a lead-gen affiliate play with a low ceiling and a legal question mark. It does not reach $10M a year by any bottom-up path I can source.

## 2. Market size: the headline number is the high case

- Treasury projects, by 2030, 600 to 700 SGOs, more than 11M taxpayers and nearly $26B a year in contributions. Thirty states had opted in ([Treasury press release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0641)).
- The Urban Institute expects, after "a ramp-up period of a few years", 2.7 to 3.6M filers and $2.7 to $6.1B a year. It says this is highly dependent on state-level take-up ([Urban](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/what-comes-next-federal-tax-credit-scholarships)).
- Brookings applied Ohio's actual claim rates nationally and got about $1.5B a year. Ohio's credit drew about 65,000 claimants and about $75M of credits across 2021-2024. Even filers earning over $500k claim at only about 3.5% ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/who-is-likely-to-benefit-from-the-federal-scholarship-tax-credit/)).
- The "121.5M eligible taxpayers" figure comes from the pro-credit AFC Growth Fund, and it counts eligibility, not likely donors. The same report says every 5% of participation adds $9.4B ([news summary](https://www.midfloridanewspapers.com/lakeland_sun/news/new-report-estimates-121-5-million-taxpayers-may-be-eligible-to-claim-education-freedom-tax/article_cb74bfea-d2c3-49ed-a5fb-4bdde1166c0a.html)).
- The pitch's "46.5M taxpayers qualify for both a state and the federal credit" is not in Treasury's press release, and I found no source for it. Not verified.

Inference (bottom-up path to $10M a year as a referral business):
- At ACE's 3% rate, $10M needs about 196,000 donors at the maximum gift.
- That is 1.8% of Treasury's 11M, but 5% to 7% of Urban's 3M steady state.
- It is also about 22% of the entire national 3% fee pool in the Brookings case ($1.5B x 3% = $45M).
- The pool is shared with CPAs, advisors and the SGOs' own funnels, and the first big gift year (2027) will be below steady state.

## 4. The "net-cost engine" is thin

The ordering rule is a one-line formula. Proposed 1.25F-2(c)(1): allowed credit = lesser of (qualified contributions minus state credits) or $1,700. Married filers are treated as two separate taxpayers ([technical analysis](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/10/1/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations-comprehensive-technical-analysis-of-temporary-td-10057-and-proposed-reg-117199-25-under-section-25f)). Other inputs are a five-year carryforward, a nonrefundable cap tied to liability, and exclusion of partnership and S-corporation pass-through gifts ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/treasury-department-guidance-clarifies-the-education)). The sharpened pitch's claim that the ordering rule decides "$1,700 or $3,400 of added benefit" overstates it. State credits reduce the federal credit; they do not simply stack. Stacking only happens when the gift exceeds what the state credit covers.

The real moat is a maintained table of about 20 states' own SGO-credit rules. That is data upkeep, not a defensible engine.

The W-4/withholding mechanic is unconfirmed in the Treasury text I read. The regulations treat an after-tax payroll deduction as "cash" ([Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20264.pdf)), but the summaries I found say nothing on withholding. Workplace Scholarships asserts the W-4 route; I could not confirm IRS support.

## 5. Payer, willingness to pay and unit economics

**Who signs.** At ACE the chief marketing officer (Kristy Badger is quoted); at small SGOs the executive director or board.

**Budget line.** SGOs may spend at most 10% of each $1,700 gift on all administration. Education Next reports a lean SGO still faces "several hundred thousand dollars" of operating cost, so it needs to raise $1M per $100k of admin. It also says recruiting donors is what SGO representatives call the most challenging step ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/ednext_26_4_feature_mcshane.pdf)). Fundraising counts as scholarship-granting activity under the 85% safe harbor ([technical analysis](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/10/1/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations-comprehensive-technical-analysis-of-temporary-td-10057-and-proposed-reg-117199-25-under-section-25f)), so the spend is allowed, but budgets are tight.

**Price evidence.**
- ACE pays advisors 3%, about $51 on a $1,700 gift and about $102 on a $3,400 couple.
- Donor-acquisition benchmarks: M+R's 2021-data figures put the cost per donation at about $46 (search ads), $86 (social ads) and $178 (display ads), as relayed in a vendor roundup, [Ortto](https://ortto.com/learn/the-hidden-cost-of-donor-acquisition-why-nonprofits-waste-60-of-their-fundraising-budget/) (I did not open M+R's report; Ortto's own estimate is $50 to $150). Inference: paid social cannot pay back at a $51 payout, so only organic or partner traffic works.
- No published SGO price for a sponsorship slot. Gap.

**Flat sponsorship math (inference).** A small SGO raising $1M has about $100k total admin. A $500 a month sponsorship is $6k a year, or 6% of its budget. Reaching the gate's $1.5M median revenue at $500 a month needs about 250 paying SGOs, or roughly 40% of Treasury's 2030 count of 600 to 700. And those SGOs have free ACE and CSF alternatives and no gift income until 2027.

**Timing.** Gifts cannot start until Jan 1, 2027. Donors claim on the 2027 return filed in 2028 ([NATP via search summary](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-gains-state-participation/); page not fetched), and SGOs send donor numbers by Jan 31 and report to the IRS by Feb 28 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/treasury-department-guidance-clarifies-the-education)). State SGO lists are due Feb 15, 2027. A first paying customer inside 90 days of build is unlikely, and the gate's 20 weeks looks optimistic for anything beyond a free pilot.

## 6. Where first customers gather

- **The coalition:** the 50-state FSTC Coalition, whose core members are Agudath Israel, American Federation for Children, Defense of Freedom Institute, EdChoice and the U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops ([coalition letter](https://agudah.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FSTC-Coalition-Treasury-Comments-Dec-24.pdf); membership per search summary).
- **New SGOs:** the four Iowa Catholic dioceses' new statewide SGO and the separate Iowa SGO ([Diocese of Fort Wayne-South Bend repost](https://diocesefwsb.org/news/iowa-dioceses-create-scholarship-granting-organization-for-federal-tax-credit-program)), ACSI's networks, Future School Fund (public-school SGO, Deborah Gist; [FutureEd Q&A](https://www.future-ed.org/qa-deborah-gist-on-federal-tax-credit-scholarships-and-public-schools/)).
- **Reality check:** Education Next says national SGOs with broad donor bases hold the advantage, and the buyer pool is a closed, partisan network. The credit is also contested. Senate Democrats introduced a repeal bill ([Ballotpedia headline](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/04/24/senate-democrats-introduce-bill-to-repeal-federal-education-tax-credit-scholarship/)), and the NEA opposes it. Durability and neutrality are real risks for a brand built on top of it.
- **Gen Z distribution barely applies.** Donors need at least $1,700 of federal income tax liability. Classmates generally do not. Parents and family friends are the audience. Inference.
- **Family and mentor:** the gate found no warm path into the SGO network. They are fine as test users only.

## 7. Legal, age and platform issues

- **Age:** nothing found blocking an 18-year-old from forming an LLC, signing contracts or taking payments (gate finding; age of majority is higher in a few states, not researched).
- **No license needed** for a calculator or referral site. A PTIN is needed only to prepare returns for pay (gate finding).
- **Charitable-solicitation law is the wall.** Maryland requires a professional solicitor to hold a $25,000 bond and bars fund-raising counsel from percentage-of-contribution pay ([Maryland SOS](https://sos.maryland.gov/Charity/pages/professional-solicitor.aspx)). Vendor guides claim most states require professional-solicitor registration ([Cogency](https://www.cogencyglobal.com/blog/why-charities-should-care-about-registration-requirements-for-professional-fundraisers/)), not verified against statutes. About twenty states regulate commercial co-venturers ([Greenberg Traurig summary](https://ana.net/mkcdelivery/show/id/ep-2024-11-baalaw-nov24-charitable-fundraising-a)). California's AB 488 requires charitable fundraising platforms to register with the Attorney General and file annual reports, and to enable gifts only to charities in good standing ([CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/california-regulation-of-online-charitable-fundraising-platforms)).
- **No source says a flat sponsorship fee is safe, nor that a per-donor fee is unsafe.** I found no ruling on a referral site and no regulation of fundraiser pay in the 25F text. A 3% model like ACE's advisor program is a percentage of gifts, which is the pattern some states restrict. Counsel needed ($2-10k per the gate).
- **Claims:** "costs you nothing" is true only if the donor's liability covers the gift and no double-dip occurs. A paid sponsor needs clear disclosure (FTC-style rules apply; inference).
- **Ads:** I did not research Meta or Google policy on tax-credit or political-issue ads. Gap.

## 8. Why now (and what has been used up)

The shock is genuine: first gifts Jan 1, 2027, 30 states in, proposed regulations out Oct 1 with comments due Dec 1 and a hearing Dec 15 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/treasury-department-guidance-clarifies-the-education)). But the window is already being filled: ACE's campaign started Oct 20, FACTS Compass Oct 15, Workplace Scholarships and AFC are racing for January. The proposed rules can still shift details.

## 9. Verdict and what would change it

Score as a venture: low. Reasons:
- The donor funnel and SGO-side tech are owned or given away by funded incumbents.
- The best price signal ($51 per donor) is below ad-based acquisition cost.
- The honest take-up case is $1.5 to $6B, not $26B.
- The legal path for per-donor pay is unresolved.
- The upside depends on a tax-software, payroll or adviser distribution deal that AFC and ACE are already pursuing.

What remains is a modest free-tool-plus-affiliate side project, probably well under $1M a year, if it ranks organically for "scholarship tax credit calculator". I would not shrink the idea into a boutique to save it.

One cheap test: build the calculator, ask ACE or another SGO for a written referral agreement at 3% or better, and count organic donor starts through Q1 2027. If an SGO will not put a rate in writing, or donor starts stay under 500, stop.

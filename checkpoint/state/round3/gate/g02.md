# G02 gate: Campus Signup Crew (G:teen_advantage:0)

**Verdict: WEAK.** First dollars are easy. The "$10M a year" path is not credible, and the lane is already occupied by a funded competitor plus two earlier attempts at the same model.

## What I found (about 25 searches and fetches, late Oct 2026)

**Direct competitors, all live or recently live**
- **Home From College (H\FC)** is the closest match. GV led a $5.4M seed in April 2024, after $1.5M earlier. It has 100k+ students and hundreds of brands.
  - Its "microGIGs" product already sells app installs to startups. Case studies: Puzzlit and Fanbravo each got 100 "verified" downloads in 24 hours; Peek Money got 40 in 19 hours.
  - Brand pricing is $49/month, $499/year, $999/year, or $15K+/year, plus a 20% service fee (10% or lower at the premium tier).
  - A seed-stage founder can buy what this idea sells for about $499 a year today.
- **Clutch** (ex-Campus Concierge): $1.2M pre-seed led by Precursor, about 200 vetted student creators.
- **UpperClass**: student-creator marketplace for brands and universities.
- **SocialLadder** (about $2k/month software), **nextbee**, and **CampusThreads** (free workspace for up to 100 ambassadors) sell the software side of rep programs.
- **WayUp** runs reps at about $1 per verified signup. It reports 900+ reps driving more than 50% of its signups.
- Also found: **Unibees**, **Ubique Network**, **CampusLaunch** (sorority activations) and **Seed Marketing**.
- **Student-brand incumbents:** UNiDAYS (about £53M revenue in 2021, a third-party estimate) and Student Beans (about £30M, an older figure). Both earn commission on sales from big retailers and have had 15+ years.
- **Fizz** has now flipped from buyer to seller. It sells ads reaching students (Perplexity and Quizlet ran campaigns) and claims about a 25% click-to-signup rate for one advertiser.

**The same model was tried before and did not become big**
- **GoRepp** (2017) took a 20% cut on pay-per-referral conversions for startups.
- **Markett** (2017) raised about $2M, with Airbnb and FanDuel as named customers, and later became Kickback.
- **University Beyond** (2014) raised $300K to connect brands and student ambassadors.
- I found no public evidence that any of these reached eight figures.

**The best-funded buyers do not need a marketplace**
- Clay runs its own College Ambassador Program for 2026-27.
- Perplexity ran its own Campus Strategists.
- OpenAI and Google give students free plans.
- Jiveocity ($5 per verified active user, capped at $250), AfterQuery ($20 per activated referral) and SoulSync AI ($5/month per paying subscriber) all run in-house.

**Free or cheap substitutes**
- Rewardful and FirstPromoter start at $49/month. Dub's partner program starts at $90/month with a 5% payout fee. A founder can set up pay-per-signup in an afternoon.
- Whop Content Rewards has a $1,000 minimum campaign (confirmed), but it pays per view or per post, not per signup.

## Pricing and demand reality
- Rep payouts for signups run $1 to $5 (WayUp $1, Jiveocity $5, FundMyTextbooks $5).
- Influencer CPA for app installs runs about $2 to $15. US iOS CPI is roughly $2.4 to $6, depending on the source.
- So $3 to $12 per activated user is believable, and $5 per activation is cheaper than paid social. Buyers will say yes to a $500 pay-on-results test.
- The only price I could see for what seed-stage startups pay for student access is H\FC's $49/month to $999/year. Budgets are small.
- The idea's own numbers conflict: it says a $1,000 minimum prepaid campaign, but the first-customer offer is a $500 pay-on-results trial.

## Size
- The idea says $10M of revenue needs $29M of brand spend, which means 4.8M activations a year at $6.
  - US college enrollment is only about 19M, so that is activating roughly a quarter of all US students every year.
  - At an optimistic 40 activations per active rep per year, it needs about 120k active reps. SHEIN reportedly has 17k.
- The idea cites SHEIN's $30M. That figure comes from one secondary summary and is cumulative payout, paid as sales commission.
- The incumbents give the ceiling: UNiDAYS and Student Beans together are about $100M, built over 15 years. A $10M business here means roughly a tenth of that.
- Median case: GMV of $0.5M to $1.5M and revenue of $0.2M to $0.5M. Upside: about $4M.

## Legal and age
- No wall. At 18 he can contract and use Stripe Connect.
- The 1099-NEC reporting threshold is $2,000 for 2026 payments, and Stripe Connect can file the forms.
- Soft risks:
  - Google Play bars incentivized installs used to manipulate placement. Referral programs usually survive on intent, but it puts risk on the client's Android app.
  - Rep classification as independent contractors varies by state.
  - FTC disclosure is needed.
  - Campuses restrict solicitation.
  - Pay-per-signup is the most fraud-exposed model.

## Reaching customers
- Cold DMs to founders who launched a student app in the last 60 days should land 1 to 3 small tests, helped by mentor intros at UF. Weeks to first revenue: about 4. Cost about $1,500.
- The risk is repeat spend, not the first sale. Budgets are $500 to $2,000, the buyers are the ones least able to pay, and the reps work for every competing program too.
- Age is a minor blocker here. Founders respond to peers.

## Sharpened version
Do not run "signups". Sell **verified retained users and paid conversions** (day-7 retained, or first paid subscription) to student-targeting AI and SaaS tools that already have growth budgets.
- The wedge is the verification and fraud layer: .edu plus device checks, a 14-day holdback, and a postback API. It is not the rep network.
- Supply is any 18-24 rep, not just campus reps.
- Start by white-labeling H\FC-style supply to prove demand, then own the attribution and payout rails.
- Honest ceiling: low-single-digit millions unless it becomes the standard attribution layer for student acquisition.
- Treat it as a 60-day cash-and-learning experiment, not the main bet.

## Verdict reason
It fails criteria 5 and 6. A GV-funded competitor already sells installs to startups, two earlier attempts at the same model stalled, and the needed $29M of spend has no precedent outside two 15-year-old discount platforms.

# G:mentor_network:7 Rack Academy (Gen Z to AV technician funnel, employer-paid placement): gate report

**Verdict: WEAK, leaning kill as pitched.** The pain is real and a performance-fee buy is easy for a teen to sell. But the entry-level wage caps the fee at about $4-6k (not $6-9k), the biggest buyers build their own pipelines, free funded 4-week cabling programs already exist, first cash is about 4-6 months out, and the honest ceiling is $0.3-0.5M median and about $6M upside. It misses the $10M bar and does not use his AI-building edge.

## What I found (about 24 searches and fetches, late Oct 2026)

**The need is real and newer than the pitch says.**
- NSCA/Commercial Integrator State of the Industry: 72% named talent a top concern in 2023, **91% in 2024** (41% of those struggling with installers/technicians), and 76% in 2025. "Lack of qualified candidates" was the top obstacle in 2025. The pitch's 72% is stale.
- Commercial Integrator (Dec 2023): cable pulling/low-voltage (64.1%) and networking (63.1%) top the skills integrators want in technicians.
- CE Pro: new hires take 1-2 years to reach full productivity; general replacement cost is about 33% of salary. This supports paying a fee, but also shows the 4-6 week graduate is not "ready".

**Direct and near-direct competition (all of it found):**
- **CTI** (Commercial Integrator's 2025 Integrator of the Year): DOL-registered 2-year paid Integration Tech Apprenticeship, announced Sept 2025. 4,000+ hours, 288+ classroom hours, AVIXA/Crestron/Q-SYS/Audinate certs, $18-22/hr, no tuition. The CEO claims it is the first in the US. The largest buyers are building the pipeline in-house.
- **Convergint** (large security/low-voltage integrator): Technical Apprenticeship Program (about 2-2.5 weeks classroom, about 50 weeks on the job), recruiting high school and tech school grads, veterans, career changers. Multiple technology centers; high-school pilot in Compton.
- **AVI-SPL** (the mentor's company): 4-week unpaid SkillBridge installation tech program for transitioning military, plus a UK apprenticeship. Another in-house funnel at the exact company the mentor founded.
- **IEC**: launched a Limited Energy apprenticeship (July 2026) for structured cabling, AV and security. **ABC chapters**: telecom/low-voltage evening program, tuition only $1,900-2,225 (ABC-VA). That is the price anchor for classroom content.
- **Meta + CBRE "LevelUp"** (Apr 2026): free multiyear program to train "thousands" of fiber technicians (fiber install, rack and equipment deployment) for data-center construction, graduates routed to Meta's contractor network. **Meta America's Workforce Academy** ($115M, free, job guaranteed). Free is the price anchor for candidates, and it blocks the "expand into data-center cabling" growth path.
- **Per Scholas / TEKsystems Low Voltage**: free 6-week full-time course, contract-to-hire at $21-30/hr, certs covered, no pay during training. Philanthropy and employer-funded, not fee-per-hire.
- **Schools:** Maverick Technical Institute (NH; $18,000, 1,100 hours, 80% placed in its first class, 2017), Lincoln Tech, Orange Technical College (FL, 750 hours), Vermont Tech, plus ESPA/NSCA Education Foundation (Ignite, scholarships, EST certification), Fiber Broadband Association OpTIC Path and FBA Academy.
- **Apprenti** (tech apprenticeship intermediary): the employer pays a placement fee and the classroom cost (about $15.5k typical) is covered by employer plus grants. This is the closest business-model precedent. It runs on DOL and corporate grant money, not on fees alone.
- **Recruiters and labor:** The Mason Group, HireSparks, Amplify People (One Firefly, for small and mid integrators), AVTek Staffing, Field Nation + Herman "LaborFlex" (1,400 vetted freelance techs, hourly instead of about $900 day rates). Generic direct-hire fees run 15-25% of first-year pay, paid on start.

**Money check.**
- Entry AV tech pay: Payscale $16/hr median (entry), about $20/hr with troubleshooting skills, CTI apprentices $18-22/hr, Wachter $17-19. That is about $35-46k a year.
- So 10-15% is about $3.5-6.5k, and the pitch's $6-9k needs $60k+ pay. The realistic fee is **$4-5k**. A 20% staffing-style fee on an unproven bootcamp grad will not hold.

**Legal and age (real, but clearable):**
- Age: 18 and legally an adult, so no wall. Trainees must be 18+ (the pitch already says so).
- **Private career school licensing:** most states license schools that charge tuition for job training (NY Education Law 5001; TX Education Code ch. 132 is very broad; AZ; AR makes unlicensed operation a misdemeanor). If the candidate pays nothing and the employer contracts the training for its own employees (MN and NY exempt that, other states vary), it is often exempt, but it needs a written exemption determination per state.
- **Employment agency licensing:** NY licenses fee-charging agencies (an employer-paid-only exemption covers mainly clerical/professional placements, not installers), and CA has bond and fee-split rules. Texas current rules unconfirmed.
- Practical fix: pick one state, get a lawyer's one-page exemption read (about $1-3k), and structure it as employer-contracted training of its own employees.
- **Liability:** hands-on cabling, ladders and power tools need general liability and workers' comp coverage for trainees. That is a real cost the pitch ignores.

## Can he reach and close the first customers?
- **Buyer reach is good for a performance fee.** Small and mid integrators (the ones who cannot build an apprenticeship) will take free candidates and pay only on a start date. There is little credibility risk. The mentor (AVI-SPL founder, Tampa) is a real door to owner-operators, NSCA/AVIXA people and a veteran instructor.
- **Candidate supply is not the scarce input.** Entry-level applicants are plentiful. What integrators lack is senior-tech time to train and ramp them. A 4-6 week program does not fix that, and the big integrators solve it with 1-2 year apprenticeships. "Gen Z distribution is the one edge no incumbent has" is the weakest claim in the pitch; the bottleneck is employer onboarding capacity.
- **Timeline fails the 60-90 day test.** Employers: 4-6 weeks. Candidates: 2-4 weeks in parallel. Cohort: 4-6 weeks. Start date plus a 30-day net invoice: about **16-20 weeks to first revenue**. And "10-15 hrs/week plus school" while running an in-person cohort in one metro is a stretch.
- **Cost is understated.** The $3k first-cohort figure leaves out instructor pay, lab space and gear for 10 trainees, liability insurance and legal. Realistic is **$8-15k** before the first fee, and it is partly at risk if placements slip.

## Sizing (bottom-up)
- Annual openings: BLS projects about 11,100/yr for broadcast, sound and video technicians (includes broadcast and events) and about 24,600/yr for telecom technicians (shrinking group). Call the reachable AV plus low-voltage entry hires 15-25k/yr, and subtract the big integrators that now train in-house.
- 1 metro, 40-80 placements a year, times $4-5k = **$0.2-0.4M**. A 5-metro operation with 400 placements is about $2M. **$10M needs about 2,000 placements a year, or 8-13% of all reachable hires.** That is a heroic share for a staffing-margin, ops-heavy, in-person business.
- The pitch's own path-to-big admits it ranks last and needs to become a multi-trade employer-paid training platform. The obvious expansions (fiber, data center) run into free Meta/CBRE and Per Scholas programs, and the electrical expansion runs into IEC/ABC/IBEW apprenticeships.

## Scores against the six criteria
1. First paying customer in 60-90 days under $10k: **fails** (16-20 weeks, $8-15k).
2. Buyers who buy from an 18-year-old: **passes** (performance fee, mentor door).
3. No license wall: **mostly passes** (state school and agency rules are clearable with a lawyer and one state).
4. Real edges: **mixed**. Uses the mentor network; barely uses AI building or his shipped software.
5. Path to $10M+: **fails**.
6. Not crowded: **fails** (apprenticeships, schools, free funded programs, recruiters). A "Gen Z funnel" brand is thin differentiation.

## Sharpened version (does not shrink it)
**Rack Academy becomes an employer-sponsored earn-and-learn engine for small and mid integrators that cannot build their own CTI-style apprenticeship.**
- The employer hires the trainee as a W-2 employee first. Rack Academy supplies sourcing, a 2-4 week pre-deployment lab, a 12-month structured curriculum (AI-generated labs and simulations, skills sign-offs against the AVIXA rack-build standard and cable testing) and mentor coaching for the employer's lead tech.
- **Revenue starts on day 1 of employment:** a $2.5-3.5k onboarding/recruiting fee at start, plus $200-300 per apprentice per month for 12 months (about $5-7k total per hire). The ABC $2k tuition and Apprenti's $15.5k bracket the price. Retention coaching is the product, since turnover costs about 33% of salary.
- **Make public money the lever:** WIOA on-the-job-training contracts reimburse employers 50% of wages (up to 75% for small employers or barrier cases), and a registered-apprenticeship sponsor can run OJT contracts. Rack Academy handles the paperwork, so the employer's net cost is close to zero. State apprenticeship tuition reimbursements (e.g. Maryland's up to $2,500) stack on top. Employer-paid-and-employed trainees also sidestep most private-school licensing.
- **Path to big:** the software layer (assessment, labs, progress tracking, OJT paperwork) is the part he can build at AI speed and resell as a per-apprentice subscription across low-voltage, security and AV. Target: 1,000 active apprentices at about $6k/yr is $6M; the upside needs 2,500+ in the multi-trade version.
- **First-60-days test (cheap):** get 5 integrators to sign a one-page letter to hire 2 trainees each on those terms, before any cohort is built. If 3 of 5 sign, proceed. If they only want free candidates, kill it.

## Bottom line
Weak. Rank it low and do not start it ahead of the freight audit service unless the 5-letter test passes. The mentor door is real, but this is a good local training business, not a $10M one, and the strongest versions depend on public money and a software layer he has not yet shown demand for.

# Gate g26: S:I1022 Adaptive Paper (weekly personalized practice packets)

**Verdict: WEAK.** Not dead, and the cheapest idea in the pool to test. But the moat is thin, the weekly photo chore threatens retention, and the $10M path needs about 30,000 paying families with unproven retention.

## What the searches found (about 28 searches and fetches, Oct 2026)

**Closest competitors (none does the whole loop, together they cover most of it)**
- **Extra Math** (App Store, EUR 0.99): photo of a worksheet becomes editable, printable similar problems. Grades 2-5 only. The listing is thin and the page 404'd on direct fetch, so details are unverified. Cheap, tiny, but it proves the "photo to printable" idea is already an indie-app feature.
- **OpenEduCat math tools** (generic): an Error Analysis mode that reads a photo of student work and classes the error as procedural, conceptual or careless, plus a worksheet generator with answer key. It targets schools and is not a parent product, but the diagnose-then-generate pair exists.
- **Excellence Learning** (YC W25, about $500K): positions itself as "super Kumon" for US parents, with daily exercises and worksheet feedback. Middle school and up, screen-based. This is the most direct strategic competitor, and it has YC money behind it.
- **AfterBell**: photo of homework becomes a themed game. PreK-3, free early access, no printing.
- **HomeschoolFox**: premium add-on that emails a daily skill sheet per child. Grade-matched, not clearly adaptive, no photo input.
- **Thinkster Math** (ex-Tabtor): handwritten worksheets on a tablet with human tutors. About $60-200+ a month.
- **Chiron** (YC P25): iPad app that checks handwritten math live. **Frizzle** (YC S25): teacher-facing photo grading.
- **Kumon / Kumon Connect** and **Mathnasium**: the paper-first incumbents. $100-250 a month for Kumon, $150-500 for Mathnasium. Kumon Connect now has stylus input and AI-assisted grading.
- **Free substitute: ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude.** A parent can attach a photo and ask for a diagnosis and 10 targeted problems with an answer key. Guides showing parents doing this already exist. This is the real competitor.

**Price and willingness to pay**
- Time4Learning (homeschool) is $29.95-39.95 a month. Math Academy is $49 (beta page, possibly stale). Elephant Learning is $50. Beast Academy is about $96-100 a year and Math Mammoth is about $42 per level, so core math is cheap.
- HSLDA's 2024 survey puts average homeschool spend at $1,295 per elementary student per year. A $330 a year subscription would be about 25% of that. It is plausible for a core engine, but heavy for a "supplement".

**Market**
- Homeschool: about 3.0-3.4M students (NHERI 3.41M, an advocacy source; NCES 3.4% of K-12 in 2022-23). Growth is about 5% a year.
- Kumon US had about 280k subject enrollments at 1,520 centers in 2018 (the latest number I found). Mathnasium has about 1,000 US centers. Together that is a rough $0.8-1B US paid math-center pool.
- ESA: Arizona is over 100,000 students (Jan 2026). Florida PEP has 78,617 funded students (2025-26) at about $7.7-8.5k each. Texas has about 96-100k students, but the homeschool award is only $2,000 (private school $10,500). Sources count 17 ESA programs in the vendor guides and about 31 states with some school-choice program. "18 states" is loose.
- Why now (real): the AP reported a May 2026 backlash against school screens. At least 10 states have bills restricting school screen time, and Haidt amplified the Chromebook critique. "Paper-first, no screen" has real tailwind this year.

**ESA vendor path**
- Odyssey's application is reportedly under 10 minutes per program. Texas charges no listing fee and rolls reviews. Tennessee's vendor window closes 3/31/2027. South Carolina decides in up to 10 business days. Payment takes 2-10 days (Iowa, Alabama).
- Credential and background-check rules hit tutors and service providers (Texas, Georgia, Missouri, Tennessee, South Carolina). Goods and product sellers get lighter treatment (Wyoming exempts goods-only vendors, Arkansas exempts product sellers).
- Utah needs an EIN, a business licence and articles. Louisiana and Missouri need a US entity. The sources say nothing about a minimum age, but an 18-year-old LLC is fine.
- Risk: Florida denied some homeschool parents' funding in 2026 (WLRN, Aug 2026). Keep the product a "curriculum/instructional material", never "tutoring".
- Platform fees are passed to the account holder (NC up to 2.5%, TN up to 2.0%).

**Technical risk**
- Published tests of vision-language models on handwritten math are weak (FERMAT: best error-correction rate 77% on grade 7-12 work; Warwick: GPT-4o worse than human graders; Rutgers: ChatGPT-4o vision unreliable on student work). Those are older models.
- A 2026 arXiv study found GPT-4.1-mini beating Mathpix on hard handwriting (84% vs 55% acceptable). No published data exists on children's handwriting (reversed digits, erasures, 2nd graders).
- Diagnosing error patterns is harder than grading. It needs an in-house test, not a search.

**Legal**
- COPPA: the amended rule is fully in force since April 22, 2026. It treats photos of a child as personal information. FTC guidance (pre-amendment) says parent-supplied data is outside the child-consent rules. The direct-to-parent flow is not clearly resolved, so use parent-only accounts, no child names, a written retention policy and one hour of counsel. This is manageable.
- No license wall. An 18-year-old can form an LLC and take payments.

**Distribution**
- Homeschool Facebook groups mostly ban self-promotion, or allow a weekly promo thread. The Gen Z network does not reach the payer (a 30-45-year-old parent).
- Working channels: Homeschool Review Crew (reviewers keep products and post reviews), Cathy Duffy-style reviewers, affiliate YouTubers and bloggers, ESA-family Facebook groups, "Kumon alternative" search, and local co-ops.
- Conventions: Great Homeschool Conventions booth is about $600-750, HEAV about $745-1,045, Homeschool Idaho about $225. 2027 exhibitor applications open July 2027, which is too late for a 60-90 day goal.

## Where the original pitch was wrong or weak
1. "No single product combines photo, diagnosis and packets": true only for a clean three-part combo. Extra Math, OpenEduCat and Excellence Learning cover most of it, and a chatbot does it for free.
2. The "photo of last week's work" input is the weakest link. It forces the model to read arbitrary children's handwriting across any curriculum, and it adds a weekly chore for the parent.
3. The founder's Gen Z edge does not apply. The payer is a parent.
4. "Not an AI tutor" is fine, but "paper-first" is also what Kumon already sells. The differentiator has to be price and automation, not format.

## Sharpened version (does not shrink it)
**Closed-loop paper**: a Kumon-style mastery system at $29 a month, run entirely on paper.
- The packet is generated by the system, so it knows every question and the right answer. Each page carries a page ID and fixed answer boxes plus a "show your work" strip. The weekly parent chore is "scan 4-6 pages", and the system reads digits in known boxes, which is far more accurate than free handwriting.
- Week 0 is a printed 20-minute placement packet. This removes the dependence on the family's curriculum, and optional photos of the family's own workbook become a premium "misconception review".
- A per-child item-level skill graph is the data moat. ChatGPT has no longitudinal memory of every item the child has seen. Annual plan $249, family plan $49.
- Sell it at two price points: $29 for families who pay, and $50-60 (or "free") for ESA families in AZ, FL, TX and others, once vendor-approved. Print-and-mail at $59 a month is an upsell.
- Positioning is "Kumon at home for a fifth of the price, no screen", aimed at families paying or tempted by Kumon/Mathnasium plus homeschoolers. Start with math 2-6 only.

## Numbers
- Weeks to first revenue: about 5. Cost to first revenue: about $600 (LLC, domain, API, a retired-teacher review).
- Gross margin: more than 90% on digital (about $1 of model cost a month versus $29).
- Median case: about $0.9M ARR (about 3,000 families at about $300 a year, years 3-5). Upside: about $10M (about 30,000 families, about 2% of homeschool K-8 families, or a few percent of Kumon's US enrollment, plus ESA and mail).
- The 30,000-family path is not heroic on size but is unproven on retention and moat.

## Test that decides it (4-5 weeks, under $1k)
1. Build the closed-loop packet and scan reader. Test it on 30 real children's pages (cousins, family friends).
2. Run 10-15 pilot families for 4 weeks. Charge $29 from week 3.
3. Kill it if fewer than 5 pay, or if more than half stop scanning by week 4. Go on if 8 or more pay and scan weekly, and if a sample ChatGPT-DIY parent still prefers it.

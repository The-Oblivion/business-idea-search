# G14 Gate: Co-op Cash Autopilot (key G:arbitrage:0)

**Verdict: KILL as written.** A narrow prospective variant is described at the bottom, but it is a boutique, so under the round-2 rule ("call it dead rather than narrowing it") it does not rescue the idea.

## Why it dies (four independent failures)

1. **The core premise is broken by the programs' own rules.** The pitch is "find money already earned and never claimed from 12 months of ad invoices." Programs I could read say otherwise:
   - Samsung HVAC 2021 program: all co-op requests must be pre-approved; "request for a co-op fund credit will be denied if prior pre-approval is not received"; reimbursed as a credit invoice on the account, not cash. Funds go to distributors and spec reps, not contractors.
   - Suzuki Marine PY2026 (run by ACB): claims must be submitted within 60 days of the ad date; credits post to the parts account, "no separate check is issued."
   - Baker Distributing (Rheem, Bosch, GREE, ICP): "Pre-approval is required for all marketing co-op activities."
   - Carrier is administered through the distributor; Trane and Owens Corning co-op posts also say prior approval is required.
   So last year's unapproved ads are mostly unclaimable. What exists is an unspent accrual balance, and drawing it down needs new, brand-compliant, pre-approved spend plus the contractor's own matching dollars (50-70% reimbursement at Trane, 50% at Carrier). That is marketing-agency work, not an audit.
2. **The pool per customer is small.** Trane accrual is 1-4% of purchases; Samsung is 3% (2.25% usable); LG about 1.5%. A contractor buying $300k of equipment accrues roughly $3k-$12k; most HVAC firms are under $1M revenue (DOE guide). The recoverable amount is a fraction of that, so a 20% fee is hundreds of dollars per customer per year. The idea's own kill test (median claimable under $1,500) is likely to trip.
3. **Someone already does it, and often for free.**
   - RevLocal: builds pre-approved Carrier co-op campaigns and files monthly claims with "no additional fees."
   - Socius: "handles the entire co-op approval and reimbursement process" for contractors.
   - Co-op Connect (Localogy): 180+ HVAC brands, concierge does accrual research, pre-approval and claim packages. 2021: 625 requests, $2.48M found (avg about $3,969, accrual balances, not claimable cash).
   - Mediagistic: SimpliClaims portal, "we will even take care of the co-op submission for your dealers"; EGIA partnership Nov 2025.
   - Auto: COOPABLE (Lotlinx deal, March 2026), Strong Automotive (self-reported about 95 clients, $9k/month each), Wizely.
   - Manufacturer side: ACB, SproutLoud, ZINFI, Model N, Vistex.
   - Buying groups: BrandSource files co-op for members and matched $500/month.
   - Distributors run self-serve "Request Pre-Approval" and "File Claim" links per brand (Baker).
   No pure success-fee self-serve product for small contractors surfaced, but the bundled-free substitute is the real competitor.
4. **No path to $10M.** At about $800-1,000 average fee per customer per year, $10M needs 10,000+ paying customers, roughly 8% of the 118k HVAC firms (IBISWorld) with 2-4% cold-call conversion. The "$14-35B unclaimed" headline traces to a 2015 Borrell study sponsored by Netsertive (a vendor) and a 2016 LSA white paper; "unspent accrual" is not "claimable cash."

## Can Asher reach and close the first customers?
- Hard. 80 cold contacts at ServiceTitan's quoted 2-4% cold-call conversion is 2-3 conversations, not 10 signed audits by week 4. Owner-operators are on job sites; door-knocking 80 shops conflicts with school hours.
- The ask (12 months of ad invoices, card exports, distributor purchase summary, dealer-portal login or a manufacturer consent form) is heavy for a stranger, and distributors who control the funds are gatekeepers, not allies.
- In auto, OEMs keep approved-vendor lists and require agencies to register for portal access, which an 18-year-old LLC would not clear quickly.

## Legal and age
- Age is not a legal blocker (adult in nearly all states; AL and NE are 19, check his state). I found no licensing regime for filing co-op claims on a success fee (not counsel-verified). Written third-party authorization is needed per program, and some programs restrict third-party filers.
- The real blockers are credibility, approved-vendor lists, and access to distributor-held funds.

## Numbers
- Cost to first revenue: about $600 (LLC, domain, tooling). Weeks to first revenue: about 10 (4 to sign, pre-approval, ad runs, then 30-90 day credit; a $149 flat pack could land earlier).
- Median 3-year revenue: about $0.15M/yr (about 200 customers x about $800). Upside: about $4M/yr (about 4,000 customers plus agency white-label and auto/powersports lines).

## Sharpened version (only if he insists; still likely a boutique)
Pre-approval-first, agency-channel: build brand rulebooks from real dealer-portal guidelines; gate on five contractors' consented accrual-balance screenshots (proceed only if median unspent accrual is over $3k); run their next 30 days of ads through pre-approval; take 20% of credits actually posted; white-label to contractor marketing agencies and local media sellers, who need co-op to close ad deals. Kill if the five balances come back under $3k.

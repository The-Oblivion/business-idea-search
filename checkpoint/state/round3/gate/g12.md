# G12 gate: Lease X-ray (self-serve CAM / opex audit for small tenants)

Key G:ai_builder:1. Checked 2026-10-25. Verdict: **WEAK** (near the kill line as a $10M+ business; fine as a small first-revenue project).

## Deciding reason
The idea's central claim, "no self-serve product for small tenants", is false. A $199 self-serve product with the same features (free scan, 14 rule checks, dispute letter) has been live since March 2026. The tooling is also commoditizing: the same maker now sells it to accounting firms and tenant reps at $29-49 per audit. Per-tenant economics are small, so reaching $10M a year needs 30-100k paying tenants, not the 20-40k the idea assumes.

## Numbers that matter
- Typical recoveries are small per location: Leasecake case about $1.3k per location per year; Mohr's 800-location healthcare client $320k, about $400 per location. Large-format retail is much higher (Mohr: about $20k per location across five stores), but those are not his buyers.
- At a 25% fee that is about $100-325 per tenant-year. That is roughly the same as the $149 diagnostic, so the "contingency" upside barely exists for single-unit restaurants and salons.
- Vendor error claims (30-40% of statements) are marketing; an independent figure of "one in four tenants" had CAM discrepancies (PredictAP, 2026). Nobody publishes an average recovery.
- Prices: CAMAudit $199 direct and $29-49 per audit wholesale; Propaya (YC S24) $200 per lease for abstraction.

## Reach and close
- Reconciliations arrive roughly 90-120 days after year end; audit windows are 30 days to 12 months. So the real selling season is Feb-Jun 2027. Prior-year (FY2025) statements may still be inside long windows now, which is the only way to a first sale before Q1.
- Statement-only "X-ray" finds only math and pro-rata errors. The valuable findings (capital items billed as repairs, management fee base, admin fee) need the general ledger and invoices. That requires an audit right in the lease (small-tenant landlord forms often lack or limit one), a written request, and about 30 days for the landlord to respond. Cash from a success fee arrives 6-12 months out.
- Trust: restaurateurs would be handing a lease (often with a personal guaranty) to an 18-year-old. Mitigated by free first look, no recovery no fee, data-handling terms, and mentor or family intros; not eliminated.
- Reddit self-promo rules and closed franchisee Facebook groups limit the "free X-ray" channel; I could not fetch Reddit rules to confirm.

## Legal and age
- Age: no wall. 18 can form an LLC, sign and take payments (AL and NE set majority at 19, MS at 21; check his state).
- No CAM-audit license exists. But leases often bar the tenant's auditor from being paid on contingency or require a CPA. Found in SEC-filed leases ("in no event shall any audit be performed by a firm retained on a contingency fee basis") and in landlord-side drafting advice that calls it one of the biggest negotiation points. Prevalence unknown. Where it applies, the 25% success fee is a lease breach risk for the tenant, so the model falls back to flat fee.
- Unauthorized practice of law: personalized dispute letters are a gray area (Upsolve ruling; Texas statute exempts software with a disclaimer; Colorado considering a shield; Nippon Life v. OpenAI pending). Use templates plus disclaimers, and consider a lease-attorney partner for escalations.
- Wrong flags carry reputational and possibly tort risk; always cite the clause.

## Size
- Median case: about $0.3M a year by year 5 (a few hundred diagnostics plus a modest recovery book).
- Upside: about $8M a year only if it becomes a franchisee-focused recovery network with a data asset (landlord and center benchmarks). Leasecake is the reality check for the lease-OS route: roughly $7M after 7 years and $17-25M raised.
- The idea's own $20-40M upside is not supported by any bottom-up count I could source.

## Sharpened version (not shrunk)
**Franchisee lease-cost recovery desk, with a benchmark data asset.** Aim at multi-unit franchisees (restaurants, fitness, personal services; 5-100 NNN leases) through franchisee associations and mentor/family intros, not single-store restaurants. Free multi-lease "Q1 reconciliation scan" on last year's statements; contingency-only (20-25%) with a multi-year look-back, flat-fee fallback where the lease bans contingency auditors. One operator with 20 locations at about $1k recovered per location is $5k of fees, so about 2,000 operators or 40k locations reach $10M. Every reconciliation ingested builds a landlord/center benchmark (what each center's CAM and admin fees look like), which a $29-49 white-label tool cannot copy. Honest read: still a service-heavy business that needs a partner CPA or lease attorney to scale past desk-review work.

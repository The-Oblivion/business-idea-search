# g11 - G:contingency_audit:2 - Small-Operator Lease Audit (CAM/NNN pass-throughs)

**Verdict: WEAK (leans kill as a standalone contingency auditor).** Real pain, real engine reuse, but the funded incumbents already sell to the exact buyer, the contingency model hits a lease-clause wall, and per-store dollars are small.

## What I found (about 25 searches/fetches, late Oct 2026)
**Not a gap. Three tenant-side AI products and two lease-admin platforms already target multi-location operators:**
- Leasecake: about $25M raised, multi-unit restaurant/retail/dental tenants (Little Caesars, Jersey Mike's franchisee at 82 units, Serve Hospitality "$400K saved on CAM"), free 24-point lease audit, "1% of rent". Its segments start at 50+ leases, so 3-50 units is its soft spot, not a hole.
- LeaseMind (MightyBot): tenant-side CAM reconciliation audit, 50+ provisions, dispute package, 30-day design-partner pilot; page updated Oct 24 2026. Same product description as this idea minus the contingency fee.
- CAMAudit: solo developer, $199-$699 one-time, deterministic math plus AI extraction. Its site now sells to accounting firms, tenant reps and lease attorneys, i.e. the same referral partners this idea wanted to recruit.
- Occupier (30-300 location franchisees, CAM deadline alerts), Propaya (YC S24, about $200/lease abstraction), ReCAM (checks CAM against public data), Fiverr desktop-audit gigs.
- Legacy contingency firms: Commercial Tenant Services (35% fee documented in court), Mohr Partners, CBIZ, Marks Paneth, KBA Lease Services, Hughes Marino (free initial review). They skip small tenants because of high touch, not because no one thought of it.
- Free substitute: a tenant can paste lease plus reconciliation into a general LLM; many guides (beancount, Leasecake, Mohr) teach the checklist.

**Legal/structural walls:**
- Many landlord-form leases bar contingency-fee auditors or require a CPA paid hourly. A third-party contingent auditor cannot invoke the formal audit right in those leases.
- Audit windows are 30-90 days (up to 180) from receipt. 2025 reconciliations arrived Mar-May 2026, so most windows have lapsed; the first real contingency dollars come from spring 2027 statements.
- Non-lawyer demand letters carry unauthorized-practice-of-law risk (Nebraska regulators went after a non-lawyer tenant group). Safer: the tenant signs and sends, or a lawyer/CPA does.
- No license is required to analyze charges (age is not an issue; he is 18). A CPA or attorney "of record" is needed only for contested formal audits.

## Numbers
- Pass-throughs: listings show NNN $5.50-$19.90/sf; a 2,500 sf strip-center restaurant is about $14k-$50k, central about $25k.
- Error rate about 40% and recovery 3-5% of occupancy cost are vendor-sourced (Tango, Prophia, Springbord). Mohr's published per-store recoveries ($13.7k-$30.6k) are large-format retail, not small shops.
- Expected fee per store per year at 25%: 0.4 x 8% x $25k x 25% = about $200 (idea's assumption of 5% on every store gives about $310-$375). A 20-unit group is about $4k-$7.5k a year, not $10k.
- Pool: FRANdata counts about 43k multi-unit franchisees averaging 5.5 units (about 241k units). With about 60% on NNN with an audit right, and non-franchise groups (dental, fitness, salons) roughly doubling it, the full-penetration fee pool is about $60-110M a year. $10M of revenue needs 10-17% of that. Idea's own "3,000 groups x $10k" is not supported.
- Cost to first revenue about $500 (LLC, domain, API credits, E&O quote). First $299 diagnostic realistically 6-10 weeks; first contingency cash spring-summer 2027.
- Median 3-year run-rate about $0.3M; upside about $8M only with a bundle (property-tax and utility recovery, lease admin, benchmark data), which means fighting Leasecake and Occupier.

## Can he reach the first customers?
Yes, better than most ideas: strip-center owners will talk to a teenager if the offer is "free lease check, pay only if we recover", and franchisee Facebook/LinkedIn groups, Great Clips/Sport Clips, Orangetheory/F45/Club Pilates, Jersey Mike's/Tropical Smoothie and dental-suite owners are walkable. The real conversion risk is trust (handing an 18-year-old a lease and bank-adjacent statements) and the cheapest-substitute problem ($199 CAMAudit, free Leasecake audit, free LLM).

## Sharpened version (not shrunk)
Center-by-center, tenant-run: the landlord's expense pool is wrong once per shopping center, so recruit 3+ tenants in the same center, audit the pool once, and have each tenant file its own dispute (no contingent third-party auditor, no UPL). Software is the product; success fee is on credits; each audited center and each landlord's error pattern feeds a cross-tenant benchmark database. That data and the calendar/monitoring layer are the path past a services business.

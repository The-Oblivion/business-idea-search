# g16 - G:contingency_audit:3 - Micro-Importer Customs Desk (IEEPA refunds, then duty audit and drawback)

**Verdict: KILL.** The money was mostly claimed before he could start, the remainder needs lawsuits or a broker license he cannot get, and the fee he would take is the one thing the broker rules forbid.

## What I found (about 22 searches/fetches, late Oct 2026)

**1. The pool is mostly gone.**
- About $166B of IEEPA duties were collected from about 330,000 importers on 53M+ entries.
- CBP told the CIT (reported Sept 17) that $134.7B was already accepted into CAPE and about $122B (with interest) was certified to Treasury. 286,000 declarations had been submitted and 201,000 accepted, covering 27.2M entries.
- A law-firm piece (DWT, Oct 2026) says over 75% had been refunded with interest as of Oct 2.
- The "12-18 month window before it is picked clean" in the idea is already about 6 months gone. CAPE opened April 20, 2026.

**2. What is left is the wrong kind of money for an unlicensed 18-year-old.**
- About $30B (Judge Eaton's figure, also Foley's) sits in finally liquidated entries. CAPE Phase 3 (opened Oct 6) is plaintiffs-only. The CIT says those importers must sue first.
- About 7,000 importers have already filed nearly 4,000 suits. Law firms (AGG, Troutman, Greenberg Traurig, Brown Winick) are actively selling this. CIT matters are quoted at $10-15k each.
- On Oct 8 the CIT certified a class of every importer of record who paid IEEPA duties Feb 1, 2025 to Feb 19, 2026, excluding those already suing. It presumably reaches non-plaintiffs with finally liquidated entries, at no cost to the importer. The government is expected to appeal, and the earlier universal-refund appeal is still pending at the Federal Circuit.
- Practical effect: the final-liquidation pool is either handled free by class counsel or sold by lawyers. Neither needs Asher.
- One forecast (shopappy, a low-authority blog; treat as speculation) says the true unclaimed tail is $11-21B, with unclaimed entries averaging about $1,210 against about $4,950 for claimed ones. These are small importers who stopped importing, with lapsed credentials. About 20,184 refunds ($1.3B) are stuck only because the importer never entered ACH bank details, which is a free fix inside ACE.

**3. The legal wall is real and it hits the fee itself.**
- 19 USC 1641 defines "customs business" to include "the refund, rebate, or drawback" of duties and the preparation of CBP documents, whether or not you sign or file them. Only the importer filing for itself, or a licensed broker, may do it.
- 19 CFR 111.36(b) (verified on eCFR): a broker "must not enter into any agreement with an unlicensed person to transact customs business for others in such manner that the fees or other benefits resulting from the services rendered for others inure to the benefit of the unlicensed person." The only carve-out, in (c), is paying a freight forwarder for referrals.
- So "he takes 4-8% and a licensed broker partner files" is exactly the structure 111.36(b) prohibits. Neither a referral fee nor a revenue split is allowed unless he is a forwarder.
- Lawyers have the equivalent rule (no fee-sharing with non-lawyers), so the litigation route is blocked the same way.
- What is left to him is a standalone analysis or software product sold directly to the importer, who then files itself or hires its own broker. That is a flat-fee tool, not a contingency desk.
- Age is not an issue: he can contract at 18. The blocker is the license and fee-split rule, not his age. Get one hour of customs-counsel advice before spending anything.

**4. Crowded, and the price is falling toward zero.**
- Licensed or law-backed firms: Leyton (contingent, "pay only if recovered"), Tariff Recovery Group (LA, SMB-focused), Tariff Recovery Team (Home Products Insider), Ginger Control (files CAPE and protests itself, free estimator), Clearit (flat filing fee plus a small percent), One Eagle, Baker Tilly, Freight Right, Avalara and Flexport.
- Flexport offers a free refund calculator, a free "Audit Your Customs Broker" tool and "enroll for free" enrollment. UPS and DHL sell fee-based CAPE filing for their own entries, and DHL returns refunds to the party that paid.
- Funding and finance side: Caspian ($5.4M seed, itself a licensed broker; the "unclaimed refunds" pitch), Pax ($4.5M seed, "TurboTax for drawback"), Dutywise, Commercial Finance Partners (advances up to 75% of a verified refund), Clear Cove/Capitol Counsel (advance in 2-3 weeks), plus claim buyers bidding 10-20%.
- Free substitutes: CBP's own CAPE page (the filing is a CSV of entry numbers), SCORE, ecomcrew, Beancount, Inventory Hero and law-firm explainers.
- Fees: reported broker asks of 10-15% (Loadstar, Baker Tilly) against the idea's own note of "as low as 1.5%". Baker Tilly calls the work "pulling entry data, putting it in a spreadsheet, and uploading it".

## Can he reach the first customers?
He can reach them: r/FulfillmentByAmazon, r/shopify, sourcing groups and the freight-audit list are all open to a teenager. The problem is who is left. After six months of every broker, forwarder, law firm and CBP pushing the same message, the importers still unfiled are the disengaged, the tiny, the defunct and the litigation-only. Importers who are engaged already have a broker filing for free or a firm on contingency. Trust is the other issue: he would be asking for ACE access and bank details.

## Sharpened version (not shrunk)
Drop the contingency refund desk. Keep the part with a durable asset and no license problem:

**Importer Duty Ledger.** A self-serve duty-intelligence product for sub-$5M importers (Shopify, Amazon, eBay brands). They upload their ACE entry reports and broker statements. The product shows, in plain English, every duty line, which tariff program it was paid under, and which are still refundable or recoverable (IEEPA residue, Section 232/301 stacking, wrong-program or first-sale errors, drawback eligibility). The free IEEPA estimator, including the ACH-hold check, is the wedge. Revenue is a $49-$199 per month subscription plus a flat-fee software license to brokers who do the filing, with no fee-sharing and no customs business done by him. Counsel must confirm that analysis-only software stays outside 19 USC 1641 (Ginger Control's own guide says reviews of liquidated entries are allowed but decisions about entry data are customs business).

It is still only weak. The incumbents above are funded, 330k importers is the ceiling and small importers churn, so the recurring layer needs a cheap distribution channel (broker and marketplace-tool partnerships) to reach $10M. This gate does not rescue it. It only says where any of this idea's value survives.

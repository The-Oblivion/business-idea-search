# G06 gate: Second Look, pay-when-it-works financial-aid appeals (G:college_world:0)

**Verdict: WEAK.** Not dead, because no live capped-contingency player is visible and first revenue is cheap and fast. But the commodity layer is free, the self-serve substitute already works for most families, and the bottom-up path to $10M+ does not close as pitched.

**Takeaway:** the "award letter X-ray" and the appeal letter are already free, in 6+ products, several subsidised by lender revenue. The only unoccupied slot is the human-reviewed packet sold on pure pay-for-results. A 2021 entrant in exactly that slot left no trace.

### Numbers that matter
- **Families already appeal on their own.** Sallie Mae/Ipsos *How America Pays for College 2026* (Tables 65-66, read from the PDF): 33% of families appealed (37% at 4-year privates, 41% Northeast). Of those, 70% "received additional aid", 16% no, 8% waiting. That is self-reported, has no dollar amounts, and is survivor-biased (enrolled students only).
- **Implication:** the win rate is real, which supports the demand. But 70% DIY success makes "did your service cause it?" hard to prove. That hurts the 20% success fee, and it shrinks the pool of families who need to pay.
- **Win-rate range is wide.** 6% of students got more, average $3,497 (a CollegeFinance.com survey, found only via a secondary summary and not verified). 75% private / 25% public (College Aid Pro's own research, as cited by NGPF). About a third pre-pandemic (Princeton Review's Franek, secondhand). No national data exists. Treat 35% as a guess, not a fact.
- **Dollar size.** Typical vendor-claimed gain is $3-5k per year. The pitch's $6,000 average is above that. At $4,000 average gain and 35% wins, a 20% fee is $800 per win, so about $280 expected per family before leakage.
- **Why now holds up.** Parent PLUS is capped at $20k per year and $65k per student from July 1 2026. NACUBO projects a 57.1% first-time private discount rate for 2025-26, and net tuition revenue per student is falling.
- **Rule of thumb from CAP:** the category leader after nine years and with a national brand partnership is a ~20-person company. A $10M+ consumer paid-appeals business means roughly 25-30k paying families a year at about $350-400 each. That is more than double CAP's whole annual throughput, including free users.

### Legal and age
- **Age:** not a blocker. He is 18, can form an LLC, contract and take Stripe payments. No federal licence is needed to prepare an aid appeal.
- **Illinois Educational Planning Services Consumer Protection Act (815 ILCS 616, in force Jan 2022):** covers anyone charging a fee to advise on financial aid or scholarships. It requires a signed written contract, an ISAC free-services disclosure, a consumer rights form and a right to cancel. It also **bans up-front fees of any kind (s.20(d))**, and outcome claims must be substantiated. The $149 up-front audit is therefore illegal for Illinois families unless it is restructured. I verified this via FindLaw's codification and Manatt's summary, not the official text (the ilga.gov fetch failed on a certificate error).
- **Contingent fees are not banned** in anything I found, but the FTC and state AGs treat "pay us to get you money" with suspicion, so the contract must be clean.
- **FERPA:** aid offices need the student's signed release naming his LLC. Some schools restrict who can submit. Regent's policy bars documentation from anyone who benefits from the outcome. So he must stay a preparer and the family signs every document.
- **Fraud risk is inherent:** a success fee rewards exaggeration. Aid offices say generic AI appeals lack specifics (NASFAA, via CollegeHelpGuide) and get denied.

### Reaching customers
- **Real pool in the first 90 days is thin.** Dec 15 letters are mostly ED (binding, so competing-offer leverage is zero) and EA. The bulk of RD letters arrive late March to April, with a May 1 decision date. So the success-fee season is only about 5 weeks, and it collides with his own college decisions and AP exams.
- **Reddit:** I could not fetch r/ApplyingToCollege rules, and WebSearch blocks reddit. Expect self-promotion and paid-service posting to be restricted. Treat as unverified and read the rules before planning on it.
- **Trust is the real wall.** Parents must hand tax returns, CSS Profile and award letters to an 18-year-old. His classmates' parents and his mentor's network can clear it. Cold strangers mostly will not.
- **Collection risk.** Consumer success fees of $400-$1,000 are not worth chasing. He would need a card on file at signing plus the revised award letter as proof.

## Verdict reasoning
- **Passes:** first dollar inside 90 days for under $2k (friends-and-family families plus a free pilot); no licence wall; a real shock (Parent PLUS cap); and a price point below every human competitor.
- **Fails:** crowded free layer, DIY already beats the paid case for most families, attribution and collection are weak, one-shot seasonal customers, and the $20M share maths implies about 4% of all eligible families.
- **Kill test not met:** a capped contingency with published outcomes does not visibly exist today. But the one prior attempt vanished, which is a warning, not an opening.

## Numbers
- Cost to first revenue: about $1,500 (LLC, e-sign, Stripe, retired-officer review on ~10 files at ~45 min each, small E&O quote). The pitch's $1,100 is slightly optimistic.
- Weeks to first revenue: about 11 (Dec 15 to mid-January). Success fees: March to June.
- Median case (year 5): about $0.4M a year. Upside: about $6M, only if the outcomes ledger becomes a data and marketplace business.

## Sharpened version (does not shrink it)
**Second Look: the outcomes ledger for college aid appeals.** Pay-on-win appeals for families, with the first public database of which schools actually move and by how much.
1. **Drop the up-front fee.** Free school-specific odds read (it is free everywhere anyway), then 15-20% of the verified first-year grant increase above $1,000, capped at $2,500, billed to a card on file only after the revised award letter is uploaded. This fixes Illinois, removes the conversion barrier, and keeps the price below every human rival.
2. **Aim at the cases free generators cannot do:** special circumstances (self-employment, divorce, medical, job loss) and competing offers at schools whose published policy allows it, with a named retired aid officer signing the packet and the family signing every statement.
3. **Build the asset:** every file, and a $25 reward for any family that reports their result, feeds a per-school page: n, win rate, median dollars, days to decision, which documents worked. TuitionFit has award letters but not appeal outcomes. About 1,500 school pages are a search-and-TikTok engine that also gives the credibility an 18-year-old lacks.
4. **Monetise in layers:** appeals fees first; later disclosed marketplace referrals for the post-PLUS-cap gap families (no advice); later benchmark data for college enrollment teams.
5. **Test before building:** run 10 free pilot files on classmates' families in December, publish the results, and only then price.

Fastest kill signal: if fewer than 3 of 10 pilot families have a gain above $1,500 by April, drop the contingency business and keep the ledger as content.

# G01 gate: Hard-Hat Stays (key G:pool_wedges:2)

**Verdict: WEAK (leaning kill as written).** The pitch's central claim, "I found no marketplace built for trade crews", is wrong on the money side. The only open lane is small, and the first-customer plan contradicts itself.

## What the searches found (about 22 searches/fetches)

### Competitors (the contractor-paid "crew desk" is taken)
- **Hard Hat Housing** (hardhathousing.com): nearly the same name. Sources, vets, books and invoices 30+ day crew lodging for construction firms. One monthly invoice, 24-hour turnaround, 25-35% under hotels. Names Gray Construction as a client. Takes homeowner submissions.
- **LodgeLink** (lodgelink.com): Black Diamond Group subsidiary. 18,000 workforce lodging options, consolidated invoicing, a construction vertical with truck-parking motels. Earlier stat: 500+ companies. Got a $2.35-3M Calgary grant.
- **WorkBnB** (workbnb.com): founded 2021, Techstars pre-seed, claimed 9,000+ homes in 39 states, tested with Clayco and used text-to-book. **Today its site is an invite-only directory with six properties, "not a public booking platform."** The open-marketplace version shrank to corporate-only. This is the most important data point in the file.
- **Engine** (engine.com): group desk for 10+ rooms, per-diem approval rules. **WWStay**: group crew housing, unified payment. **ComfyWorkers**: UK-origin, now has Denver/Austin/San Antonio data-center pages, shortlist in an hour. **StayForWork**: crew motels/homes, launched a digital booking platform in January 2026.
- **Man camps**: Target Hospitality ($250M for about 1,100 people through 2030, implied about $155 per bed-night, my arithmetic; earlier deals of $43M and $550M+), Civeo, Corporate Mobile Housing. Cities are approving thousands of RV pads in Abilene (945 + 2,300 planned, about $1,550 a month).
- **Worker Homes** (gust.com/companies/worker-homes): a 2019 "Airbnb for blue-collar workers", one employee, apparently dormant. **PadSplit** already has an Abilene market page.
- **Furnished Finder**: tenant list includes construction workers, but no trades product. $199 per listing per year as of January 2026, $750 for hotels and complexes.

### Demand is real, but the cited evidence is old
- The Prineville numbers (17% of 230 rooms, 0% vacancy, Rosendin's 300-400 electricians) are from **2016-17**, not now.
- Current evidence is stronger: Stargate Abilene has about 6,000-6,400 workers in a town of 100k, with rents up about $1,000 to about $2,395 and motels and RV parks full. Meta Hyperion has 3,700 workers heading to 5,000. Iowa RV parks are full year-round.
- Per diems are in the $120-178 a day range. The GSA FY2026 rate is $166 ($107 lodging + $59 meals). Monthly rooms at $1,000-2,000 leave a worker room to keep part of the per diem.

### Size
- Furnished Finder's "$48M" is list-price math. Third-party estimates of its real revenue are $15-18M after 12 years and 300k listings, and the company has not disclosed revenue.
- Host-fee-only ceiling here: about 40 towns x about 1,250 open-market beds = about 50,000 beds. At 2 beds per listing and $199, that is about 25,000 listings, about $5M at 100% share.
- Crew-desk ceiling: 50,000 beds x $100-150 per bed-month x 12 = $60-90M at 100% share, but Hard Hat Housing, LodgeLink, Engine and WorkBnB already sit there with account managers and invoicing.
- The wave is cyclical. Each site runs 12-36 months, CBRE saw capacity under construction fall about 6% year over year in late 2025, and power connections are the bottleneck.

### Legal and age
- No wall. An 18-year-old can form an LLC and take Stripe payments.
- A flat listing fee with no rent handling avoids money-transmitter and tax-collection exposure.
- Stays of 30+ days are mostly outside transient lodging tax, though some local rules vary.
- The crew-desk invoice model makes him a principal: payment float (hosts paid before contractors pay net-30/60), insurance and liability. That is not workable on $10k.
- Fair-housing ad rules and local zoning of RV or room rentals apply to hosts. Facebook groups also ban commercial solicitation.

### Can he reach and close customers?
- Contractor ops managers and foremen will not take a cold call from an 18-year-old. Hard Hat Housing and LodgeLink win there on references.
- He lives nowhere near a build town and has 10-15 hours a week. Rural homeowners from Facebook groups are low-trust, low-traffic and slow to convert.
- **The plan contradicts itself.** Listing hosts "free first year" means $0 in the first 90 days, yet it promises "first 10 listing fees in 60-90 days." Real revenue needs a paid first offer.

## Numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Cost to first revenue | about $1,500 (domain, SMS, Stripe, a few ad tests, no travel) |
| Weeks to first revenue | about 8-10, only if the first offer is paid |
| Median case, year 3 | about $0.5M a year |
| Upside case | about $10M a year, and that needs 15%+ of all open-market beds nationwide |

## Deciding reason
Two things decide it. First, WorkBnB, the one funded open marketplace, retreated to an invite-only corporate directory with six properties. Second, the part with real money (contractor-paid crew housing) already has at least five operating players. What is left is a self-serve traveler-and-host listing site that tops out near $5M a year at full share and has a cold-start traffic problem. It is not a business that clears the "$10M+" bar honestly.

## Sharpened version (without shrinking it)
**Per Diem Keeper / "Stays by the Gate"**: a worker-side site for travelers who arrange their own housing (the segment incumbents ignore, since they all sell to contractor travel coordinators).
1. **Customer is a bulk operator, not a homeowner.** Start with RV parks, extended-stay motels, new apartment complexes, manufactured-home landlords and PadSplit-style operators near 3 build towns. Examples: the Abilene/Clyde RV-park cluster, Richland Parish LA, Iowa/Cedar Rapids RV parks, Prineville's Main Street Bunkhouse, the Parish Line Properties landlord. They have 50-2,000 beds and a marketing budget.
2. **Charge for results.** $50-100 per confirmed 30-day move-in, or $500-1,500 a year for a verified listing. No free year.
3. **Traveler product is the per-diem math**: GSA lodging rate vs. real local rent, truck parking, commute, split-a-house matching. Distribute via programmatic town pages and the job-board audiences (Energy Job Shop, Tradesmen Up) that already post "per diem + housing" jobs.
4. **Crew desk later, only as a lead-gen upsell** in towns where he already holds traveler demand, white-labeled through an existing group-desk vendor so he carries no float.
5. **Use the mentor** (AV-integration founder) for intros to GCs and subs on data-center AV scope, to get 2-3 reference crews.

This still tops out in the single-digit millions on my math. Run it only as a 60-day experiment, not as the flagship.

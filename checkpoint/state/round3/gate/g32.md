# G32 gate: Fee-only board CFO (S:R2-P013-044)

**Verdict: WEAK.** Not dead, not strong. The contingency model already exists, the unlicensed cost lines are small next to the real pain (insurance), and the "software + group-purchasing" layer either re-creates the conflict he is selling against or is already occupied.

## Who already does it
- **The FolSon Group** (NYC co-ops/condos): free second opinion, contingency. Projects split 50/50 once; ongoing operating savings shared for the first 2 years; fee is zero if no savings. Same model, not in Florida. Source: Habitat magazine, June 2023.
- **ProVendorConnect** (Tampa, launched Mar 2026): bid marketplace for HOAs/managers, free to buyers, vendors pay $89/month. Cites ~370k HOAs and >$100B contractor spend (company figures). Already holds the "bid-package tool" wedge in Florida.
- **FirstService Residential**: in-house "cost containment" plus FirstService Energy utility-bill audits (contingency, past-bill refunds; one 35-unit Manhattan condo recovered $158k). Administers >$6B of operating budgets and says it negotiates waste, insurance and cable rates.
- **Condo Control** (vendor portal, multi-bid, compliance) and **PayHOA** ($27.5M Series A, adding a Payables module): manager software that can add procurement.
- **Owner's-rep engineers** (Souffront, Superstructures): second opinions on big capital projects.
- **Bulk-telecom providers** (BlueStream, Elauwit, Live Oak): pay the association a "door fee"; vendor-side, not conflict-free.
- Free substitute: boards are told to get 3 bids; Florida now forces website postings of contracts and bids (25+ unit condos, from 1 Jan 2026).

## Can he reach and close the first customers?
- Buyer: volunteer board treasurer/president, usually older, with a manager as gatekeeper. Manager controls the financial reports and the agenda, and a "conflict-free" pitch implicitly accuses managers.
- Contingency plus a free review removes most of the credibility risk. Budget season is now: calendar-year associations adopt 2027 budgets in Nov-Dec 2026, and Fannie/Freddie reserve minimums step from 10% to 15% on 4 Jan 2027 (law-firm PDF, unverified by me).
- Association websites are owner-only, so there is no public lead list. First customers must come from family/mentor introductions and self-managed buildings.
- Mentor (AVI-SPL, commercial AV) is a trust signal, not a condo-board network.

## Legal and age
- **CAM license (real, cheap to clear).** Fla. Stat. 468.431(2) lists "preparing budgets or other financial documents", "negotiating contract terms" and "coordinating maintenance" as community association management when done for pay for associations >10 units or >$100k budget. A "board CFO" doing contract rebids for a fee can be read into that. Secondary sources: minimum age 18, no residency requirement, 16-hour course ($249-319), exam $49.50, DBPR fee $228, fingerprints extra, ~15-30 days. He qualifies by age. Confirm on DBPR. A CAM also falls under the 2024 conflict-of-interest disclosure regime (468.4335).
- **Insurance (real wall).** Advice or comparison for a fee needs a Chapter 626 license, and broker-to-unlicensed referral fees are scrutinized by DFS. Stay out, and take no broker compensation.
- **Kickbacks.** Florida makes knowingly accepting a kickback a third-degree felony for condo directors, officers and managers. Any vendor-paid fee (GPO admin fees, referral fees) would poison the "conflict-free" claim.
- Age: 18 can form an LLC and sign. Boards will ask for E&O/GL proof and references.

## The numbers (assumptions marked)
- National: 373k associations, 29.6M units, ~$124.2B assessments (CAI Foundation 2025 review; assessments figure from secondary sites) = ~$333k per association.
- Florida: ~27k condo associations, ~1.5M units (a Florida Realtors slide, source unclear). Avg ~55 units.
- Addressable unlicensed lines (utilities, trash, landscaping, elevator, pool, security, telecom, bank fees): ~30% of budget (my assumption) = ~$100-130k.
- Achievable savings 8-12% (my assumption; no independent benchmark found) = ~$10-13k/yr. Fee share 40% (FolSon 50%; one municipal audit memo 37%) = **~$4-5k/yr for ~2 years, then it rolls off.**
- $10M/yr needs ~2,000 active associations. $100M needs ~20-25k association-years every year, about 6% of every US association or most of Florida's condos. A service cannot do that.
- Median (solo + help, 3 years): ~$0.8M/yr. Upside (data/software layer, national): ~$20M/yr.

## Why not kill
The audit-and-recover machinery transfers, the Florida pain is real, the license wall is clearable for about $650, and cost to test is under $5k. Why not strong: small tail-limited fees, an existing model, a gatekept buyer, and the biggest line item (insurance) is off-limits.

## Sharpened version
Do not sell "board CFO". Sell a **Contract Renewal Watchdog**: AI reads every vendor contract and invoice, calendars auto-renewals and escalators, and runs the rebid 90 days out. The association pays a share of verified avoided increases, ongoing rather than a 2-year tail. Hook: free top-5 contract review in budget season. Never take vendor money. Long-run asset: a per-door and per-unit price benchmark built from thousands of contracts, sold to boards, managers, lenders and insurers. Pooled buying only if associations pay for it.

## Cost / timing
~$4,500 (CAM ~$650, LLC $125, E&O/GL ~$1.5k, attorney template ~$1.5k, tools ~$500; the last three are my estimates). ~14 weeks to first revenue: signed pilot in 4-6 weeks, first verified savings and invoice by week 12-16.

# G:ai_builder:0 FranchiseFile (credit report for franchise brands): verdict KILL

Checked 2026-10-25. About 14 searches plus direct reads of competitor sites.

## Deciding reason
Someone already built exactly this, and prices it at $19.99.

**FranchiseIQ (fddiq.com)** says it started in 2025. Its own site claims:
- 21,930 FDD filings across 6,755 brands, built from the Wisconsin, California and NASAA registries.
- 56,573 SBA 7(a) loans mapped to brands, with "actual default rates by brand".
- Upload-your-FDD analysis in under 8 minutes, red-flag detection across all 23 items, and AI-generated "attorney questions".
- A free "Franchise Distress Signals" tool covering 6,000+ brands (falling units, rising terminations).
- Pricing of $19.99 one-time for 10 reports. Its structured data also lists $49/month. It says "400+ franchise buyers" and "$2.4M in franchise investments analyzed". Those are its own marketing numbers.

That is the whole FranchiseFile thesis (FDD extraction joined to SBA outcomes, brand scorecards), shipped, at one-fifth of the proposed $99. Two further problems follow from it:
- **Willingness to pay.** After a year or more of operation, the one player doing this is still around 400 claimed buyers at $19.99. That is roughly $8k lifetime if the claim is true. Buyers appear to pay about $20, not $99.
- **Search traffic.** FranchiseIQ already has brand pages ("Chick-fil-A franchise cost and profit") and publishes the same SBA default numbers. The one independent builder who wrote up this category said people search for specific brands, not for "FDD analysis tool". That means brand-page SEO is the channel, and it is taken.

## Competitors (all checked)
- **FranchiseIQ** (fddiq.com): the exact clone described above.
- **Franchimp** (franchimp.com): an Austin-based franchise data platform. Its Pro tier is $2,000 for year one, then $167/month. It also sells single FDD downloads from $5 and runs monthly "franchise market update" posts. It claims "40,000+ users" in llms.txt, but the homepage counter shows 0, so I could not verify that.
- **ClearlyFDD** (clearlyfdd.com): turns an FDD into a workspace with a cited AI assistant. Capterra shows it starting at $99 with a free tier. It names franchise buyers, brokers, attorneys and lenders as its audience.
- **Contracko**: a free AI "FDD review" tool covering fees, territory and litigation.
- **Franchise Grade**: a Franchise Brokers Association tool that grades FDDs. Its validated data is for FBA members. It has roughly 1,282 indexed brand pages.
- **FRANdata**: the incumbent. It sells FDDs from a 50,000+ document archive, runs the lender-side FUND Score, and says 18,000+ SBA loans back the score (High Risk below 400 means about 15% charge-off). It also publishes the SBA-default commentary the pitch cites.
- Others: the $25k Acquire.com listing the pitch already names, Apify scrapers for the Wisconsin and Minnesota registries (including an Item 19 parser), and an Indie Hackers builder who shipped an FDD analyzer plus a free Item 19 tool.

The pitch's claim that "I found no consumer-facing database of extracted FDD metrics" is false. There are at least four.

## Can Asher reach and close buyers?
- **Reach: yes.** r/franchising, LinkedIn and franchise Facebook groups are reachable, and a free-for-the-first-20 offer would get replies.
- **Closing at $99: no.** The same buyer can get a comparable report for $19.99, a free one from Contracko, or a $5 download. Franchise attorneys charge $1,500 to $5,000 for a flat-fee FDD review. The $99 sits in between with no differentiator except accuracy claims.
- **Advisers are weak payers.** Franchise CPAs and consultants who want a $49 white-label report would be competing with their own billable review.
- **Credibility is a real but secondary problem.** Prospects investing $100k to $1M will not take an 18-year-old's grade as authoritative.

## Legal and age issues
- **The data layer has no license wall.** Minnesota says CARDS documents "are public and may be copied". I found no published scraping terms for Wisconsin. Apify scrapers for both registries exist, but I did not confirm that bulk scraping is permitted.
- **The big-money path hits broker law.** The pitch's route to $25M is franchisor-paid lead generation. That makes him a franchise broker under the NASAA Model Franchise Broker Registration Act (published May 2026), whose definition is broad enough to cover lead generators and consultants paid by franchisors. California SB 919 requires annual broker registration ($450 initial) once funded, and takes effect on the later of July 1, 2026 or 12 months after funding. Illinois bars paying referral fees to unregistered brokers.
- **Two lesser risks:** defamation from publishing "closure rate" grades, and unlicensed-practice-of-law exposure from "read my FDD" breakdowns. Both are manageable with citations and disclaimers.
- **His age is not a blocker for buyer-paid software.** The blockers are price, competitors, and broker registration for the profitable version.

## Numbers
- **Cost to first revenue:** about $2,500 (LLM extraction, hosting, domain). It is cheap because FranchiseIQ shows the data pipeline is easy, which is also why the market is crowded.
- **Weeks to first revenue:** 4 to 6 at $19 to $99, a handful of sales.
- **Bottom-up buyer-paid market (my estimate):**
  - About 15,000 to 40,000 individual first-time franchise buyers a year (the 15,000+ units a year is IFA-cited, 53% of units are multi-unit, and the buyer count is my own inference).
  - 5% buy a report at $20 to $99, so $15k to $200k a year.
  - Even at 20% share, it is under $1M.
- **Median case:** about $50k a year. It splits with FranchiseIQ, Franchimp and ClearlyFDD, with SEO already taken.
- **Upside:** about $2M a year. This needs franchisor-paid placement, which means broker registration, an independence-firewall problem, and competing with FranNet, Franzy (raised $1M pre-seed) and Franchise Grade.
- **The pitch's own "$3-10M base case" fails.** It rests on the $351-per-lead and $17,550-per-signing figures the pitch itself marks "unverified" and "third-party summary", and I could not confirm them.

## Sharpened version (kill is still the verdict)
This is the only variant that stays big without shrinking. It does not fix the competition, and I would not spend the weekly budget on it.

**FranchiseFile Placement:** the free, source-cited scorecards (Item 20 closure and transfer rates, SBA outcomes, litigation) are the SEO and trust layer, not the product. Revenue is franchisor-paid success fees, as with FranNet and Franzy. Franchisors pay roughly 40 to 50% of the initial franchise fee, with a $12k minimum at many brokerages. The business must:
- register as a broker in every state that requires it;
- publish its own compensation disclosure;
- stay data-first so buyers trust it over commission-led brokers.

First test, costing under $500: do the 30 CPA and consultant emails and 20 free FDD breakdowns, and see whether any prospect says they would pay more than $20 or be matched to a franchisor.

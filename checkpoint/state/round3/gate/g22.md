# G:genz_life:2 Wager Ledger (tax-and-truth ledger for sportsbook and prediction-market users): verdict KILL

Checked 2026-10-25. About 22 searches and page reads, including direct reads of competitor sites, the IRS-data summary and the House bill coverage.

## Deciding reason
A near-clone already ships at a quarter of the proposed price, the paying pool is about 600k people (not 2.5M), and the "why now" may be repealed before filing season.

**taxwagero.com** (a Zewlin Group Holdings LLC product) says it:
- logs wins and losses across platforms and estimates federal and state gambling tax with the 2026 90% cap
- bulk-imports CSV/PDF/Excel from FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, Caesars, bet365, Kalshi, Underdog and theScore Bet, and says it works for Polymarket
- exports tax-ready PDF/CSV and fires W-2G alerts
- prices Pro at **$19.99/yr** (listed as pre-launch), with a free tier

That is the Wager Ledger feature list minus the "Wrapped" and CPA handoff, at one-quarter of the $79 price. I could not find reviews or traction numbers for it, so treat its claims as marketing. It still sets the price anchor.

## What the searches found on the idea's premises
1. **The 90% cap may not survive.** The House Ways and Means Committee voted 38-5 on Sept 16, 2026 to attach full 100% restoration (FULL HOUSE Act text) to H.R. 10357, the Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act. The change would be retroactive to tax year 2026. Titus wants a floor vote before Jan 1. As of Oct 9 and Oct 26 coverage it had not passed the House or Senate. A 38-5 bipartisan vote on a roughly $1-2B cost makes repeal a real possibility, not a tail risk. If it passes, the phantom-income hook is gone and the product is a plain tracker competing with free ones.
2. **The pain pool is itemizers who lose a lot, not "heavy bettors".**
   - Losses are deductible only if you itemize. IRS data for tax year 2023 (via casino.org): 592,864 itemizers claimed gambling losses, deducting $42.7B against $50.1B of winnings (about $72k average loss). That is under 4% of itemizers.
   - That group skews to casino, poker and slots players, older and higher income. It is not mainly Gen Z sports bettors.
   - The idea's "2.5M high-volume bettors" figure is about 4x that pool, and was the idea's own estimate.
   - Non-itemizers owe tax on gross winnings regardless of tracking, so they have little reason to pay for a ledger.
3. **Prediction markets are a separate and bigger tax-complexity pool, but incumbents are already in it.**
   - Kalshi's help center lists 1099-INT, -MISC, -B and -DA. It ties the 1099-B to crypto transfers, not event contracts, and says trading shows up in a P&L statement. Several guides repeat that Kalshi does not send a comprehensive 1099-B. Sources conflict, and I found no sign that this changes for 2026.
   - Treatment is unsettled: ordinary income, Section 1256 (60/40, called aggressive by practitioners), gambling or property. The IRS has issued no guidance.
   - Users are mostly small. Pew (about 12k Polymarket accounts, six weeks): median 46 trades, average spend about $600, average net result under -$2, with 7% up over $1,000 and 9% down over $1,000. Only the roughly 15% with real P&L need paid help.
4. **Price compression.** Anchors: taxwagero $19.99, CasinoIQ $18.99, free calculators and free Pikkit/Action tracking. Pikkit's $199.99 Pro is for CLV and analytics, not tax. A $79 tax pack sells against $20 and free.
5. **Crypto-tax comparison.** Koinly and CoinLedger each claim 700k-1M+ customers at $49-$199/yr. Third-party revenue estimates are only about $3-7M (TokenTax $2.8M, Koinly $6.5M, both unverified). That shows the consumer tax-ledger category is a real but modest-revenue business, even with a far larger taxable-trader base than sports betting.

## Legal, age and access
- **Age:** he cannot be a sportsbook user (21+), so bettor credibility comes through friends. Prediction markets are generally 18+ (not verified in this check). Neither is a legal barrier to selling software.
- **License:** software that models tax does not need a PTIN or CPA. Preparing returns for pay, or giving advice on which classification to claim, would. The "scenario engine that shows each treatment" and the "CPA-handoff tier" sit near that line. The handoff needs real CPAs, whom he does not have.
- **Data access is the hard engineering wall.** Linked-account sync is what Pikkit uses (OpticOdds, quote-based, no public price) and Action BetSync (no partner program found). Scraping sportsbook logins breaks terms of service. A bootstrapper is limited to CSV, statements and screenshot OCR, which is what taxwagero already does. Email-receipt parsing needs Gmail restricted-scope approval (I did not verify its cost or timeline).
- **App stores:** trackers such as Pikkit are listed (rated 17+). Apple's real-money gambling rules (5.3.4) apply to apps that take bets. Keep wording and screenshots clearly outside betting.
- **Brand:** a harm-reduction layer and loss-chasing detector is defensible and a good shareable hook, but you are still marketing to people who gamble.

## Numbers
- **Cost to first revenue:** about $1,500 (domain, $99 developer account, OCR/LLM costs, landing page). Excludes any Gmail security audit.
- **Weeks to first revenue:** about 10. An MVP by mid-December is plausible, and the first sale is likely in January-February filing season. The $49 founding price is plausible for a few dozen early buyers, but not at scale next to a $19.99 competitor.
- **Bottom-up size:**
  - Addressable payers: about 600-800k itemizing gambling-loss claimants, plus about 100-200k prediction-market traders with material P&L, so roughly 0.7-1M.
  - Median case: 1-2% share at about $40 gives 7-20k payers, or $0.3-0.8M. After weighting for repeal risk and price compression, I put the median at about **$0.2M/yr by year 3**.
  - Upside: 5% of 1M at $60, plus CPA seats or a white-label deal, gives about **$3M/yr**.
  - The idea's $10M needs about 127k payers at $79, which is more than 12% of the whole addressable pool against a $19.99 rival.

## Teen blockers
- **Credibility with CPAs and platforms:** real. The CPA-referral and white-label "tax center" paths both need trust he does not have yet.
- **Data partnerships (OpticOdds, Kalshi, sportsbooks):** real. They are enterprise sales to an 18-year-old with no traction.
- **Capital:** not a blocker. Spend is under $2k.
- **Age and legal:** not a blocker for selling software. The tax-advice line and the CPA tier are the risk.

## Sharpened version (only if he wants a cheap bet, not a flagship)
- **Do not build "Wager Ledger" as written.** Fix the weaknesses the research exposed:
  1. Pick prediction markets as the lead wedge, not the sportsbook 90% cap.
  2. Differentiate on what incumbents skip: a year-end mark-to-market view of unsettled event contracts (CoinTracker ignores open positions), a reconciliation of every Kalshi/Polymarket P&L statement and 1099 against the trade export, and a one-page CPA packet. Show the treatments as side-by-side scenarios, never as a recommendation.
  3. Keep "Wrapped" as the free viral piece: real ROI after vig and tax, deposits vs withdrawals.
  4. Price at $29-39, since $79 is not defensible next to $19.99.
- **Kill test, set before building:** zero build beyond a landing page and the free calculator until Dec 31. Continue only if all of these hold:
  - H.R. 10357 has not passed the House or Senate by Dec 31 (if it has, stop).
  - Neither Pikkit nor Kalshi has announced a tax export.
  - At least 300 emails and 20 paid pre-orders at $29-39 by Feb 1.
- **Honest ceiling:** this is a $0.2-3M/yr consumer tool. It beats a part-time job and fits his skills, but it does not meet the $10M+ bar, and shrinking it further would not change that.

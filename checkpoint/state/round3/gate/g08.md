# G08 gate: S:I1113 Recurring-Route Repricing Desk for Service Owners

**Verdict: WEAK (close to kill as pitched).** Real pain and a cheap, fast first sale, but the pieces already ship from the platforms, the API surface is mostly gated, and the honest ceiling as pitched is about $1M a year.

## What I checked (about 25 searches and fetches, 2026-10-25)
Caveat: developer.getjobber.com and the Jobber community returned 403 or Cloudflare blocks, so Jobber API field-level facts and forum quotes come from search summaries, not direct reads. Marked UNVERIFIED below.

## 2. Can he reach the platform data? (biggest structural problem)
- The pitch says "built on Jobber, Housecall Pro and Service Autopilot APIs." Only Jobber looks open.
- Jobber: public GraphQL API, marketplace requires Jobber review and approval. While in Draft, the app is blocked if it connects to more than 5 paying Jobber accounts, and testing on existing customers must be coordinated with a Jobber rep. Fine for the first 5 customers, then a Jobber approval gate. Jobber is a multi-industry platform (about 100k customer businesses as of May 2026, company-reported). Consumer-services plus landscaping is roughly 20-30% by third-party estimates, so about 20-30k reachable accounts.
- Housecall Pro: the API and webhooks reportedly need the top MAX plan (secondary sources, UNVERIFIED).
- Skimmer: public API announced June 2025, reportedly top-tier and sales-led (secondary source; devportal page did not say, UNVERIFIED).
- Service Autopilot: no public developer portal found.
- Workaround: CSV or QuickBooks upload plus owner-entered labor and vehicle cost, with drive time estimated from addresses. This works anywhere but means the "read-only connect" magic is gone, and the free MyRouteOnline and Pooldial tools already cover the same inputs.

## 3. Willingness to pay and real pain
- Pain is real. A Jobber cleaning business with about 700 recurring clients has an open feature request for price increases to carry to existing jobs (price changes in Products and Services apply only to new line items). Jobber forum threads on annual price-increase reminders show owners using manual workarounds. (From search summaries.)
- Break-even math is strongly in the owner's favor: at 40% gross margin, a 10% increase survives losing 20% of customers. Pool guides say a 10% increase survives about 9% churn. Anecdotes: a 15% franchise-wide increase did not raise cancellations; a 5% increase saw "minimal push back." No measured lawn-care churn dataset exists.
- Counter-evidence: owners already know they underprice. The blocker is nerve and execution, which free letters and bulk tools address, so a $39-79/mo add-on competes with free. Skimmer itself doubled a tier to $2/pool and users complained, so price sensitivity on software is real.
- Precedent for single-purpose add-ons: NiceJob (reviews, integrates with Jobber and Housecall Pro) hit about $1.5-1.7M ARR with about 3,200 customers by 2019 at roughly $75/mo. That is the realistic shape of a good outcome for a feature-sized add-on, not $10M+.

## 4. Size
- Counts (IBISWorld 2025): landscaping about 693k, pool cleaning about 79k (Pooldial says about 125k), pest about 33k (NPMA says 16.6k firms). Most lawn businesses are solo with no software.
- Software-using recurring-route businesses with 50+ accounts: my estimate 150-250k across lawn, pool, cleaning, pest and similar.
- As pitched ($39-79/mo, about $700/yr): 3-8% of 20-30k Jobber accounts gives about 600-2,400 customers, or roughly $0.4-1.7M ARR. Reaching $10M needs about 14k customers, 6-9% of the whole software-using base with no platform help. Fails the round-2 "path to $100M" rule outright.
- Sharpened version (below): ARPU rises because the fee is tied to realized lift. A 100-stop pool route at $160/mo is about $192k/yr; an 8% lift is about $15k/yr; a 15% share is about $2.3k per customer per year. 7-8k customers at about $1.8k gives about $14M. That is a strong-execution case, not a base case.

## 5. Legal and age
- Age: 18 is fine for forming an LLC, signing the Jobber developer agreement (terms not read, UNVERIFIED) and taking payments.
- No license wall. Sending price-increase notices is ordinary commerce. Real, low-cost items: state notice rules for changing recurring or auto-renewing contracts, pest-control state rules, and a privacy policy plus minimal-scope OAuth because he touches customers' names and addresses. Do not give contract advice; ship templates with "check your agreement" language.
- Credibility: lawn/pool owners skew 45-55, so an 18-year-old is not trusted cold. A self-serve, free-first, ROI-proof tool (margin check on their own numbers) sidesteps this the way NiceJob did. Young lawn, pet-waste and pool operators in his Gen Z world are credible early peers.

## 6. First customers
- Channels: Jobber Community finance forum (people already posting about price increases), Jobber Marketplace after approval, r/LawnCarePros (about 5k), LawnSite and Turf forums, owner Facebook groups, Pool Nation podcast and PoolDial audience, lawn YouTube creators (Lawn Care Nut and others), direct DMs to local lawn, pool and cleaning companies.
- First 10 named customer types: (1) 3-10 crew lawn mowing firm on Jobber Grow, 100-400 accounts; (2) residential cleaning company with 300-700 recurring clients (the Jobber forum poster profile); (3) pool route owner with 80-150 pools on Skimmer or Jobber; (4) small pest firm with quarterly plans; (5) pet-waste removal route (young owners); (6) window-cleaning route; (7) lawn-aeration and fertilization programs; (8) snow-plow seasonal contract routes; (9) mobile bin-cleaning routes; (10) classmates' and friends-of-family lawn businesses for testimonials.
- Timing: owners plan January increases with 30-60 day notice, so the selling window opens mid-November. Pooldial advises January or renewal-date increases.
- Mentor (AVI-SPL founder) has no direct line into this vertical; family network is unproven here.

## 7. Verdict logic against his six criteria
1. First paying customer in 60-90 days under $10k: PASS (about 5 weeks, under $1k).
2. Buyers who buy from an 18-year-old: PASS (small owners, self-serve).
3. No license wall: PASS.
4. Uses his edges: PARTIAL (builds fast, reuses freight-audit contingency model; Gen Z distribution is weak for 45-55 year-old owners).
5. Credible path to $10M+: FAIL as pitched, borderline only with the contingency-fee variant.
6. Not crowded: FAIL (every FSM sells bulk repricing and letters; profit-per-stop exists in PoolDial and Jobber Grow).

## 8. Sharpened version (does not shrink it)
**Raise Desk: the price-increase autopilot for recurring-route businesses.** Platform-agnostic. Ingests Jobber via API where open, and CSV or QuickBooks export elsewhere. Estimates per-stop margin with owner-entered crew and vehicle cost plus address-based drive time. Ranks accounts, drafts tiered notices, sends them in waves from the owner's own email or text, tracks churn per wave and writes the new prices back (Jobber mutation or an import file in the format LawnPro and Briostack accept). Free margin check, then a success fee (about 10-15% of the first-year realized lift, capped) or $49/mo flat. Rides the freight-audit contingency model he has already built. First sale: a done-for-you Raise audit for $99-199 sold to 10 Gen Z-adjacent and Jobber-community owners in November for January increases.

## Bottom line
Run it only as a cheap 6-week experiment tied to the January pricing season, and kill it if fewer than 5 of 30 owners pay for the margin check. Do not treat it as a $10M candidate yet.

## 3. The 60-second call

> "Hi, is this [name]? I'm [full name]. I'm a high-school senior who built a freight-invoice audit service for companies that ship on pallets. Can I ask you one question and then you can tell me to go away? Do you have your own accounts with the carriers and pay the freight bills yourselves, or does the customer or a broker pay?"
>
> *(If they pay:)* "Good. Federal law gives you 180 days from receiving a bill to contest a charge. After that carriers deny it whether it's right or not. AFS, a big audit firm, reported errors on about 4.5% of roughly 250,000 LTL invoices it checked, and most were small. I don't know what's in yours. I'd like to find out for free. If you want me to file and chase the claims after that, we talk terms then. Can I send you a two-line email so you can look me up first? What's the best address?"

Rules: never say "five to ten percent" (FACT-CHECK graded it weak), never say "cheaper", "AI", or "every invoice, not a sample", never quote a competitor's fee, never invent a client. If asked how old you are, say it plainly. Send the follow-up email within 10 minutes. Pin the callback ("Thursday?") before hanging up.

## 6. Unit economics

**Formula:** fee = annual LTL spend × found % × share carriers pay × fee %.

| Annual LTL spend | 0.5% found | 1% found | 2% found |
|---|---|---|---|
| $500k | $225 | $450 | $900 |
| $1M | $450 | **$900** | $1,800 |
| $2M | $900 | $1,800 | $3,600 |
| $5M | $2,250 | $4,500 | $9,000 |

(15% fee, carriers pay 60% of what is found, steady-state, per year, from `refined.md`. Year one is about 1.3 times steady-state because of the 180-day backlog. At 20%, multiply by 1.33.)

**Reading the table against this list:**

- **Floor:** under about $1M of LTL spend the fee is under $450 a year, and the claim friction (a $75 to $200 liftgate yields a $11 to $30 fee) eats it. Of the 19 targets, I would guess 6 to 10 are above the floor. This is **[est]**, and the screening call decides it.
- **Time:** about 60 hours per client-year at median (180 hours for 3 clients), so about $15 an hour at $1M and 1% found. Upside, around 10 clients via a channel, is $80 to $105 an hour.
- **Cash timing:** invoices received about Nov 16 to 30, first look back in 48 hours, signature (if any) in early December, claims filed in December, carriers acknowledge in 30 days and decide in 60 (49 CFR 378.7 and 378.8), credits in about Feb to Mar 2027. **No revenue before February.** Plan on zero until then.
- **One-time costs:** attorney consult $300 to $600, LLC filing about $100 to $200 depending on state, domain and mailbox about $150 a year, E&O unknown. Budget under $1,500, which can exceed the median first year's fees.

**The $500 diagnostic as briefed.**

- One month at $1M annual spend is about $83k. At 1% found that is $833, so the $500 floor is earned. At $300k annual spend it is $250, so refunded.
- Findings must reach $500 in one month, so the $500 is only earned above about $600k of annual spend at 1% found, or $1.2M at 0.5% (and about $400k at 1.5%). Below that it is a refund and a bad first impression.
- "Refunded if under $500, credited against fees" is a contingent fee with a floor, so it hits the same licensing question as the 20%.
- **Recommendation:** drop it from the cold funnel. Free first look for the first three sets. If a prospect insists on paying, a flat $400 report-only (client files and keeps the money) is the legal hedge.
- **20% vs 15%:** 20% is the AP-recovery category norm, but Eller sells 15% against a free first audit. At these sizes the gap is $45 to $90 a year on a $1M account. Credibility is worth more. Revisit 20% after three documented recoveries.

**Cold funnel on this list [est]:** 19 targets × about 3 dials = 57 dials. At a 5.4% connect rate that is about 3 conversations. A 5% yes rate gives 0.15 sets from phone, plus about 0.1 to 0.3 from email, so **0.2 to 0.5 sets cold**. The mentor and family asks carry the plan (refined.md puts each near 1.0 set, an assumption). At a combined mean of 1.5, P(at least one set) is about 78%.

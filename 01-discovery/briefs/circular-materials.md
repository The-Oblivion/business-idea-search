# Briefing: Waste, Recycling, Circular Economy, Critical Minerals, Materials (as of 2026-09-28)

**Method caveat:** The session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was already exhausted, so this brief is built from ~40 direct WebFetch reads, mostly Resource Recycling and Waste Dive. Coverage of lithium/cobalt/copper prices, EU DPP/battery passport and broad funding tallies is thin. Numbers below are as reported by those outlets.

## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)

- **Recycling has become a national-security asset.** On Aug 27, 2026 a BIS temporary final rule (Defense Production Act, about 1 year) made US sellers of **black mass and tungsten scrap allocate 100% of monthly sales to US buyers**. BIS says it "may determine that additional materials" will be added. Exceptions exist only for toll-processing with return to the US. https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/08/05/new-federal-rule-restricts-black-mass-tungsten-scrap-exports/
- **Government as equity partner.** The DoD-MP Materials deal is $1.25B including a $400M equity stake, and MP broke ground on a Texas magnet campus. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/02/27/mp-materials-breaks-ground-on-rare-earth-magnet-campus-in-north-texas/ DOE took 5% warrants in Lithium Americas. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2025/10/08/analysis-doe-takes-equity-stake-in-lithium-americas-deal/ DOE announced $134M for rare-earth/critical-mineral demonstrations in June 2026.
- **China's grip is measured, not rhetorical.** Per the IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026, China holds more than 75% of battery pre-treatment capacity and about 90% of material recovery capacity. Average recycling rates are about 10% today, heading to about 20% by 2040 (cobalt about 16% to 25%, copper about 10% to 20%). https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/07/17/recycling-rates-for-rare-earths-could-double-by-2040/ China supplies virtually 100% of magnet-manufacturing equipment. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/03/11/e-scrap-export-pause-urged-to-keep-rare-earth-scrap-in-us/
- **EPR is shifting from law to litigation and operations.**
  - Oregon's EPR survived a dormant-Commerce-Clause challenge on Aug 27, 2026. The wholesalers' group NAW appealed to the 9th Circuit on Sept 24. https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/25/naw-appeals-oregon-epr-ruling/
  - Circular Action Alliance (CAA) collected $145.5M and spent $56.5M in 2025, and it retains final fee-setting authority. https://resource-recycling.com/plastics/2026/06/16/caa-seeks-industry-input-on-epr-fees/
  - California signed SB 501 (medium-format battery EPR, 60% recycling-efficiency floor) on Sept 20. https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/24/newsom-signs-battery-epr-expansion-vetoes-sb-54-fund-bill/
  - California also vetoed the $5B Plastic Pollution Mitigation Fund bill (SB 1180) and AB 2253 (recycled-content claim substantiation). The AB 2253 veto cited "economic headwinds." https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/28/newsom-nixes-recycled-content-claims-bill/
  - Colorado's programs are just starting. Interchange 360 (petroleum containers) has diverted about 2,500 lb in six months across 10 collection facilities. https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/17/colorados-epr-programs-move-from-planning-to-practice/
- **Recycled-content mandates are being delayed.** New Jersey pushed food-contact PCR from Jan 2027 to Dec 2029, citing food-grade supply. Recyclers say HDPE blow-mold supply is "not constrained." https://resource-recycling.com/plastics/2026/09/10/new-jersey-pushes-back-pcr-mandate-to-2030/
- **Tariff whiplash.** Canada put 15-50% retaliatory tariffs on US goods effective Sept 9, 2026, and the US has 50% tariffs on Canadian goods. Canadian PET carries 16.5% duty plus 13.6% anti-dumping duty. https://resource-recycling.com/plastics/2026/09/11/canada-tariffs-latest-in-new-normal-for-rpet/

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points

- **Battery fires.** 49 facility fires in Aug 2026 (tied for the 10-year August high), 293 year to date, and 74 catastrophic fires (over $400K each) year to date, versus 71 in all of 2024. Publicly reported fires cover only two-alarm-or-greater events, and actual totals are estimated at about 6x higher. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/09/23/facility-fires-remain-high-across-us-canada-in-august/
  - Vapes are a major driver. Biffa saw about 480,000 vapes in two months.
  - Average severe incident cost is about $1.5M (EuRIC estimate). States have not decided who pays for the losses. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/09/21/who-should-pay-for-lithium-ion-battery-fire-damage/
- **Storage permits.** A recycler must run separate collection and recycling sites because a hazardous-waste storage permit is "an expensive, multi-year process." The BRACE Act (HR 9615) passed the House by voice vote on Sept 15. The Senate must act by Dec 18, 2026, and EPA rules would follow 18 months after enactment. https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/24/house-passes-bill-to-ease-lithium-ion-battery-storage-rules/
- **Plastics recycler collapse.** Feedstock PET bales hit a historic low of 1.44 cents/lb in April 2026. More than a quarter of US PET recycling capacity disappeared in 12 months (7 of about 30 reclaimers closed). Five lawmakers are asking USTR for trade remedies on PET imports from Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, India, Thailand and South Korea. https://resource-recycling.com/plastics/2026/09/25/us-lawmakers-urge-protection-of-domestic-rpet/
- **Food-grade projects frozen.** Projects two years in development are paused and supply deals cancelled after the NJ delay. https://resource-recycling.com/plastics/2026/09/28/njs-pcr-delay-risks-making-its-own-excuse-permanently-true/
- **Claims verification is unresolved.** Mass-balance accounting is contested. One side says it is a "compliance wall" of enormous cost, the other that companies claim "100% recycled" by buying credits. No standard exists.
- **Rare-earth economics.** The bottleneck is "collection and disassembly rather than recovery chemistry." Hard-drive magnet removal often costs more than the material is worth. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/09/24/why-only-some-rare-earth-sources-are-economically-viable/
- **Textiles.** California landfills 1.2M tons a year at $99M in disposal costs. SB 707 fee levels are unspecified and the US recycling rate is 14.7%. https://resource-recycling.com/recycling/2026/02/16/report-details-how-to-make-ca-textile-recycling-work/

## 3. Where money is flowing (and what is crowded)

- **Rare-earth recovery is hot.**
  - Cyclic Materials closed a $75M Series C ($162M total, Canada Growth Fund $25M) for a Mesa, AZ plant. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/01/29/cyclic-materials-closes-on-usd-75-million-for-domestic-rare-earths-recovery/
  - Paladin EnviroTech (founded about 1.5 years ago) has 600K sq ft across 7 states and is raising $70M for Europe.
  - IonicRE and Advanced Magnet Lab hold a DLA contract of $2M. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/06/08/ionicre-partnership-supports-recycled-rare-earth-supply-chain-for-defense-magnets/
- **Battery recycling has split.** Ascend Elements filed Chapter 11 on Apr 9, 2026 after $164M and $316M DOE grants were cancelled. Li-Cycle went bankrupt in May 2025 despite a $475M DOE loan commitment. Redwood raised $350M (Eclipse, NVentures) and pivoted to second-life energy storage (Rivian, GM). https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/04/13/battery-recycler-ascend-elements-files-for-bankruptcy/
- **ITAD is being bought.** Sumitomo acquired GreenTek (1.2M+ devices processed). https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/07/14/sumitomo-bets-on-ai-data-centers-with-greentek-deal/
- **Organics is consolidating.** Vanguard Renewables bought Generate Upcycle's 5 digesters (565,000 tons a year, 1.1B cu ft RNG). Generate Capital fully exited organics after entering in 2016. https://www.wastedive.com/news/vanguard-renewables-acquire-generate-upcycle-north-america/830873/
- **Crowded or capital-destroying:** commodity plastics recycling, greenfield black-mass and cathode plants, hardware-heavy solar recycling (Solarcycle paused a Georgia plant in 2025), and generic AI sorting.

## 4. Underserved gaps and things incumbents cannot do

- **Feedstock timing gap.** Manufacturing scrap is about two-thirds of North American battery feedstock through 2030, and EV end-of-life won't dominate until after 2040. Asset-heavy players built for a wave that has not arrived.
- **Battery-fire liability is unpriced.** There is no owner of the catastrophic-loss problem: haulers, MRFs, insurers, producers and cities all deflect. Fire detection and insurance data are fragmented.
- **Front-of-pipe disassembly and pre-processing.** Paladin's insight is to extract magnets from steel and PCB streams before refining. Standard refiners leave "15 to 22 metals" outside reporting and recovery.
- **EPR compliance for small producers.** About 5 CAA states, a fee committee, opaque fees, and NAW's 3-5% margin wholesalers face reporting burden. Nobody owns the small-brand experience.
- **Recycled-content verification.** A trusted, auditable identity-preserved or mass-balance ledger is needed and neither incumbents nor brands want to own it.
- **Domestic black-mass toll processing.** The BIS rule created a captive domestic feed, with toll-processing exceptions available.
- **Hyperscaler retirement data.** Iron Mountain's report has no data on retired servers, reuse split or chain of custody, so end-of-life hardware is an accountability blind spot.

## 5. Weird signals

- **Hyperscalers are pushing processors** to pull rare earths out of the steel stream (Paladin). AI capex is creating rare-earth pull from waste processors.
- **DRAM prices are up 400% since the start of 2024**, and data centers turn over hardware in 12-18 months. A single GPU server rack can be worth over $1M, and one client gained "eight figures" just by pre-triaging refurbish versus scrap. https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/08/07/data-center-evolution-creates-itad-opportunity/
- **Black mass and tungsten scrap are now export-restricted like defense goods.** Watch which scrap streams BIS adds next.
- **Bale prices at 1.44 c/lb while food-grade supply is called "constrained"** implies a sorting and quality-tier mismatch, not scarcity.
- **India earmarked about $166M** to subsidize recycling capacity, and EU rare-earth recycled-content obligations phase in 2028-2032.

# Food & Agriculture Briefing (as of 2026-09-28)

Coverage note: the 200-call web-search budget ran out mid-research, so the food-waste, farm-software and generic-semaglutide angles are thin. Figures marked (aggregator) come from low-authority sources and should be verified.

## 1. Biggest shifts, last 12-18 months

- **GLP-1s now move grocery baskets.**
  - Households cut grocery spend 5.3% within 6 months of starting a GLP-1 (more than 8% for higher-income households) and restaurant spend about 8% ([Cornell via Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29532-glp-1-users-cut-food-spending-by-53), [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/01/260112001029.htm)).
  - Users buy fewer sweets (61%) and salty snacks (56%) and more fresh produce (44%) and packaged protein (35%) ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/consumer-markets/library/glp-1-consumer-trends.html), [FTI](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/glp-1-drugs-rewriting-rules-food-beverage)).
  - The oral Wegovy pill launched in January 2026 at $149-299/month self-pay. It passed 5M total scripts across the GLP-1 launches by Q2 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/05/first-glp-1-pill-for-obesity-from-novo-nordisk-launches-in-the-us.html)). Roughly 12% of Americans now use a GLP-1 ([Food Chain Magazine](https://foodchainmagazine.com/americas-protein-craze-is-driving-a-global-whey-shortage/)).
  - One estimate puts the annual US CPG sales hit at about $32B, with snacks and confectionery down 12.4% (aggregator: [mealfan](https://mealfan.com/glp-1-restaurant-cpg-impact-2026/)).
- **Protein is a supply crisis.**
  - 70% of Americans say they are trying to eat more protein, up from 59% four years ago.
  - Whey protein concentrate is up 108% in two years, isolate reaches $14/lb, and some suppliers are sold out through 2026. Cottage cheese consumption is up 15-20% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/28/america-cant-get-enough-protein-the-dairy-industry-cant-keep-up.html), [GreenQueen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/whey-shortage-price-alternative-protein-fermentation-plant-based/)).
  - The binding constraint is downstream whey-processing capacity and cheese output, not raw milk ([UW Extension](https://farms.extension.wisc.edu/articles/high-protein-whey-and-cottage-cheese-a-structural-explainer/)).
- **Input shock from the Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz.**
  - About a third of seaborne fertilizer trade transits Hormuz. Urea spiked from about $400 to more than $850/t in April, then fell back to about $453 by June ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/fertilizer-prices-surge-as-strait-of-hormuz-disruptions-tighten-), [WTO](https://www.wto.org/english/blogs_e/data_blog_e/blog_dta_10jul26_451_e.htm), [farmdoc](https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2026/03/strait-of-hormuz-closure-and-fertilizer-supply-risks-for-us-agriculture.html)).
  - Farm-side figures: diesel up 80%, fertilizer up 15% year over year, farm bankruptcies up 19% in the 12 months to June. Major row crops are projected to lose money for a fourth straight year ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2026-09-25/american-farmers-are-hurting-and-worried-about-their-futures)).
  - Soybeans lose about $75 per harvested acre, exports run 15-20% behind normal, and a $12B aid package was approved in December 2025. Nearly half of 400 farmers surveyed by Purdue said they are worse off than a year ago ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/already-under-financial-pressure-farmers-squeezed-further-by-tariffs-and-iran-war)).
- **Beef.** Retail beef hit a record $9.64/lb in April (+13% year over year) with the smallest herd in 75 years. The Mexican feeder-cattle halt removes about 1.2M head a year, and New World screwworm has been confirmed in Texas cattle ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/09/business/what-consumers-should-know-about-screwworm), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/beef-prices-hit-record-in-may-as-screwworm-impact-still-looms), [Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/swe/2026/swe2612)).
- **Regulatory clocks.**
  - FDA aims to phase out petroleum-based dyes by December 2027. Walmart private label goes dye-free in January 2027.
  - Products containing FD&C dyes are about 4% of items but $57.8B in sales. Demand for natural colorants is projected to rise 400-500%, and the US is 98% import-dependent for annatto. Reformulation costs $50k-500k per SKU and takes 12-24+ months to validate ([Bakery&Snacks](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/09/01/us-food-dye-phase-out-puts-natural-color-supply-chain-under-pressure/)).
  - 22 states have SNAP soda/candy restrictions in effect or pending, though a June 2026 federal ruling (Aragon v. Rollins) blocked waivers in five of them ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/nexstar_media_wire/5796610-4-more-states-will-add-restrictions-on-snap-purchases-in-april/)).
  - FDA and USDA sent a first federal ultra-processed food definition for final review, alongside GRAS reform ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-proposes-gras-rule-advances-ultra-processed-food-definition)).
  - The EU has binding food-waste cuts pending: 30% per capita in retail, food service and households, and 10% in processing, by 2030 ([IGD](https://www.igd.com/articles/2026-european-grocery-retail-legislation-outlook/72733)).
- **Farm labor.**
  - The workforce reportedly shrank by at least 155,000 after the immigration crackdown. Strawberry and almond growers lost up to 30% of harvest crews in Jan-Mar 2026 ([VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/news/trumps-immigration-policies-and-their-impact-on-u-s-agriculture/), a secondary source).
  - About 40% of hired crop labor is undocumented. H-2A visas exceeded 300k in 2024.
  - The October 2025 wage-rule change cuts H-2A pay. EPI estimates $4.4-5.4B a year in farmworker wage losses and growth toward 500k H-2A jobs ([EPI](https://www.epi.org/blog/trumps-new-h-2a-wage-rule-will-radically-cut-the-wages-of-all-farmworkers-new-estimates-show-farmworkers-stand-to-lose-4-4-to-5-4-billion-annually-under-dols-updated-adverse-effec/)).

## 2. Quantified pain points

- **Input squeeze with no pricing power.** "Our biggest struggles are our inputs... so much drastic markup" ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/already-under-financial-pressure-farmers-squeezed-further-by-tariffs-and-iran-war)).
- **Repair and downtime.** Repair restrictions cost US farmers about $4.2B a year (about $3.3k per farm) ([Moneywise/PIRG](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/john-deere-has-agreed-to-pay-99-million-to-farmers-over-claims-theyre-monopolizing-repairs-and-overcharging-farmers)). Deere's $99M settlement obliges it to open diagnostic and repair tools for 10 years.
- **Unharvested crops.** Grapes, blueberries and peaches rotted in the field in 2025 for lack of labor. Late or absent H-2A arrivals mean lost revenue and waste.
- **Protein buyers can't source whey.** Buyers are shifting from sustainability goals to supply-chain risk mitigation ([GreenQueen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/whey-shortage-price-alternative-protein-fermentation-plant-based/)).
- **CPG and food service.** Manufacturers face dye reformulation deadlines and volume loss. 48% of GLP-1 users say they would dine out more if smaller portions were offered ([Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/consumer-trends/not-all-glp-1-users-are-dining-out-or-spending-less-find-new-surveys)).
- **Spray drones.** DJI was added to the FCC Covered List in December 2025. DJI Agras hardware runs about 80% of the US spray-drone market, and the new T55 and T100 have no committed US launch ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/07/01/dji-agras-t55-and-t100-launch-globally-fcc-drone-ban/)). The Texas Farm Bureau warns of limited drones and parts.

## 3. Where money is flowing

- **Overall (2025).**
  - Agrifoodtech funding was $16.2B (-3%) across 12% fewer deals. Upstream drew $9B (+7%).
  - Debt was 18.2% of the total, the highest in a decade. Agrifood climate tech rose to $3.9B from $2.8B. Wonder raised $600M.
  - Deeptech at seed gets a 78% premium that vanishes by Series B ([AgFunder News](https://agfundernews.com/agrifoodtech-funding-is-flat-the-interesting-part-is-where-it-isnt)).
- **Alt protein.**
  - Funding fell to $881M in 2025 from $1.1B. Plant-based rose 39% to $450M, fermentation fell 43% to $357M, and cultivated fell 48% to $74M.
  - H1 2026 raised only $197M, of which $132M was fermentation. More than 70 alt-protein companies have merged, been acquired or shut down since September 2024 ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/02/18/alternative-protein-funding-shifts-in-2026/), [vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/studies-numbers/gfis-2026-state-of-industry-reports-detail-mixed-year-alternative-proteins-across-three-sectors/)).
  - Beyond Meat carries roughly $1.2B in debt and faces Nasdaq delisting risk ([Food Institute](https://foodinstitute.com/focus/beyond-meat-on-the-verge-of-financial-ruin/)).
  - Verley raised a €32M Series A for precision-fermented whey. It is FDA-cleared and claims price competitiveness with conventional whey ([GreenQueen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/verley-precision-fermentation-funding-animal-free-whey-protein/)).
- **Robotics and autonomy.**
  - Weeding and field robotics took about $180M, roughly 27% of tracked precision-ag capital in 2025. Ecorobotix raised a $105M Series D ([Precision Farming Dealer](https://www.precisionfarmingdealer.com/articles/7113-autonomous-weeding-robots-lead-2025-precision-ag-funding)).
  - Sabanto's Series B in July 2026 was led by Leaps by Bayer with Yara participating ([Fertilizer Daily](https://www.fertilizerdaily.com/20260717-sabanto-series-b/)).
  - Deere's autonomy kit costs about $40-45k plus $10k a year ([AgWeb](https://www.agweb.com/news/john-deere-introducing-next-generation-perception-autonomy-kits)).
  - Oishii acquired Tortuga AgTech ([The Packer](https://www.thepacker.com/news/packer-tech/oishii-acquires-robotics-company-tortuga-agtech-extends-harvesting-capabilities)).
- **Food waste.** Small European rounds: Foodforecast €8M, Freshflow €6.5M ([EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/02/german-ai-foodtech-startup-foodforecast-raises-e8-million-to-tackle-ultra-fresh-food-wastage/)).
- **Crowded or damaged categories.** Plant-based meat (US sales declining), cultivated meat, vertical farming, and generic food-waste marketplaces and forecasting.
  - Vertical farming had 14 bankruptcies in 2025, including Plenty (about $1.19B raised) (aggregator: [Foodlore](https://foodlore.blog/why-vertical-farms-go-bankrupt/)).
  - Autonomous weeding is filling up fast.

## 4. Underserved gaps and structural blind spots

- **Post-GLP-1 formats.** Small-portion, high-protein, high-fiber, nutrient-dense products and menus. Incumbent P&Ls are built on volume and price-per-ounce, so shrinking portions cannibalizes revenue (inference).
- **Protein diversification.** Whey and casein are capped by cheese output. Buyers want non-dairy or fermentation sources, and Leaft (rubisco) and Alpine Bio (fractionated soy) are early entrants. The gap is functionality: whey uniquely gives clear drinks at pH 2.8-3.5 ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/24/in-a-global-shortage-what-are-the-alternatives-to-whey/)).
- **Natural color supply chain.** The US is almost fully import-dependent for annatto, butterfly pea and saffron, and demand is set to multiply. Domestic sourcing, extraction and stabilization is open, as is a reformulation-as-a-service business.
- **Mid-size and specialty farms.** They sit below the point where Deere's kits pay off. Orchard and vineyard retrofits (Bluewhite: 50k autonomous hours over 150k acres) show the model works.
- **Post-settlement repair diagnostics.** Deere must open its tools for a decade, which creates an independent repair software market.
- **Farmland succession.** One-third of landlords are 75 or older, and only about 1 in 4 farmers has a formal succession plan. Over 60% of farmland may change hands in 10-15 years ([Schrader](https://www.schraderauction.com/resources/farmland-succession-wave-landlords-over-75-estate-planning), [The Bullet](https://www.wwbl.com/2026/07/14/the-invisible-farmland-transfer-why-millions-of-acres-wont-hit-the-market/)).
- **Labor-arbitrage robots-as-a-service.** Per-box pricing near human wages removes capex risk for growers ([AgTecher](https://agtecher.com/en/robotics/tortuga/)). Robots reportedly pick at 50-60% of human speed but run about 20 hours a day.

## 5. Weird signals

- **Cocoa whiplash.** Cocoa went from more than $12,000/t in 2024 to about $3,845/t on May 20, 2026. Chocolate makers are reportedly restoring cocoa in recipes, which strands cocoa-alternative capacity such as Planet A Foods and Fermtech ([Prism News](https://www.prismnews.com/news/chocolate-makers-restore-cocoa-recipes-as-prices-plunge), [Bakery&Snacks](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/03/10/the-race-to-reinvent-cocoa-lab-grown-chocolate-and-cocoa-alternatives/)). Commodity-hedged alt-ingredient models are fragile.
- **The byproduct ate the product.** Whey, formerly a cheese waste stream, is now scarcer than the cheese, and factories are re-tooling for cottage cheese ([ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/food/byproduct-ate-product-inside-americas-proteinmaxxing-cheese-glut)).
- **GLP-1 hits spirits and pets.**
  - US spirits sales fell 15.4% and Diageo's US tequila fell 23% ([Poached](https://blog.poachedjobs.com/2026/05/13/restaurant-business/restaurant-insights-news/is-glp-1-use-cutting-into-your-alcohol-sales/)).
  - "Ozempic for cats" is emerging, and pet spend is shifting from premiumization to medicalization ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/18/cats-glp-1-ozempic-pets-weight-loss.html)).
- **Vertical farming survivor.** AeroFarms is reportedly profitable after abandoning multi-site expansion and pivoting to microgreens ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91330232/aeorfarms-turned-itself-around-after-bankruptcy-is-it-a-sign-that-vertical-farming-can-succeed)). Niche and margin beat scale.
- **China's ag-biotech surge.** Agrifoodtech funding in China grew 43% and in South Korea 171% on state-backed biotech money, while US deeptech tops out below $200M rounds ([AgFunder News](https://agfundernews.com/agrifoodtech-funding-is-flat-the-interesting-part-is-where-it-isnt)).
- **Farmland "invisible transfer."** Millions of acres will move through estates rather than the open market, so listing-based land data will miss much of it.

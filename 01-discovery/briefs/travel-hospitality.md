# Travel / Hospitality / Remote-Work Travel / Overtourism: Briefing (as of 2026-09-28)

**Method caveat:** The session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was exhausted before I began, so 0 searches ran. This briefing rests on ~45 direct page fetches (trade press front pages, Wikipedia, HospitalityNet article pages). Numbers were extracted by a summarizer model, so treat single-source items as "reported", not verified. Reddit and Guardian were blocked, so forum-level pain points are thin; pain points below come from trade-press practitioners.

## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)

- **Demand is at record volume but fragmented.** ~1.52B international arrivals in 2025, +~60M vs 2024 ([Wikipedia: Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism)). Spain ~97M, Italy 61.5M, Japan 42.7M (vs 36.9M in 2024), Morocco 19.8M ([Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Spain), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Italy), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Japan), [FHS World via HospitalityNet](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134587.html)).
- **Winners and losers are diverging.** Thailand 2025: 32.97M arrivals, -7.2% YoY and -17% vs 2019. Chinese arrivals fell 33.5% (6.73M to 4.47M) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Thailand)). US inbound was hit by tariffs and immigration policy: Canada -17% (Mar 2025), WTTC projected -22.5% ($12.5B) inbound spend, and the 2026 World Cup only gave a "small boost" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_United_States)). Air Canada is reshaping its network after a reported 27% collapse in US demand ([Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/)).
- **2026 energy/geopolitical shock.** Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed Mar 2, 2026; tanker traffic fell >90% (~10M bpd); 20% of world oil transits it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz)). By Sept, ULCCs report "soaring jet fuel costs" with capacity cuts and fare hikes ahead ([Skift Airlines](https://skift.com/category/airlines/)). Hotel developers report construction and logistics costs "repriced sharply" and UAE hospitality disrupted in H1 ([HospitalityNet](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134587.html)).
- **Rerouting is measurable.** Summer 2026 Europe: ADR +4-6%; Turkey flights +25% YoY (absorbing displaced Middle East trips) while wider Middle East volume fell ~13%; average booking lead time shortened to 33 days from 37.5. "Value for money" topped booking criteria ([RateGain/Sojern via HospitalityNet](https://hospitalitynet.org/news/4134598.html)).
- **K-shaped market.** Luxury led H1 2026 RevPAR while lower tiers were pressured ([Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/)). Skift reports airlines/hotels chasing premium while Carnival deliberately stays affordable ([Skift](https://www.skift.com/)). Airlines are densifying (AA 777-300ER +26 seats, no First; BA movable exit-row curtains) ([Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/)).
- **Border friction rising.** EU Entry/Exit System began Oct 12, 2025, fully live Apr 10, 2026; ETIAS (EUR 20) slipped to "2027" ([EES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entry/Exit_System), [ETIAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Travel_Information_and_Authorisation_System)). US ESTA rose to ~$40 on Sep 30, 2025, with annual inflation indexing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_System_for_Travel_Authorization)).
- **Regulation of supply is hardening.** Barcelona bans all short-term rentals Nov 2028 (Spain's top court upheld Oct 2025); Palma full ban; Madrid bans listings without private entrances; Spain fined Airbnb $75M (Dec 2025); Brazil (May 2026) requires 2/3 condo-owner approval; Glasgow levy GBP 5/night from Jan 2027; Chicago hotel tax 19%, highest in the US ([Airbnb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbnb), [STR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-term_rental), [Overtourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtourism), [Tourist tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_tax)).

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points

- **Hotels losing price control to Booking.com's merchant model.** 51% of European hotels say OTAs sell below their loaded rate (up from 40% two years ago); Booking is 66.1% of OTA bookings at surveyed hotels; 70% of Booking Holdings' 2025 bookings used its payment service; ~1 in 5 independent room nights affected. Hotels load $200, guest sees $180, hotel is paid after checkout. Markups by Agoda, Citi, AA, Southwest and loyalty partners are invisible to rate-shopping tools. 26% of hotels didn't know if rates were passed on ([HospitalityNet](https://hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4134595.html)). Skift also reports OTA commissions at a record 22-28% (summarizer output, unverified).
- **Search is eating referral traffic.** Skift reports Google AI Overviews now show hotel prices, cutting metasearch click-through 31% in tested markets (unverified snippet) ([Skift](https://www.skift.com/)).
- **Hotel labor.** Chicago hotel workers authorized a strike at 85% (potentially largest in city history); Seattle Hilton strike lasted 85 days ([Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/)).
- **AI everywhere, ROI unmapped.** 109 AI use cases catalogued across 39 hotel systems, but the ranking "does not measure ROI, vendor quality, or market share" ([HospitalityNet](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134590.html)).
- **Nomad visa mismatch.** 40+ countries have nomad visas with thresholds from under $1,000 to over $7,000/month, but with restrictions (Philippines bans local employment; Taiwan's requires prior nomad-visa history and $10K deposits) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_nomad)).

## 3. Where money is flowing

- **Big deals:** Royal Caribbean buys 50% of Sandals/Beaches (~$3B, closes early 2027); GIC buys 16 Japanese hotels from KKR (JPY200B); Expedia acquiring Tiqets (Dec 2025); Caesars/Fertitta ([HospitalityNet](https://hospitalitynet.org/), [Expedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedia_Group)). Skift flags L Catterton as a quietly major travel investor.
- **Hotel tech:** Mews raised $300M at $2.5B (Jan 2026, EQT Growth; ~$19.7B payment volume in 2025), got a Dutch EMI license, then cut 15% of staff in Jul 2026 citing AI ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mews_(company))). Dextr AI raised a $6.7M seed for hotel voice agents (Sep 24, 2026) ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/)). AIHG is pitching an "AI-native hotel operating company."
- **Incumbent scale:** Booking $26.9B 2025 revenue; Expedia $14.7B; Airbnb $12.2B (net $2.5B); Trip.com $8.9B, but under a China SAMR antitrust probe since Jan 2026 ([Booking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booking_Holdings), [Trip.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip.com_Group)). Sabre sold Hospitality Solutions to TPG and has negative equity of ~$1B ([Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabre_Corporation)).
- **Crowded:** generic AI trip planners, hotel voice/chat concierges, PMS/payments consolidation, OTA-style marketplaces. Investors now describe capital as "disciplined and selective."

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps

- **Independent hotels vs OTA price opacity.** OTAs cannot audit themselves. There are no neutral tools tracing every downstream reseller markup, only in-house monitoring (Radisson across 1,600 properties). Independents lack this.
- **Direct booking inside AI assistants.** Blastness/DirectBooker now lists hotel inventory on ChatGPT and Claude at "less than one-third" of average OTA commission, no setup fees, but discloses no booking volumes ([HospitalityNet](https://hospitalitynet.org/news/4134550.html)). Airbnb's own agent isn't due until 2027.
- **Short-stay supply in exile.** Bans and caps (Barcelona 2028, Palma, Madrid) strand inventory. Europe has >110,000 co-living beds, expected to double before 2030 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coliving)). Mid-term conversion and compliance tooling is a gap (inference).
- **Nomad compliance, not discovery.** Nomads.com sells lifetime membership for $1 (47,310 members), so information is commoditized; its "residence calendar for tax planning" hints at where value sits ([Nomads.com](https://nomads.com/)). The EES now tracks 90/180-day stays automatically, yet travelers can't easily see their own balance (inference).
- **Allocation, not fees.** Venice's day-tripper fee reportedly didn't reduce numbers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Italy)); Mount Fuji uses a hard 4,000/day cap plus a JPY2,000 fee; Boracay caps ~19,200. Destinations need timed-entry and dispersal tooling, and levy administration is fragmented (Glasgow, Chicago, Balearics).

## 5. Weird signals

- **Booking lead times shrinking** (37.5 to 33 days) while demand "fragmented": last-minute, rerouted, value-driven travel, so rebooking and disruption products matter more.
- **Chinese outbound is changing shape.** Thailand lost a third of Chinese arrivals; Golden Week travelers "go farther and spend less on hotels" ([Skift](https://www.skift.com/)).
- **Anti-flight sentiment from inside the industry:** Intrepid's co-founder wants travelers "closer to home" ([Skift](https://www.skift.com/)).
- **Uber is reportedly "coming for STR"** while Airbnb adds mainstream tours, services, and (per Chesky) a softer stance on hotels/cars/flights ([Skift](https://www.skift.com/)).
- **Rogue-agent risk (single opinion source, unverified):** a HospitalityNet piece claims 2026 incidents of AI agents breaching systems and warns hotel concierge/booking agents often hold API access built for trusted staff; it recommends server-side authorization and human approval for irreversible actions ([HospitalityNet](https://hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4134583.html)). An "agent auth and audit layer for hospitality" is a possible angle if the risk proves real.

# Water briefing (as of 2026-09-28): scarcity, infrastructure, PFAS, desalination, ag water

**Research caveat.** The WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200) before this scout ran, so this brief rests on about 25 direct fetches of primary and trade pages. No forum or Reddit mining was possible (Reddit is blocked to the fetcher). Funding-round coverage is thin. Items marked **[unverified]** come from my background knowledge (cutoff June 2026) and were not re-checked. Everything else was fetched this session.

## 1. Biggest shifts in the last 12-18 months

- **Data centers became a water-regulation target.** This is the fastest-moving shift.
  - Texas: on Aug 3 Gov. Abbott froze ERCOT grid-connection approvals pending a water and energy audit. There are 474 GW of load requests in the queue, about 90% of them data centers, and up to 1,800 projects are halted. Only 17% of 341 surveyed data centers answered the mandatory 2025 water survey, and the maximum penalty was $500. [smartwatermagazine](https://www.smartwatermagazine.com/news/smart-water-magazine/water-enters-the-ai-rulebook-australia-moves-to-mandate-data-centre-standards-texas-freezes-grid-connections)
  - Texas is now making non-reporting a crime and a bar to TCEQ permits and interconnection. TWDB owes the Governor an update by **Oct 14, 2026**. [waterfm](https://waterfm.com/texas-governor-wants-penalties-for-data-centers-water-use/)
  - Australia announced national data-centre standards on Jul 15, with legislation targeted for early 2027. They cover minimizing water use and funding extra water infrastructure.
  - Ohio scrapped a statewide general discharge permit for data centers after more than 7,000 comments. New York has a one-year moratorium, Illinois paused tax incentives and Nebraska ended them. Cheyenne revoked a discharge permit after a rare bacterium in its wastewater was traced to Meta's construction site. [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/supply/water-quality/ohio-scraps-controversial-plan-to-ease-data-center-wastewater-discharges/)
  - Ten Mississippi River states are drafting a "Mississippi River Compact" modeled on the Great Lakes Compact. It needs 3 more states to go to Congress. [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/newsletter/the-stream-september-22-2026-mississippi-river-states-launch-effort-to-protect-water-resources-from-ai-data-centers/)
  - Estimates disagree. Smart Water Magazine cites a global data-center fleet at 4.5T litres in 2025 rising to 9.3T by 2030. Wikipedia's AI-impact page cites an IEA-based 12T litres by 2030, up from 560B in 2023. Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) runs from 1.85 L/kWh (open-loop evaporative) to 0.1 L/kWh (closed-loop). The voluntary ceiling is 0.4 L/kWh.
- **Colorado River hit a structural break.**
  - Interior issued a Record of Decision in Aug 2026 for a 10-year "Decision Framework" covering 2027-2036, with final 2027-28 operating guidelines. [USBR](https://www.usbr.gov/ColoradoRiverBasin/)
  - Combined Powell and Mead storage is about 36% of capacity, the lowest since before Glen Canyon Dam filled (1963).
  - Circle of Blue puts 95% of the basin in drought and about $1.4T of annual activity at risk. [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/inthenews/situation-colorado-river-water-crisis/)
  - About 70% of the river goes to agriculture, so the next round of cuts lands on farms.
- **US drought is broad.** 49.6% of the US was in drought (D1-D4) as of Sept 24, 2026, after the warmest June-August on record. [drought.gov](https://www.drought.gov/) Smart Water Magazine reports WMO called this one of the driest years for the world's rivers in 35 years.
- **PFAS rules are loosening at the margins.**
  - The 4.0 ppt limits for PFOA and PFOS stand. In May 2026 EPA proposed pushing the compliance deadline from 2029 to 2031, and proposed rescinding the limits for PFHxS, PFNA, GenX and the Hazard Index. Both proposals are in comment. Initial monitoring is still due 2027. [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas)
  - EPA says the rule protects about 100 million people, and $1B of IIJA money is earmarked for PFAS testing and treatment.
- **Lead:** the Lead and Copper Rule Improvements (LCRI) action level tightens to 0.010 mg/L on **Nov 1, 2027**. [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/national-primary-drinking-water-regulations)
- **Europe is billing the polluter.** The revised Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive (UWWTD) makes pharma and cosmetics makers pay at least 80% of quaternary (micropollutant) treatment, with the payment scheme due by end-2028. Treatment rolls out to 20% of large plants by 2033, 60% by 2039 and 100% by 2045. Annual cost estimates diverge by roughly 10x: EC €1.2B, JRC €1.56B, EurEau €3.6-11.2B, Spain alone €24.5B. [smartwatermagazine](https://www.smartwatermagazine.com/news/newsroom/europe-sends-the-polluter-the-bill)
- **Desalination cost.** Wikipedia cites about $0.50/m3 typical, with a 2025 Chinese plant (Rizhao) reported at $0.28/m3 using waste heat. SWRO energy is about 3 kWh/m3 against a practical floor under 2. This is **not fully verified**. Cyprus approved an €85M mobile desal unit in April 2026. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desalination)

## 2. Acute pain points (inferred from the trade press; no forum data)

- **Nobody can measure or verify data-center water use.** The 17% survey response and $500 cap show regulators have no data. Disclosures are non-standardized and unverified, and the only cooling metric (WUE) ignores local scarcity and indirect water from power generation, which is about two-thirds of the total per the Wikipedia estimate.
- **Nitrate.** More than 60M Americans are served from elevated-nitrate sources. The federal limit (10 ppm) dates to 1991. The EPA cancer review has stalled since 2023, and EPA's Office of Research and Development, which housed IRIS, was eliminated in 2026. Roughly 80% of contamination in the Des Moines and Raccoon rivers is agricultural. [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/agriculture/epa-stalls-long-planned-nitrate-cancer-review-as-pollution-crisis-grows/) Small rural systems bear the treatment cost.
- **Small systems** face funding gaps for infrastructure and compliance. That was a recurring headline theme on Circle of Blue's supply page.
- **Regulatory whiplash.** Utilities face a 2027 monitoring deadline, a possible 2031 compliance date and an uncertain scope for four PFAS compounds. Capex planning under this uncertainty is hard.

## 3. Where money is flowing

- **Semiconductor water.** Gradiant has won **$300M** of contracts since Jan 2026 across five fabs (NY, VA, ID, UT). The work is ultrapure water, zero-liquid-discharge (ZLD) and scrubber reclaim above 80% recovery. [WaterTech](https://www.watertechonline.com/news-reports/news/55405273/gradiant-secures-new-fab-contracts)
- **Produced water.** Select Water Solutions signed a 12-year Delaware Basin deal with 60,000 b/d of extra recycling, 3M bbl of storage and about 100 miles of pipe. It covers about 500k dedicated acres, and the $100-120M build finishes by end-2027. [WaterTech](https://www.watertechonline.com/produced-water/news/55398877/select-water-solutions-amends-delaware-basin-water-agreement)
- **Corporate conservation.** Amazon put **$20M** into the Water Resilience Coalition's Colorado River effort ($32M raised toward a $100M goal). One Arable sensor project saves about 238M gallons per year. Against a roughly 1 trillion gallon annual supply-demand gap, that is small. [WaterTech](https://www.watertechonline.com/water-reuse/article/55407846/corporate-collaboration-aims-to-save-the-colorado-river-amid-drought-crisis)
- **Public capital.** EPA announced **$7.2B** in FY2026 State Revolving Fund money. A $251M WIFIA loan went to LA, and Georgia got $500M. The 15 largest regulated private water companies invested about **$6.8B** in 2025 and $43.5B over the decade. [waterfm](https://waterfm.com/nawc-investment-by-private-water-companies-on-the-rise/)
- **Corporate moves.** ACCIONA re-made Water an independent business area after 12 years. Cape Town opened its first water PPP (the Faure scheme, direct potable reuse) in September 2026, with RFP in late 2027. [smartwatermagazine](https://www.smartwatermagazine.com/news/newsroom/cape-town-opens-tender-for-faure-water-reuse-ppp)
- **Crowded [unverified]:** PFAS treatment media and equipment (GAC and ion exchange), AI leak detection and smart metering, and atmospheric water generation hardware.

## 4. Underserved gaps and incumbent blind spots

- **Water-compliance infrastructure for data centers.** Every jurisdiction now wants audited water and siting data. There is no standard, no verification, and no scarcity-adjusted metric. Hyperscalers cannot credibly self-certify. Utilities and engineering firms are not structured to sell independent verification.
- **Farm-side conservation markets.** 70% of the Colorado River goes to agriculture and the 2027-2036 framework forces cuts. Buyers (cities, corporates, states) want verified acre-feet at a price per acre-foot, and sellers (farmers) want simple metering and payment. Amazon's $20M is a demand signal with no clear settlement layer.
- **Nitrate and small-system treatment.** The federal push has stalled, which leaves demand with states and local farm-belt utilities. Incumbents are built for large systems.
- **EU producer-pays micropollutant allocation.** Cost estimates diverge 10x, and attributing load to pharma and cosmetics products will need data and dispute resolution. Nobody yet owns that dataset.
- **Spent PFAS media and concentrate disposal [unverified].** If the treatment mandate holds even at 2031, disposal and destruction capacity is a downstream bottleneck.

## 5. Weird signals

- **Ethiopia cut power to Bitcoin miners by 75%** during an El Nino drought. Miners use over a third of the state grid, which is 90% hydro, and reservoir inflows fell about 20% in a month. Compute-versus-water triage is now an explicit grid decision. [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/newsletter/the-stream-september-22-2026-mississippi-river-states-launch-effort-to-protect-water-resources-from-ai-data-centers/)
- **A pathogen was traced to a data-center construction site.** Cheyenne revoked Meta's discharge permit, so water quality (not just quantity) is a siting risk.
- **The enforcement gap is a business signal.** A $500 maximum fine and 17% compliance mean the first real enforcement wave, with criminal penalties in Texas, will create sudden demand.
- **Government science capacity is being removed.** Eliminating EPA's ORD and IRIS leaves a hole in health-risk assessment that private, academic or state groups might fill.
- **Semiconductor fabs are becoming water-reuse anchor customers.** $300M in awards to one vendor in nine months suggests fab water is a bigger market than most industrial-water forecasts assume.

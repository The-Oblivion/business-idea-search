# Climate Adaptation Briefing: Heat, Flood, Wildfire, Insurance Retreat (as of 2026-09-28)

Confidence note: figures marked (P) come from primary or trade sources (regulator, company release, Artemis, Howden). Figures marked (S) come from secondary or SEO-style blogs and should be verified before anyone relies on them. My search budget ran out mid-session, so the "weird signals" section is thinner than I wanted.

## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)

- **Reinsurance is softening fast while retail homeowners are still being squeezed.** Howden Re: property-cat risk-adjusted rates down up to 25% at June 1 2026, after -16% at April 1 and -14.7% at Jan 1 (P). https://www.howdenre.com/news-insights/1-june-2026-property-catastrophe-renewals. Reinsurance capital is at a record, about $790B (S per https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/reinsurance/news/breaking-news/catastrophe-reinsurance-prices-post-historic-annual-fall-583643.aspx). Howden says returns are compressing and could fall below cost of capital by 2027. Nothing in the report says savings reach homeowners. That lag is where the money is.
- **Cat bonds hit records.** H1 2026 issuance was about $18B across 83 deals, with about $65.6B outstanding and 12 first-time sponsors. 81% of risk capital is indemnity-triggered (P). https://www.artemis.bm/news/catastrophe-bond-market-records-that-were-broken-in-h1-2026/
- **Florida's market flipped, California's did not.** Citizens (FL) fell from 936,182 policies at the start of 2025 to about 395,000 (P). https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/01/citizens-policies-plummet-2025 and https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-12-27/citizens-property-insurance-now-has-fewer-than-400-000-policies. The California FAIR Plan had 696,562 policies in June 2026, up 8% since Sept 2025. Exposure was $768B, up 250% since Sept 2022 (S). https://beinsure.com/news/california-insurers-pledge-new-policies/. FAIR's 2026 rate hike was about 29.1% (https://www.kqed.org/news/12094860/california-fair-plan-announces-29-1-rate-hike-for-homeowners-this-fall).
- **The regulatory rulebook is being rewritten.**
  - California's Sustainable Insurance Strategy allows forward-looking cat models and reinsurance cost in rates, in exchange for an 85% writing commitment in distressed ZIPs. Zurich, Mercury and CSAA are expanding, but the June 2026 CDI release gave no numbers (P). https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2026/California-s-Insurance-Market-Strengthen.cfm
  - Colorado HB25-1182 has applied since July 1 2026. Insurers using wildfire models must disclose the parcel's score, credit documented mitigation, and offer an appeal (S). https://coloradosun.com/2026/07/18/colorado-homeowners-insurance-discounts-mitigation-hail/
  - Fannie and Freddie (LL-2026-03, Mar 18 2026) now accept actual-cash-value roof coverage on single-family homes and condos (P). https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/03/20/862768.htm
- **Cooling is turning into a legal duty for landlords.**
  - Maryland: 80F cap in buildings of 10+ units, effective June 1 2026.
  - LA County (unincorporated): 82F cap enforced from Jan 1 2027. Small landlords need one habitable room by 2027 and all rooms by 2032.
  - NYC Local Law 2026/023: 78F cap, tenant opt-in from Mar 2028, owner duty from 2030.
  - Washington: landlords generally cannot bar portable cooling devices from June 11 2026.
  - Source: https://www.readyourlease.ai/guides/air-conditioning-heating-laws (S) and https://www.rhawa.org/blog/new-state-law-portable-cooling-devices-for-renters
- **Federal heat rule slipped.** OSHA's supplemental proposal is targeted for Dec 2026 and the final rule for Oct 2027. OSHA averaged about 2,400 heat inspections a year in 2022-2025. California (82F indoor trigger), Washington and Oregon already have their own rules (S). https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure/rulemaking
- **Summer 2026 was a shock.** Reported: hottest US July on record at 76.89F. A European heatwave with a provisional death toll of about 38,000. A UK cooling-supply stockout with 900%+ inquiry spikes (S). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_North_American_heat_wave and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_European_heatwaves. DOE let PJM force data centers onto backup diesel (S). https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/3/us-heatwave-raises-alarms-over-ai-data-centre-energy-demands

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points

- **NFIP expires Sept 30 2026, two days from today.** More than 30 short-term extensions since FY2017. An extension is expected inside FY27 appropriations (P). https://www.nar.realtor/flood-insurance/faq-national-flood-insurance-program-expires-september-30-2026. In the 2010 lapse, about 1,400 closings a day were delayed or cancelled (S). Risk Rating 2.0 premiums must roughly double toward full risk, with median $689 versus $1,288 needed (S). https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN11777
- **Aerial-imagery nonrenewals.** Homeowners report being dropped over roof photos, including a Florida owner with 52 claim-free years and no in-person inspection (S). https://www.aol.com/finance/no-protection-florida-homeowner-dropped-105500990.html. California AB 75 (notice, copy of photos, dispute and remediation window) became operative July 1 2026. CT, NH and PA regulators say imagery alone is insufficient, and a NY bill is pending (S). https://blog.pia.org/2026/05/06/n-y-bill-would-set-standards-for-use-of-aerial-imagery-in-homeowners-insurance/. Even so, "owners still have to prove their case."
- **Mitigation credit is unclaimable in practice.** The retrofit baseline is $8K-25K (S). Insurer discounts run 5-35% (S, https://roofvista.com/resources/guides/california-wildfire-prepared-designation-guide). The Colorado Sun quotes a homeowner on "1,000 bucks towards a $75,000 metal roof." Boulder's rebate is capped at $500. Premiums "quintupled in a decade" for one Parker, CO owner.
- **Florida condos.** SB 4D inspections plus milestone and reserve-study requirements mean carriers want documents before they bind a master policy. Reported special assessments are $50K-$200K+ per unit, and insurance can be 40-60% of the HOA budget (S). https://www.liveinsurancenews.com/florida-condo-owners-assessments/8576147/
- **Post-fire rebuild gap.** Only about 13% of affected Palisades single-family owners have begun rebuilding. Insurer estimates were about $462/sq ft against contractor budgets up to $1,065/sq ft (S). https://www.smobserved.com/story/2026/03/30/news/very-few-pacific-palisades-residents-pull-building-permits-or-plan-to-rebuild-heres-why/9748.html
- **Trades bottleneck.** HVAC labor shortages are structural, and installs and repairs are slower (S). https://hvac365.com/blog/hvac-labor-shortage-worsens-ahead-of-summer-peak/

## 3. Where the money is flowing

- **Climate-risk data is consolidating.** MSCI bought First Street for $120M cash plus a two-year earn-out, closing Q3 2026 (P). https://www.msci.com/discover-msci/media-room/msci-acquires-first-street-to-enhance-physical-climate-risk-capabilities-for-financial-decision-making. Moody's already owns Four Twenty Seven and RMS. A cash price this modest suggests scoring alone is not a big venture outcome.
- **Parametric and wildfire startups are small.**
  - Kettle: about $25M total raised.
  - Satellites on Fire: $2.7M, working with Aon on a parametric product (https://www.theinsurer.com/parametric-insurer/news/wildfire-detection-startup-raises-27-million-and-plans-parametric-and-us-2026-04-08/).
  - Descartes: about $200M+ raised (S).
  - Crowded: risk scoring, parametric MGAs, cat bonds and wildfire detection.
- **Public money is unstable.** FEMA cancelled BRIC in April 2025. A court ordered it restored and FEMA reinstated it Mar 18 2026 with $1B. Applications closed July 23 2026 (https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20260325/fema-announces-1-billion-federal-funding-help-states-mitigate-impact). California launched a Safe Homes Grant in Jan 2026 for low- and moderate-income owners and a revolving loan program under SB 894 (S). https://readyfireone.com/learn/articles/california-home-hardening-grants

## 4. Underserved gaps and structural blind spots

- **A neutral evidence layer.** Insurers cannot advocate for the policyholder, and homeowners cannot cheaply prove roof condition, vent type or 5-ft clearance. New laws (AB 75, HB25-1182) now require disputes and documented credits, but nobody owns the workflow.
- **Small landlords and HOA boards.** They face cooling duties and reserve-study or insurance underwriting packages, and lack tools. LA County's phase-in (one room by 2027) fits small operators.
- **Retail pass-through lag.** Reinsurance is softening, but primary carriers file slowly, and regulators are the choke point. Insurers cannot cut rates fast without hurting their own rate cases.
- **Cash buyers of "uninsurable" homes.** Financing fails, so these sell to cash buyers at a discount (S). https://www.fortune.com/2024/09/24/uninsurable-homes-selling-all-cash-discount
- **Neglected perils.** Hail drives 26-54% of Colorado premiums (S). Heat and smoke business-interruption cover for outdoor workforces is barely productized.

## 5. Weird signals

- Florida "take-out" insurers get Citizens' policy data to cherry-pick which policies to assume (https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/05/07/citizens-insurance-private-data-information). A CBS investigation (headline only, unverified detail) says whistleblowers allege owners of a receiving company diverted millions to investors (https://www.cbsnews.com/projects/2026/florida-insurance/). The fast Citizens shrinkage may have moved risk onto thinly capitalized carriers.
- Howden's own note that reinsurer returns may fall below cost of capital by 2027. A soft cycle and a growing protection gap can coexist, and the reversal would hit the retail layer first.
- Only 13% rebuilding in the Palisades, more than 18 months after the fire. Lot-sale liquidity, builder-financing and underinsurance-bridging may be a bigger market than rebuilding services.
- Cooling mandates arrive on a 2027-2032 phase-in for small landlords, so retrofit demand is pre-scheduled and predictable, unlike disasters.
- IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home just expanded to Washington (https://www.insurance.wa.gov/about-us/news/2026/ibhs-wildfire-prepared-home-designations-now-available-washington), which suggests home-hardening certification is going national.

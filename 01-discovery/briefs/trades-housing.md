# Briefing: Construction, Housing Shortage, Trades Labor, Home Services (as of 2026-09-28)

Caveat: several numbers come from vendor blogs or aggregator sites (flagged "vendor stat"). Cross-check before betting money on them.

## 1. Biggest shifts, last 12-18 months

- **The labor problem changed shape.** ABC now says construction must add [349,000 net workers in 2026 and 456,000 in 2027](https://www.abc.org/News-Media/News-Releases/abc-construction-industry-must-attract-349000-workers-in-2026-despite-macroeconomic-headwinds). That is down from 439,000 for 2025 and 500,000+ in earlier years, and most of it is retirements. NAHB puts the gap nearer [300,000](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/09/immigration-enforcement).
- **Immigration enforcement is the acute shock.** Immigrants are [over 25% of the construction workforce](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/09/immigration-enforcement). About 34% of trades workers are foreign-born, up to [61% in some trades](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/how-immigration-enforcement-will-impact-construction-in-2026/809482/). ICE arrests rose from 32,545 in May to [49,571 in July 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ice-enforcement-labor-costs/), with about 40% in Texas and Florida. Documented workers and citizens avoid sites too. One Minnesota roofing crew went from [80 workers to 6](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/22/home-builders-immigrant-workers-ice) while agents were nearby.
- **Data centers are absorbing electricians.** Data center work is now [nearly a third of the total shortage](https://www.classet.ai/blog/data-center-labor-shortage-2026). Journeymen on heavy overtime gross $130-170K (vendor stat). Texas homebuilders report [electrical delays of up to 2 months](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/05/137471-texas-data-centers-demand-electricians-delaying-housing-construction-2-months).
- **Housing shortage estimates disagree by 4x.** Zillow says [4.5-4.7M units](https://www.thestreet.com/real-estate/zillow-housing-shortage-update-analysis). Freddie Mac says [3.7M](https://www.resiclubanalytics.com/p/freddie-mac-housing-shortage-has-delayed-the-formation-of-1-million-households). NAHB says about 1.2M. Builders are not building into it.
- **Builders are financing shops now.** August starts were [1.275M SAAR, and completions fell 27.1% YoY](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/housing-starts-august-2026/). 63% of builders use incentives and 35% cut prices. 80-90% of new-home sales [need rate buydowns](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/09/17/housing-starts-more-like-stops-numbers-decline-august/), with 30-year rates near 7%. Forecasts have 2027 starts down 2-4%.
- **Tariffs.** Builders estimate [$10,900 per home](https://www.nahb.org/advocacy/top-priorities/building-materials-trade-policy/how-tariffs-impact-home-building). Brookings sees [about $30B](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/recent-tariffs-threaten-residential-construction/) added to residential construction and renovation cost by 2027. Only about 7% of inputs are imported, but those are mostly items with no quick domestic substitute. Reported lumber and steel tariff rates conflict across sources.
- **New law.** The [21st Century ROAD to Housing Act](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/centers-initiatives/mit-gcfp/21st-century-road-to-housing-what-congress-accomplished-2026-and-what-remains-to-be-done) became law on July 11, 2026. It:
  - kills the manufactured-home steel chassis rule (about $10K or 10% of unit cost);
  - streamlines environmental review for multifamily of 15 units or fewer;
  - funds standard duplex and townhome blueprints;
  - bars investors with more than 350 homes from buying existing houses.
  
  [HUD must still publish new standards](https://hoodline.com/2026/09/hud-s-national-mall-showcase-bets-on-factory-built-homes-to-fix-housing-crunch/) before chassis-optional homes ship. MIT's read is "modest at best" in the short run.
- **HVAC is at a cyclical trough.** 2025 residential shipments fell [25-50% YoY](https://www.thehardwirenews.com/hvac-market-correction-2026-data-rebound-2027/) after A2L pre-buying, with equipment prices about 40% above 2020. Carrier plans for 7.5M units versus a historical 9M. Rebound is expected in 2027. The tech shortage of about 110,000 persists, and A2L certification adds a qualification layer.

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points

- **Site continuity for small private builders.**
  - Austin framing costs rose 25-50%, adding $2-5K per home.
  - Foundations sit 30+ days waiting for crews. One executive described [15-20 houses stuck on foundations for 6+ weeks](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ice-enforcement-labor-costs/).
  - A West Virginia builder is at risk of losing about 40 closings.
  - A typical home needs about two dozen subs, and loan interest keeps accruing.
- **Permit friction.** Honolulu residential permits average [218 days, commercial 469](https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2026-09-24/honolulu-building-permit-applications-rise-under-ai-processing-tools). A reported Aug 2026 paper claims a 25% cut in approval time could lift housing production about 24% (unverified).
- **Homeowners are waiting on quotes.** Angi's data: [73% of homeowners have unstarted projects](https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/15972206-the-backlog-boom-73-of-homeowners-have-projects-waiting), 78% of them have no quotes yet, and 72% of postponers cite budget. The average project is $5,368. Barriers to hiring a pro: "too busy" 21%, trust 21%, no response 14%. Cash payment fell 57% to 51% and credit cards rose 27% to 30%. 62% now choose repair over replace, up from 51%.
- **Missed calls.** Invoca data puts about [48% of home-services calls as never reaching a person](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/09/28/how-an-ai-receptionist-for-contractors-helps-plumbers-and-hvac-shops-stop-missing-calls/). A LeadAngel stat (vendor) says a 2-minute reply converts 62% of leads versus 28% at 42 minutes.
- **Cash flow.** GCs are [4x more likely than subs to be paid within 30 days](https://thebirmgroup.com/why-contractors-still-cant-find-workers-in-2026-and-whats-going-wrong/). One in five subs waits 60+ days.
- **Lead-gen distrust.** Angi/HomeAdvisor charges about $288-300 a year plus [$15-85 per lead](https://www.leadtruffle.co/blog/complete-guide-angi-leads-home-service-contractors-2026/), and the leads are shared with many bidders. There was an FTC settlement in 2023.
- **Aging stock.** Median home age is [44 years](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/blog/remodeling-growth-set-downshift-late-2026), up from 28 in 1993. Remodeling spend hit a record $524B, but growth is slowing to about 1.6% by year-end.

## 3. Where money is flowing (and what is crowded)

- **PE roll-ups of trades.** [Apollo is putting about $2B into Apex at a roughly $10B valuation](https://profitabilitypartners.io/home-services-private-equity-acquirers/) (May 2026). Blackstone bought Champions in Feb 2026. Bain and Mubadala bought Service Logic in Dec 2025. Reported multiples are [10-14x EBITDA for platforms above $25M revenue versus 2-3.5x for small owner-operators](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-home-services-statistics-2026/). The same source scores roofing as the least consolidated trade.
- **Big-box vertical integration.** Home Depot bought SRS ($18B) and GMS, and [Mingledorff's (HVAC distribution) in May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm). Lowe's bought [FBM for $8.8B](https://www.retaildive.com/news/lowes-acquire-foundation-building-materials-pro-business/758228/) and Artisan Design for $1.3B.
- **Physical AI for homebuilding.** TerraFirma raised a [$100M Series A led by Kleiner](https://bricks-bytes.com/funding-ma/latest-construction-technology-funding-rounds-7th-sep-2026/). Higharc raised a $95M Series C. Reframe Systems raised $40M (10 homes built, 114 planned). Monumental raised a [$32M Series B](https://www.deadfront.ai/blog/august-2026-ai-construction-roundup) for bricklaying robots.
- **Contech M&A.** Procore-Datagrid, Autodesk-Rhumbix, Trimble-Document Crunch, and Nemetschek-HCSS are all in [one Construction Dive roundup](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/merger-acquistion-roundup-construction-tech-autodesk-trimble/818155/). Procore now ships 20 pre-built agents.
- **Crowded:** AI takeoff and estimating, GC project-management agents, contract review, AI phone receptionists (many vendors), and permit-review platforms sold to governments.

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps

- **Solo to 5-tech shops and sub-$10K jobs.** ServiceTitan is the category leader. After its Q2 (Sept 2026) it fell [about 26%](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-servicetitan-sinks-after-q2-2026-results-and-outlook-93CH-4892559) on GTV growth slowing to 17%, softer HVAC leads, and a decision to delay new commercial trades and residential exteriors. It expects only about 700 locations on its agentic "Max" tier by fiscal year-end, so most tiny shops have no AI operations layer.
- **Roofing and exteriors.** They are the least PE-consolidated trade and the one ServiceTitan just deprioritized.
- **Exit market for sub-$5M trade shops.** [McKinsey](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alisoncoleman/2026/01/14/the-silver-tsunami-is-reshaping-small-business-why-exit-planning-cant-wait/): 6M SMBs face ownership transitions by 2035. A third of owners over 50 struggle to find a buyer. Roll-ups mostly skip shops at that scale, which is a 3-5x valuation gap.
- **Applicant-side permit work.** Government AI (Denver's [$4.6M five-year contract](https://stateline.org/2026/08/20/cities-turn-to-ai-to-speed-housing-permitting/), Honolulu CivCheck) serves reviewers. Small builders, ADU owners and rural jurisdictions have few tools.
- **Sub-tier labor compliance.** NAHB is asking that employers' verification duty be limited to direct employees, not subcontractors' workers. Builders currently lack reliable tools for crew continuity and verification across two dozen subs.
- **Site work for factory-built homes.** Chassis-optional homes still need foundations, utility hookups, set crews and local financing, all of which stay local and human.
- **Incumbents that cannot fix this.** Angi's economics rely on reselling the same lead to many pros. Home Depot cannot neutrally serve competing contractors. PE platforms rarely touch tiny shops. Governments cannot build advocacy tools for applicants.

## 5. Weird signals

- **Faster permit review, bigger backlog.** Honolulu says review cycles are down 58% and review time down 55%. Yet applications rose 34% while permits issued rose only 5%, and the [backlog is 9,252](https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2026-09-24/honolulu-building-permit-applications-rise-under-ai-processing-tools). Cheaper filing may induce demand, so the bottleneck moves downstream (inspections, post-permit, contractors).
- **Starts are steady but completions fell 27.1%.** The binding constraint may now be finishing homes (subs, financing, rate buydowns), not starting them.
- **The trades "boom" is partly an illusion.** A Quartz headline of "+1,421% trade school enrollment" is called ["a demographic mirage"](https://qz.com/trade-school-enrollment-1421-percent-gen-z-explained-071026) (page returned 403; this is from the search snippet). Other claims, such as Gen Z at 31% of new apprentices and a 48% rise since 2021, are single-source. The pipeline may be thinner than the headlines suggest.
- **ICE and data centers hit the same bottleneck.** Both remove the same scarce, verified, skilled crews from homebuilding. The price signal (data center overtime versus $500K-home framing budgets) is pulling labor away from housing.
- **Customers like blended work.** DIY-only fell from 50% to 39% while "homeowner plus pro" rose from 27% to 46% (Angi). That suggests demand for unbundled, part-time pro help, not full-service jobs.

# Briefing: Reshoring, Tariffs, Supply-Chain Rewiring, Industrial Automation (as of 2026-09-28)

Method note: about 20 searches and fetches (the shared web-search budget ran out). Figures from SEO aggregators are marked (agg). Figures from primary or trade press are unmarked.

## 1. Biggest shifts, last 12-18 months

- **Tariff regime rebuilt on Section 232 after IEEPA fell.** The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs 6-3 on 2026-02-20 ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/02/the-supreme-court-ends-ieepa-tariffs)). Metals duties then moved to **full customs value** from 2026-04-06. Tiers: 50% on primary metal articles, 25% on derivatives, 15% temporary for selected machinery and HVAC through 2027-12-31, and 10% if made with US-melted steel or US-smelted aluminum/copper. Products under 15% metal weight are excluded ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/what-every-multinational-should-know-about-the-new-rules-for-section-232-tariffs-on-steel-aluminum-and-copper-derivatives/), [C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q2/06-02-2026-client-advisory-updates-to-section-232-tariffs-on-steel-aluminum-copper/)). A new round of additional derivative articles was proposed on 2026-08-06 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/06/2026-15961/request-for-public-comments-on-the-proposed-implementation-of-duties-on-additional-aluminum-steel)).
- **More 232 actions are pending.** Semiconductors got a narrow 25% tariff on 2026-01-15. Polysilicon gets 15% plus minimum import prices from 2026-12-04. The robotics and industrial machinery 232 investigation had no final proclamation in what I found ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/04/current-and-forthcoming-section-232-actions-by-the-trump-administration), [BIS](https://www.bis.gov/about-bis/bis-leadership-and-offices/SIES/section-232-investigations)). A robot tariff would raise the cost of the automation that reshoring depends on.
- **A war shock landed on top of tariffs.** US-Israeli strikes on Iran began 2026-02-28. The Hormuz strait is effectively closed again after a July deal collapsed, with about 10 transits a day against a baseline of 88-130. Freight rose up to 50% and war-risk surcharges run $1,500-4,000 per container ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis), [Automotive Manufacturing Solutions](https://www.automotivemanufacturingsolutions.com/analysis/iran-conflict-sends-shockwaves-through-auto-production-and-supply-chains/2616710)).
- **USMCA was not renewed on 2026-07-01.** Annual reviews now run through 2036. Expect tighter rules of origin and more origin audits, and USTR flagged "simplified compliance for smaller suppliers" ([FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/usmca-2026-joint-review-not-renewed-importer-impact)).
- **Factory activity is up, factory buildings are not.**
  - ISM July PMI was 55.6, the highest since May 2022. Employment entered expansion for the first time in 33 months, Imports were 55.7, and Prices were 71.1 ([ISM](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/manufacturing-pmi-at-55-6-july-2026-ism-manufacturing-pmi-report-302840669.html)).
  - Factory construction is down 22-32% from the 2024 peak as CHIPS projects finish ([IoT Analytics](https://iot-analytics.com/us-manufacturing-reshoring-boom-what-the-data-says/)). Their verdict is "no reshoring boom, normal cycle".
  - Manufacturing jobs are +58k since the Dec 2025 low, at 12.64M. August was +16k, with machinery +6.1k and autos -4.5k ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/us-manufacturing-adds-16000-jobs-in-august/829655/)).
- **Announced reshoring is cooling.** Reshoring Initiative jobs were about 244k in 2024 and about 174k in 2025 (agg: [Industrial Sage](https://www.industrialsage.com/manufacturing-reshoring-statistics/), [KORE1](https://www.kore1.com/reshoring-manufacturing-jobs-2026/)). Hiring lags announcements by 12-24 months.
- **Automation demand is broadening away from autos.** H1 2026 North American robot orders were 17,995 units and $1.166B, up 2.0% and 6.6% ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/q2-2026-robotics-demand-increased-across-industries-reports-a3/)). By segment:
  - Semiconductors and electronics +35%, pharma +32%, auto components +24%, food +17%.
  - Auto OEM -25%, metals only +3% for H1 (+18% in Q2).
  - Cobots were only 15.4% of units and 9.8% of value.
- **Humanoids left the lab, mostly in China.** An estimated 13-18k were sold in 2025, about 90% Chinese. AgiBot shipped its 10,000th unit in March 2026, and Unitree passed STAR-market IPO registration in July (agg: [Robotomated and similar](https://theresarobotforthat.com/blog/humanoid-robot-companies-2026-complete-guide/)). Agility's Digit lists at about $200k plus $20k deployment plus $36k a year (agg).

## 2. Acute pain points

- **Price and lead-time volatility.** 57% of negative ISM comments cited pricing volatility, 43% the Iran war, 22% lead times and 18% tariffs. One respondent said "The pricing volatility and lead-time extensions in this market are arguably worse than the pandemic era." Supplier deliveries have been slowing for eight straight months (ISM link above).
- **Refund float is trapped.** About 330,000 importers paid about $166B on 53M entries. By 2026-07-10, CBP had accepted about $121.75B in claims and repaid about $86.3B including interest. Small importers and delivered-duty-unpaid (DDU) shipments are likely to go unclaimed. Refunds run in phases, and AD/CVD and liquidated-entry cases are later ([GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/), [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/03/tariff-refund-mechanism-takes-shape), [Cato, not fetched](https://www.cato.org/blog/ieepa-refunds-update-good-progress-still-ways-go)).
- **Compliance is now BOM-level.** Full-value duties mean HTS class, metal weight, and melt/pour or smelt/cast origin set the rate. Advisers tell importers to collect mill certs and bills of materials per SKU. Thomson Reuters counted more than 155M tariff updates in 2025. CBP's 2026 priorities are HTS misclassification audits on machinery parts, origin verification and AD/CVD evasion ([Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/what-todays-tariff-authorities-actually-demand-the-data-visibility-and-compliance-gaps-most-manufacturers-havent-closed/), [Mallory](https://www.mallorygroup.com/blog-posts/trade-policy-changes-audit-hts-classifications-before-they-cost-you)).
- **Shop-floor voice (Practical Machinist snippets).** Owners report supplier "tariff surcharges" (one at 2.7%) and buying a full year of inventory to lock prices. Tungsten and carbide tooling costs are a worry, and "the worst thing is the uncertainty" ([Practical Machinist](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/tariffs-that-are-hitting-on-you.438254/)). I could not fetch the refund thread (403).
- **Labor.** Deloitte and the Manufacturing Institute project 3.8M hires needed by 2033, with 1.9M possibly unfilled. Their 2024 report stresses retention, not just applicants ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/manufacturing-labor-shortage-2033-deloitte-mi-report-2024/713133/)).
- **Electrical gear.** Transformer and switchgear producer prices are up 89-96% since 2019, about triple general inflation, driven by the data-center boom ([Piedmont Crescent](https://piedmontcrescentcapital.com/construction-spending-july-2026-building-fewer-factories-buying-more-equipment/)).

## 3. Where the money is flowing

- **Physical AI is at record levels.** Q2 2026 was $18.6B over 450 deals ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q2-2026-robotics-physical-ai-report-the-money-is-in-the-motion)). Crunchbase counts $18.8B YTD by June against $15B for all of 2025. Big rounds: Skild AI $1.4B (about $14B valuation), Neura $1.4B, Apptronik $520M, and Mind Robotics $500M+$400M for industrial automation ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/robotics/startup-venture-funding-surges-2026-data/)).
- **Defense-oriented "factory as software."** Hadrian raised a $1.37B Series D at about $7.87B on 2026-08-06. It has 700 staff, plans 2,000, and claims its Opus system trains technicians in 30 days. Customers include the Navy, Army ($80M) and Lockheed. It has a $2.4B Alabama submarine site and plans three more factories ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/hadrian-automation-1-4b-funding-manufacturing-workforce-growth/827136/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/defense-tech-hadrian-raises-1-37b-at-8b-valuation/)).
- **Rare-earth and magnet reshoring.** Between 2026-06-02 and 06-26, the US committed about $2.9B in federal money and about $1.4B of private capital arrived alongside it. Examples: USA Rare Earth's 6,400 t/yr magnet plant, Energy Fuels and Phoenix Tailings DoD loans ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/washingtons-five-week-rare-earths-funding-surge-how-the-us-is-rebuilding-a-supply-chain-outside-china/)). China held about 94% of magnet manufacturing in 2024, and heavy rare earths stay tight through 2027 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/012726-rare-earth-supply-bottlenecks-set-to-persist-in-2026)).
- **Crowded:** humanoids and robot foundation models, defense manufacturing, on-demand machining marketplaces (Xometry, Protolabs, Fictiv), and rare-earth mine-to-magnet. Also crowded: tariff-tracking and HTS-classification software.
- **Under-funded relative to need:** small-shop robot leasing. Formic (robots-as-a-service) has raised only about $59M in total, with a $27.4M Series A in 2024 ([Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/article/formic-raises-27-4-million-automation-manufacturer)). Welding and machine-tending were absent from Crunchbase's big-round list.

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps

- **Machine-shop succession.** Reported figures: 60% of US manufacturing firms have owners aged 55+, about 150k small and mid-size firms are near retirement, and customers often leave when an owner retires ([American Machinist](https://www.americanmachinist.com/shop-operations/article/55365690/how-to-preserve-the-legacy-of-americas-manufacturing-businesses-american-operator), [Modern Machine Shop](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/5-reasons-why-machine-shop-ownership-is-changing)). MMS admits no dataset exists. Compass Precision has bought 10 shops since 2019 ([Manufacturing Today](https://manufacturing-today.com/news/multiple-acquisitions-and-ceo-succession-help-compass-continue-its-expansion-from-local-shops-to-global-operations/)).
- **Refunds and duty-engineering for the sub-$5M importer.** Big brokers focus on large accounts. The person who owes duty (a small manufacturer or a consumer on DDU shipments) has no incentive or tooling to claim.
- **Metals and job-shop automation.** Metals robot orders rose only 3% in H1 even as reshoring rhetoric peaked. Capital is chasing humanoids and defense, not welding cells or machine-tending for 10-50 person shops.
- **Incumbent blind spots.** Customs brokers are paid per entry, not per dollar saved. Big integrators do not serve 10-person shops. Marketplaces do not certify melt-and-pour origin.

## 5. Weird signals

1. **Reshoring is happening inside existing walls.** Nondefense capital goods orders hit a record $85.9B in July, the highest since 1992, and unfilled orders are $953B. Manufacturing construction is down 22% YTD ([Piedmont Crescent](https://piedmontcrescentcapital.com/construction-spending-july-2026-building-fewer-factories-buying-more-equipment/)). That points to a retrofit, equipment and electrical-gear market, not a new-building market.
2. **Auto OEMs are retreating while their suppliers automate.** OEM robot orders are -25% and auto components +24%. Autos lost 4.5k jobs in August while machinery gained 6.1k (A3 and BLS links above).
3. **Military bases are becoming industrial parks.** Critical-mineral processors are getting leases at Anniston, Pine Bluff, Red River and Tooele depots (Fastmarkets link above).
4. **Imports index at 55.7, the highest since June 2021,** despite tariffs. Firms appear to front-run and re-route rather than stop buying. One respondent said customers in Asia "procure elsewhere to avoid paying a tariff" (ISM link above).
5. **About $80B of the $166B collected had not been repaid as of July,** and part of it will likely never be claimed. That is real money owed to hundreds of thousands of small firms (GHY link above).

Caveats: aggregator statistics (Reshoring Initiative jobs, humanoid prices) should be checked against primary sources. The 2026 Deloitte/MI update, the actual refund backlog, and the machinery 232 outcome were not verified.

Everything below comes from direct page fetches (trade press, TechCrunch, IFR, Wikipedia). Wikipedia can lag, so figures from it are flagged "(wiki)". ## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)
- **Robotaxi is real but capital- and depot-limited.** Waymo raised $16B at $126B (Feb 2026), does ~500k paid rides/week with 3,871 vehicles across 10-14 metros ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo), [Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/from-14-cities-to-15000-what-it-will-take-to-scale-robotaxis/)). Tesla went unsupervised in Texas/Florida but has only ~420 cars, plus 45 Cybercabs registered ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Robotaxi)). Zoox began charging fares on Aug 10, 2026 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoox)).
- **Driverless trucking crossed from pilot to early commercial.** Aurora is exiting 2026 with 200 driverless trucks (500k+ driverless miles, ~$80M run rate). It targets 30,000 trucks and $5B revenue by 2030 ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/aurora-30000-driverless-trucks-2030-plan)). Gatik reports 85k driverless orders and $600M+ contracted revenue, raising a $200M Series D ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/gatik-driverless-freight-series-d), article text says Aug 2025). Einride (Nasdaq: ENRD) expects 1,500-2,000 trucks by 2028 ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/einride-nvidia-hyperion-autonomous-trucking)).
- **Humanoids moved from demo to units, dominated by China.** ~22,000 humanoids shipped in H1 2026; the top 5 makers (all Chinese) hold 86% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/30/the-u-s-is-building-barriers-around-drones-and-robots-china-still-has-scale/)). Unitree IPO'd in Aug 2026 (~$9B, +~500% day one; G1 ~$16k) ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitree_Robotics)). Figure claims ~350 Figure 03s delivered by Apr 2026 and a planned $600/mo lease ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_AI)). 1X NEO is $20k or $499/mo, and early demos were largely teleoperated ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1X_Technologies)).
- **Drone delivery scaling via retail.** Wing passed 1M deliveries; Walmart/Wing targets 270+ sites by 2027 (+150 stores in a year), reaching ~40M Americans. Lowe's launched Wing/DoorDash delivery on Sep 28, 2026 (2.5 lb payload, ~20 min) ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/09/28/lowes-drone-delivery-wing-doordash/), [DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/09/28/drone-delivery-regulations-local-rules/)). Zipline passed 2M deliveries ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipline_(drone_delivery_company))). Amazon paused after an Oct 2025 crane collision and quit Italy in Dec 2025 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Air)).
- **Geopolitics reshaping hardware.** FCC barred new DJI/Autel models (Dec 2025) ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI)). New US drone tariffs took effect Sep 2026, with component tariffs in 2027. China's export controls (Announcement No. 34) cover motors, batteries, radios and sensors ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/08/05/china-drone-export-controls-us-manufacturers/)).
- **Industrial base:** 5M industrial robots in operation, 600k+ installed in 2025 (China 354k = 59% of global; US 38.5k, +12%; Japan -19%; Germany -8%). IFR forecasts 655k in 2026 and 806k in 2029 ([IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally)).

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points
- **Robotaxi utilization is poor:** only 54% of Waymo's first 86M miles carried a passenger, and each reset cycle takes ~2 hours. Depot power is the choke point: California standard charging site ~6 months, new circuit 1.9 years, substation upgrade 2.8 years, new substation 8.9 years. Robotaxi depots compete with data centers for powered industrial land ([Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/from-14-cities-to-15000-what-it-will-take-to-scale-robotaxis/)).
- **Trucking:** insurers price AV trucks "slightly above" human drivers "purely on that unknown" (expected to fall 15-25%/yr). Werner's CIO: "we have a gap" on pricing. Hub-to-hub autonomy leaves "hundreds of dollars" of human transfer cost per handoff, and most freight is door-to-door ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/aurora-30000-driverless-trucks-2030-plan), [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/autonomous-freight-needs-to-solve-the-predictability-problem)).
- **Driverless trucks have no one to talk to an inspector.** The CVSA enhanced-inspection clearance lasts 24 hours and requires a trained inspector. PrePass/Kodiak's integration covers 581 weigh-station sites but is live only in Texas and Louisiana ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/prepass-kodiak-driverless-truck-weigh-station-bypass)).
- **Yards and paperwork:** detention and OTIF penalties accrue while trucks queue. Paper BOL processes delay cash ~60 days and create six-figure invoice disputes. YardFlow says a customer moved ~5% more freight with the same headcount and is expanding from 26 to 200+ sites ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/yard-flow-200-site-yard-automation-deal)).
- **Last-mile labor squeeze:** Amazon put $1.9B into DSP driver pay (+$1/hr to ~$24/hr) ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/amazon-rolls-out-safety-tech-pay-bump-for-delivery-providers)), while small delivery contractors are closing (4XH: 230 jobs; Ardor: 179) ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/freight-distress-report-logistics-manufacturing-cuts-put-1850-jobs-on-chopping-block)).
- **Robot training data is the humanoid bottleneck** ("dearth of physical-world data") ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/mecka-ai-nears-500m-valuation-in-sequoia-led-deal-amid-rush-for-robot-training-data/)).
- **Tire-shop and service labor** churns within months to a year (PitPro, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/pitpros-first-tire-changing-robot-goes-live-in-canada/)).
- **Drone siting:** cities cannot regulate flight paths but can zone launch hubs (e.g., Northglenn: special-use permit, 200 ft residential buffer, 65 dB at property line) ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/09/28/drone-delivery-regulations-local-rules/)).

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps
- **The "picks and shovels" of scaling AVs:** depot power and charging, cleaning and inspection, remote assistance. Waymo runs ~70 remote agents on shift, with two centers in the Philippines; NHTSA sent a first-responder letter on Jul 8, 2026 ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/bot-auto-remote-assistance-operators)). Incumbents own their own stacks and are unlikely to build neutral infrastructure.
- **Insurance and underwriting for autonomy:** priced on "unknown," with a 15-25%/yr expected decline. Data that proves risk faster is a wedge.
- **Robot deployment and integration:** Maven's pitch is explicitly "steal your robot deployment deal". Mid-size 3PLs and retailers want outcome pricing (e.g., 48-hour product-mix changes) rather than a robot purchase.
- **Transfer hubs and door-to-door legs for autonomous trucks:** the handoff economics are unresolved. California requires 1M total miles (500k with a driver, 500k driver-out) before commercial hauling. Its rules took effect Apr 28, 2026, and the Teamsters sued Aug 5 ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/california-driverless-truck-rules)).
- **Domestic drone supply chain:** Amprius won a $75M award (500 Wh/kg cells, ~12M cells/yr by early 2028). Motors, radios and flight controllers are similarly exposed ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/09/28/amprius-us-drone-battery-production/)).
- **Drone-hub siting and permitting:** federal preemption of the sky but local control of the ground.
- **Retrofit robotics for small operators** (tire shops, farms). Robot makers chase chains and giants first.

# Briefing: Healthcare delivery, GLP-1, longevity, consumer health (as of 2026-09-28)

## 1. Biggest shifts, last 12-18 months

- **GLP-1s went from injectable scarcity to a cash-pay pill market.**
  - Wegovy pill launched Jan 5, 2026 at $149-$299/mo cash; >2M scripts written ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/05/first-glp-1-pill-for-obesity-from-novo-nordisk-launches-in-the-us.html), [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/novo-nordisk-wegovy-pill-sales-prescriptions-q1-2026/819418/)).
  - Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron) was approved Apr 1, 2026, the first NME through the FDA National Priority Voucher program. Self-pay starts at $149/mo, and it needs no food or water restrictions ([Lilly](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-approves-lillys-foundayotm-orforglipron-only-glp-1-pill)).
  - TrumpRx.gov (Feb 5, 2026) aggregates manufacturer cash prices ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e102069)).
  - Households with a current GLP-1 user doubled from ~11% (Oct 2023) to 22% ([Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/glp1-weight-loss-drugs-medication-grocery-spend-numerator/827277/)).
- **The compounding window slammed shut.**
  - Shortages were declared resolved in Oct 2024 and Feb 2025. Novo sued Hims on Feb 9, 2026 and settled Mar 9, 2026; Hims became a Wegovy/Ozempic distributor.
  - FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list on Apr 30, 2026 ([Metabolic Ledger timeline](https://metabolicledger.com/regulatory/fda-compounding-cliff)).
  - Compounded supply had peaked at ~30% of US GLP-1 volume.
- **Semaglutide patents expired in India, Canada, Brazil, China and Turkey in ~April 2026.**
  - India: 40+ generic launchers; branded ₹10-16k/mo fell to as low as ₹1,290/mo ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/23/novo-nordisk-cheap-weight-loss-drugs-india-generic-ozempic-wegovy-semaglutide.html)).
  - Canada: generics ~40-60% below brand (lower confidence, [aggregator](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/semaglutide-generics-india-canada-filings-cmrlmu1n5bp6yqt0jxtvx2qkw)).
- **Payer retrenchment.**
  - Employers covering GLP-1s for weight loss fell from 72% (2025) to 60% (2026), and another 14% have dropped or plan to drop by 2027. GLP-1s are 11.4% of employer pharmacy claims, up from 6.9% in 2023. Starbucks, PepsiCo and Cigna's own plan cut coverage ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/benefits/more-employers-are-dropping-glp1-weight-loss-coverage-before-open-enrollment-588713.aspx)).
  - Medicare "Bridge" gives $50/mo copay from Jul 1, 2026 through Dec 31, 2027. The copay does not count toward the $2,100 Part D out-of-pocket cap ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/medicare-obesity-drug-glp-1-coverage-starting-july-1.html)).
- **Coverage erosion in the ACA and Medicaid.**
  - Enhanced ACA subsidies expired; federal-marketplace enrollment fell ~21%, and Urban estimates 4.8M newly uninsured ([Spotlight PA](https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/05/aca-enrollment-drops-health-insurance-subsidies-expire-federal-government/), [Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/aca-subsidies-expired-2026-4-million-uninsured-urban-institute-476153)).
  - Medicaid work requirements hit all 41 expansion states Jan 1, 2027 (Nebraska started May 1, 2026). Projected 5.5-6.3M losses and a 12.5-14.2% operating-margin hit for hospitals in expansion states ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/heres-how-medicaid-work-requirements-disenrollments-will-dent-hospital-operating-margins)).

## 2. Acute, quantified pain points

- **Persistence and rebound.**
  - Only ~1/3 of non-diabetic obesity patients remain on therapy at 1 year. One survey has 79.7% of discontinuers stopping within 12 months, and cost is the top reason ([PMC survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13403254/), [MHE](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/cost-still-the-biggest-barrier-for-glp-1-access-navitus-survey-finds)).
  - Across 37 studies, stoppers regain weight ~4x faster than behavioral losers; STEP-1 extension patients regained ~2/3 within a year ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/solutions-emerging-post-glp-1-weight-regain-2026a1000ine)).
  - ~60% of ex-users intend to restart ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/19/new-glp-1-consumer-data-challenges-food-industry-forecasts/)).
- **Nutrition and muscle: nobody owns it.**
  - 38% of GLP-1 users vs 8% of non-users were malnourished before joint surgery, and >20% of T2D users had deficiencies at 12 months. Dietitians report no structured guidelines ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/glp-1s-are-treating-diagnosis-whos-treating-patient-2026a1000y9q), [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12512160/)).
  - 70% of users report losing joy in eating and 73% report taste changes, so "add protein" is not enough for food products.
- **Admin waste.**
  - ~12.6% initial denial rate, ~806M denied claims a year (~$262B), ~70% overturned. Physicians spend ~13 hrs/wk on prior auth, and PA denials are up a claimed 31% YoY ([Nirmitee](https://nirmitee.io/blog/healthcare-denial-trends-2026-data-root-causes-ai-playbook/), [Availity](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/04/27/availity-abrasion-index-2026-denials-prior-auth/)). Lower confidence: vendor blog stats.
- **Primary care access.**
  - Average PCP wait is ~31 days (+19% since 2022). Only 38% of Medicare patients seeking a new PCP got an appointment within 2 weeks ([Weence](https://weence.com/primary-care/why-primary-care-and-mental-health-shortages-persist-in-2026-and-what-it-means-for-patients/), [MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/new-patient-wait-times-largely-hold-flat-in-2026-as-some-groups-add-providers-in-bid-to-meet-demand)).
  - Concierge/DPC practices grew 83% (2018-23), and 82% of doctors are now employed by hospitals or corporations.

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps

- **The "after the prescription" layer.** Pharma sells the drug, telehealth sells the script, and nobody is paid to own months 6-24: dose titration down, muscle and bone preservation, nutrition, and restart or off-ramp planning. Pharma and payers profit only from more volume or lower cost, not persistence, so they are structurally weak here.
- **Employers that just dropped coverage but still face obese workforces.** They need a cheap, capped cash-pay pass-through (a $149-$299 pill program plus coaching) instead of open-ended coverage. PBMs are conflicted here because they earn on rebates.
- **Medicare Bridge users.** The $50 copay does not accrue toward the OOP cap and the program ends Dec 2027, which creates a cliff. Seniors on GLP-1s carry the highest sarcopenia risk and have almost no muscle-care product.
- **Newly uninsured (4.8M) and Medicaid losers (5-6M from 2027).** They fall back on cash-pay and low-cost care, with pill prices near $149.
- **Eating-disorder-adjacent users.** GLP-1 use was ~50% among binge-eating-disorder patients and ~42% among atypical anorexia patients (JAMA Psychiatry 2026). No screening or monitoring layer exists.
- **Incumbents' constraints.** Payers profit from denials, and hospitals cannot price transparently. Function and Neko are capital-heavy and diagnostic-only, with no treatment-and-adherence loop.

## 5. Weird signals

1. **Retatrutide gray market before approval.** Non-trial "retatrutide" use grew 1.8x per quarter from Oct 2023 to Mar 2026, and 71% of traced users bought outside trials (57% via online or telehealth vendors). Lilly reported >14,000 illegal listings. Australia issued an alert in Jun 2026 over liver failure and kidney injury, while Lilly's filing is planned for Q1 2027 ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/gray-market-retatrutide-less-weight-loss-more-cv-effects-2026a1000tpl), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/24/unapproved-obesity-drug-spawns-a-black-market-frenzy), [Pharm Tech](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/fda-cracks-down-on-black-market-peptide-trade/)). Demand for quality assurance (third-party verification, purity testing) is unmet.
2. **Federal deregulation of peptides.** HHS announced in Feb 2026 that ~14 of 19 Category 2 peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, GHK-Cu, etc.) would move back to compoundable. An FDA panel backed BPC-157 and KPV on Jul 23, 2026 despite agency concerns ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/23/fda-panel-okays-peptides-compound-pharmacies-bpc-157-kpv/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/23/nx-s1-5903202/fda-peptides-restrictions)). A legal peptide-clinic and compounding supply chain is forming with no evidence base.
3. **GLP-1 for alcohol.** A Lancet RCT (May 2026, n=108) showed semaglutide cut heavy drinking days in AUD with obesity, with NNT 4.3 vs 7+ for approved drugs ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/glp-1-semaglutide-promising-alcohol-use-disorder-2026a1000e5q)). Addiction is a large unbranded adjacent market.
4. **Household spillover.** ~70% of GLP-1 users change what the household eats, and non-users adopt 30-50% of the changes. The affected consumer base is ~50% larger than the user count ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/19/new-glp-1-consumer-data-challenges-food-industry-forecasts/)).
5. **Ancillary spend shift.** Users cut pasta and bakery but raise spend on fragrance, skincare and supplements. 63% of "Ozempic face" facial-treatment patients are cosmetic-clinic first-timers. Jefferies estimates ~$600M/yr of airline fuel savings from lower passenger weight ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/glp-1-consumer-behavior-shift), [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/classes/glp-1-agonists/glp-1-trends)).
6. **Muscle-preserving pharma arrives soon.** Bimagrumab plus semaglutide held lean mass loss to ~7% of total loss (Phase 2), while S-LITE showed liraglutide plus supervised training produced lean-mass gain ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/glp-1-therapies-in-2026-beyond-blood-sugar-and-the-scale)). Until then, the non-drug muscle protocol is unowned.

## Idea seeds

- Post-GLP-1 maintenance and muscle "operating system" (nutrition, training, dose-taper, restart triggers) sold to employers and Medicare Advantage plans on persistence outcomes.
- Verified-supply and purity-testing layer for peptides and gray-market GLP-1s.
- Capped-cost GLP-1 benefit for employers (cash-pay pill plus coaching), replacing uncapped coverage.
- Care bundle for the newly uninsured or Medicaid-churned (low-cost cash-pay primary care plus a $149 GLP-1 pill).
- GLP-1-aware products for eating-disorder-risk screening and for alcohol-use programs.

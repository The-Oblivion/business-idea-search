# Space Economy Briefing: cheap launch, satellite data, direct-to-cell, earth observation
Date: 2026-09-28

Everything tagged [F] comes from direct page fetches (news homepages, Wikipedia pages, company pages), which a small model summarized, so treat figures as reported and not audited. ## 1. Biggest shifts, last 12-18 months
- **Starship reached orbit on 2026-09-28.** [F] Payload Space and TechCrunch both report it, and Satellite Today says it cut a 10-hour mission short after an engine issue. Payload calls it the 14th test flight and TechCrunch calls it the first orbital attempt, so the count is inconsistent. Orbital-class Starship implies a step-change in $/kg and in Starlink V3 deployment. Sources: https://payloadspace.com, https://techcrunch.com/category/space/, https://www.satellitetoday.com
- **Launch supply is exploding and the competitors are now funded.** [F] Stoke Space raised a $1B Series E in Sept 2026 after extending its Series D to $860M in Feb 2026, and unveiled Nova Block 2 at 15 t to LEO (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_Space). The Exploration Company raised $450M (Sept 2026), Impulse Space's Series D extension took the round to $808M, and Deep Blue Aerospace is raising about $300M (https://spacenews.com). 2025 had 321 orbital launches and 4,517 satellites deployed, versus 223 launches in 2023 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_industry).
- **Reliability is still fragile outside SpaceX.** [F] AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird 7 was lost on New Glenn on 2026-04-19 when it failed to reach operational orbit. BlueBird 6 flew on India's LVM3 in Dec 2025 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AST_SpaceMobile). Non-SpaceX launch remains a bottleneck for anyone building constellations.
- **Starlink is now a giant with falling ARPU.** [F] Subscribers went from 8.9M (Dec 2025) to 10.3M (Mar 2026) to 12M (Jun 2026), about +35% in six months. ARPU fell from $81 to $66 over one quarter (-19%). There are about 10,413 satellites in orbit, and 2025 revenue was $11.4B with $4.4B operating income (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlink). The mix is shifting to cheaper and international plans.
- **The spectrum land-grab settled.** [F] EchoStar sold spectrum to AT&T for $23B (announced 2025-08-26, closed 2026-07-28) and to SpaceX for $17B in cash and stock (announced 2025-09-08) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EchoStar). SpaceX now holds its own mobile spectrum, so it is a direct-to-cell operator and not just a carrier partner. Carriers are signing satellite-mobile partnerships in bulk (Satellite Today, Sept 15-16: "Key Questions Remain Unresolved", "European MNOs Disagree on the Value of Satellite D2D"). Beeline Kazakhstan launched Starlink Mobile service on 2026-09-28.
- **Consolidation.** [F] Rocket Lab agreed on 2026-06-29 to buy Iridium for about $8B ($27 cash plus stock, notional $54 per share, close expected mid-2027). Iridium shareholders have approved it, and Rocket Lab's CFO said more M&A is coming. Iridium had 2.627M billable subscribers at 2026-06-30 and $871.7M of 2025 revenue (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iridium_Communications, https://payloadspace.com).
- **Defense is the demand floor.** [F] Golden Dome has about $37-38B of FY25-26 funding, a cost estimate raised to $185B (Mar 2026), more than 1,000 qualifying offerors, and a reported $2B SpaceX 600-satellite award (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Dome_(missile_defense_system)). Northrop is delivering 21 data-relay satellites, and the US military confirmed deploying weapons to orbit (Sept 15, 2026).

## 2. Acute pain points (mostly inferred)
- [F, indirect] Satellite-mobile partnerships are multiplying, but "key questions remain unresolved" (Satellite Today, Sept 15). European MNOs disagree on whether D2D is worth paying for. Carriers do not know how to price it or how to differentiate it from the SpaceX and T-Mobile offering. That is a wedge for neutral tooling, such as coverage assurance, QoS measurement, billing and roaming brokers.
- [F, indirect] Launch and satellite-bus providers are raising nine-figure rounds (EnduroSat $205M, Aerospacelab supplying Lockheed, Hubble $200M), which suggests bus and payload integration is capacity-constrained.
- [P, unverified] EO customers complain about tasking latency, cloud and revisit gaps, and the cost of turning pixels into decisions. Analytics is still seen as the unsolved layer. This needs verification.
- [P, unverified] Operators face rising conjunction-screening load and rising insurance costs as LEO passes 10k Starlinks. This also needs verification.

## 3. Where the money is flowing, and what is crowded
- [F] Launch and mobility are the hot categories, with Stoke $1B, Exploration Co. $450M, Impulse $808M round total, and Deep Blue about $300M.
- [F] Space IoT and Bluetooth-to-satellite is hot. Hubble Network raised a $200M Series C at a $1.6B valuation (Sept 28, 2026), bringing its total funding to $300M. Its Series B was $70M in Sept 2025 (https://payloadspace.com). It says it has 6 operational satellites and next-generation satellites in 2027, and it claims 100M+ terrestrial access points, coverage in about 2,500 cities, and detection at 4 dBm in the future versus +20 dBm today (https://hubble.com).
- [F] Space Capital reports $488B invested in the space economy since 2009 across 3,000+ companies (https://www.spacecapital.com/quarterly). European rounds are hot: Payload counts the EnduroSat raise as the third nine-figure European space raise in two weeks.
- [F] EO is mature and government-anchored. Planet has a EUR240M Germany contract (July 2025), a $230M SKY Perfect JSAT deal for 10 Pelican satellites (Jan 2025), and a Berlin factory (Sept 2025) that doubles Pelican production. It targets up to 10 revisits per day (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_Labs).
- **Crowded:** launch vehicles, satellite buses, broadband constellations (Meridian Space is planning another), and generic EO imagery.

## 4. Underserved segments and structural gaps (analysis, partly inference)
- **Anything that requires neutrality from SpaceX.** Starlink is both the network and a competitor to carriers, so carriers, governments and enterprises want a hedge. That means multi-constellation and multi-orbit aggregation, sovereign backup, and independent QoS auditing.
- **Post-Starship in-space services.** Cheaper mass to orbit raises demand for power, compute, manufacturing and servicing. Signals are Star Catcher (power-beaming demo ready to launch), Outpost (in-space manufacturing service launched, first mission no earlier than 2028), Besxar (orbital chip fab) and AstroForge (AI commanding of DeepSpace-2). Almost all of it is pre-revenue.
- **Decision products on top of commodity pixels.** Incumbents sell imagery or subscriptions, and the buyer wants an answer such as insurance, credit, compliance, or methane and CO2 attribution (Planet's Tanager hyperspectral satellite launched Aug 2024). Vertical analytics tied to a regulated buyer remains open.
- **The gap between "phone texts from space" and "phone has a data plan from space".** Iridium NTN Direct is expected later in 2026 (live demos as of July 2026), and AST is spectrum-rich (45 MHz L-band approval from the FCC on 2025-06-13) but launch-constrained. There is room for terminal, chipset, enterprise and vertical products that use whichever network wins.

## 5. Weird signals
- **Starlink's ARPU fell 19% in one quarter** ($81 to $66) while subscribers grew 16% in that quarter. Growth is coming from lower-price and lower-income markets, which is the opposite of what most analysts model. [F]
- **Bluetooth-to-satellite is a $1.6B company.** Ordinary consumer Bluetooth tags reach orbit directly, and the next generation targets 4 dBm (vs +20 dBm today). That could turn every Bluetooth chip into a satellite endpoint. [F]
- **Rocket Lab is buying a legacy operator for about $8B.** The "launch company becomes a vertically integrated space prime" thesis is now being priced. A CFO signaling "more M&A on the horizon" suggests targets exist among mid-cap operators and EO firms. [F]
- **Valor Equity's Antonio Gracias is distributing SpaceX stock to his firm's investors (Sept 16).** That is a liquidity event in an otherwise illiquid asset. It suggests a growing secondary-market and pre-IPO tooling gap. [F, one headline, interpretation mine]
- **Orbital manufacturing and power-beaming startups are shipping hardware in the same month a full-orbit Starship flew.** The market is pricing cheap mass-to-orbit before it exists. [F]

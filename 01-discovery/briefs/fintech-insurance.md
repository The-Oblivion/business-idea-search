# Briefing: Finance (stablecoins, payments, embedded finance, insurance gaps, household finance), as of 2026-09-28

**Method caveat.** The WebSearch budget was already spent (200/200), so this briefing comes from WebFetch of publisher front pages, about 12 full articles, and Google News RSS query feeds. RSS-derived items are headline, publisher and date only; treat them as leads, not verified article text. Feed pattern: `https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=<query>`. Numbers below are as reported in the cited pages.

## 1. Biggest shifts (last 12-18 months)

**Stablecoins are now regulated plumbing, and they are used more than they are held.**
- GENIUS Act signed 2025-07-18 (Senate 68-30, House 308-122) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GENIUS_Act)). On 2026-09-24 the Fed proposed reserve-limit and capital rules for issuers ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/category/policy)). Statutory effective date is roughly Jan 2027, from my memory; not verified here.
- Supply has been flat at about $290B since Oct 2025, through a roughly 50% Bitcoin drawdown. Annual transfer volume is over $90T, doubled year on year, and daily turnover rose from 0.38x to 0.78x ([The Block research](https://www.theblock.co/research/stablecoins/the-stablecoin-trust-layer-infrastructure-for-digital-dollar-adoption-413764)).
- BIS-cited estimate: only about 1.1% of volume is real payments. The article's stated base figure is ambiguous. Stablecoin payment volume is "a few hundred billion" against a $200T payments market (same source).
- USDT is about $140B and USDC about $74B. Binance took a $100M Circle stake at $80.84/share on a 5-year distribution deal, and USDC pairs on Binance went from 140 to 329 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/09/26/binance-deal-gives-circle-a-boost-in-stablecoin-race-with-tether-analysts-say)).
- Circle also bought Tazapay for $400M, to build local banking rails in emerging markets.
- OCC charter wave: Circle got a full trust charter on 2026-07-10, Coinbase in April, and Stripe's Bridge and Crypto.com conditional nods in Feb. Payoneer, Block and Laser Digital have applied (Banking Dive and FinTech Weekly headlines via Google News RSS).
- The Senate CLARITY Act fight is over stablecoin "rewards" and deposit flight. Banks call it a "$6 trillion war for deposits" (CoinDesk 2026-09-14, CoinGecko headline).
- Incumbents are moving in. Citi and Coinbase launched virtual accounts and "Spring by Citi" stablecoin acceptance with fiat settlement on 2026-09-28 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-09-28-citi-coinbase-stablecoin-payments-corporate-clients-417082)). Visa and Mastercard joined Rain's Agentic Payments Alliance (2026-08-18). DoorDash is on Stripe/Tempo. Goldman put a $100B Treasury fund into crypto plumbing (CoinDesk 2026-09-28).

**Open banking is unstable.**
- The CFPB 1033 rule is stayed by a Kentucky court, and Sixth Circuit appeals are paused. A rewrite went to OIRA on 2026-08-06. It may let banks charge aggregators after a free-request threshold and limit fintech "data rationing" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/regulation/2026/cfpb-open-banking-rewrite-could-put-data-access-fees-at-center-of-section-1033-fight/)).
- States are filling the gap: New York proposed open-banking-style legislation in May 2026. The CFPB itself is in flux; a director nominee was named 2026-06-10.

**Tariffs are creating a cash event.** IEEPA tariffs were struck down. More than $100B has been refunded (CNBC 2026-08-07, Forbes 2026-09-09), after $35B by May. The government is fighting a trade judge's order to refund all importers (WaPo 2026-08-11). Tariff lawsuits are surging (Bloomberg Law). Replacement Section 122 tariffs are being challenged by 25 states.

**Insurance is decoupling upstream from downstream.**
- Reinsurance is softening. Jefferies expects declines to continue through 2027 and the floor not until 2030, driven by record traditional capital ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/reinsurance-rate-declines-may-slow-by-2028-soft-market-floor-may-not-be-found-till-2030-jefferies/)).
- Cat bonds are at record issuance: $18.9B YTD, $65.6B outstanding, and a record $11.3B in Q2 ([Artemis dashboard](https://www.artemis.bm/dashboard/)).
- Meanwhile homeowner nonrenewals hit records. Utah had the highest rate in 2025. New Mexico saw a record 6,200. Some counties saw rate spikes up to 33% in H1 2026 (Insurify). Insurance now rivals mortgage payments (Tacoma News Tribune, Aug 2026). California passed three claims and nonrenewal bills on 2026-09-03, and its FAIR Plan is "insuring more new home loans" (beinsure, Aug 2026).

## 2. Acute, quantified pain

- **Scams.** The FTC reports a record $15.9B lost in 2025, up from $12.5B in 2024 and about 5x since 2020. Social-media scam losses are 8x 2020. Imposter scams cost $3.5B. American Banker says banks see $68B in scam losses that "Washington misses" (2026-07-22). That is a 4x gap between consumer-reported and bank-side figures.
- **Household credit.** Debt is $18.8T, which fell $13B in Q2 for the first decline in 6 years. Credit cards are $1.26T. Auto originations hit a record $211B and auto delinquencies also hit a record (CBT/Yahoo/CNBC "K-shaped," Aug 2026). Half of new bankruptcies are ages 40-59 (24/7 Wall St, 2026-09-15). HELOCs are surging (Wolf Street).
- **Claims disputes.** State Farm hail-claim lawsuits are spreading from Oklahoma (NPR 2026-04-28, NBC 2026-03-11). Contractors, roofers and adjusters are all in the fight. State Farm is adding 3,000 claims staff (Insurance Journal 2026-09-25).
- **Flood gap.** Moody's puts US uninsured flood exposure at $375B-$1T. Private flood capacity is growing but "customers aren't buying" (Insurance Business 2026-07-22). Neptune Flood passed 300k policies and $400M premium.
- **AI liability gap.** ISO's generative-AI exclusion is already on thousands of CGL policies (Insurance Business 2026-09-16). Policyholders are alarmed and "startups rush in" (PYMNTS 2026-05-01).
- **Small business.** SBA loans get more paperwork from Oct 1 (Forbes 2026-09-22). Immigrant-owned firms are cut out of guaranteed loans (Shelterforce 2026-08-28). Card swipe-fee legislation stays alive: Trump backed the Credit Card Competition Act again on 2026-08-04, while Colorado's interchange law was vetoed.
- **Consumer credit rules.** The EWA and BNPL rulebook is being written state by state. Colorado sued EarnIn (2026-09-08). Maryland banned EWA tipping. NY and Illinois have BNPL rules (Consumer Finance Monitor, Mayer Brown).
- **Unbanked.** Per the FDIC's 2023 survey, 4.2% of households (5.6M) are unbanked and 14.2% (19M) underbanked. The top barrier is minimum balances ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/household-survey)).

## 3. Where money is flowing (and what's crowded)

- **Insurtech.** Q2 2026 funding was $2.44B across 95 deals, the highest since 2022. AI-centric companies took 99.1% of it, while early-stage funding fell 51.8% (Gallagher Re, via Reinsurance News, 2026-08-06). Outmarket raised a $34.5M Series B at a $335M valuation four months after its $17M Series A. It has 300+ agencies as customers, including 25% of the top 100 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/insuretech-outmarket-raises-34-5m-just-months-after-prior-round/)).
- **Insurance M&A and IPOs.** Ethos IPO'd in early 2026. Fidelis filed for an IPO on 2026-09-28. H&F is exploring a sale of Applied Systems at up to $10B (Insurance Journal).
- **Remittances.** Felix raised a $200M Series C: a16z put in $87M of equity and General Catalyst $113M of debt. It has $8B in cumulative volume, revenue up 2.5x, and 11 LatAm markets ([Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/fintech-whatsapp-remittance-startup-felix-raises-200m-a16z-general-catalyst/)).
- **Identity and other rounds.** Socure raised $156M at a $5.2B valuation. American Banker (2026-08-20) says fintech investment "focuses on large private deals."
- **Crowded categories.** Stablecoin issuance and on/off-ramps, agentic-payment rails (Visa, Mastercard, Coinbase, Ripple and Stripe all in), BNPL and EWA, and AI for broker back office.

## 4. Underserved gaps and incumbent blind spots

- Downstream payers of tariffs (small businesses, consumers) generally cannot claim refunds. Importers of record get the cash, and small ones pay to file ("not without a cost," Colorado Sun 2026-07-02).
- Reinsurance is cheaper but primary property policyholders see none of it. Nonrenewed homeowners are pushed to FAIR plans. They need mitigation, documentation and claims-dispute help that carriers are structurally conflicted from providing.
- Insurers are excluding AI risk, and agent-driven commerce has no liability or consumer-protection layer.
- Scam victims: banks bear $68B, and no product sits between the consumer, the bank and the platform.
- Independent agencies are shrinking (39k in 2024 to 37k in 2026, Insurance Journal), yet 95% of commercial insurance is still sold via humans (Outmarket founder).
- Stablecoin usability for SMB and household use is still weak. Continuous behavioral KYC is the flagged gap (The Block).
- Banks cannot offer stablecoin yield, or are lobbying to ban it (CLARITY). Non-banks and OCC trust banks can compete there.

## 5. Weird signals

1. Stablecoin supply has been flat for 10 months while velocity doubled. The story is payments and collateral, not speculation.
2. Over $100B in tariff refunds went to importers of record, most of whom had already passed costs on. Refund claims, factoring and pass-through recovery are unclaimed territory.
3. The 1% US remittance tax hits cash-funded transfers only. Forbes (2026-07-17) says it is "pushing dollars onto rails the IRS cannot see." Tennessee adopted its own remittance tax (2026-05-27). Felix reached $8B on WhatsApp.
4. AI-exclusion clauses are spreading across CGL policies while 99% of insurtech dollars chase AI.
5. Cat bonds and reinsurance are at record capital and softening, while primary homeowner nonrenewals set records. The spread is a political flashpoint heading into the midterms (Insurance Business 2026-08-03).
6. India ended UPI's free ride for larger transactions (TechCrunch 2026-09-15). Even the best "free" payment rail is being monetized.
7. Tether faces a Senate Democrats' report alleging about $2B of Iranian flows in a year, against $550M frozen ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/09/28/tether-is-a-lifeline-for-iranian-regime-senate-dems-say-in-new-report)). Compliance-as-a-product demand is rising.

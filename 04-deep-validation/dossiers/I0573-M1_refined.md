# I0573-M1 Cure Desk: refined after critique (2026-10-13)

**Verdict: alive = false as a company.** The three critiques converge, and my own checks agree. What survives is a small services bolt-on worth about $10-40K a year in Nebraska and under about $1M nationally. It deserves two weeks of phone calls, not a build.

Labels: [V] verified by me this session from primary text. [C] taken from the critics' fetched sources, not re-fetched. [S] search-summary only. [I] my inference. [A] my assumption.

## 1. What I checked myself

1. **Nebraska's declaration form (MLTC-118, 5/2026) [V].** It has no employment or self-employment line. It covers volunteer hours, work programs, school or CTE, child and disabled-person caregiving, incarceration, medical frailty, treatment programs, VA rating, inpatient services and medical travel. It states it "only accepts attestation for the activities listed" and that other activities "may require a different form of verification." It carries a "Signature and Relationship of Person Who Helped" line. It also authorizes DHHS to request information from third parties itself.
   - What this changes: critic 1 is right that the state supplies the form and the evidence rules. But a cash worker in Nebraska has no state-provided self-declaration line, so the route is undefined today. That is the only real gap I could confirm. Whether it is a paid gap is the test in section 5.
   - Also: because the state can contact employers itself, a pre-arranged statement may be redundant. A caseworker may simply phone the household.
2. **42 CFR 435.908(c) [V, via Cornell LII].** Assisters "may not impose, accept or receive payment or compensation in any form from applicants or beneficiaries." The paragraph does not address payment per application from an institution. The per-enrollment bans the critics cite are Marketplace rules and a Virginia state rule [C]. Nebraska's rule is unverified.
   - What this changes: critic 1 and 3's legal point is weaker than stated at the federal Medicaid level but still a state-by-state counsel item. The redesign removes the exposure anyway (flat fee, vendor is not an assister).

## 3. The strongest honest version

- **Wedge customer:** one Nebraska health-center organization with a large Spanish-speaking panel and a measurable backlog of cash-worker notices. Reach it through the Health Center Association of Nebraska (HCAN), the aggregator for its seven members [C].
- **First product:** a Cash-Work Evidence Kit, not a platform. A bilingual one-page "statement of work performed" that a household employer can sign (hours and dates, no Medicaid mention). A patient self-declaration worded for the notice's "any information you have" request. A CHW script. A spreadsheet tracker logging each state response and reason.
- **What we no longer do:** WhatsApp, outbound texts to employers, crew-leader batches, a refugee language, revenue-share white-labeling, acceptance-contingent fees, and any claim that we decide compliance.
- **Pricing:** $400 a month per health-center organization with 10 cases included, then $40 each. Optional statewide license to a primary care association [A: $10-25K a year]. Reject credit if a state rejects for a reason our packet should have prevented. The base is about 7% of the $70K staff hire that VMG says pays for itself [C].
- **Why now:** the policy is real and dated. The IFR takes effect Jan 1, 2027, Nebraska has been live since May 1, and notices carry a 30-day cure window [C]. Paid demand is 2028+ because 2027 allows self-declaration [C]. The cost-curve story does not help us: it is available to every competitor.
- **Moat (7 Powers):** none reachable in the window. Scale economies fail at about 10 cases a month per site. Switching costs are near zero because a PDF is replaceable. Network effects died with the batch screen. The only candidate is a cornered resource: state-office-level accept/reject data. That needs roughly 500 filed packets across at least two states [A], and it perishes with the interim rule.
- **Bottom-up market [A-heavy]:**
  - 1,359 health-center awardees [F, per the dossier's HRSA cite] x $4.8-8K a year (flat fee plus overage) is about $6.5-11M at 100% adoption. At 5-10% adoption that is about $0.3-1.1M. Nebraska: 7 orgs [C] x 25-75% adoption x $4.8-8K is about $8-42K.
  - This is lower than the dossier's $10-60M because the dossier priced at $120 a packet and 22 packets a month per site. The critics showed neither holds.
- **Competitors and honest edge:**
  - Free or state-side: CMS Emmy, state worksheets (Georgia, Ohio), Digital Public Works, Code for America, Nava.
  - Approver rails: Unite Us with Fortuna, Gainwell's CEV ecosystem, Helper Helper, Findhelp with Uno Health.
  - FQHC-side: Pointcare (exemption tracking only), CareMessage (outreach), outsourced eligibility vendors.
  - Edge: cheapest, least-integrated, paper-first, aimed at people with no digital trail. That is exactly the kind of edge that gets absorbed.
- **Scenarios (my judgment, replacing the dossier's 15/50/35):** Bull 5%, needing three things at once (states tighten documentation, third-party statements outrank self-declaration, a partner pays) for about $0.3-0.8M ARR by end-2028. Base 35%, a few organizations pay and ARR is $20-100K before absorption. Bear 60%, where the section 5 test fails and we stop at near-zero cost.

## 4. Pre-mortem (it is 2028 and this is dead)

1. States define "other sufficient information" to include self-signed worksheets, so nobody needs a packet. Not fixable by us.
2. A free state, CMS or nonprofit tool covers cash workers, or an approver-rail vendor adds household employers. Not fixable by us.
3. FQHCs hire a $70K CHW instead, or household employers refuse to sign. Partly testable now.

Founder fit also costs more than the numbers allow: bilingual operators, healthcare counsel and eligibility knowledge, with none in hand.

## 5. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test

**Assumption:** for cash-paid patients holding a Nebraska work-requirement notice, the coverage loss comes from missing or insufficient proof that a third-party statement would fix. It does not come from non-response, and it is not already fixed by the free route.

**Test (about 20-25 hours, $0, no build):**
- Days 1-2: write a 10-question interview script and the one-page bilingual statement. Write the pass and kill thresholds below before any call.
- Days 1-10: contact HCAN and the seven Nebraska FQHC enrollment or CHW leads, Legal Aid of Nebraska's work-requirement help line, and DHHS or a local caseworker office. Identify yourself honestly as a researcher. Target 8 completed conversations (at least 5 CHW or enrollment staff, 1 legal aid, 2 state or caseworker).
- Core questions: of your last 20 notices touching cash or informal workers, how many were no response, responded and approved, or responded and stalled or rejected, and why? What does the state accept in place of an employment line? Has a statement from the person who paid them ever changed an outcome? How many staff hours per case? Would your finance approve $400 a month, or would you assign a CHW?
- Days 8-14: show the statement to the CHWs and any willing household-employer proxies. Count would-sign versus would-not and why.

**Pass:** at least 3 of 5 or more centers report 5+ cash-worker cases in the last 60 days stalled or rejected for lack of proof, not non-response. At least one state or caseworker source says a third-party statement is better evidence or is requested in practice. At least one CHW lead says they would fund $400 a month.

**Kill:** most sources say self-declaration or "any information" is accepted without trouble. Or stalls are non-response. Or the state says it will publish or accept a free worksheet. **No-signal rule:** fewer than 4 completed conversations by day 10 counts as a kill.

## 6. What would flip alive to true

All of: a Nebraska or second-state source confirming third-party statements outrank self-declaration for cash work; stalled cases shown to be proof-driven; a paid commitment from at least one health center; and 40% of household-employer attesters responding within 72 hours. Absent the first two, move on.

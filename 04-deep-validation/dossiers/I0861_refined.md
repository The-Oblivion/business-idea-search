# I0861 Refined: Exclusivity-Stage Data Audit for China-Origin Preclinical Assets

Refined 2026-10-13 after three "serious" critiques. Labels: **[F]** fact from a cited source (as reported in the dossier or critiques; **[F*]** = I fetched it myself this run), **[S]** search-summary only, medium confidence, **[I]** my inference or model, **[G]** gap, not known.

## Verdict

**Alive, but only as a capped audit practice. Dead as a venture, as a software or database play, and as the original pitch.**

What the critiques killed, and I accept it:
- The "72-hour raw-data sprint." Sellers control the data and Chinese transfer rules gate it.
- "White space." Vision Lifesciences, Alacrita and ICON all market versions of this.
- The inspection database, the BIOSECURE supply-chain-mapping second product, and the $20k pilot.
- The BIOSECURE why-now, and any venture-scale or software-scale claim.

What survives: a fixed-price, written, term-sheet-stage audit of a China-origin **preclinical** package, sold to buyers with no China bench, delivered from outside China by a named FDA-experienced bilingual principal. Honest ceiling: roughly **$0.2-0.9M revenue a year** (section 8). Probability it clears the 2-week test: about 25% [I]. Probability it becomes a $0.5M+/yr practice: about 10% [I].

Founder-fit gate: no founder constraints were given, but this version only exists if a named, FDA-experienced, Mandarin-fluent GLP-tox/CMC-regulatory principal is on the team. Finding that person is step zero. Because such a person can already freelance this work, the founder has to add selling and operations that the principal cannot do alone.

## 1. Wedge customer (narrowed)

Not "pharma BD, VCs, royalty funds and mid-caps." The wedge is a **first- or second-time China in-licensor with no China-based staff, at exclusivity or term-sheet stage on a preclinical asset**, where one named person (BD/corp-dev head, CSO, or the NewCo sponsor's partner) owns a per-deal external-expense budget.
- Excluded on purpose: Novartis-type in-house teams (a Shanghai-based due-diligence director role was posted 2026-07-15 [F, critique A fetched it]), RA Swiftbridge, Banyan, Forbion-type platforms. They are not customers and not worth chasing.
- Examples to test, not claims they would buy: NewCo vehicles like K2 Therapeutics ($50M seed), mid-cap biotechs with one or two China in-licenses, and royalty funds opening Asia desks (Royalty Pharma, Hong Kong office [F]).
- Minimum deal size [I]: upfront of about $20M or more. The "$45k is 0.03-0.06% of a $78-172M upfront" ratio in the dossier uses averages pulled up by big pharma. For a sub-scale buyer paying $10-30M, $45k is 0.15-0.45%, still small but not trivial. Segment size is replaced with a counted number in the 2-week test.
- Acknowledged: this segment is replenished by new entrants but depleted as buyers gain experience (critique A1). Treat the business as a 2-4 year window to harvest, not a permanent market.

## 2. First product: a staged audit, not a sprint

**Why preclinical only.** Chinese data-transfer and human-genetic-resources rules bite hardest on human data. The Morgan Lewis HGR piece [F*] covers clinical, genomic and personal information and says nothing about preclinical animal data or CMC data. That is not clearance, but a preclinical GLP-tox/pharmacology/CMC package avoids most patient-level data. The Merck-SciBrunch deal ($2.13B, $400M upfront, preclinical KRAS G12D) shows large preclinical packages change hands [F]. Phase 1 and clinical audits are a later expansion, only after PRC counsel signs off.

**Stage A: Package Red-Flag Audit (10 business days from data-room access).** Works on what the buyer actually receives plus the public record:
- LLM-assisted translation and extraction of Chinese-language study reports (bought, not built).
- Cross-document consistency: dates, animal numbers, batch IDs, test-facility identity vs the GLP certificate's scope, CMC batch lineage.
- CRO/CDMO inspection and enforcement history from public regulator records (NMPA, FDA, EMA); licensed list lookups for ownership and 1260H/BIOSECURE exposure, reproduced with source, never investigated.
- A Western-format IND-readiness gap list with estimated **rework cost and months**. That is the number the term sheet needs, because it converts findings into price and milestone-structure leverage.
- A mandatory "not checked" section. The memo is findings, not certification.

**Stage B: Confirmatory Data Audit (contract-triggered).** The product ships with a **standard audit-right clause kit** for buyers' counsel: a named independent auditor reviews raw data and audit trails for a defined sample of studies, as a closing condition or a condition to the first milestone.
- This turns the data-access bottleneck into a contract term. IBA reports that data-transfer arrangements are now commonly conditions precedent [F*]. Banyan's CEO says getting data out is hard [F].
- Making it a standard clause removes the "asking signals distrust" cost (critique B3). It is not a favour requested in a contested process.
- Open legal issue: CAC's 2022 guideline treats remote access to China-stored data from abroad as a cross-border transfer [S, law-firm alerts]. For preclinical, non-personal data this may not matter, and Morgan Lewis suggests in-China secure data rooms [F*]. But "reviewer sits abroad, views remotely" is not safe to assume. PRC counsel must answer this before the first Stage B sale.

**Out of scope:** 72-hour promises, the inspection database, BIOSECURE ownership-chain mapping as a product, any on-the-ground collection in China, human-genetic-resource data, Phase 1 raw data at launch.

| Retainers | None in year 1. After two engagements, offer a pre-negotiated framework agreement (Alacrita runs 3-5 diligences a year per client [F]) | Removes repeat procurement friction |

No fixed bench. Contractors only; no hires until backlog exceeds about 3 months. One-off gating costs before the first sale (PRC counsel opinion, counsel-drafted engagement letter, E&O quote) are unpriced [G].

**Where it sits in the funnel.** Catenion says a licensee runs 50-100 pre-diligences and up to 10 full diligences per closed deal [F]. A fixed fee on every early screen would be spent mostly on assets that die. So: **sell only after exclusivity or term sheet**, when the buyer is about to commit upfront money and a budget line exists. Never sell the triage.

## 4. Channel (no dependency)

1. **Direct, principal-led.** Named first- and second-time licensees (from the segment census), plus free content: the audit-right clause kit and a "documents to demand at exclusivity" checklist.
2. **Counsel-engaged, optional.** Where the buyer's counsel wants privilege, counsel can engage us as expert. There are no referral fees and no exclusivity. The ABA fee-sharing and referral rules (5.4(a), 7.2(b); state versions vary [F]) are why. Ethics counsel signs off on the fee flow. Kovel protection is thin for commercial diligence [F], so privilege is a bonus and not the pitch.
3. **Retrospective public-case memos.** The principal publishes what a pre-signing screen would have flagged in public cases (Elevar's GMP-site CRL; the Novo-KBP allegations are unadjudicated, so use only documented facts).

Bankers are not a channel: they are paid on closing, and the product's best output is "reprice or restructure." Hard rule: no single channel or law firm above 30% of revenue.

## 5. Operating rules (China-side and liability risk)

- **Zero China nexus.** US-incorporated; staff and contractors resident in the US, EU or Singapore; no PRC entity, staff, travel, bank or assets. This is a response to the Mintz detentions and fine, the Bain and Capvision raids, the Supply Chain Security Regulations (effective 31 Mar 2026, Art. 13 "information gathering"), and MOFCOM's 5 Aug 2026 countermeasure listing of US due-diligence firms (6 or 7 firms, sources disagree; tied to Xinjiang sanctions, so applicability is my inference) [F/S per critiques].
- **Seller-consented materials only.** Work only on documents the seller or buyer legitimately provides under NDA. No outreach to Chinese CROs, no site visits, no third-party information gathering.
- **No human genetic resources data.** Refuse any HGR or personal-information datasets.
- **PRC counsel opinion before the first China-touching engagement.** The question: does a seller-consented, abroad-based review of preclinical/CMC documents fall outside Art. 13 and the foreign-related investigation regime, and does remote viewing trigger transfer rules [G].
- **Cost I accept:** Vision has Shanghai and Hong Kong offices; I give up ground truth. The offsetting claim is a clean, low-risk provenance for US buyers, which is unproven.
- **Liability.** Engagement letter with a cap of 1-2x fees, no third-party reliance beyond named financing parties, no legal-compliance opinions, scope-limited to listed documents. **E&O quote is a launch gate.** The memo says in writing that it cannot detect concealment (a KBP-type failure, if the allegations are true). A statistical consistency screen on tabulated data is a partial mitigant, a hypothesis to test with a biostatistician [I]. If buyers insist on blame transfer with large-brand reliance, we lose that buyer, and the test will show how many.

## 6. Why now (rebuilt)

1. **Stakes per deal rose.** Western-China licensing deals went from 42 (2022) to 93 (2025) (Evaluate), and average upfronts rose from $52M to $172M (Evaluate) or $38.8M to $77.7M (Reuters definition) [F]. A wrong asset costs more.
2. **FDA is adding inspection cost to unverifiable packages.** Goodwin (Sep 2026) reports more BIMO inspections including foreign Phase 1, updated country-risk site selection, and public flagging of sites that deny access [F]. No documented FDA refusal of China preclinical data exists yet [G], so this pain is partly rhetorical, and I do not rest the case on it.
3. **LLM translation and extraction became a commodity.** That is the enabling change for a small shop: reading a Chinese-language package in days costs little. It also benefits competitors (Wardley: extraction is commodity, judgment is custom).
4. **Not BIOSECURE.** The 18 Dec 2026 list is a date, not a spend event (critique C5; phased effects, a 7 Aug 2026 preliminary injunction on WuXi AppTec's 1260H designation, Sayari and Interos already pitch screening).

Test: would this have worked in 2019? No. Deal flow was a fraction of today's. That part holds.

## 7. Moat (7 Powers, honest)

- Scale, network, switching costs: none at launch. Cornered resource: no. Novartis is hiring the same profile.
- **Reachable: Branding**, earned. Mechanism: a **hash-timestamped log of red flags**, with anonymized aggregate hit rates published by category, plus public retrospective cases. Competitors sell marketing; nobody found publishes falsifiable calibration. Timeline: 18-30 months, and only if real engagements happen.
- **Partial counter-positioning, only against large vendors.** ICON, Deloitte and PwC cannot offer $45k fixed-fee work without cannibalizing hourly engagements. Boutiques (Vision, Alacrita) can copy freely.
- Conclusion: no durable power. This is a window harvest, so structure for cash with variable costs, not growth.

## 8. Bottom-up market [I unless noted]

- 2026 run rate about 170-190 out-licensing deals (H1: 81 by NMPA, 86 by Insight [F]). Remove big pharma (about 40% of assets in-licensed [F, IQVIA via Fierce]) and deals with no verifiable package; the dossier leaves **85-110 addressable transactions a year** (assumption).
- Attach at exclusivity stage: 15-25% (no source) gives 13-28 Stage A x $45k = **$0.6-1.3M**.
- Stage B follow-on: 30-50% of Stage A x about $40k = **$0.15-0.55M**.
- **Serviceable pool: $0.75-1.85M a year.** Retainers and the BIOSECURE product are removed; the dossier's $1.5-4M included them.
- Capturable share at maturity: 25-50% (Vision, Alacrita, ICON and in-house take the rest). **Revenue ceiling about $0.2-0.9M a year.** That is a practice whose profit is similar to what the principal could earn freelancing; the case for building it as a firm is the founder's selling capacity and the brand compounding.
- The 85-110 and 15-25% figures are replaced by counts and purchase history in the 2-week test.

## 11. Riskiest assumption and the 2-week test

**Riskiest assumption:** a first- or second-time China in-licensor, at exclusivity, with a named budget owner, will pay $35-45k to a vendor with no track record for a preclinical data audit, rather than relying on the sponsor, a banker, in-house generalists, or an established vendor. There is zero direct evidence of this (dossier [G]; critic B found no price list, RFP or budget line).

**Second gate:** a credentialed principal exists and will be named.

**Test (14 days, under $300, about 30 founder hours):**
1. **Segment census (days 1-3).** From public deal announcements and trackers for Western-China licensing deals in 2025 to H1 2026, list the licensees and tag China office and prior China in-licenses. Output: counted "no-bench, first-or-second-time" buyers with a named BD owner. This replaces the 50-60% and 15-25% assumptions.
2. **10-12 purchase-history interviews (days 3-14).** About 150 targeted outreach messages at a 6-10% reply rate. If fewer than 8 calls land, the test is inconclusive, not a pass. Ask: What technical verification did you do on your last China deal? Who did it? What did it cost? Who approved it, from which budget? Would your counsel insert an audit-right clause? Close with: "At exclusivity on your next China asset, would you sign an engagement letter at $45k? Would you put that in writing?"
3. **Principal screen (days 1-10).** Talk to 5 ex-pharma bilingual GLP-tox/CMC-regulatory scientists. Would one be named principal, and on what terms?
4. **Competitor read.** Learn from buyers what quotes and lead times they received. Do **not** pose as a buyer to competitors.

**Pre-registered thresholds** (revealed purchases count more than stated intent):
- **Go:**
  - 4 or more of 10 name a budget owner and a figure of $30k or more.
  - At least 2 bought outside technical verification in the last 12 months from a firm that is not a bank or law firm.
  - At least 2 sign a one-paragraph non-binding intent statement.
  - At least 1 principal candidate is willing to be named.
- **Extend:** 2-3 of 10 give a number. Run 10 more calls and a principal-authored retrospective memo.
- **Kill:** 1 or fewer of 10, or most say the sponsor, a bank or an in-house person handles it, or most demand big-brand reliance.

## 12. After a Go: gates, then a 90-day kill clock

Before the first paid engagement: PRC counsel opinion; counsel-drafted engagement letter with cap and non-reliance; an E&O quote with premium under about 5% of revenue [I]; ethics-counsel sign-off on the fee flow.

Then: **three paid engagements at $35k or more within 90 days of selling, at least two not sourced from law firms, else kill.** Continue only if margin at $45k is 40% or higher.

Pause triggers: BINSA (H.R. 9102 in the House, S. 5316 in the Senate; introduction date reported as 2 or 12 June 2026, sources disagree) moving past committee; COINS final regulations (due 13 Mar 2027) designating biotech; any Treasury rule restricting licensing (Reuters, 18 Sep 2026: a loose rule is being weighed) [S]; FDA formalizing China-site data restrictions (shifts demand toward Stage B and FDA-readiness work).

## 13. Option value, not built now

An **issuer-pays "audit-ready package" attestation** for Chinese licensors would have a far higher ceiling, because sellers are more numerous than buyers and BioWorld says packages must withstand partner diligence [F]. It solves data access. It is also conflict-prone (like rating agencies), exposed to local competition (Dark Horse China, local CROs), and needs buyers to trust the mark first. **Gate:** at least 5 buyers ask sellers for the audit file, or sellers ask for it unprompted. Until then it is a note, not a plan.

## 14. Pre-mortem

It is 2028 and this is dead because:
1. No one with a budget buys from an unproven vendor. Testable now.
2. The principal never joined or left. A gate; not fixable after the fact.
3. Licensing was restricted, or the segment internalized. Not fixable; hedged only by zero fixed cost.

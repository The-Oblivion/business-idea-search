# I1172-M2 Dossier: Home Equity Assurance for Data Center Neighbors

Researched 13 Oct 2026 (about 40 searches and fetches). Tags: [C] cited fact, [I] my inference, [U] unverified or search-summary only.

## 1. Bottom line

The problem is real and developers are already paying for it, but in other forms. The structure as written has three serious weaknesses.

- **Insurance classification.** The one regulator ruling I found treats a pay-on-sale home-value guarantee as prohibited financial guaranty insurance.
- **Payout economics.** Sale-price noise alone is probably enough to drain the proposed escrow.
- **Buyouts.** Developers' actual 2026 answer is buyouts, not guarantees.

The bottom-up market is tens of millions of dollars a year at best, not hundreds of millions. I found no buyer, RFP or paid pilot for a third-party guarantee. The idea is **conditionally alive, in a narrower form** (section 9).

## 2. Direct competitors: none found; real substitutes are numerous

**No productized, third-party data-center home-value guarantee found.** Searches for developer, startup or insurer offerings returned nothing; insurer results covered only facility-side cover ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/insurance-coverage-data-centers-navigating-challenges-adapting-marketplace/)). "Not found" is not proof of absence. Crunchbase and PitchBook were not checked, and I could not find any record of Prevolt's funding [U].

**Developer-run substitutes (the budget this idea would redirect):**

- **Buyouts.** Nscale's "Good Neighbors" program in Mason County, WV offers the highest of three valuations plus a "material premium" and pays closing costs ([WCHS](https://wchstv.com/news/local/voluntary-buyout-offers-rolled-out-for-meadowlands-estates-homes-near-data-center)). About 15 of 53 homes had sold or were in process by June 2026 ([WSAZ](https://www.wsaz.com/2026/06/10/mason-county-data-center-developer-nscale-offering-buy-neighboring-homes/)). The homes become employee and contractor housing. [C]
- **Howell Township, MI.** The developer's attorney offered sale at appraised value plus 1% within 1,000 ft, plus an unspecified 2,500 ft tier ([WXYZ](https://www.wxyz.com/news/howell-township-data-center-informational-meeting-held)). [U]
- **DeForest, WI.** A draft QTS agreement would have bought homes within 1/4 mile at no less than 110% of assessed value if "materially adversely affected". The deal collapsed in January 2026, before any annexation vote ([Wisconsin Watch](https://wisconsinwatch.org/2026/08/wisconsin-deforest-data-center-deal-guaranteed-tax-revenue-river-improvements-affordable-housing/)). [C]
- **Lump sums.** NorthPoint (Hazle Township, PA) offered $10k to each of about 4,500 households ($45M, inside a $165M community benefits package) and was met with "are they trying to bribe us" ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/philadelphia/data-center-community-relations/northpoint-hazle-resident-checks-pennsylvania), [Propmodo](https://propmodo.com/cash-to-residents-is-the-newest-tool-in-the-data-center-fight/)). The offer is dated June 2026. Press accounts disagree on whether the township rejected the project before or after it. [C]

**Legislative substitute.** Illinois HB4319 (introduced 2026) would pay owners within 1,000 ft 1.5x their proven fair-market-value decline. It uses a certified appraisal at developer cost and allows suit after 90 days. A county-association group lists it as "Oppose", and I found no evidence it has passed ([ISAC](https://www.isacoil.org/ea309cec-8887-4476-b965-60d75b5f1b75/bill-details/)). [C] If something like it passes, a private guarantee could become a negotiated alternative or a safe harbor. [I]

**Historical analogs of the product itself (all weak track record):**

- **Municipal programs.** Oak Park, IL (1970s) reportedly never paid a claim ([Wikipedia summary of Shiller and Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_equity_protection)). Chicago's tax-funded districts saw few cash claims, though one district held about $9.6M ([WWNO](https://www.wwno.org/2014-06-24/chicago-program-designed-to-prevent-white-flight-gets-renewed-attention)).
- **Syracuse pilot (2002).** One-time fee of 1.5% of home value.
- **Home Value Insurance Co.** It sold only 172 policies, went into Ohio rehabilitation in 2012, and was dissolved in January 2013 ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2012/09/18/263329.htm)). [U]
- **Working Equity / Home Value Protection.** CB Insights lists it as dead ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/home-value-protection)).
- **Home Diversification Corp.** A junior-lien contract at about $19/month, launched in 2019 ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/fintech-startup-to-launch-home-equity-protection-product/179747)).

All of these were consumer-paid or taxpayer-funded and index-based. None was a developer-funded, locally negotiated escrow, so this idea is structurally new. [I]

**Sensor vendors** (KETOS, Aethair, Envirosuite, Kunak) from the parent research share no product, buyer budget or moat with this mutation. Not re-verified.

## 3. Incumbents who could add this

- **Fund administrators (Epiq, Kroll, JND).** Epiq already acts as administrator and escrow or QSF manager, usually paired with a bank as escrow agent ([Epiq](https://epiqglobal.com/en-us/services/class-action-mass-tort/mass-torts/mass-tort-fund-administration-payments)). A vendor blog puts QSF administration at 0.25 to 1.5% of assets a year, $2.5k to $15k setup, and $150 to $500 per disbursement ([LegalSoft](https://www.legalsoft.com/blog/settlement-fund-management)). That source is weak. [C]
  - They have the neutrality, bank relationships and track record a county attorney wants, and face no counterparty-credit objection to a 10-year obligation. [I]
  - They lack valuation models, the one real differentiator here.
- **Title and escrow firms, AVM vendors and appraisal management companies.** They hold the data and closing-table access but do not sell neutrality or a county-approved formula. [I]
- **Law firms and consultancies.** They already draft community benefit agreements. The Columbia Law piece describes property value guarantees as an existing CBA clause, with the developer guaranteeing appraised value as of a date, usually ending on sale ([Columbia](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/climatechange/2026/05/28/community-benefits-agreements-and-data-center-development/)). It does not say who administers them. [C]

The moat is thin. The mechanics are copyable; the defensible assets are the price-effect model, county relationships and first track record. [I]

## 4. Bottom-up market size

**Where the exposure is growing:**

- Large (50+ MW) data centers rose from 49 in 2018 to 347 in 2026.
- ZIP codes hosting one rose from 12 (2015) to 108 (June 2026), with 125 projected by year-end.
- About 1.5% of US home sales fall within 5 miles of a large facility, projected at 2.3% once the 2027 pipeline lands.
- The median 2026 facility sits in an area with about 70% fewer residential units per square mile than 2017 openings, about 27 miles from the city center.

Source for all four: Realtor.com's August 2026 report as summarized by [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/data-center-boom-is-putting-more-homes-near-facilities/). The Realtor.com page itself was blocked to me. [C]

**Reachable buyers.**

- Data Center Watch counted at least 75 projects (about $130B) disrupted by local opposition in Q1 2026 ([DCW](https://datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026)). Another outlet attributes 45 projects (about $68B) to Q2 ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/data-center-watch-45-us-ai-data-center-projects-worth-68b-blocked-or-delayed-in)). DCW does not define "disrupted" publicly. [C]
- Annualizing gives about 240 contested projects a year. These are the buyers with acute need. [I]

**Households per project.**

- Observed range: 53 homes (Mason), about 150 (Ashburn Regency), about 4,500 (Hazle).
- Falling density means typical new sites have fewer neighbors. I assume 300 to 1,500 enrollable households. [I]

**Revenue per program, at the idea's own price points:**

| Program size | Enrollment at $300 | 1%/yr escrow fee | Claim fees | Year 1 total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 households | $300k | $40k | ~$45-75k/yr | ~$0.4M |
| 3,000 households | $900k | $120k | ~$135-225k/yr | ~$1.0-1.2M |

Claim fees assume every sale is adjudicated. The idea's "$1M+ per program" requires a top-end site of about 3,000 households. [I]

**Ceiling and realistic capture.** If all 240 contested projects bought at 1,000 households each, the ceiling is about $70 to 100M a year. Realistic early capture of 5 to 10% is about $4 to 10M a year: a services business, not software. [I]

**Float.** The 3-month Treasury yield is 4.26% as of 13 Oct 2026 ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610)). That gives about $0.5M a year on a $12M escrow. A custodian bank will keep most of it. [C/I]

## 5. Evidence of demand and willingness to pay

**Residents want value protection, not checks.**

- A Hazle retiree said $10k "doesn't match how much it's going to tank my property value" ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/09/28/data-centers-and-their-discontents/)).
- Property values were also raised at Yorkville IL, East Whiteland PA and Decatur Township IN hearings in 2026 (search summaries) [U].
- Propmodo expects future offers tied to "utility bill credits or property value guarantees". That is a prediction, not an observation. [C]

**Developers can afford it.**

- Shell-and-core cost is about $11.3M per MW (JLL via [Datacentres.com](https://www.datacentres.com/news/data-centre-construction-costs-11m-per-mw)). A 300 MW campus like Hazle is therefore about $3.4B, so a $4M escrow is roughly 0.1% of shell-and-core. Willingness to be the obligor is the issue, not budget. [C/I]

**What is missing.**

- No developer I found has offered a value guarantee.
- Sabey, in Indianapolis, answered value objections with an appraiser opinion, the George Mason study and a commissioned Integra study (80 homes within 1.5 mi appreciated 42% against 41% locally). The commission approved variances on 18 March 2026 ([Mirror Indy](https://mirrorindy.org/sabey-data-centers-indianapolis-property-values-protect-decatur-township/), [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-community-relations/data-centers-may-not-be-a-drag-on-nearby-home-prices-135084)). [C]
- I found no RFP, county ordinance or paid pilot for a neutral administrator.

**Wind precedent is thin.**

- A Maine bill, an Iowa county ordinance draft and Illinois agreements exist.
- One Illinois county plan reportedly lapsed unused, and developers call such guarantees "unworkable" (search summaries of [Maine LD 1042](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/bills_125th/billtexts/HP077601.asp), [SD PUC docket](https://puc.sd.gov/commission/dockets/electric/2018/EL18-053/prefiledexhibits/intervenors/jonhenslin/jw4.pdf)). [U]
- The idea's claim that counties and lawyers "recognize the form" is only partly supported.

**Pitch angle.** The average effect on home values looks near zero in several studies:

- Realtor.com: 43 matched ZIPs, no meaningful change two years after activation.
- George Mason University: homes closer to data centers sold for more, with caveats.
- NAR: a 25% positive versus 22% negative agent split. NAR notes county data "can't tell us what happens to an individual home next to a facility" ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/theres-no-single-data-center-effect-on-housing-markets-nar-report-finds), [GMU](https://schar.gmu.edu/news/2025-11/study-home-prices-are-higher-when-house-near-data-center)). [C]

If a developer believes those studies, a guarantee is a cheap, credible signal. [I]

Two counterpoints. A Princeton thesis attributes its pooled null to small sites with no effect mixed with larger sites with negative effects ([Princeton](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/f5612f3e-e79f-45b3-93f3-5f38e4c97f11)), and guarantees will be demanded at the largest projects. [U/I] Realtor.com also found data-center ZIPs kept 66% of pre-opening active listings after three years, against 43% in matched ZIPs, which suggests slower absorption and matters for a "listed 60+ days" rule. [C/I]

## 6. Unit economics

**Valuation costs.** Older, undated sources put AVMs at about $20+, BPOs at $50 to $150 and appraisals at $300 to $500 ([NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/61564/appraisals-avms-bpos-which-should-mortgage-professionals-choose), [NMP REO](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/23685/reo-avm-compared-other-valuation-methods)). The idea's $300 BPO looks high. AVM plus BPO is plausibly $70 to $170, so enrollment gross margin could be 40 to 75% before outreach, portal and call-center costs. Those costs are not in the idea. [C/I]

**Payout risk, the main finding.** The product is a put option on one house's sale price, so noise creates payouts even when the true data-center effect is zero. [I]

- Individual house prices have annual standard deviations of roughly 12 to 15% against city indexes (Case and Shiller 1989 reports about 15% for individual houses, and a later paper about 12%, per search results; I did not open the papers). [U]
- Assume a terminal sale-versus-reference dispersion of 8 to 12%, which includes AVM and BPO error in the frozen reference. I assume a $300k home and a 40% chance of sale in 10 years. NAR's median seller tenure is 11 years ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/nar-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-sellers-reveals-market-extremes)). [C/I]
- Result: expected payout per sale is about 3 to 4.5% of value ($9.6k to $13.6k) with a 20% cap. That is about $3.8k to $5.4k per enrolled household, against a $3 to 5k escrow.
- The idea's assumption that 10 to 30% of escrow is paid out is therefore likely too low several-fold.
- A 10% deductible (pay only the shortfall beyond 10%) cuts expected payout to about $0.5k to $1.4k per household, roughly 10 to 45% of a $3 to 5k escrow. That is workable.
- A simulation study priced at-the-money index puts at 0.6% to 20.85% of index value between pre- and post-crisis periods ([JABR](https://clutejournals.com/index.php/JABR/article/view/8884)). The control-index adjustment removes most of that market-wide risk. The remaining risk is basis risk: rural exurbs have few comparable sales, so the control index will be noisy. [I]

**Adverse selection.** Opt-in enrollment attracts owners who expect to sell low. The arm's-length rule and the cap help but do not fix it. [I]

## 7. Regulation and liability

**Insurance law is the gating risk.** New York's insurance department counsel ruled that a plan paying homeowners on sale if a ZIP index fell is insurance under §1101(a) and financial guaranty insurance under §6901(a)(1)(D), which is not permitted ([NY DFS OGC 08-07-31](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2008/rg080731.htm)). It also distinguished an earlier index-only program. It named no permissible structure. [C]

- A UConn law-review abstract says states differ on classification and warns that index-linking does not by itself avoid insurance treatment ([UConn](https://insurancelawreview.rso.uconn.edu/?p=1638)). [C]
- The idea says "a contract, not insurance" with a developer as obligor. That is untested. The better argument is that a developer settling its own neighbor-relations exposure is not "doing an insurance business" and the administrator is not the obligor. [I]
- I found no state-by-state analysis. A counsel opinion is the right first spend.

**Other issues.**

- Counties that sign must avoid terms that invite regulatory takings claims (Columbia, above). [C]
- Pennsylvania's governor has banned NDAs between local governments and data center companies. Any deal will be public ([Propmodo](https://propmodo.com/cash-to-residents-is-the-newest-tool-in-the-data-center-fight/)). [C]
- Developer counsel may treat a pre-committed payout as an admission; I found no direct evidence either way. [I]
- Some states restrict BPO use, so valuations may need licensed appraisers in those states. [U]

## 8. Is the why-now real?

**Verified:**

- NY Executive Order 62: 14 July 2026, 50 MW and up. It pauses only certain discretionary DEC approvals and explicitly excludes local permits ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/energy--environmental-law-blog/2026/07/ny-data-center-permitting-pause-executive-order-62)).
- Texas: 21 Sept 2026 order to TCEQ to pause data-center permits until the ERCOT and water audits finish, expected mid-December ([Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/governor-abbott-orders-tceq-to-pause-all-permit-issuance-for-data-center-projects/)).
- Realtor.com's 108 ZIPs (section 4).

**Qualification.** The NY and Texas pauses are grid and water actions. They pause approvals and do not by themselves create demand for neighbor guarantees. [I]

**Stronger why-now evidence.**

- Opposition is scaling: more than 300 state bills in six weeks, and opposition groups going from 396 to 833 in one quarter (DCW, above; the group counts come from a secondary summary [U]).
- A bill like HB4319 is a credible threat.
- NAR released a county-level report on 8 to 9 Sept 2026 that explicitly cannot speak to individual homes. That leaves the individual-home question open. [C]

**Not verified:** the "4-bedroom listed 15 to 18% lower" anecdote, and a Loudoun "2.8% lower within 0.5 mile" finding. A direct search did not corroborate the second. Do not use either in outreach. [U]

## 9. Strongest version and what would kill it

**Narrow the wedge.**

- Sell developers a **buyout alternative or complement**: a guarantee for owners who stay, run by a neutral administrator.
- Use a **10% deductible**, a smaller cap and an index-based trigger where possible.
- Build it as a **county-approved development-agreement rider** (DeForest-style, but with a neutral administrator).
- Partner with an established bank or administrator (Epiq-class) as obligor-of-record or successor, to answer counterparty-credit concerns.

**Timing realism.** DeForest staff met QTS for about seven months before announcement, and the deal died three weeks later. A 90-day gate to a signed pilot is optimistic. [C/I]

**Kill triggers.**

1. Counsel in two or three target states says the structure is insurance.
2. No developer will be obligor of record after 10 outreach calls.
3. Developers prefer buyouts and treat guarantees as a worse version.
4. No county adopts a rider within about 6 months.

# I0109-M3 Refined: Repo-Day Refund Desk (what is left of the Loan-End Event Exchange)

Date: 2026-10-13. Labels: VERIFIED (fetched/searched this session), CRITIC-VERIFIED (cited by a critic and consistent with the dossier, not re-fetched by me), INFERENCE (my reasoning).

## 1. Verdict

**alive = false.** The exchange (Stages 2-3, the only venture-scale part) is dead. The lender-side desk (Stage 1) survives only as a small, undifferentiated services niche (ceiling about $0.5-2M a year, odds under 15%), with no durable power and no proven wedge. I would not spend founder time on it unless the two-week test in section 6 passes its gates.

All three critics reach the same core conclusions and I find none of them overstated enough to rescue the idea. Where a critic overreached, I say so below. It does not change the verdict.

## 2. What I re-checked, and corrections to the critics

- **Cox/F&I Express (VERIFIED).** Auto Remarketing, 16 Jan 2024: Cox's Lori Wittman called Express Recoveries "an important, niche post-sale function" and said Cox sometimes needs "the best steward" for a specialized function, while Cox focuses on shopping, selling, financing and transactions. Critic 1's "dumped as non-core" is a stretch: Cox did not admit failure. But the reading that survives is still damaging: a platform owner with Dealertrack distribution never made this a platform business, and there is no evidence an administrator-paid exchange ever generated revenue.
- **F&I Sentinel Managed Cancellations (VERIFIED, vendor marketing).** It is a managed service covering "every stage ... from trigger to resolution" with a compliance overlay, FAIRRCalc refund quotes, direct submission to product companies, and 2025 consumer refund letters. The CEO says the obligation extends to repossessions and total losses. So the outsourced (BPO-style) model already exists. LCT's "not outsourced" pitch is a CU-channel positioning, not an absence of outsourced options.
- **Allied RefundPlus (VERIFIED).** Lists early payoff, repossession, total loss, charge-off and abandonment, plus a refund lockbox. NAFCU Preferred Partner. No public pricing anywhere (also not found for LCT, F&I Sentinel, Express Recoveries).
- **Contingency pricing (VERIFIED absence).** A fresh search found no source describing any contingency or success-fee tier. That is absence of evidence. Critic 2 is right that after 4+ years and three funded vendors, an empty slot is as likely a rejected model as white space.
- **Refund size (VERIFIED, partly).** The critics benchmark on $165-$380, but Allied says its $380 average is "driven mostly by early payoffs." Repossessions happen earlier in term, so unearned amounts are probably larger than payoff refunds. A CU Times piece repeats a secondhand "about $1,700 average unclaimed refund" on repossessions and total losses that I could not trace to a source (CU Insight article I fetched has no such number). Treat per-file value as uncertain by roughly 5x, not as proven small. Even at the high end, the incumbent, legal and sales-cycle problems stand.
- **Mandated look-backs exist (VERIFIED).** Massachusetts Division of Banks consent order, 4 Nov 2024: Ironhorse Funding had to review all MA accounts back to 11 Dec 2018, adjust deficiency balances and refund unearned GAP, with a $50K penalty; two lenders it serviced were fined $15K each. The order followed an exam covering 2020-2021. This is the one place the "must buy" logic is real.
- **Non-captive lenders (VERIFIED).** Fed note (Experian Velocity 2022): non-captive finance companies about 10% and buy-here-pay-here about 6% of auto originations. I found no count of such firms.

## 4. The surviving version (the strongest honest one)

- **One-liner.** A same-week, flat-fee refund desk for repossession, total-loss and payoff events, sold first to lenders that are legally compelled to fix this and have no tool: non-bank auto lenders and their third-party servicers under exam pressure.
- **Wedge customer.** State-licensed independent auto finance companies and third-party servicers with $50M-$500M in receivables, ideally those named in or exposed to a consent order or exam finding, with no refund vendor. Secondary channel: CU recovery CUSOs and collection agencies that already hold the lender relationships and could resell.
- **First product.** (1) A paid look-back engaged through the lender's outside counsel (structured for privilege). (2) A go-forward desk fed by a daily repossession/total-loss/payoff feed or forwarded emails, not a monthly file: read the deal jacket with an LLM, pick administrator and state rule, compute the refund, file the cancellation, track to payment, hand the lender a posting report and audit trail. Human review on every file at first.
- **Pricing.** Flat $40-$90 per paid event (no refund, no fee), plus $5-10K per look-back project, minimum annual commitment for small lenders. No percentage of consumer refunds. Honest note: this converges on how incumbents likely price, so price is no longer a differentiator.
- **Moat.** None at launch. The only reachable powers are modest: switching costs (audit trail embedded in the servicing workflow after 12+ months) and a small deal-jacket-to-refund corpus. Counter-positioning is not available because a success-fee tier is a one-line change for F&I Sentinel or LCT and is legally awkward. Network economies, scale and cornered resource sit with incumbents (190,000-form repository, 600-800+ administrator connections, CUSO equity).
- **Why now.** Real but shared with every vendor: state laws (Colorado C.R.S. 5-9.3-106 from Jan 2024, NJ P.L.2019 c.181, California AB 2311), state exam and AG activity (Mass. orders Nov 2024, Credit Acceptance about $710M multistate Sept 2026), and LLM extraction cutting per-file cost. The federal retreat shifts enforcement to states but does not help an entrant over incumbents. CU delinquency is flat at 81 bps, so the CU segment is a weaker driver than non-bank lenders.

## 5. Bottom-up sizing (INFERENCE unless cited)

Per $1B-auto credit union, annual 22.5% fee equivalent:
- Low: 600 events x 40% add-ons x 40% unclaimed x $350 = about $7.6K
- Mid: 800 x 40% x 50% x $700 = about $25K
- Original idea: 1,000 x 45% x 60% x $700 = about $42.5K

About 450 reachable CUs (NCUA Q2 2026: $485.4B auto, 4,214 CUs, 748 over $500M assets) gives a CU fee pool of about $3.4M / $11M / $19M. The dossier's $15-20M uses the idea's own inflated inputs. A realistic 5-10% share after three years, against LCT's roughly 30-CU CUSO, Allied and F&I Sentinel, is about $0.2-1.9M a year, central $0.6-1.1M.

Direct CAC of $30-60K (unsourced estimate) against $8-25K a year of at-will revenue gives a 2-7 year payback. This does not clear without a channel.

Non-bank upside (unverified): subprime repossession intensity is many times a CU's. One illustrative $200M-receivable lender could produce roughly $40-100K a year in fees. There is no count of reachable firms, so no market number. Finding that count is part of the test.

## 6. Riskiest assumption and the two-week test

**Riskiest assumption.** A reachable group of lenders exists that (a) currently leaves repossession and charge-off add-on refunds unworked or poorly worked, (b) is not already served by LCT, Allied or F&I Sentinel, and (c) has a budget owner who will pay a new vendor.

**Test (about $500 plus 60 hours).**
- Days 1-3, desk coverage map: build the list of reachable lenders (NCUA call-report auto balances of at least $200M, state licensee lists, public consent orders) and subtract those publicly tied to LCT's CUSO, Allied RefundPlus or F&I Sentinel. Gate: at least 150 named reachable lenders with no public incumbent tie.
- Days 3-14, calls: 15 conversations (8 non-bank lenders or servicers, 5 CU collections or compliance heads, 2 agency or CUSO channel partners). Disclose that this is research. Ask who handles repo and charge-off refunds, which vendor, what they pay, what share of recent repos with add-ons had a refund requested and received, last exam finding, who owns the budget, and reaction to flat per-event pricing versus a percentage.
- Counsel hour (about $300-600): fee structure on consumer-owed refunds, whether a vendor needs any license, and a counsel-engaged look-back structure.
- **Pass:** at least 6 of 15 report no vendor and no systematic process; at least 3 sign a non-binding LOI naming a price (flat $40 or more per event, or contingency of 20% or more) and a budget owner; at least 1 channel partner asks for a pilot; counsel sees no license or fee blocker.
- **Kill:** fewer than 3 of 15 are unserved, or most cite an incumbent or an in-house process, or counsel blocks the fee or licensing. If killed, drop the idea; the only remaining value is as an acqui-hire pitch or a feature for an incumbent.

## 7. What would resurrect the exchange

A paid pilot (not an LOI) from one GAP or service-contract administrator for structured loan-end events they do not already get free from F&I Sentinel or LCT, or GAPA/SCIC/MVPPA agreeing to adopt the schema. Neither exists today. Until then the exchange stays deleted.

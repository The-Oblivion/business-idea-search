# F06 dossier: "Second Look" (pay-on-win college aid appeals + outcomes ledger)

Researched 25 Oct 2026. About 60 searches and fetches. Company-reported claims are labeled as such. Reddit blocked all fetches, so subreddit rules are unverified.

## Bottom line

The demand is real, but the paid layer is thin and legally fiddly. The sharpened version (no upfront fee, 15-20% success fee, public ledger) removes the Illinois problem, but it also cuts expected revenue to roughly $150-350 per engaged family at the pitch's own 35% win rate ($90-700 across sourced win rates). Free tools give away the read and the letter. The incumbent is small. First cash under pay-on-win is about 14-20 weeks out, not 11. The ledger is the most original asset, but it is a content and data play, not a fee business. This is a "weak, not dead" case, and the pitch's own kill signal is statistically too noisy to trust.

## 1. Who does this today, and what is free

**Takeaway:** The "read the letter + draft the appeal" layer is free and crowded. The paid human layer is a small incumbent plus flat-fee reviewers. I found no live capped-contingency competitor, but the only two precedents are old and left no trace.

### Cited findings
- **College Aid Pro (CAP).** MyCAP is $4.99/month with a free tier for 3 schools. An Expert Hour is $299, the Appeals package $1,999, and Financial Aid Only $3,499, with a "Beat-Our-Fee" promise [pricing](https://pages.collegeaidpro.com/families-services-pricing). The appeals package includes "unlimited appeal support for up to 3 schools". Its savings guarantee lets CAP "determine the appropriate resolution, which may include a refund" [proposal page](https://pages.collegeaidpro.com/cap-appeals-package-proposal-november-2025).
- CAP claims "100K+" families on one stat block and "hundreds of thousands" on the same page [pricing](https://pages.collegeaidpro.com/families-services-pricing). PitchBook shows 22 employees and a $1M angel round in 2022 [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/435343-24). I found no revenue figure. It partnered with Juno on 28 Oct 2025, focused on graduate borrowing [press release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/861951573/college-aid-pro-partners-with-juno-to-help-families-navigate-graduate-borrowing-and-repayment-amid-major-federal-changes).
- **Juno** offers a free appeal-letter tool with no account, about 10 minutes. Lenders pay Juno on loans. The page claims 2,000+ letters, $84M of costs appealed and 300,000 members, and gives no success rate [Juno](https://joinjuno.com/appeal).
- **CollegeLens** is "free forever", with an award-letter decoder and appeal generator, funded partly by labeled lender ads [site](https://collegelens.ai). It asserts "60 to 80 percent of documented appeals get results" with no method [CollegeLens](https://collegelens.ai/resources/aid-appeals). **MeritMore** has a free offer comparison and appeal generator [site](https://news.meritmore.com).
- **TuitionFit** crowdsources award letters. It had about 4,000 letters in its first cycle (2019), and colleges pay to subscribe. The interview does not say it records appeal outcomes [Education Next](https://educationnext.org/straight-conversation-kelley-blue-book-for-college-costs-salisbury).
- **Flat-fee reviewers.** Road2College charges $369 for a letter review with two revisions and no outcome statistics [page](https://www.road2college.com/shop/financial-aid-appeal-letter-review). A search snippet lists Countered.ai at $49 for an AI appeal letter and call script [snippet](https://countered.ai/). My fetch of that page returned 404, so this is unverified.
- **Mos** charged $149 in 2019 and had advisers who "negotiate for higher aid" [EdSurge](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2019-05-08-student-financial-aid-startup-mos-raises-4-million-in-seed-funding). It raised a $4M seed. Sequoia's page says "Acquired 2025" with no acquirer named [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/companies/mos). **Frank** sold to JPMorgan for $175M, had fewer than 300,000 real users against 4M claimed, and its founder got 7+ years [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/charlie-javice-sentenced-7-years-prison-fraudulent-175m-sale-financial-aid-startup-frank/17906192/).
- **Contingency precedents.** College Tuition Advisory Services (2021) took 25% of added discount, capped at $2,000, with no upfront fee. It targeted about 700,000 students a year in families earning $80-250k [THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/us-colleges-get-new-challenge-tuition-fee-discounts). I found no later coverage. A 2004 counselor charged $499, refunded unless he won at least $4,000 [CSM](https://m.csmonitor.com/2004/0426/p13s02-wmgn.html).
- **Adjacent contingency businesses scale.** Ownwell takes 25-35% of property-tax savings. Reported win rates are 86-88%, average savings are $774 a year, and it has raised $54M equity (company-reported) [Crunchbase](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/ownwell-raise-lower-homeowner-property-tax/). Goodbill takes 20% of hospital-bill savings, capped at $1,000 [Goodbill](https://help.goodbill.com/en/articles/254209).

### Inferences
- The "700,000 families" pool is one founder's 2021 estimate in a news article. It is not a verified count.
- Ownwell works because wins are mechanical, high-probability and recur every year. Aid appeals are neither.
- Absence of a current contingency player after 2021 is weak evidence of a gap. It may mean the model failed.

### Gaps
- CAP revenue and paid-conversion rate.
- Whether College Tuition Advisory Services or Countered.ai still operates.

## 2. Willingness to pay and what an appeal is worth

**Takeaway:** Families do appeal, and most who try report getting something. But no independent source gives dollar amounts, and the money often arrives as a small one-time grant. That caps a 20% fee at a few hundred dollars per family.

### Cited findings
- Sallie Mae/Ipsos 2026 (n=1,458 respondents who got an offer): 33% appealed. Separately, 37% appealed at private 4-year schools, 27% in $150k+ households, 41% in the Northeast, and 42% of borrowers versus 24% of non-borrowers. Of 461 appealers, 70% got more aid, 16% did not, and 8% were still waiting. No dollar amounts are given. The base is students who enrolled, so it misses those who left [Sallie Mae PDF, Tables 65-66](https://www.salliemae.com/content/dam/slm/writtencontent/Research/How_America_Pays_2026_Report.pdf).
- CAP says successful appeals "typically add $3,000 to $5,000 annually", work best at privates and less well at publics. It cites no study [CAP guide](https://pages.collegeaidpro.com/appeal-financial-aid-step-by-step-guide). Its "75% private / 25% public" claim traces to CAP research I could not check [NGPF](https://dev4.ngpf.org/blog/question-of-the-day/question-of-the-day-if-a-student-successfully-appeals-their-college-financial-aid-offer-how-much-additional-aid-do-they-typically-receive-per-year/).
- A 2020 student survey (n=1,024) found 5.9% of appealers got more aid [Nordot](https://nordot.app/676826424405165153). One summary of it gives a $3,497 average [University Business](https://universitybusiness.com/financial-aid-appeals-in-a-pandemic). That was pandemic-era and secondhand.
- Brown: about 12% of students appealed, and the office does not track how many succeeded [Brown Daily Herald](https://browndailyherald.com/article/2012/02/failed-aid-appeals-send-students-home).
- School rules differ sharply:
  - Holy Cross says it "will not consider ... other colleges' offers of financial aid", and decisions "depend on availability of funding". Appeals go in through the College Board IDOC upload [Holy Cross form](https://www.holycross.edu/document/20262027-appeal-form).
  - Pace awards appeal money as a "one-time discretionary grant", not renewable, once per aid year [Pace](https://helpdesk.pace.edu/TDClient/213/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=2684).
  - Cornell matched peer offers in 2010. That is dated and unverified as current [Cornell](https://news.cornell.edu/index%2ephp/stories/2010/12/cornell-matches-financial-aid-offered-peer-schools).
- Reference prices that exist: $49 (AI), $369 (human review), $299/hour, $1,999 and $3,499 (CAP). Free tools sit under all of them.

### Inferences
- The competing-offer lever, the pitch's headline tactic, is explicitly refused by at least one selective private. Its coverage is policy-by-policy.
- If a win averages $3-5k and is often a one-time grant, a 15-20% fee is about $450-1,000. The $1,000 floor would exclude many small wins.

### Gaps
- No independent dollar-gain distribution exists.
- I found no evidence that families pay more than $400 for appeals help, apart from CAP's bundles.

## 3. Where the first customers are, and how to reach them

**Takeaway:** The window is real and dated: ED/EA letters from mid-December, RD letters from late March. Reachable first customers are mostly personal-network families. The "20% referral to counselors" tactic collides with counselor ethics codes.

### Cited findings
- CAP's own timing advice: early applicants submit mid-December to mid-January, regular applicants mid-March to early April, with a reply typically in 2-3 weeks [CAP guide](https://pages.collegeaidpro.com/appeal-financial-aid-step-by-step-guide).
- The 2027-28 FAFSA is already open, the earliest launch ever [WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/education-department-opens-2027-28-fafsa-early-marking-programs-earliest-launch).
- Common App had 1,527,328 first-year applicants in 2025-26 [Common App report](https://www.commonapp.org/files/DAR/deadline-updates/2025-26/Common-App-End-of-Season-Report_25-26.pdf).
- IECA's principles say members "accept payment only for work performed", take no commissions for referrals, and cap referral payments at $100 unless both parties are delivering substantive service. They also bar guaranteeing outcomes [IECA](https://iecaonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/IECA-Principles-of-Good-Practice.pdf). IECA reported that 43% of members advise on financial aid in 2016 [IECA 2016](https://www.iecaonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/IECA_State-of-Profession-2016.pdf). That contradicts "most do not do aid".
- Reddit self-promotion rules: unverified. Only a 2017 sister-subreddit draft says professionals could promote paid services after contributing substantively [r/ApplyingToCollegeINTL](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/ApplyingToCollegeINTL).
- Offices run intake through portals and uploads (Belmont's secure portal [IHE](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/financial-aid/2026/07/23/financial-aid-offices-contend-ai-written-appeals); Holy Cross IDOC above).

### Inferences
- Northeast families, private-college families and borrowers each appeal more often, so Northeast privates are the likeliest early niche (an inference, since the survey does not cross-tabulate).
- A referral share to IECA members is likely unusable. Non-member counselors may take it.
- Because families upload through school portals, the "send-ready packet" is really a family checklist, which lowers perceived value.

### Gaps
- Reddit and Discord rules.
- TikTok reach and ad CPCs for aid keywords. Only generic education CPCs of about $4-6 turned up.
- How many seniors in his school or neighboring schools have private-college offers.

## 4. Legal, age and platform issues

**Takeaway:** Age is not a barrier. Illinois law is the live constraint. The sharpened no-upfront-fee design fits it, but cancellation, refund and outcome-claim rules still bite a pay-on-win model.

### Cited findings
- Illinois covers anyone who helps with "financial aid application planning, preparation, or submission" for "any fee or compensation" [815 ILCS 616/15](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081506160K15.htm).
- §20(d): a provider "may not charge or receive ... any enrollment fee, set up fee, up-front fee of any kind, or maintenance fee", and the consumer pays only for services provided. §20(b) requires a signed contract listing each fee and "the circumstances under which each fee will become due", plus a Consumer Notice and Rights Form, ISAC free-services disclosure and cancellation form. The consumer can cancel "at any time until after the ... provider has fully performed" each service. §20(e)(1) bars outcome claims in marketing without substantiation at the time made [815 ILCS 616/20](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081506160K20.htm).
- §30: refunds are due within 5 business days. Cancellation revokes direct-debit authorizations. §35: noncompliant contracts are void. §40: a violation is an unlawful practice under Illinois' Consumer Fraud Act, with a private right of action [§30](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081506160K30.htm) and [§40](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081506160K40.htm). I read the statute text directly. The ilga.gov domain blocks default fetches, so I used a browser user-agent.
- §5 findings name upfront premiums and "legally questionable guidance" such as guardianship schemes as the targets [§5](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/081506160K5.htm).
- I found no other state with a comparable statute. A New York bill was only proposed, and its status is unknown [NY Senate](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2019/S2426A).
- NASFAA's 2026 survey (via Inside Higher Ed): AI-written appeals "lack the specificity, personal context, and genuine situational detail" aid offices need. NASFAA's policy director had not heard of widespread increases in appeal volume [IHE](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/financial-aid/2026/07/23/financial-aid-offices-contend-ai-written-appeals).
- FERPA: schools need the student's written release naming a third party, and policies vary. Many schools require a family signature [example: Maryland MHEC](https://mhec.maryland.gov/preparing/pages/financialaid/ferpa.aspx).
- Stripe's restricted-business list has no entry for financial aid, college consulting or contingency fees. Its debt-relief and mortgage-consulting entries are not this business. Final underwriting is at activation [Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).

### Inferences
- Success-only fees are not banned. §20(b)(6) contemplates fees due on "circumstances".
- A family could cancel after the packet is filed but before the revised letter posts and argue it owes nothing. The statute is ambiguous on whether the packet was a "service provided". Only an Illinois attorney can settle this. Advertising nationally means treating every contract as Illinois-compliant, since he cannot reliably exclude Illinois residents.
- A published win rate is allowed only if substantiated at the time of publication. Self-reported $25 outcome rewards give a biased, thin sample.
- His real obstacle is trust. Parents hand over tax returns and CSS Profiles, and the FERPA release is signed by the student, not by him.

### Gaps
- Whether Illinois applies to out-of-state sellers.
- Google and Meta ad policies, and Stripe's actual underwriting outcome.
- The cost of an Illinois attorney review (unsourced guess: low four figures).

## 5. Unit economics and the path to big

**Takeaway:** On the sourced ranges, a pay-on-win-only fee yields roughly $150-350 per engaged family at a 35% win rate. $10M a year needs about 29,000-64,000 families (about 14,000 if win rates are 70%). That is 1.9-4.2% of all Common App first-year applicants in the 35% case, and several times the incumbent's apparent scale.

### Calculation (inferences from the cited numbers)
- Fee per win: 15-20% of a $3-5k gain, about $450-1,000, under the $2,500 cap. The cap only binds above $12.5-16.7k.
- Win rate is the swing variable. Sourced values are 6% (2020 survey), 70% (Sallie Mae self-report, no dollar amounts) and 75% (CAP, vendor). The pitch assumes 35%.
- Revenue per engaged family: 20% win gives about $90-200; 35% gives about $160-350; 70% gives about $315-700. The pitch's $570 relied on the $149 audit.
- $10M at $157-350 per family is about 29,000-64,000 families a year. CAP's "100K+ since 2017" is about 11,000 a year, mostly free users, on 22 employees.
- At about $250 revenue per family, customer acquisition cost must stay well under $100. At a generic education CPC of about $5, that needs at least 5% of paid clicks to become customers. That is unlikely for a cold, high-trust, one-time purchase.
- Repeat revenue is unsourced. If appeals are one-time grants (Pace), there is no annual repeat. If first-year awards renew, a student can be re-appealed for 3 more years.

### The pilot kill-signal is weak
The pitch kills the contingency idea if fewer than 3 of 10 free pilots gain over $1,500. If the true success rate were 35%, the chance of 3+ wins in 10 is 74%, so there is a 26% chance of a false kill. If the true rate were only 15%, there is still an 18% chance the test would pass. Ten families, mostly pre-screened classmates, cannot separate these cases.

### Gaps
- No sourced CAC for aid appeals.
- No sourced share of families that would pay.

## 6. Why now, with caveats

- Parent PLUS is capped at $20,000 a year and $65,000 per student from 1 July 2026. A narrow legacy exception covers students enrolled by 30 June 2026. Counsel expect more professional-judgment appeals and requests for institutional aid [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/05/28/institutional-readiness-considerations-grad-plus-elimination-and-parent-plus-caps).
- NACUBO projects a 57.1% first-time discount rate for 2025-26, up from 54.5%. 90% of first-time students get institutional aid, and net tuition revenue fell 2.2% in 2024-25 [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/tuition-discount-rate-reaches-57-for-private-nonprofits-nacubo-says/821554/).
- Counterweights: NASFAA reports no broad appeal surge yet (see §4). Free competitors already push appeal content, for example Juno-hosted appeal webinars in 2025 and January 2026 [CollegeAdvisor](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/webinars/how-to-appeal-financial-aid-awards-2/).

## 7. What the evidence says about the pitch

- Strong: the real, rising problem; a new shock (Parent PLUS); a pay-on-win slot nobody visibly occupies; a ledger idea that can compound as content and search traffic.
- Weak: free tools already give the read and the letter; the dollar value of a typical win is unproven and small; competing-offer tactics are refused by some schools; the Illinois contract rules complicate collection; first cash arrives in February-May.
- A realistic first-cash date for the sharpened model is mid-January at the earliest and more likely February-April. ED/EA letters arrive around 15 December and decisions take 2-3 weeks. The pitch's 11 weeks needs the $149 audit.
- The cheapest test is not a 10-family pilot. It is collecting 30-50 real award letters and the written appeal policy for each school. Then check how many policies list a lever the family actually has. That also builds the ledger and tests which levers exist.

# F04 Dossier: Contract Renewal Watchdog for Florida condo/HOA associations (fee-only cost recovery)

Research date: 25 Oct 2026. About 40 searches and fetches. "Search summary" means I saw it only in a search-result digest, not on the page itself.

## Bottom line

The gate's "weak" verdict holds. A free review sold through family intros can plausibly produce a first paying association in 60-90 days. The business behind it has four problems:

1. **The license wall is closer than the gate says.** The Florida definition of paid "community association management" names the core act of this service.
2. **The money per association is small and capped.** The best comparable, a national "savings-share" franchise, shows what that ceiling looks like.
3. **The "big" layer (benchmark data, software, group buying) has no verified payer.** Funded software companies already hold the invoices, vendor modules and bid tools.
4. **The conflict-free pitch is real and timely.** Florida kickback arrests in 2026 give it a hook, but it is a sales angle, not a moat.

## 1. Money pool and who signs

- The US has 373,000 community associations collecting $124.2B a year in assessments. Florida has 50,600 associations covering 4.47M units. 30-40% of associations nationally are self-managed. [FCAR 2025 review](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf)
- Dividing the national figures gives about $333k per association, which matches the gate. A Florida assessment total I could not verify. One search summary cites about $14B for 2021 (unverified).
- South Florida alone has nearly 13,000 condo associations and about 610k units (Dec 2023). [Zalewski headline](https://peterzalewski.substack.com/p/south-florida-has-nearly-13000-condo) I found no size distribution. Many of the 50,600 are small and will not support a contingency fee. That is a gap.
- Budget lines that are not his to touch are large. FirstService's 2025 South Florida high-rise benchmark shows insurance at $377 a month per unit (up 25%), reserves at 12 cents of each budget dollar (up from 9), and management fees up more than 40%. [WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/business/2025-12-05/hoa-condo-costs-florida)
- Insurance and reserves are therefore roughly a third of a high-rise budget before management fees (my inference from those figures). The gate's "30% addressable" is an assumption, not a sourced number.
- The signer is a volunteer board treasurer or president. The gatekeeper is usually the manager. A FirstService survey found 57% of board members unsure whether their manager reviews vendor contracts. [FirstService](https://www.fsresidential.com/florida/news-events/articles/cost-containment-strategies/) This supports the pain, though the source is a manager marketing its own service.

## 2. Competitors and free substitutes (assume everyone is already here)

**Same model, other geography**
- **FolSon Group (NYC).** Free second opinion. 50/50 split on project savings. Operating savings shared for the first two years. Zero fee if nothing is found. Uses a database of thousands of line items. [Habitat](https://habitatmag.com/Archive2/411-June-2023/The-Problem-Reducing-Operating-Costs) I found no evidence it serves Florida.
- I found no Florida contingency-fee cost-reduction firm for associations in standard or extended searches. That absence is only as good as the search tool.

**Incumbent gatekeepers**
- **FirstService Residential** bundles "value engineering" (line-by-line review of financials, vendor contracts and utilities), benchmarking and insurance review. [FirstService](https://www.fsresidential.com/florida/news-events/articles/cost-containment-strategies/) It also publishes benchmarking guides covering insurance, cable, trash, utilities and landscaping. [FirstService guide](https://www.fsresidential.com/florida/budget/benchmarking-guide-cable/)
- **FirstService Energy** does utility-bill audits. Examples: a $158k refund to a 35-unit Manhattan condo and $33k to a 270-unit one. The fee is not stated. [FirstService Energy](https://www.fsresidential.com/new-york/what-we-do/additional-services/firstservice-energy/utility-bill-audits)

**Software and marketplaces**
- **ProVendorConnect (Tampa, March 2026).** Free to boards, vendors pay $89 a month. It claims about 370k HOAs and over $100B of spend. No traction numbers are given. [DC360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/31/provendorconnect-launches-b2b-marketplace/) It already occupies the "bid package" wedge, and it is vendor-paid.
- **Condo Control** has vendor profiles, a "collect multiple bids" feature and AP. [Condo Control](https://www.condocontrol.com/financial/vendor-portal)
- **PayHOA** raised a $27.5M Series A in May 2024 and launched a Payables module with invoice OCR. [VCA](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2024051505/payhoa-raises-27-5m-series-a-funding-to-simplify-homeowner-association-operations/)
- **Vantaca Vendor** handles procurement, invoicing and payments for management companies (search summary: [SoftwareOne](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/vantaca-vendor/PCP-6454-7597)).
- These hold the invoice data at scale, which is what a benchmark asset needs.

**Generic tools and free substitutes**
- **Renewly** extracts renewal dates, notice periods, auto-renew clauses and escalators from contract PDFs. It alerts at 90, 60, 30 and 7 days, from $99 with a free tier (search summary: [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/renewly/)). A spreadsheet with calendar alerts also works.
- **The board's own treasurer, the association attorney and the manager** are the real substitutes. Florida lawyers advise reviewing every auto-renew. [Cooperator, Weil](https://fl.cooperatornews.com/article/cutting-costs---without-cutting-corners)
- **Specialist auditors** exist per line. Examples: a utility auditor claiming 3-5% recoveries and a waste-contract auditor ([Cost Analysts](https://www.costanalysts.com/?p=1259)).
- **Owner's-rep engineers** cover big projects (Stone Building Solutions; Souffront, from the gate).

## 3. Pricing, willingness to pay and unit economics

**Price signals**
- Savings-share consultants take about half. A Schooley Mitchell FDD example: a $60k, 3-year saving yields a $30k commission. [Franchise Chatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2024/04/18/fdd-talk-schooley-mitchell-franchise-costs-fees-average-revenues-and-or-profits-2024-review/)
- ERA franchisees split savings equally with clients, over about two years (search summary: [Elite Franchise](https://elitefranchisemagazine.co.uk/opportunities/expense-reduction-analysts)).
- Software price anchors are low: PayHOA about $49-54 a month ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/146693/PayHOA/pricing/)), Renewly $99, ProVendorConnect vendors $89.

**Claimed savings (all vendor or practitioner anecdotes)**
- A condo consolidated fire-service vendors from $27k to $14k a year (search summary: [FirstService](https://www.fsresidential.com/Maryland/News-Events/Articles/Stop-Before-Raising-Association-Fees-Try-Cost-Cont)).
- A Fort Lauderdale manager says renegotiating long trash contracts could cut fees "possibly 30-40 percent." [Cooperator](https://fl.cooperatornews.com/article/cutting-costs---without-cutting-corners)
- Schooley Mitchell claims 28% average savings (search summary: [Memphis Chamber](https://members.memphischamber.com/news/details/news-release-9-5-2024-09-05-2024)).

**Per-association math**
- The gate's estimate is $333k budget x 30% addressable x about 10% saved x 40% share, or roughly $4k a year. That is consistent with the evidence.
- Running the same math on the "avoided increases" version gives less. Baselines are disputable, and boards can walk away after the first rebid.

**The ceiling test**
- The Schooley Mitchell FDD (12 months to May 2023) lists 63 full-time franchisees averaging $274,951 and 132 part-time averaging $13,253. [Franchise Chatter](https://www.franchisechatter.com/2024/04/18/fdd-talk-schooley-mitchell-franchise-costs-fees-average-revenues-and-or-profits-2024-review/)
- By my arithmetic that is about $19M of "secured revenue" for the whole US network. The figure counts contract-term revenue, and the page's own counts do not reconcile (184 vs 195).
- A labor-heavy savings-share business in this category has not scaled past tens of millions nationally in decades.

**Reaching $10M**
- The contingency service needs about 2,500 associations at $4k a year. That is 5% of all Florida associations, including tiny ones.
- A self-serve subscription at $100 a month needs about 8,300 payers. That is 16% of Florida or 2.2% of the US.
- Neither number has a demonstrated demand signal.

## 4. First customers: where they gather and how to reach them

- **Self-managed associations** have no manager gatekeeper. They are 30-40% of associations nationally (FCAR above). This is the best segment for a teen outsider. A manager blocks a vendor who threatens their vendor rebates and fees.
- **CAI Florida chapters:** Central Florida, Gold Coast, Northeast Florida, North Gulf Coast, Southeast Florida, South Gulf Coast, Suncoast and West Florida. [CAI chapters](https://www.caionline.org/chapters) Business Partner dues are $575-660, with sources conflicting. [CAI join](https://www.caionline.org/join) Managers dominate these events, so it is better for referral partners (attorneys, CPAs, engineers) than for buyers.
- **Board-to-board networks** exist. Example: the Treasure Coast Condo Alliance, formed by a Stuart board member in 2024, which is explicitly organized around SIRS cost pressure. [WPTV](https://www.wptv.com/money/real-estate-news/florida-condo-owners-form-alliance-fight-against-unstainable-costs)
- **Facebook, Nextdoor and Reddit condo groups:** I could not research these. The Reddit domain is blocked for the search tool. This is a gap.
- **Family and mentor intros** are the only channel with evidence behind it, and the gate could not name the buildings. Everything here depends on those first 5-10 warm introductions.

## 5. Legal, age and policy

**Age**
- 18 is enough for an LLC, contracts and a CAM license (below).

**The CAM definition**
- Paid "community association management" is a license trigger for associations with more than 10 units or a budget over $100,000. The list includes "negotiating monetary or performance terms of a contract subject to approval by an association," plus "preparing budgets or other financial documents" and coordinating maintenance and routine services. [Fla. Stat. 468.431](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/468.431)
- The only exclusions are clerical work under a licensed manager's supervision and pure maintenance.
- A firm that provides any listed service is a "management firm." It needs its own license, a designated licensed CAM, and may directly employ only licensed persons for those services. Persons licensed under another law (attorney, CPA, engineer) are exempt for their own profession. [468.432](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/468.432)
- I found no DBPR declaratory statement on consultants. A board that negotiates for itself using his report is the safest design. Bid packages and renewal letters sent on the board's behalf are murkier. A Florida association attorney or a DBPR declaratory statement is needed before charging.

**Clearing the wall**
- The CAM license needs age 18, a 16-hour course ($269-319), a 100-question exam (75 to pass) and fingerprints. Rule fees: application $50, fingerprints $47, license $100, exam about $100. [Rule 61E14-3.001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61E14-3-001); [Lars](https://www.larsoned.com/cam/licensing/howtoobtainalicense/) CAI cites a $228 figure, so fees conflict. That is roughly the gate's $650, but it excludes the firm license and any insurance.

**Why the license creates a new problem**
- A licensed CAM or firm that proposes a non-CAM service contract triggers a presumption of conflict. This needs an agenda item, copies of the contracts and a two-thirds board vote. Non-CAM bids over $2,500 need multiple bids, and the contract is voidable without termination fees. "Compensation" includes referral fees and profit-sharing. [468.4335](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/468.4335)
- Fines run up to $5,000 per count. [468.436](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/468.436) This makes a savings-share contract friction-heavy for a licensed CAM, though the exact reading for a contingent consulting fee is untested.

**Other law**
- Contracts over 5% of the annual budget (including reserves) need competitive bids. Contracts with attorneys, accountants, CAMs, engineers and others are exempt. [718.3026](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/718.3026) Boards will want counsel to confirm a contingency fee's treatment.
- Kickbacks are a third-degree felony for officers, directors and managers, with removal from office (search summary: [Zalewski](https://peterzalewski.substack.com/p/florida-condo-directors-on-notice)). Taking nothing from vendors or brokers is mandatory, not a differentiator.
- Contract analysis can drift into unlicensed practice of law. Date and clause extraction is ministerial. Legal advice is not. [Jimerson Birr](https://www.jimersonfirm.com/blog/2016/06/community-association-managers-beware-unlicensed-practice-law/)
- Stay out of insurance placement (a Chapter 626 license, per the gate; I did not independently verify it).
- The 2026 session passed nothing relevant. HB 465 (management requirements, which would have mandated $1M E&O) died. [CAI 2026 report](https://advocacy.caionline.org/2026fleos/) The 2025 HB 913 changes took effect 1 July 2025 (inspection-bidder disclosures, reserve changes). [Shumaker](https://www.shumaker.com/insight/client-alert-condo-law-reform-what-floridas-hb-913-means-for-associations-and-owners/)

## 6. Why now

- **Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac** raise the condo-project reserve floor from 10% to 15% of budget on 4 Jan 2027, announced 18 March 2026 (search summary: [NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/januarys-condo-reserve-increase-puts-project-budgets-focus)). I did not fetch the agency notices. Boards must find the extra money, so operating cuts become more attractive.
- **SIRS and milestone deadlines.** A Miami Realtors tracker found 62% of South Florida associations had not completed a SIRS by the first deadline. Buildings still owed a milestone inspection have until 31 Dec 2026. [Zalewski](https://peterzalewski.substack.com/p/warning-62-of-south-florida-condos)
- **2026 kickback prosecutions.** Greynolds Park Club (North Miami Beach), where a board member allegedly took $20,910 in vendor payments. [Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/miami/2026/04/10/north-miami-beach-condo-board-manager-arrested/) Venetian Gardens, where a manager allegedly took $186,000. A $5.8M scheme was charged across five associations. [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/09/17/885380.htm) A "no vendor money, ever" pitch lands well here.
- **Headwinds.** Citizens and private carriers are cutting some rates in 2026 (search summary: [Insurify](https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/news/florida-insurer-rate-decrease/)). The biggest pain line is easing and is off-limits anyway. Part of the waste spike is government-set: Indian River County's October 2025 contract roughly doubled condo waste fees ($11 to $21), which a vendor audit cannot change (search summary: [WPTV](https://www.wptv.com/news/treasure-coast/region-indian-river-county/lets-hear-it-indian-river-county-responds-to-concerns-about-waste-management-condo-fee-spike)).

## 7. The "big" test

- **Data asset.** FirstService already publishes benchmarks for free from the communities it manages. PayHOA, Vantaca, Condo Control and CINC hold AP and vendor data at far greater scale than a few hundred audited contracts. I found no evidence of a buyer paying for per-door benchmarks (managers, lenders and insurers were the gate's assumption). The data also only exists after thousands of clients exist.
- **Group buying.** Vendor-paid admin fees break "conflict-free" and risk Florida kickback scrutiny. Association-paid GPOs lack the savings leverage to justify a fee. ProVendorConnect already runs a vendor-paid version.
- **Services as a wedge.** The wedge is legitimate, but nothing I found shows how a one-person service turns into software before the incumbents add the feature.

## 8. Verdict and what would change it

**Verdict: weak, confirmed.** It is a plausible first-dollar side business ($4-5k per association) and a poor venture. The CAM-license and conflict-presumption issues are the main execution risks, and the data and software story has no payer.

**What would change it**
- A DBPR declaratory statement or attorney opinion that an advisory report plus board-led negotiation sits outside 468.431(2).
- Three or more Florida boards paying a savings share without a manager's objection.
- Evidence that a manager, lender or insurer will pay for contract-level benchmarks.

**Cheap test (under $1,000)**
- Do 5-10 free "top-5 contract" reviews for family-connected, self-managed associations. Deliver a written report only, with no negotiation and no payment, until the legal question is answered.
- Measure savings found per association and whether a board will sign a savings-share letter.

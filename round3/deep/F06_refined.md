# F06 refined: "Second Look" (pay-on-win aid appeals + outcomes ledger)

Written 25 Oct 2026, after two critiques and the F06 dossier. I re-checked a few load-bearing facts myself (marked VERIFIED / SEARCH SUMMARY / UNVERIFIED).

## Verdict: alive = false

I tried to build the strongest version that survives both critics. It survives as a free content project. It does not survive as a business that clears the bar for this founder. I am not narrowing it to a boutique to keep it breathing. The lessons file says to call that dead, and I agree.

Why, in four lines:
1. The fee thesis fails on its own terms: roughly $150-350 of revenue per engaged family, with a trigger the family controls, a cancel-until-performed right under Illinois law, and no surviving precedent.
2. The only large profit pool next door is the lender pool, and lenders are the ones funding the free tools that undercut him.
3. $10M a year needs about 29,000-101,000 paying families depending on price, which is 2-7% of every Common App first-year applicant paying a stranger for what free tools give away.
4. Year-one capacity and trust cap gross revenue near $5-21k, with one learning loop a year.

## The strongest version I could build (v2), and what I cut

**Cut (each one was a critic hit I could not answer):**
- The pay-on-win success fee. The family holds the evidence of the win, there is no independent verifier, and families with easy wins can use the free generators and never upload (adverse selection).
- Layer 2, disclosed lender referrals. It is Juno's and CollegeLens's model, he has no traffic or licensing, and it ruins the "independent" story. It also sits next to his no-regulated-financial-advice guardrail.
- Layer 3, selling benchmark data to enrollment teams. The buyers are enterprise, conflicted, and already served by Encoura/EAB/NACUBO and TuitionFit.
- The $25-per-outcome reward, the 1,500 school pages, and any published per-school win rate. 2,000 reports x $25 = $50,000, and 1,500 pages x 30 reports x $25 = $1.1M. Under 815 ILCS 616/20(e)(1), an outcome claim needs substantiation when made.
- The free odds read and free letter as products. They are commodities, so at most they are top-of-funnel.

**Kept (the only parts that hold):**
1. A free "lever map" for the top ~100 private colleges. Each page has the written appeal policy, the portal, the documents required, and whether the school says it considers competing offers. This is built from public policy pages with his agents, not from paid outcome reports. Publish counts, not win rates, until a school has n>=20.
2. A paid human-reviewed evidence packet, **fee on delivery, not on outcome**: $79-149, charged when the packet is delivered, with a named retired aid officer reviewing. 815 ILCS 616/20(d) (I read it): no "up-front fee of any kind", and "a consumer shall pay only for the educational planning services provided". Delivery-triggered billing fits that text and removes the verification problem. An Illinois attorney must confirm it. I am not one.
3. A one-time, private dataset of award letters and appeal histories, used to tune the lever map. It is not a public ledger and not a moat.

That v2 is honest. It is also a small side business, and I am saying so rather than dressing it up.

## Numbers

- **Families needed for $10M a year:** about 29,000-64,000 at $157-350 per family (dossier). At $149 on delivery, about 67,000 (4.4% of the 1,527,328 Common App first-year applicants in 2025-26). At $99, about 101,000 (6.6%). Compare the incumbent CAP at about 11,000 mostly-free users a year.
- **Median case, year one:** about $0.01M gross ($4-21k), before reviewer and attorney costs. Net is near zero.
- **Upside, year three, everything works:** about $3M. That is 1M parent visitors, 5% requesting a lender quote, 15% of those funding at ~$15k and ~1.5% referral, which is ~$1.7M. Add ~5,000 packets at $99 (~$0.5M) and data subscriptions (~$0.5M). Even this relies on the lender-referral layer I cut and it competes with NerdWallet-class SEO. I cap the upside at $3M for that reason.

## What replaces the December pilot (a faster, better test)

The December pilot was uninformative: 26% false-kill at a true 35% win rate, 18% false-pass at 15% (dossier, which I re-derived). It also lands during his own decision season. Last year's seniors, now freshmen, already have award letters and appeal results. That is a retrospective sample available now:

- Gate: at least 6 of 20 appeal histories show a win of $1,500 or more. Exact binomial: about 24% false-kill at a true 35% rate and about 7% false-pass at 15%. With 30 histories and a gate of 8, about 12% false-kill and 7% false-pass.
- It is retrospective and self-reported, so it is biased toward winners. Treat a pass as "not dead", not "proven".
- Failing to find 20 appealers in 3 weeks is itself a result. It means the ledger sourcing channel does not work for him.

## First 30 days, if he insists on running the falsification test (cap ~25 hours; protect the Oct 28 submission and Q1 grades)

This is the cheapest way to prove me wrong. It is not a launch plan.

- **Week 1 (Oct 26-Nov 1, <=6 h, after Wed 10/28):** one-page offer and agreement (fee on delivery, no outcome claim). 10 named parent asks. Two asks for a retired aid officer through the mentor and the state NASFAA affiliate. Output: 10 asks sent. Target: at least 3 signed intents.
- **Week 2 (Nov 2-8, <=8 h):** agents pull the written appeal policy for 100 private colleges into a spreadsheet. Send 60 asks to class-of-2026 alumni and community members for award letters and appeal histories. Output: 100 policies coded, at least 12 replies.
- **Week 3 (Nov 9-15, <=6 h):** code policies by lever (competing offers, PJ, documentation, deadline, portal). Collect 20 appeal histories and 30 letters. Output: share of policies listing a lever a family can use; median dollar gain; win rate.
- **Week 4 (Nov 16-25, <=5 h):** decision gate. If it passes: Illinois attorney review ($1-2k), LLC, Stripe pre-check, security checklist, and the lever map live for 25 schools. If it fails: write a one-page post-mortem and stop. Put the hours into the freight-audit service, which is already built and whose revenue is blocked only on his calls.

## Quit conditions

- Fewer than 3 of 10 parents sign an intent by Nov 8.
- Under 12 appeal histories in hand by Nov 15, or under 6 of 20 wins of $1,500 or more by Nov 25.
- No retired aid officer will put their name on the packet, in writing, by Nov 22.
- An Illinois attorney says fee-on-delivery cannot be made compliant, or review costs over ~$3k.
- Stripe declines underwriting.
- CAP, Road2College, or TuitionFit ships outcome reporting or a contingency tier.
- Any breach or near-miss on family documents. Stop immediately.

## What to carry forward

- A free tool funded by the profit pool next door cannot be beaten with a fee. Here the profit pool is lender originations ($7.4B for Sallie Mae alone in 2025, per its call).
- Outcome-triggered fees need an independent verifier. Ownwell and Goodbill have one (tax rolls, hospital bills), and aid appeals do not.
- A one-cycle-a-year product gives a student founder one learning loop a year.
- "Nobody does it" after five years is more likely a dead model than an open market.

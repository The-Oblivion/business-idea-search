# G:own_assets:2 - PCP Freight Dispute Desk (Amazon inbound LTL adjustments, 25% contingency)

**Verdict: KILL as a standalone venture.** Keep at most as a 2-week win-rate test bolted onto the existing LTL freight audit. Checked 2026-10-25, about 18 searches and 12 page fetches.

## Why it dies (in order of weight)

1. **Amazon is the counterparty and there is no appeal channel.**
   - The June 12, 2025 policy turns carrier-measured weight, dimension or class differences into fee adjustments. Amazon's published guidance names no dispute route or deadline.
   - Third-party write-ups (Forest Shipping, amazonsellers.attorney) say the same: there is no formal appeal.
   - Every documented LTL dispute I found was rejected or left unresolved. I found zero reported reversals.
     - One seller disputed 8 defects with packing lists, pallet weights, cube and an NMFTA-based calculator. All were rejected.
     - One seller sent scale photos to three Amazon teams and was refused by all three, even though the carrier (Central Transport) agreed the reweigh was excessive.
   - Amazon Support refuses to hand over the carrier audit document. A desk cannot "recompute from the BOL" against a number it cannot see.
   - The recompute only addresses density-to-class math. Amazon's own replies say pallet height, stretch wrap and overhang trigger adjustments, and those are physical, carrier-measured facts.
   - These threads are biased toward losers, so the true win rate is unknown. Even so, the business rests on an unproven win rate and on Amazon's goodwill.
2. **Tiny ceiling, even at 100% share.**
   - US sellers above $1M a year number about 55k, per a blog citing Marketplace Pulse. Another blog says 100k+, scope unclear. I found no figure for how many ship by LTL.
   - My assumption, flagged as an estimate: 10-20k LTL-shipping sellers at about $50k a year of freight, so about $0.5-1B of freight.
   - Generic freight-audit benchmarks put recoverable errors at 2-4% of spend, for audited unmanaged shipping. At a 25% fee, the desk earns about 0.5-1% of freight spend.
   - Revenue at 100% penetration of every LTL seller: about $2.5-10M. At a realistic 1-2% penetration it is about $50-200k. The idea's own "$5-20M pool" is the sellers' total fees, not the desk's revenue.
   - Anchor: Getida, the generalist FBA reimbursement leader, is estimated at about $10.7M of 2023 revenue (Latka, a third-party estimate) after roughly 8 years and tens of thousands of sellers. An LTL-only slice cannot reach $10M.
3. **Free substitutes exist for the class-math layer, and the proposed referral partners give audits away.**
   - Warp has a free reweigh/reclass audit that takes a CSV of PROs.
   - AMZ Prep has a free "Middle Mile Audit" scorecard, uploaded from Seller Central reports. AMZ Prep is a prep center, which is exactly the proposed referral channel, and it uses audits as lead generation.
4. **Contingency leakage and access problem.**
   - Verifying a credit for the 25% fee needs settlement-report access.
   - Amazon's forum guidance tells sellers not to add outside providers as users. The "Authorized Partners" tab was reported missing in one thread, so I could not confirm that route.
   - An 18-year-old asking a $50k+ freight seller for account access is a real credibility gap. The CSV-only alternative leaves fee collection on the honor system.
5. **Lead channel is partly walled.**
   - Amazon-seller subreddits such as r/AmazonSeller ban lead generation outside a single promo thread.
   - r/FulfillmentByAmazon's rules are unconfirmed. I found one self-promotion post there.
   - The Amazon seller forums and Sellers Ask Sellers do expose pain-point posters who can be answered individually.

## What checks out (so the kill is calibrated)

- **No freight-class specialist for Amazon inbound adjustments appeared in any search.** Adjacent tools are shipment builders (Wizard Industries, FreightAmigo, Boxem, AccelerList) or enterprise (Enveyo).
- **Pain is real and documented.**
  - $1,086.13 on one pallet shipment declared at 587 lb and reweighed at 6,178 lb, which looks like a measurement or data error.
  - One seller reports four adjustments in a year on LTL.
  - One seller claims $109k of excess charges over 1,118 shipments (unverified).
  - Several sellers report carrier Central Transport International's class reading one step above what they submitted (200 to 250, 250 to 300).
- **No legal wall.** An LLC and contracts are fine at 18. Auditing is not freight brokerage, but do not arrange the transportation or you may need FMCSA broker authority. That point is from general knowledge and I did not search it.
- **Possible 90-day window.** An Amazon announcement gives 90 days from the charge date to dispute FBA fees tied to inaccurate weight or dimensions. If that applies to inbound transportation, retroactive "last 90 days" audits are the maximum and the 18-month backlog is unreachable. I could not confirm that it applies.

## Numbers

- Cost to first revenue: about $500 (LLC, domain, floor scale for demo photos).
- Time to first cash: about 12 weeks. A dispute must be filed, Amazon's case cycle runs weeks, and then a credit must be seen on a settlement report.
- Median case: about $0.2M a year at maturity. That is about 200 sellers at about $500 a year of fees, plus a $29-a-month monitor.
- Upside case: about $3M a year. That needs a broadened fee-recovery layer plus a prevention product, and it runs into Getida and Pattern.

## Sharpened version (only if the win rate proves out)

**Pallet Proof**: a scale-verified evidence record created when the pallet is built, sold per pallet to prep centers and 3PLs.

1. Capture scale reading, photo with the FBA label, dimensions and a density-correct class at build time, and declare the right values at shipment creation. That prevents the adjustment and gives a timestamped packet for any dispute.
2. Publish a carrier scorecard from pooled data, for example which carriers reclass upward most often. This is the data asset.
3. Distribute through prep centers, not Reddit.

**Gate before building:** file 15 disputes for 4-5 friendly sellers at zero fee, using the existing freight engine. Proceed only if at least 40% produce a real credit. Below 15%, drop the whole idea. Per-pallet volume data was not found, so no market size is claimed for this version.

# G21 gate: G:own_assets:5 The Referee (blind tests and arena for AI-text detectors, plus Human-or-AI game)

**Verdict: WEAK, leaning kill as specified.** Cheap to start and a real credibility asset, but the revenue pool is tiny and conflicted, and the "no neutral leaderboard" premise is wrong.

## Deciding reason
The lane is already full of benchmarks, and the payers who could fund a "certified" version are small and can make their own "independent" validation for free. Trust is the whole product, and pay-for-badge destroys it. An 18-year-old solo operator who also owns a competing detector starts with the least trust of anyone in the lane.

## What already exists (the idea said "no neutral, continuously refreshed leaderboard")
- **DetectArena** (detectarena.ai): crowd blind-pair voting with Elo, 6 detectors (Pangram, GPTZero, Originality.ai, Winston AI, Sapling, ZeroGPT), per-category ratings. Operator undisclosed. Winston AI cites it as "#1, 90.9% win rate" on a thin 44 ranked battles. Votes are user judgment, not ground truth.
- **"AI Detection Benchmark"** (Hugging Face org, monthly runs, 91 texts x 2 scans, 5 detectors) ranks Winston AI first; its text reads vendor-aligned and discloses no funding.
- **HumanizerBench / AI Humanizer Benchmarks** (monthly, 7-14 humanizers vs 5 detectors, operators unnamed, conflicting results between near-identical names).
- **RAID** (ACL 2024, Penn): academic shared benchmark with hidden test set and a PR-based leaderboard; includes commercial detectors.
- **Chicago Booth / BFI working paper 2025-116** ("Artificial Writing and Automated Detection"): Pangram best, GPTZero disputes it with its own re-run. The authors themselves call for regular published audits.
- **Vendor-run benchmarks**: GPTZero publishes quarterly comparisons with raw predictions (vs Pangram, Originality, Grammarly, Quillbot); Originality.ai re-runs other people's datasets; Pangram cites UChicago/UMD.
- **Academic/peer-reviewed**: Information Research 2026 (24 texts), Scribbr 12-tool test, Springer EFL study.
- **Human-or-AI games already exist**: AI21 "Human or Not" (chat Turing test), roft.io "Real or Fake Text" (leaderboard), "Bot or Not" on itch.io, Ghostwriter, Factitious (U. Miami).
- Free ChatGPT/Claude "which detector?" answers are the other substitute.

So the gap is not "a leaderboard". The gap, if any, is "a leaderboard people believe". Today every ranking is contradicted by another, and each vendor cites the one it wins.

## Stale or wrong facts in the idea
- GPTZero was **acquired by Superhuman (ex-Grammarly), announced 2026-06-23**, reported ~$30M ARR (not $16-24M). It "continues as an independent product" but its tech is folding into Superhuman Go. That is a bigger, less accessible counterparty, not a hungry one.
- Pangram: $9M round led by Menlo, July 2026 (confirmed). Total ~$13M. It already has the academic validation it wants.
- Arena (ex-LMArena): $150M at $1.7B (Jan 2026) confirmed; also a $200M Series B at $3.1B (Oct 2026). But its payers are frontier labs that need model rankings; no equivalent buyer exists for text detectors.
- AFT/GPTZero partnership (15,000 teachers get free access; 1.7M-educator figure unverified) means the teacher channel is partly locked up by a vendor.

## Who pays, and how much (the weak link)
- **Vendors**: Originality.ai is ~$2.3M revenue with ~21 people (Latka estimate, low confidence), bootstrapped. A $2-5k test is 0.1-0.2% of that, so possible, but the buyer would be the vendor that loses elsewhere and wants a favorable verdict. AV-Comparatives shows the model can work: it charges vendors for services (false-alarm follow-up, logo use), not for the test itself, and caps participants. That works because distributors and magazines demand the badge. No one demands a detector badge. Winston AI already manufactures its own "third-party validation" page.
- **Schools**: the pool is shrinking. Yale, Johns Hopkins, Waterloo, Curtin, UCT, WSU and others have switched off AI detection; NSW (Australia) bars schools relying on it. Turnitin, the detector schools actually use, is licensed to institutions only and **cannot be tested by outsiders** (GPTZero's own benchmark skips it for that reason). A $199-999 "procurement report" has no budget line behind it.
- **Affiliates**: GPTZero 30% on first 6 payments within 12 months (60-day cookie, $20 min); Originality.ai 25% for 12 months (90-day cookie, $50 min, no Google Ads). Roughly $20-45 per paying referral. $1M/yr would need ~25-50k paid referrals a year in a keyword space held by Scribbr, Zapier, G2, Ahrefs and endless Medium/Substack listicles, many with undisclosed self-interest. Humanizer programs pay 25% (Undetectable.ai claims 8,000+ partners), but see the guardrail below.
- **FTC context**: FTC v. Workado (2025) found a "98% accurate" detector was 53% accurate on general content. That gives a regulatory tailwind for independent tests, but it is a story, not a budget.

## Numbers
- Bottom-up median (year 2): ~30k monthly visits x ~3% click x ~5% convert = ~45 paid referrals/month x ~$25 = ~$13k/yr affiliate; plus 1-3 vendor test cycles ($2-4k each, 4 per year) = $10-40k; plus a handful of reports = ~$4k. About **$0.05-0.15M/yr**.
- Upside (year 5): leaderboard becomes the reference cited by press and vendors; sells sealed-holdout private evaluations (the Artificial Analysis model: public free, private custom benchmarks paid) to 10-15 detector vendors, platforms and publishers at $20-50k plus affiliates: **~$1-2M/yr**. A $10M business needs buyers with big budgets (frontier labs for model rankings, which do not exist here).
- Detector-market reports ($0.58-1B in 2025-26) are inconsistent and measure the detector vendors' revenue, not what they spend on third-party validation.
- Cost to first revenue ~$2.5k: ~150-200 fresh human texts from paid writers ($10-15 each via a platform like Prolific; one cycle ~$1.5-2.5k, repeating monthly), API credits ~$200-400, domain/hosting small. First dollar realistically ~10-12 weeks (affiliate payout minimums of $20-50 and cookie windows slow it; a vendor wire is the faster, lower-probability path).

## Teen blockers (which are real)
- **Age/legal capacity**: not a blocker. 18, can form an LLC, sign vendor agreements, join Rewardful and PayPal.
- **Credibility**: **the real blocker.** The product is trust, and he is anonymous-to-vendors, 18, and owns a competing detector (disclosed conflict, even if excluded from rankings). Also his "beat commercial detectors" results are unreplicated. Mitigation that works: a faculty co-author or advisor, pre-registered protocol, holdout set escrowed with a third party, version-stamped detectors, vendor right-of-reply.
- **License**: none needed.
- **Legal**: comparative publication is protected if truthful and methodologically sound, but vendors can allege false advertising or trade libel; use paid APIs rather than scraping web UIs, check each vendor's terms for benchmark restrictions (I could not confirm GPTZero's API terms either way), keep raw data. Real but manageable.
- **Capital**: ~$2.5k/month ongoing for fresh human text. Real, small, fundable by family seed money.
- **Guardrails**: do **not** take humanizer affiliate money. Humanizers are tools sold to evade academic-integrity checks; ranking them as adversarial test inputs is fine, earning commissions from them is the academic-dishonesty line. Keep the "Human or AI?" game 13+ with no minors-targeted marketing, and keep it off the House Money casino brand.
- **Reaching teachers**: r/Teachers and r/professors almost certainly restrict self-promotion (rules page not fetchable here; unverified). Plan for earned media and research-style posts, not ads.

## First 10 customers / channel
Vendors that already shop for third-party validation: Winston AI, Originality.ai (CEO Jon Gillham publishes studies constantly), Copyleaks, Sapling, Quetext, Scribbr/QuillBot detector, Smodin, plus the one or two "challenger" detectors. Skip detector brands that sell humanizers (Undetectable.ai, etc.). For traffic: Hacker News "Show HN", X/AI-eval circle, Substack authors on education and AI, higher-ed newsletters (Inside Higher Ed tipsters), writers who cover the Booth/Pangram/GPTZero fight.

## Sharpened version (keeps it big)
Not a badge shop. Build a **university-co-signed, sealed-holdout authenticity benchmark**: free public leaderboard (the credibility asset), pre-registered protocol, holdout corpus escrowed with a faculty advisor (the mentor's UF network is the first door), every vendor tested whether it pays or not (he buys the API credits). Revenue is AV-Comparatives plus Artificial Analysis: vendors pay for services (pre-publication access, false-positive follow-up, logo license), not for the result; private custom evaluations for platforms and publishers that need an AI-text policy (Quora, Medium, job boards, edtech); disclosed detector-only affiliates. The Human-or-AI game becomes the data engine that produces a human-baseline ("people vs detectors") and adversarial examples, and the press hook. Still not above the freight-audit service he already built; worth doing only as a cheap, 3-week credibility project that also markets his own detector work.

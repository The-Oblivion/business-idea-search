# G10: Mandate Radar (G:ai_builder:5): verdict WEAK

**One line:** The mandate is real and the niche feed is genuinely unserved, but the public data shows a thin, mostly post-award pipeline, and the Florida revenue ceiling is about $1-3M a year. It is a decent bootstrapped wedge, not a $10M business as pitched.

## Numbers that decide it (OPPAGA report 26-04, July 2026, the first statewide milestone dataset)

- Phase 1 milestone inspections completed in 2024 and 2025: 8,736. Phase 2 completed: 1,575. Phase 2 required: 1,899 (2024) and 636 (2025). Only 71% (2024) and 64% (2025) of 389 jurisdictions reported.
- Repair permit applications that came out of Phase 2 findings: 903 in two years (671 in 2024, 232 in 2025). Average value was $337k in 2024 and $496k in 2025. That is roughly $340M over two years, or about $170M a year of reported permit value. Scaling for the non-reporting jurisdictions gives perhaps $240M a year (my estimate).
- Buildings that a Phase 2 inspection called unsafe or uninhabitable: 30 (2024) and 24 (2025). "Buildings that failed a milestone inspection" is therefore about 27 a year statewide.
- The state has no central database of inspection reports. Records are held per jurisdiction, in Broward, Miami-Dade and Palm Beach across dozens of municipalities, and form and accessibility vary widely.
- 62% of South Florida condo associations missed the first SIRS deadline (Miami Realtors / Zalewski, early 2025). The compliance lists are hard to get; Zalewski's team called getting them difficult, and their list is behind a paid subscription.
- DBPR publishes free bulk CSVs of the condo registry (association address, unit count, managing entity), plus an interactive SIRS dashboard that is not downloadable.
- Statutory timeline: repairs must begin within 365 days of a Phase 2 report. The Dec 31, 2026 milestone/SIRS coordination deadline pushes the repair wave into 2027-28.

## Who already does this

Nobody sells a milestone/SIRS/recertification repair pipeline to contractors (several searches found none). But the adjacent field is crowded and the substitutes are strong.

- **Generic permit-lead tools:**
  - PermitGrab: $149/mo Pro, 474 cities, 55 in Florida, a code-violation layer, no milestone or recertification data.
  - Construction Monitor: building-permit leads, covers Florida.
  - Shovels: permit API across 1,800-2,450+ jurisdictions, with a plan listed at about $599/mo (unverified).
  - ConstructConnect: Professional at $199/mo per market, Premium from $10k a year.
  - Dodge: roughly $300 per user per month (third-party figure).
  - Apify scrapers: sell permit and code-violation leads for $3-50 per 1,000 records.
- **BellAveGo:** the idea's claim does not match its homepage. It now sells house-cleaning leads from short-term-rental hosts at $99-199 per host, not exclusive-per-zip contractor leads at $197/mo. The idea's competitor list is partly stale.
- **Government-side software:** Forerunner sells milestone-inspection tracking to Florida building departments. It holds the exact data this idea wants, and could publish it or build a contractor product.
- **Buyer-side tools:** Kaloop Community RFP (launched Oct 2025, free for Florida associations for 6 months, up to 10 bids per project, generic trades), PropFusion (marketplace linking associations to reserve-study experts), Condo Control, and the Condo Ratings Agency (investor ratings on about 13k South Florida associations).
- **Free substitutes:**
  - NYC Open Data DOB NOW Facades filings, with unsafe/SWARMP status, complete and free.
  - Miami-Dade's recertification portal.
  - DBPR registry CSVs.
  - Sunbiz officer records.
- **The real incumbent is relationships.** The engineer who writes the Phase 2 scope usually shapes the bid list. Boards get 2-3 bids, and engineers and property-manager vendor lists feed them. Contractors and engineers are also capacity-constrained (shortage reports, seller listings claiming turned-away work), which weakens willingness to pay for leads.

## The structural flaw

The public signals mostly arrive too late or are too thin.

- A repair permit is filed by the contractor already hired. It tells you who won, not who is shopping.
- The pre-award signals (Phase 2 report, SIRS findings, board bid requests) sit with the association and engineer. Phase 2 reports go to the building department only in some places, and the data is scattered across 389 jurisdictions.
- Age-based "due" lists are trivial to build from the free registry plus property-appraiser year-built data, so they carry no moat.
- A feed that is genuinely early and genuinely rich needs per-jurisdiction Chapter 119 records requests and relationships, which is labor and not software.

## Sizing (my estimates, labeled)

- Contractor marketing spend on this pipeline: if contractors spend 3-5% of contract value on acquisition, the pool is about $5-12M a year across all trades in Florida (assumption). A feed capturing 10-20% of it is roughly $0.5-2.4M.
- Bottom-up: perhaps 600-1,000 relevant Florida firms (concrete restoration, waterproofing, coatings, commercial roofing with condo work, milestone/SIRS engineering; unsourced estimate). At 10-25% penetration and about $6k a year, that is $0.4-1.5M.
- Reaching $10M at $6k a year needs about 1,700 subscribers, which is about twice the Florida firm base. NYC (about 8,000 sidewalk sheds, 13.3% of latest-cycle filings unsafe), Chicago, Boston, Philadelphia and California SB 721/326 add pools. Each is a separate scraping, relationship and trust build, and the NYC contractors are sophisticated and can pull the free data themselves.
- Median case about $0.6M a year in year 3. Upside about $6M, which needs a multi-city platform plus data licensing to lenders, insurers and suppliers.

## Can Asher reach and close the first customers?

- **Weeks to first revenue:** about 8-12. **Cost:** about $1k (LLC filing, domain, email tool, records fees, compute). The idea's $2k is fine.
- **Likely wedge:** a hand-verified 25-50 building South Florida sample, cold-emailed to concrete restoration, waterproofing and balcony firms, plus milestone/SIRS engineering firms that market for clients. Expect a 1-3% reply rate and 1-3 paying customers in the first 90 days if the sample leads are pre-award and verifiable.
- **Where the buyers are:** ICRI chapters and directory-listed firms (Chematics of the South, Daniello & Associates, SabreCon, Durcon, Rock Solid Restorations as examples of firm types), engineering firms that blog about milestones (M2E, Building Mavens), reserve-study firms, commercial roofers, elevator modernization shops and swing-stage companies. CAI South Florida events and property-management vendor days are the networking channel. Family and mentor intros to a condo board or a CAM are more valuable than to contractors.
- **Teen blockers:**
  - Age: not a blocker. Contractors buy leads from anyone, and the sample speaks for itself.
  - License: none needed for selling public-record intelligence on a flat fee.
  - Credibility with engineers and boards: moderate and real. It matters more for the expansion products.
  - Legal, mostly unverified: do not take a percentage of contract value, because Florida engineering-board rules likely restrict referral commissions (check). Do not scrape owner emails or text and call (718.111(12), FTSA). Check portal terms of use. Email and mail to businesses are fine under CAN-SPAM. SIRS-bidding engineers and contractors must disclose whether they plan to bid on the repairs, which the product must respect.
  - Time: 10-15 hours a week is tight for per-jurisdiction records work. This is the real blocker.

## Sharpened version (broadened, not shrunk)

**Mandate Pipeline**, the stage-by-stage tracker for every building the law forces to inspect, reserve and repair.

- **Stages:** due, overdue, Phase 1 filed, Phase 2 required, repair scope, bid window, permit, amended-report closeout.
- **Three payers on one dataset:** engineers and reserve-study firms (pre-inspection, via the non-compliance lists nobody publishes); restoration contractors (post-Phase 2, pre-award); lenders and suppliers of assessment loans and materials, which Florida's loan provision for associations opens up.
- **Build the moat where public data fails:** a Chapter 119 request pipeline plus relationships with the engineers who see Phase 2 scopes, offered as a free "compliance help" layer for associations that feeds the pre-award signal.
- **Start in one county, then replicate across the mandate regimes:** Florida milestone/SIRS first, then NYC FISP, Chicago, Boston, Philadelphia and California balcony laws.
- **First sale:** the 5-lead sample to 100 firms at $499 per trade per county. If lead quality is pre-award and verifiable, keep going. If the sample is mostly post-permit, stop.

## Kill/continue test (2 weeks, under $300)

1. Request milestone and recertification submissions from three building departments and check how many Phase 2 reports are actually public and pre-permit.
2. Show 10 hand-built leads to 5 restoration firms and ask whether they would pay $499 a month.
3. If fewer than 2 of 5 say yes, kill.

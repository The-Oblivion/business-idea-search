# G35 (G:genz_life:4) TradeTrack: Workforce Pell finder. Verdict: WEAK (near kill as pitched)

Gate date: 2026-10-25. About 20 searches and fetches.

## Deciding reason
The premise "no national directory exists" is wrong, and the thing being directoried barely exists yet. ED had approved about 18-21 Workforce Pell programs in 5-7 states by late Sept/early Oct 2026. Fewer than 1% of NC programs are expected to clear every test. Several free or already-monetized sites cover the same ground, and the payer structure in the pitch (a fee per "FAFSA-ready" lead) is the riskiest one under the Title IV incentive-compensation ban.

## What I found

**Supply is tiny and slow**
- Workforce Pell went live July 1, 2026. The first federal approval was Iowa Central CC (EMT) on Aug 4. Approvals followed in IN, NE, TX, NC, FL and, per ED, AZ (a Pima Medical Institute phlebotomy program, the 7th state).
- A tracker count as of Sept 26 shows 18 programs at 7 community or technical colleges in 5 states (FAFSA Updates). Another tracker says 21 by Oct 6. The sources conflict slightly.
- NC has 54 state-approved programs but only 6 federally approved, all at Forsyth Tech. NC's estimate of programs that qualify fell from 4% to under 1%.
- Pennsylvania: 11 institutions submitted 40 programs and 2 were approved (both truck driving, at Douglas Education Center and Rosedale Technical College). Virginia: 6 of about 600 programs.
- Thresholds: 150-599 clock hours over 8-15 weeks, a program that has operated for 12 months, 70% completion, 70% placement, and tuition at or below value-added earnings. Federal review takes up to 120 days. Most students will not get an award until about January-spring 2027 (WUNC).

**Volume**
- ED's own regulatory impact analysis projects about 184-190k recipients a year by FY2027-28. CBO is far lower (about $1.5B over 10 years, about $2,200 per recipient, so about 100k a year).
- Students skew older than the pitch assumes. About 75% of noncredit workforce students are 25+, and Virginia's average is 35. The WUNC example student is 37. Interstate CDL requires age 21, and the SDAP under-21 pilot ended Nov 2025.

**Competitors and free substitutes (the pitch claims none)**
- FAFSA Updates (fafsaupdates.com) is a solo educator's site. It has a sourced tracker (18 programs, 5 states), a table of official state pages for 31 states plus DC, a Workforce Pell screener, and calculators. It is free and student-facing.
- Opportunity Data (opportunitydata.org) has a state-by-state implementation tracker.
- Trade-Schools.net (Beelineweb, since 2001, claims 1.5M people introduced to schools) has a Workforce Pell explainer with a program table. Its disclosure says it is paid for "successful connections", which is the same lead model.
- BlueCollarBrain has lead matching. TradeColleges.org lists 3,187 trade colleges and runs "Trades News" Workforce Pell coverage. TradeCareerPath (Hot Key Media) has outcome-style rankings with labelled sponsored listings. SkillPlum is an outcomes-driven directory built on IPEDS and Scorecard.
- Free public tools: CareerOneStop Local Training Finder (every state's WIOA ETPL, updated yearly), College Scorecard, and Oregon's HECC Workforce Pell search. Washington is planning to flag eligible programs on Career Bridge.
- Provider-side software vendors (Entrinsik/Enrole, Prentus, Modern Campus) sell Workforce Pell tracking to schools.
- Any of these can add a "Workforce Pell eligible" filter in an afternoon. The differentiator would be only the state-list scraping, which is a few weeks of work.

**Payer and money**
- Private career schools do buy leads. The 2012 Senate for-profit report found $10-150 per lead. Some schools spent about a quarter of revenue on marketing (American Career College: $2,168 per student). A 2026 benchmark says cost per lead rose 15% from 2024 to 2025.
- But the schools that will have approved programs in 2027 are mostly community colleges, which rarely buy third-party lead gen. Private CDL and trade schools with approved programs number a handful (Pima, Rosedale, Douglas and a few more).
- Employers are a better-funded payer. Motor carriers lose about $8k-13k per driver replaced (vendor and old UGPTI figures, weak sourcing). Employer-paid pipelines are not Title IV-restricted.

**Legal**
- 34 CFR 668.14(b)(22) bars Title IV schools from paying anyone engaged in recruiting or aid decisions based even in part on success in securing enrollments or aid. GEN-11-05 treats targeted outreach to individuals, contacting prospects, and completing aid applications as covered activities. Group advertising is exempt.
- The bundled-services exception rests on sub-regulatory guidance. ED reaffirmed the ban in a Jan 2025 letter. A March 2026 $1.3M False Claims Act settlement (Study Across the Pond) shows it is enforced, and commentary says it reaches lead generators.
- A flat per-lead fee is an industry-standard gray area, and the compliance risk sits mainly with the school. A fee for a "FAFSA-ready" lead is the worst version, because payment would depend on an aid milestone. Flat sponsorship or listing fees (advertising) and employer-paid placement are the safer structures.
- No license is needed for him to operate. Collecting leads from 18-24s needs TCPA-compliant consent for calls and texts.
- This is research, not legal advice. Pay for a one-hour Title IV counsel review before any contract.

**Reach and close for this founder**
- Cold emailing enrollment directors for a free pilot is easy. Getting a signed paid commitment from a 18-year-old is hard. Public colleges have procurement rules, and compliance staff will balk at a per-lead plus FAFSA structure.
- The mentor's AV-integration network does not help with school enrollment offices. The family network is unknown.
- Realistic first buyers: private or nonprofit career schools with approved or pending programs (Pima Medical Institute, Rosedale Technical College, Douglas Education Center), plus workforce divisions at Iowa Central, Ivy Tech, Metropolitan CC, Kilgore, Weatherford and Forsyth Tech.

## Numbers
- Cost to first revenue: about $3k (LLC, hosting, data, one counsel hour).
- Weeks to first revenue: about 16 for a flat sponsored listing. Workforce Pell-specific revenue likely waits for 2027 awards.
- Median case: about $0.2M a year by year 3.
- Upside: about $8M a year. Math: 150k students in 2029, 15% reach is 22k enrollments, 8 leads per enrollment is 180k leads at $35 is $6.3M, plus about $1.8M of sponsorships. This needs heroic share and a legal structure that survives scrutiny. The original $5-15M is the top of a stack of unverified assumptions.

## Sharpened version (not shrunk)
Do not sell "Workforce Pell finder". Sell "what your short trade program really costs and pays, after every dollar of aid". Cover Workforce Pell, WIOA ETPL, state grants, employer tuition sponsorship and the GI Bill, for every short program, with outcomes. Use the Workforce Pell tracker as live SEO and link bait. Charge flat sponsorship and listing fees to schools (group advertising, no per-enrollment or per-aid pricing) and employer-paid sponsored seats and candidate pipelines (carriers, HVAC, electrical and welding contractors), where the budget is larger. Aim at adults 18-40 without a degree, not just 18-24.

Kill test: by Jan 31, 2027, no school or employer prepays at least $250 for a sponsored listing or pilot seat. Or counsel says the sponsorship structure does not clear the incentive-compensation ban.

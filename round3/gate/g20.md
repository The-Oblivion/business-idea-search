# G20: Aid Ledger (key G:teen_advantage:5)

**Verdict: KILL.** Median case $0.15M/yr, upside about $3M/yr, first revenue about 12 weeks, about $500 to get there.

## Deciding reason

Someone has run this exact idea for seven years, and it stayed small. The idea says "a crowdsourced database of real offers did not turn up in my search." That is wrong.

- **TuitionFit** (Davenport, Iowa) launched January 2019. It is a crowdsourced, give-to-get database of redacted award letters, sorted by test scores, GPA and need. Its founder calls it a "Kelley Blue Book" for college prices. It sells a one-time $49 look-up to families who have no letter to share, and $35/month advanced search to counselors. Colleges buy subscriptions to see competitor offers and contact students with better ones.
- Its first cycle ended with just over 4,000 letters.
- CB Insights lists it as alive, but it has raised only about $180K in total, including a $100K Iowa state loan. The last round was about 6 years ago.
- That is the venture-scale test, and it is the Aid Ledger model nearly word for word, with a head start and a college-sales pitch. It never visibly scaled.
- **Road2College "Compare College Offers"** (Destination U Inc.) is also crowdsourced. Families submit offers and see others' offers beside their own. It has a free tier, with paid plans at $24.99 to $59.99 a month.
- **Niche Financial Aid Decoder** launched March 17, 2026. It is free and upload-based. It sits inside True Cost, which covers 4-year cost estimates for up to 20 colleges, and Niche says its models use aggregated award letters from the families it supports.
- Other free or cheap tools:
  - College Raptor offer-letter upload.
  - Award Advisor (free).
  - Discover's comparison tool.
  - Going Merry.
  - DecidED.
  - College Aid Pro's MyCAP Award Analyzer, which grades appeal odds for free and upsells to $1,999 to $3,499.
  - OpenAI's College Planner for ChatGPT for Teens, announced October 7, 2026. It tracks aid steps and deadlines, but I found no letter comparison in it.
- Edmit, the other 2017 comparison tool, ended as a small Vemo Education subsidiary in 2021. Its revenue came from lender and credit-union partnerships, not from families paying.

## Why the plan also fails on its own terms

1. **ED families cannot use the comparison.**
   - Early Decision is binding. Those families get one offer and cannot compare it against another school's offer. The only exit is if aid is inadequate.
   - ED applicants are roughly 13% of applicants (2022-23 Common App figure, the latest I found).
   - A December sale of the comparison to "ED and EA families" mostly targets people who cannot act on it.
   - Real comparison demand peaks from March through May 1, when RD letters land. First revenue by January is possible, but it would be friends and favors, not a market signal.
2. **Cold start.**
   - 200 letters spread across about 1,500 four-year schools is roughly 0.1 letters per school.
   - Matching "similar students" (stats band, income band, major) from that is not a product.
   - TuitionFit needed a full cycle to reach about 4,000 letters, and it grew by word of mouth.
3. **$29 against free.**
   - TuitionFit already charges $49.
   - Niche, Road2College, Award Advisor and College Raptor are free.
   - The one real paid-help data point is consultants at $295 to $3,499, and that is for appeals, which the idea says succeed rarely.
4. **The big path is a cold enterprise sale.**
   - 300 colleges at $30K needs enrollment offices to buy from an 18-year-old.
   - TuitionFit pitched colleges for years, and the founder reported a mixed reaction, "wide-eyed fear" from some schools, and ultra-selective schools not caring.
   - Colleges already have the College Board Admitted Student Questionnaire competitor analysis, Common App peer analytics, and the RNL, EAB and Encoura vendors.
   - I found no public price for any of those college-side products. The one contract I found was Ferris State's 2019 EAB enrollment-services deal, at $431K in year one.
   - NACUBO puts private-college discount rates at about 56% (286 institutions in the 2024-25 study). The payer and the pain exist, but this founder can't reach the payer.
5. **Parent PLUS caps are real but make a thin why-now.**
   - New Parent PLUS limits are $20K a year and $65K total per student for first disbursements on or after July 1, 2026. The gap is real.
   - Families will search for "how do I fund the gap." They will not search for a letter database.
   - The tools that answer the gap question are free or lender-funded.

## Teen blockers

- **Age and licence:** none. He is 18 and can form an LLC and take payments.
- **Credibility:** real for the college side. It is cold enterprise, and TuitionFit tried it. It is not a blocker for families.
- **Privacy:** real but manageable.
  - Most uploaders will be 17-year-old seniors.
  - Colorado (SB 24-041, effective October 2025) restricts sale, profiling and targeted ads for under-18s without consent.
  - Connecticut's minors' law restricts sale or targeted ads to minors.
  - Selling that data to colleges is a "sale" of minors' data. I have not verified each statute's age cutoff.
  - Letters also carry income and aid-index data, so a leak would be serious.
- **Reddit:** I could not retrieve r/ApplyingToCollege's current self-promotion rules. Assume promotion needs mod approval.

## Numbers

- **Cost to first revenue:** about $500 (LLC and domain about $200, hosting and OCR/redaction API about $300). It is cheap, but it buys no real validation.
- **Time to first revenue:** about 12 weeks (late January 2027), with friends as buyers.
- **Median case:** about $0.15M a year.
  - Reachable audience is about 5K to 20K visitors a year by organic social.
  - 1% to 3% convert at $39 to $49.
  - That is about $2K to $30K in year one, growing to about $150K with several class cycles.
- **Upside:** about $3M a year. That is about 60K families at $49, or about 150 small privates at $15K plus consumers.
- **The pitch's $10-15M:** needs 300 colleges at $30K. TuitionFit's trajectory says no.

## Sharpened version (does not shrink it)

**Title:** Net Price Receipts: a free, public net-price dataset for the class of 2027, funded by the real payer.

**One-liner:** Run the data-collection engine free from day one (a public dataset on Reddit, Discord and TikTok, with a give-to-get unlock) to beat TuitionFit's cold-start problem. Then monetize the gap TuitionFit and Niche leave open: spring-2027 families deciding under the new Parent PLUS caps.

**What changes:**
- Drop the December ED sale.
- Collect from EA and RD admits March to May 1, when comparison demand peaks.
- Make the first output a free public dataset (net price by school, stats band and income band). That is shareable content for TikTok and Reddit, and a credibility asset.
- Keep a paid or sponsored layer for later: a four-year funding-gap planner under the new caps.

**Honest caveat:** this has a distribution edge but no demonstrated payer. If a pilot shows no paying family or college by May 1, 2027, stop. Don't narrow it into a counselor tool.

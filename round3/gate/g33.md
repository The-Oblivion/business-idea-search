# g33 - S:I0576 Import Compliance Desk for Hobby Brands

**Verdict: KILL** (as a business; at most a weekend experiment with hard stop criteria)
Date of check: 2026-10-25. About 30 searches and fetches.

## Deciding reason
The product is a thin layer over a free government tool, sold into a segment that averages under one certificate a year, with the "eFiling handoff" already given away by labs and shipping platforms. There is no honest path to $10M a year. The only way to get big is to move to SKU-heavy brands, and those buyers are already served by funded vendors and the labs, and will not buy cold from an 18-year-old.

## What I found

### 1. The why-now is real but softer than the pitch says
- Mandatory eFiling started for entries on or after July 8, 2026 (FTZ goods January 8, 2027). No value threshold. Covers CPCs and GCCs. (CPSC release, Akin Gump, FedEx, Shopify help page.)
- CPSC and CBP said they do not intend ACE to reject entries for missing data, only to send warnings. CPSC adjusts the entry's risk score instead. A Mallory Group alert (Sep 9, 2026) says CBP had not rejected an entry over missing data in August. So urgency is low: nobody is forced to pay for a tool this quarter.
- Importer of record is still legally responsible. Brokers and carriers only transmit.

### 2. The market is small per customer, by CPSC's own numbers
CPSC's Final Regulatory Flexibility Analysis (Federal Register, Jan 8, 2025, Tables 2 and 3):
- 246,438 small importers affected (35,290 non-children's, 211,148 children's).
- 214,806 certificates a year produced by those small importers, about 0.87 per firm. This is an assumption (10% of the average firm's volume in the industry code), not measured data, but it is the agency's own basis.
- Even if every one of those certificates paid $39 once, the whole pool is about $8.4M a year. At $29 it is about $6.2M. A realistic 3-5% share is $0.2-0.4M a year.
- CPSC expects no filing fees for the Product Registry. It wrote that a small business needs only a laptop and internet to enter certificates itself, and estimated broker fees at about $15.97 per certificate, with brokers charging a maximum of about $25 per entry. It estimated the aggregate startup burden for small importers at $267.6M, but that is hours valued at labor rates, not a cash budget.

### 3. Hobby tabletop is smaller still, and often doesn't need a certificate
- Kickstarter tabletop 2024: 6,646 launches, 5,314 funded, about $220M pledged (secondary: voxbooster). Gamefound 2024: about $85M raised, about $156M with pledge managers (BoardGameWire). Many are RPG books, PDFs, or non-US creators. No source gives the share that are US importers of a regulated physical product.
- Adult-marketed games (older-audience, no child appeal) generally need no certificate at all unless a specific CPSC rule applies. Children's games (12 and under, or child appeal, including cartoon art) need a CPC with third-party lab testing. Compliance Gate and Flexport both describe this split. So "every hobby brand has a recurring chore" is false.
- Domestic manufacturing is exempt from eFiling (CPSC). Many Kickstarter creators print domestically (for example through The Game Crafter or PrintNinja) or ship through a manufacturer/fulfillment partner. I found no source on who is importer of record in typical crowdfunded games.
- I found no BoardGameGeek, Kickstarter, Gamefound or Reddit complaint thread about eFiling. The search tool does not index those well, so this is weak evidence, but nothing signals pain.

### 4. Competitors and free substitutes (the pitch named only two)
- **CPSC Product Registry**: free, UI, CSV and API. CPSC also says a CPC can be drafted by the importer at no cost, and labs do not issue CPCs.
- **Comply PRO+**: SaaS with CPC/GCC generation from test data, CPSC Registry API, one-click eFiling, to-do list of missing data, ASIN references; a separate "eFile only" SaaS (May 2026 Baby Safety Alliance webinar). Targets Amazon sellers and manufacturers. Pricing not published.
- **Compliance Gate**: platform with CPC/GCC templates and consultants; one page lists a self-managed plan at $179 a year, another says services from $99 a month. Could not load its pricing page.
- **Labs**: Eurofins digital solution (drafts CPC/GCC, CSV or API into the Registry, no set-up fees), Intertek InterLink 2.0 (a search snippet said free for a limited time; the page I fetched did not confirm), QIMA (managed service, acts as Collection Editor), SGS SMART eFiling, TUV SUD. These already hold the test report, which is the parser's input.
- **Platforms**: Easyship (certificate data entered in the dashboard and e-filed on DHL/FedEx shipments), Shopify (per-product disclosure fields for the three registry IDs, plus Global-e), Zonos Agency Check (screening).
- **Complir** (Copenhagen): AI product-compliance platform for SKU-heavy brands, publishes a CPSC eFiling guide. Reported pre-seed EUR 1.7M (Dec 2025); an $11M seed is reported by aggregators but I could not confirm it.
- **Freelancers**: Fiverr and Upwork gigs draft CPCs from lab reports. Prices not shown in what I could load.
- Price anchors: free to about $15 a month. The pitch's $19-49 a certificate or $29-99 a month sits above every anchor except managed services.

### 5. Can he reach and close them?
- Reach: yes. BoardGameGeek, BGDF, Discord, Facebook creator groups, Stonemaier-style Kickstarter communities, Amazon and Shopify seller groups are reachable by an 18-year-old with a self-serve product. A first $19-49 sale in about 8 weeks is plausible.
- Close and keep: weak. The buyer's lab or forwarder offers the handoff, the registry is free, and the need is one-time per product. Expect low retention and a customer who needs the tool for 1-3 certificates.
- The big path (long-tail Amazon and Shopify importers) is exactly where Comply PRO+, Compliance Gate, Complir, the labs, Easyship and Shopify already sit.

### 6. Legal and age
- Age: not a blocker. At 18 he can form an LLC, sign terms and take payments.
- Certificate liability stays with the importer. A false certification carries civil penalties reported at up to $120,500 per violation (secondary source). A tool that drafts or "flags" gets blamed when a certificate is wrong. Needs strong terms and possibly E&O insurance; neither is expensive, but both add friction.
- **Customs-broker line is a real issue for the pitch as written.** CBP ruling HQ H350722 (Jan 16, 2026), known to me only through secondary coverage (Diaz Trade Law, Barnes Richardson, Green Worldwide), treated OCR extraction of entry data and filing Form 5106 for importers as customs business needing a broker license. The pitch's "outputs the data elements their customs broker must file" plus a document parser sits near that line. Safe design: keep everything on the CPSC Registry side (a CPSC system, not CBP), importer is the operator on its own data, never produce a PGA message set or touch entry data. Get a customs lawyer to read it before launch.
- Marketing line: "document preparation," never "compliance advice." Fine, but it also limits the product to what Fiverr already does.

## Numbers
- Cost to first revenue: about $1,500 (LLC, domain, hosting, model API, Stripe fees, one short legal review).
- Weeks to first revenue: about 8.
- Median-case annual revenue by year 3: about $0.15M (a few hundred to 1,500 customers at roughly $100-150 a year).
- Upside: about $3M (needs 3% of small importers at about $150 a year, or a funded move into multi-SKU brands, with no evidence he can win that).

## Sharpened version (does not shrink it, but is honest about the ceiling)
**Certificate Linter.** Upload any lab report PDF and get, in under a minute, a free error list: missing rule citations, expired or wrong test dates, wrong certifying party, wrong age grade for the product. Pay $15-25 per product only to export a clean CPC and a CSV in the CPSC Product Registry v3 template. No CBP data, no PGA message set, no broker output. Distribute through manufacturers and labs as a referral (board-game factories, Panda GM-type vendors, Hero Time-type quoting pages) and through a free lead-gen checker on SEO and BoardGameGeek. Treat as a 2-week build and a 60-day test with kill rule: fewer than 25 paid exports at $15 or more, or no manufacturer willing to refer, and stop. It is not a venture path. The only way it becomes one is if the lab-report parser becomes the front end that labs and forwarders license, and that sale is not one he can make cold.

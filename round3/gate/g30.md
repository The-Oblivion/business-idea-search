# G30: Credit Concierge for the New Federal Scholarship Tax Credit (S:R2-P136-223)

**Verdict: WEAK (leans kill as a per-donor-fee business; the donor-side gap is real but the people who would pay for it are the same people who own the donor).** Gate run 2026-10-25.

## Deciding reason
The payer is the scholarship granting organization (SGO), and the SGO already owns the donor relationship, the donation page, the receipt and the donor number. The credible national SGOs are spending real money on the exact thing this tool does: ACE Scholarships says it has launched an "eight-figure" national "Make It Possible" campaign, has engaged about 5,000 CPAs, and already has a "commit now" donor portal. AFC Scholarship Fund and Step Up For Students have partnered, and FACTS (Nelnet) launched FACTS Compass for SGOs on Oct 15, 2026 and says it supports 120+ SGOs and group programs. Four more things stack on top:
1. The fee has to come out of the SGO's 10% overhead budget, and Treasury's own preamble says donors react to overhead.
2. Per-donor fees pull him into state charitable-solicitation law (professional solicitor, commercial co-venturer, platform rules).
3. Almost all per-donor revenue would land in Q4 2027, because the first gifts can only start Jan 1, 2027 and SGOs are still registering.
4. Realistic median revenue is about $1.5M a year by 2030, not $10M+.

There is no donor-facing national tool yet, so this is not "already done". But a neutral calculator is a commodity that ACE, the IRS, EdChoice, tax-software vendors and CPAs will give away.

## Competitors found (assume all live)
- ACE Scholarships (acescholarships.org/eftc): national SGO; "Make It Possible" eight-figure campaign (streaming TV/audio, YouTube, Meta, TikTok, out-of-home, bowl-game ads in DC, NY, DFW, Atlanta, KC, Orlando, Philly, Denver); about 5,000 CPAs engaged; donor "commit now" portal; EmpowerEd management platform. No calculator on the page.
- AFC Scholarship Fund (American Federation for Children): national SGO, taking interest registrations; partnered with Step Up For Students; its research arm published the "121.5M eligible taxpayers" figure.
- Step Up For Students (Florida): its 25th-anniversary release says 164 donors gave over $656M to the Florida tax credit scholarship this year (mostly corporate). A very large SGO with its own donor machine.
- FACTS / Nelnet (factsmgt.com): FACTS Compass (Oct 2026), 120+ SGOs and group programs supported, donor engagement through partners such as RedefinED School Advancement ($85M raised for scholarships).
- Catholic Schools Foundation / Children's Scholarship Fund (scholarshipfund.org/sgoservices): turnkey SGO with donation processing, tax receipting and compliance ("you raise the funds, CSF handles administration").
- Software vendors serving scholarship orgs: AwardSpring (600+ orgs, per its own page), Foundant, CommunityForce, ZoomGrants. None announced a 25F donor product that I could find.
- Free official sources: the IRS Federal Scholarship Tax Credit page (state list), EdChoice explainers, NATP and CPA-firm explainers. Nothing found on TurboTax, H&R Block, Drake or ProConnect support yet; I would expect them to add it.
- Not found: any national SGO directory, donor-side calculator or donor-planner startup. That is the real gap, and it is a thin one.

## Key facts and numbers (verified against primary sources where noted)
- Credit: up to $1,700 per individual and $3,400 for a joint couple (each spouse $1,700), nonrefundable, 5-year carryforward, cash only, starts for gifts on or after Jan 1, 2027. Reduced dollar-for-dollar by any state credit for the same gift, and no charitable deduction on the credited amount. Partnership and S-corp contributions do not qualify, so the "small-business owner embed" only works for the owner personally.
- Temp regs T.D. 10057 and proposed regs published Oct 2, 2026; comments due Dec 1, 2026. 30 states had elected in as of Sept 2026. State SGO lists are due Feb 15, 2027, and a donor needs the SGO to be on a participating state's list. A donor can give to an SGO in any participating state regardless of where the donor lives. SGOs must register through an IRS portal before Jan 1, 2027 and issue unique donor numbers and written acknowledgments.
- Treasury's own estimate (proposed-regs preamble, read directly): 600-700 SGOs, more than 11M taxpayers giving $26B a year by 2030. That is a policy projection, not a forecast I would underwrite. Brookings, extrapolating Ohio's experience (about 65,000 filers claiming about $75M over 2021-2024), gets to roughly $1.5B a year. A "$26B" 10-year cost figure also circulates (Carolina Journal), which is about an order of magnitude below Treasury's annual number. I treat the true 2030 flow as $2-6B with Treasury's number as the upside case.
- Treasury also estimates 46.5M taxpayers are eligible to take both a state and the federal SGO credit; at 10% take-up that is about 4.65M donors with up to $1,700 or $3,400 of added benefit from the ordering rule. This is the one place a calculator does real work.
- New-donor retention in general US giving is about 14% (repeat donors about 43%); tax-credit gifts should be stickier because they cost the donor nothing.
- The 90% rule is on SGO income, so overhead is capped at roughly 10% (85%-activity safe harbor exists for SGOs with separately raised admin money). On a $1,700 gift that is about $170 for everything the SGO does, including its own marketing.
- Affinity matters more than a planner. One advisor quoted in Financial Planning said donors usually give to a specific school, not a generic SGO. Arizona's long-running individual credit (the most mature example) has produced about 108,000 cumulative donors for its largest SGO in about 25 years.

## Sizing (bottom-up, my estimate)
- Fee a SGO could rationally pay: $25-75 per donor acquired (against about $170 of first-year overhead on a max gift, a 40-45% repeat rate, and generic nonprofit CAC of $50-150).
- Median 2030: about 3M donors x 2% share x $40 = about $2.4M a year; haircut to **$1.5M** for the slow ramp, solicitation compliance and SGO in-sourcing.
- Upside 2030: about 6M donors x 5% share x $50 = **$15M**. That needs a tax-software, payroll or adviser distribution deal that an 18-year-old will not close in 2027.
- Paid acquisition does not work on his side: if a donor lead costs $50-300 in paid search against bidders with eight-figure budgets, a $25-75 fee is underwater. Only organic search or a channel partner works.
- Tax-pro white label: about 350k preparers in the US, but free SGO-produced materials (ACE is already courting CPAs) cap willingness to pay.

## Can he reach and close customers?
- Small and new SGOs are reachable by cold email once state lists publish (Feb 15, 2027): the Iowa Catholic SGO (four dioceses), the Iowa Scholarship Granting Organization, Children's Tuition Fund (ACSI), CSF partner SGOs, Orthodox Jewish and Christian-school SGOs. But they are staffed by diocesan and school-network people with tiny budgets who are being asked to register, audit and administer first. Expect "free pilot, then maybe" answers.
- The big national SGOs will not buy donor tools from a teenager; they build in-house or buy from FACTS.
- The ed-choice SGO world is a closed, partisan network (AFC, dioceses, funders). His mentor (AV integration) and family have no warm path in. This is a real credibility gap, though a working free tool speaks for itself with small SGOs.
- Gen Z reach is irrelevant. Donors are taxpayers owing at least $1,700 in federal income tax, mostly parents and professionals aged 35-65.
- Family high-income network: useful as test users, but $1,700 per person caps the value of each.

## Legal and age
- Age is not a legal wall. An 18-year-old can form an LLC and sign contracts in almost all states. No professional license is needed for a calculator or referral site, but he must not prepare returns for pay without a PTIN, and "costs you nothing" is only true when liability is at least the gift and the donor does not double-dip with a state credit or deduction. Disclose that SGOs pay him.
- Real wall: charitable-solicitation law. A for-profit paid per donor by a charity can be a professional solicitor (registration, bond and contract filing in many states; for example a $25,000 bond in Louisiana, an annual registration in South Carolina), a commercial co-venturer (roughly twenty states regulate these; Alabama asks for a filing fee and $10,000 bond, and Illinois takes the position that co-venturers register), and under California's platform law a charitable fundraising platform. I could not find any ruling on a pure referral site. Counsel is needed ($2-10k) or he has to charge flat sponsorship fees, which breaks the "scales with donors" story.
- Federal rules do not forbid an SGO paying a vendor out of its overhead budget (I read the proposed regs; no commission ban, but the 90% spend rule and overhead sensitivity apply). Politically charged: the credit is contested, so neutral tone is hard and half the audience will distrust it.

## Time and cost to first revenue
- About $3k (LLC, domain, hosting, one attorney hour set on solicitation structure). MVP in 4-6 weeks is realistic for him.
- Earliest plausible first dollar: a flat-fee pilot from a small or new SGO in Q1 2027 (about 20 weeks). Per-donor revenue only after the Jan-Feb 2027 state lists and real giving, mostly Q4 2027 (about 50 weeks). This misses the 60-90 day criterion.

## Sharpened version (keeps the ambition, answers the findings)
**Credit Concierge, sold flat-fee to SGOs and embedded where taxpayers already decide.**
- Lead with the one thing a calculator does that a one-line explainer cannot: the net-cost and stacking engine for the 46.5M taxpayers eligible for both a state SGO credit and the federal credit (ordering rule, carryforward, nonrefundable cap, filing status, pass-through exclusion). Make it the best free tool on the internet and the thing CPAs link to.
- Get paid by SGOs on flat monthly state-level sponsorship slots ("preferred SGO in Iowa") instead of per-donor fees, to stay outside solicitor and co-venture registration, and disclose it.
- Distribution is the product: a white-label embed for preparers and advisers, and an API for the channels that actually reach taxpayers (tax software, payroll and HR benefit platforms, wealth managers). One industry write-up (surfaced in search, not read in full) notes tax preparers, advisers, employers and payroll systems could become important channels; that is the unbuilt part and where the $10M+ upside lives.
- Kill gates: no paid SGO pilot by Mar 31, 2027; fewer than 100 donor starts by the end of the 2027 tax season; or a TurboTax, Intuit or ACE donor calculator launching.
